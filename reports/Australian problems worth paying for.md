# Tax Agents' ATO Debt Blind Spot Wins

The best Australian opportunity in this research is a **cross-client ATO debt register and interest-and-penalty remission desk for small and mid-sized tax and BAS practices**, launched from reports agents can already download from the ATO's Online services for agents and priced at roughly **A$99–349 a month per practice** plus A$39 per ad hoc remission pack. It was the only one of nine verified finalists whose score held under verification (35/60, or 36 with a later super-guarantee module) while the other eight fell by five to twelve points, because the checkers found what practices already pay for ATO-connected add-ons but no product that rolls every client's debt, across every ATO account, into one register and then drafts and tracks remission requests. The money is visible: the ATO imposed **A$9.4bn of general interest charge (GIC) in FY2025**, receives **more than 125,000 remission requests a year** and reportedly issued **about 84,000 director penalty notices in 2024–25, up 136%**, yet a practitioner on Xero's ideas board says no report shows "ALL the debt which is owed by our clients"; since 22 January 2026 remissions must go in as forms through practice mail, agents report waits beyond 50 days, and the Tax Ombudsman found written requests succeed less often than the old phone route. The realistic prize is an estimated **A$2.7–9m ARR**, a solid bootstrapped business rather than a venture one, and your codebase gives a real but partial head start: the CSV tables, SQL, pivots, dashboard builder and OpenAI integration map straight onto version one, while the Lightspeed connector contributes nothing. The deciding risk is that GovReports, MYOB or ATOmate already offer the cross-client view, so a two-week hands-on check of those products comes before any code, followed by a 90-day test that kills the idea unless at least 3 of 10 pilot practices pay A$99 a month; if it fails, switch to the runner-up, a **security-of-payment deadline engine for specialty trade subcontractors in Queensland and Victoria**. Every figure here comes from search-result summaries and no customer was interviewed, so treat the case as a well-supported hypothesis rather than a proven finding.

## Verification cut eight of nine finalists by up to twelve points

The research ran in two rounds. Eight first-pass files covered business regulation, care and health providers, consumers and families, employment and payroll, professional and financial services, property and construction, retail and hospitality, and demand signals that cut across sectors. Together they profiled roughly a hundred Australian problems and scored each sector's leaders out of 60 on six criteria of 10 points each: pain, evidence of willingness to pay, solution gap, Australian market size, feasibility for a solo founder, and defensibility. Six verification files then re-tested nine finalists against local competitors, licensing limits and data access, and they override the first pass wherever the two disagree. Evidence quality is uniformly limited. Every attempt to open a web page failed with DNS errors or proxy refusals, so each figure below is as reported by a search-result summary of the cited page; many of those pages are vendor or law-firm marketing; no Reddit, Whirlpool or Seek data could be read; and nobody in any target market was interviewed.

A problem qualifies only if it passes two tests together: evidence that Australians already pay money for the job, and a verified absence of a good solution. Most candidates passed one test and failed the other.

**Licensing barriers killed the family-facing ideas.** In Queensland, practising law without qualification is an offence that has drawn a three-month jail term ([LSC Qld](https://www.lsc.qld.gov.au/discipline/disciplinary-decisions/unlawful-operators/2017-2016)), and an unregistered company that prepares an estate's tax return for a fee faces penalties up to **A$412,500** ([TPB](https://www.tpb.gov.au/registration)), so the parts of estate administration families pay most for sit behind lawyers and tax agents. Meanwhile the free administrative layer keeps growing: the Australian Death Notification Service claims more than 80 participating organisations ([Digital NSW](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/article/australian-death-notification-service-reducing-burden-at-most-trying-of-times)), and AustralianSuper's 75-person bereavement centre resolves 80% of cases within four months ([AustralianSuper](https://www.australiansuper.com/about-us/newsroom/2026/08/australiansuper-bereavement-centre)). The aged-care desk passed the licence test, because RAD and DAP payments are not yet financial products — Aged Care Steps asked in February 2026 for the law to be changed so that they would be ([Financial Newswire](https://financialnewswire.com.au/financial-planning/regulatory-gaps-in-aged-care-advice-called-out)) — but it lost ground to free calculators already updated to September 2026 rates ([Richify](https://www.richify.ai/au/tools/aged-care-fees-calculator)), Challenger's free adviser calculator ([Challenger](https://challenger.com.au/adviser/How-we-can-help/Aged-care-planning)) and paid paperwork services such as Retirement Essentials at **A$335–965** ([Retirement Essentials](https://retirementessentials.com.au/age-pension/fees/)).

**Data access killed Payday Super monitoring.** SuperStream error messages pass only between the fund and the gateway that sent the payment ([ATO conformance guide](https://softwaredevelopers.ato.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/Conformance_Implementation_Guide_Contributions_V3_FINAL.pdf)), and no payroll platform or clearing house documents an API showing whether a fund accepted a contribution. The ATO is also softening enforcement until 30 June 2027 for employers who fix errors promptly ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/super-for-employers/missed-or-late-payday-super-payments/getting-it-right-compliance-in-the-first-year-of-payday-super)), and bookkeepers who want one view can already pay Wrkr **A$5–79 per client a month** for payment plus tracking ([Wrkr](https://www.wrkr.com.au/payday-super-bookkeepers)). The fuel tax credit idea fell furthest, on one ATO sentence: its A$345M gap is net, and "a gap of 3.7% means overclaims have exceeded underclaims" ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/research-and-statistics/in-detail/tax-gap/a-h-tax-gaps/fuel-tax-credits-gap/latest-estimate-and-trends)). Most of the scheme's 182,000 claimants claim less than A$10,000 a year ([ATO stakeholder group](https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/consultation/in-detail/stakeholder-relationship-groups-key-messages/fuel-schemes-stakeholder-group/fuel-schemes-stakeholder-group-key-messages-22-july-2026)), telematics vendors hold ATO class rulings ([CR 2026/69](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf/pbr/cr2026-069.pdf)), and taxpayer alert TA 2021/3 names commission-based fees as a driver of overclaiming ([TA 2021/3](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/view.htm?docid=%22TPA%2FTA20213%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001%22)), which puts a success-fee recovery desk in the regulator's sights.

**Cheap labour and bundling sank the NDIS desk.** The 90-day claim limit from 1 December 2026 is real ([Department FAQs](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/securing-the-future-of-the-ndis-for-future-generations-provider-faqs.pdf)) and extra checks on older claims began on 15 October ([NDIA](https://www.ndis.gov.au/news/11575-increasing-integrity-checks-older-claims)), but after the cliff a late line is simply rejected, so "recovery" becomes ageing prevention that client-management vendors can add as a column. Only 10,842 of 280,258 active providers served agency-managed participants in the June 2026 quarter, the rest billing through plan managers or self-managing participants ([NDIA dashboard](https://www.ndis.gov.au/media/8782/download)), and providers can already buy managed claiming for about **A$1,500–3,500 a month** ([Care BPO](https://carebpo.com.au/scaling-ndis-provider-administration-with-back-office-support/)). The retail ideas lost to the platforms. Westpac cut its flat merchant rate from 1.20% to 0.79% ([Westpac](https://www.westpac.com.au/about-westpac/media/media-releases/2026/24-september/)) and CommBank from 1.10% to 0.99% ([CommBank](https://www.commbank.com.au/articles/newsroom/2026/08/commbank-reduces-fee-card-surcharging.html)), free sites already publish which providers cut ([Merchant Compare](https://merchantcompare.com.au/articles/merchant-fees-after-surcharge-ban)), and the RBA's acquirer fee benchmarks start on 30 October 2026 ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/review-of-retail-payments-regulation/2026-03/conclusions-paper/impact-and-implementation.html)). On the reporting side, Square launched a Multi-Account Suite for organisations spanning many accounts ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/legal/general/franchise)) and Lightspeed sells franchise head-office reporting across retail accounts ([CFA listing](https://cfa.ca/supplier-directory/listing/lightspeed)), removing most of the cross-merchant gap your Lightspeed connector was meant to fill.

**Security of payment fell least among the losers and was the only finalist whose solution-gap score rose.** Queensland's registry received 559 adjudication applications in FY2025–26 and ruled 82 invalid, mostly for missed deadlines, with 61 of them small progress claims ([The Good Builder](https://thegoodbuilder.com.au/14-1-million-in-queensland-adjudication-claims-ruled-invalid-missed-deadlines-topped-the-list/)), and no software was found that drafts adjudication applications or manages enforcement. It lost points because the national pool is only about 2,000 applications a year and the cheap claim tools that exist stop at the claim ([PayClaim](https://payclaim.com.au/adjudication-explained-australian-subcontractors/)).

**The ATO desk was the only finalist whose total held.** Verification cut its gap score by a point because GovReports, HandiTax and ATOmate sit next door, and raised its willingness-to-pay score by a point because it found what practices already pay for ATO-connected add-ons. Strictly, then, every finalist lost points on solution gap, but not every finalist lost points overall. Across the nine the average fall was about seven points, almost entirely on solution gap, and the damage came from bundled features, bootstrapped local vendors, free government services and outsourced labour rather than funded startups. That repeats the global round's Australian findings, where 28 bootstrapped vendors crowded Tranche 2 AML ([CompareAML](https://www.compareaml.com.au/compare)) and bank-run Confirmation of Payee, at 150 million checks in its first year, commoditised payment-redirection checks ([eCommerceNews](https://ecommercenews.com.au/story/confirmation-of-payee-passes-150-million-checks-in-year)).

## Practices cannot see what their clients owe the ATO

**The buyer** is the principal, or the lodgement and debt manager, at a registered tax or BAS agent practice of roughly 2–30 staff that serves small businesses in trades, hospitality, construction or transport and has at least 20 clients carrying ATO debt. Secondary buyers are firms that market help with ATO debt and director penalties, and the tax-debt lawyers and insolvency advisers who take the hardest cases.

**These practices have no single view of what their clients owe.** One practitioner wrote on Xero's ideas board: "I am not aware of a report we can generate through the ATO portal which tells us ALL the debt which is owed by our clients, across all their accounts, Tax, GST, Super etc. Yes there is an integrated debt report we can run, but it only shows the ICA debt", so a client with only an income-tax debt means logging into that client alone ([Xero Product Ideas](https://productideas.xero.com/forums/967133-reports-tax/suggestions/51365269-au-tax-integrate-live-ato-client-account-balance)). Director penalty notices widen the blind spot, because they go to directors personally and so never appear on the company's running balance or integrated client account (ICA) ([ATO Community](https://community.ato.gov.au/s/question/a0JRF000003xv29/p00401175)). The agent portal offers per-client account views and downloadable reports ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-and-super-professionals/digital-services/in-detail/online-services-for-agents-user-guide/reports-and-forms/reports)), but the client account running balance report is not available through the Practitioner Lodgment Service ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-and-super-professionals/digital-services/bas-agent-online-services-guide)), and the Tax Ombudsman is reviewing the portal after agents raised "several issues and concerns" ([Tax Ombudsman](https://www.theioi.org/downloads/2081j/Tax%20Ombudsman%20-%20Quarterly%20newsletter%20-%20March%202026%20-%20EN.pdf)).

**Remission became paperwork in January 2026.** From 22 January, requests to remit GIC, shortfall interest and failure-to-lodge penalties must be lodged on standard ATO forms through the portal's practice mail, and phone requests are limited to amounts above A$2,500, which are passed to a dedicated team rather than decided on the call ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-and-super-professionals/for-tax-professionals/tax-professionals-newsroom/changes-to-interest-and-ftl-penalty-remission-requests)). The GIC form is an Excel file, one per taxpayer and interest type ([Accountants Desktop](https://accountantsdesktop.com.au/blog/news_feed/download-the-new-and-improved-gic-remission-application-form/)); practitioners download each client's transaction CSV and total the GIC by period by hand ([Blackwattle Tax](https://blackwattletax.com.au/tax/how-to-request-gic-remission/)); and a tax-law firm that sells letter templates warns "there is no formula" ([Waterhouse Tax Lawyers](https://waterhousetaxlawyers.com.au/tax-debt/template-letter-ato-remission-request-gic/)). Agents told the Ombudsman they had waited more than **50 days** for decisions, and one recalled that the phone process used to clear "four or five remissions in an afternoon" ([Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/regulation/22224-tax-agents-air-frustrations-about-delays-with-new-gic-remission-forms)). The Commissioner called the longer waits the "trade-off" for more consistent decisions ([Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/tax-compliance/22861-longer-wait-times-the-trade-off-for-more-consistent-gic-decisions-says-commissioner)).

**The money at stake is large and growing.** The ATO imposed **A$9.4bn of GIC in FY2025** ([AccountingTimes](https://www.accountingtimes.com.au/tax/gic-impositions-soared-to-9-4-billion-in-fy2025-ato-data-reveals)) and handles **more than 125,000 remission requests a year** ([Public Accountant](https://publicaccountant.com.au/features/ato-tax-gic-interest-reform/)). Secondary advisory sources report **about 84,000 director penalty notices to directors of about 64,000 companies in 2024–25, a 136% increase** ([Paris Financial](https://www.parisfinancial.com.au/director-penalty-notice-australia/)), and lawyers say ATO automation has shortened the gap between a client going quiet and a penalty notice or garnishee ([Ironbridge Legal](https://ironbridgelegal.com.au/ato-debt-recovery-director-penalty-notices/)). The Tax Ombudsman's March 2026 review, "In the interest of fairness", found the ATO's approach produced "unduly harsh outcomes" and reported approval rates of 92% for phone requests against 76% for written ones, trade-press figures that should be checked against the report; the ATO accepted the recommendations and committed to a central decision function and published criteria ([Tax Ombudsman](https://taxombudsman.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/In-the-interest-of-fairness-A-review-into-GIC-remission-Tax-Ombudsman-March-2026.pdf); [SmartCompany](https://smartcompany.com.au/tax/general-interest-charge-ato-agrees-recommendations-ombudsman)), with some implementation items due in later FY27 quarters ([Tax Ombudsman](https://taxombudsman.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Implementation-plan-GIC-remission-published.pdf)). Practitioner anger is public: a tax-body chair called one decision evidence of "a failed framework" ([Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/tax-compliance/22453-gic-remission-decision-highlights-a-failed-framework-practitioner-says)), and professional-development webinars on the Ombudsman's report are selling to advisers ([Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com.au/e/gic-under-scrutiny-what-the-ombudsmans-report-means-for-advisers-tickets-1982854636595)). The ATO has published case studies showing it will likely grant remission for natural disaster, theft, domestic violence, the collapse of a major debtor, bereavement or ill health, and likely refuse it for a general downturn or money spent on expansion ([Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/tax-compliance/22077-who-gets-a-gic-remission-ato-releases-case-studies-to-clarify-relief-provisions)). Those criteria are exactly what a drafting tool can map a client's circumstances against.

| GovReports with ATOInsights, about A$535 a year | Client account balances and transactions plus "real-time ATO data" ([GovReports](https://www.govreports.com.au/tax-agent); pricing per [a competitor's page](https://enterprisedna.co/omni/instead-of/govreports)) | Cross-client roll-up unconfirmed; no remission workflow found |
| ATOmate | AI review of ATO notices, including debt notices, for XPM, Xero Tax and APS users ([ATOmate](https://atomate.com.au/atomate/)) | Correspondence, not a live balance register |
| Xero Tax and XPM | Return prefill; job and work-in-progress reporting ([Uku](https://getuku.com/articles/xero-practice-manager-review/)) | The "Total ATO Debt" request drew only a reply that Xero is "monitoring the interest" |
| MYOB AE and Practice | Possibly a partial cross-client activity-statement and ICA report ([Xero Product Ideas](https://productideas.xero.com/forums/967127-practice-tools/suggestions/46192636-au-tax-outstanding-activity-statement-report)) | Scope unverified; appears limited to ICA |
| LodgeiT | Dashboard of forms in progress, client by client ([QuickBooks help](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-au/gst-and-bas/getting-started-with-lodgeit/00/271452)) | No debt view |
| Templates and letters | The Excel form and law-firm letter templates | GIC totalled by hand; nothing tracks requests or outcomes |

**Several changes make this the right moment.** The January 2026 form change turned a phone call into a document job, and the Ombudsman's reforms are steering decisions through a central team and published criteria, so the quality of the written evidence now matters more than it did on the phone. Rising GIC and penalty notices make client debt a growing risk for practices and directors alike. Payday Super's year-one leniency ends on 30 June 2027; the ATO already checks compliance by comparing payroll data with what funds report ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/super-for-employers/payday-super/missed-or-late-payday-super-payments/how-we-check-payday-super-compliance)) and law firms are warning directors about personal liability ([Taylor David](https://taylordavid.com/payday-super-directors-liability/)), so from FY2027–28 super-guarantee shortfalls will increasingly appear as ATO-raised debts on the same client accounts. The technology is ready too: the ATO's Standard Business Reporting client-account service already gives registered software providers each client's amounts due and an interest amount "forecast to a future date" ([SBR](https://www.sbr.gov.au/digital-service-providers/developer-tools/australian-taxation-office-ato/obligation-management-oblmgt/account-management-accmgt)), which removes the hardest calculation from a later live-data version, and language models can now draft the circumstances narrative against the ATO's criteria for the agent to review and sign.

## Build the debt register first and sell remission outcomes

| Element | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Buyer | Principal or debt manager at a 2–30-person tax or BAS practice with 20+ clients carrying ATO debt |
| Problem | No single view of client debt across all accounts; invisible penalty-notice exposure; slow, form-based, hand-drafted remissions |
| Wedge | A free 48-hour "debt health check" built from the practice's own portal exports |
| Version 1 (8–10 weeks) | Upload portal CSVs, then a cross-client debt register with ageing, an interest estimate, penalty-risk flags, remission packs and a request tracker |
| Price | A$99 a month (up to 50 debt clients), A$199 (up to 200), A$349 (unlimited); A$39 per ad hoc remission pack |
| Market | 1,500–3,000 serviceable practices; about A$2.7–9m ARR (estimate) |
| First channels | Commenters on Xero's ideas threads; firms marketing penalty-notice help; IPA members via a professional-development webinar; tax-debt lawyers |
| Kill test | Within 90 days, at least 3 of 10 pilot practices pay A$99 a month |

**Version one needs no ATO API and no payroll connection.** The practice drags in three reports it can already download from the agent portal — the per-client accounts summary with overdue amounts and balances, the client account running balance report going back to 2009, and the activity statement obligations report ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-and-super-professionals/digital-services/online-services-for-agents/online-services-for-agents-user-guide/accounts-and-payments)) — plus its client list as a CSV. From these the product builds a register across all clients by account type (income tax, ICA and activity statements, others) and age of debt. It shows estimated interest accruing at the current quarterly GIC rate, flags payment plans and debts on hold where the data shows them, and raises a "director penalty exposure" flag for companies with overdue PAYG withholding, GST or super guarantee, or late activity statements; it flags risk rather than notices, because the notices never appear on company accounts. The remission pack builds the GIC-by-period table from the transaction CSV, pre-fills the ATO's Excel form, drafts a circumstances narrative mapped to the ATO's published case-study categories and its remission practice statement with an evidence checklist, and checks that the figures reconcile to the account history before the agent lodges. A tracker then records the date each request went through practice mail, flags requests older than 50 days, sends reminders, and logs the outcome and amount remitted, producing a "GIC saved for clients" report the practice can use to justify its fee and market its service. The verification notes estimate eight to ten weeks for this scope with your stack.

**Versions two and three widen the data and the buyer.** Version two connects to Xero or MYOB to reconcile each client's ICA ledger account against the ATO balance, catching unposted GIC and misallocated payments; since 2 March 2026 Xero has priced API access in tiers by connected organisations and data volume ([Xero](https://developer.xero.com/pricing); [Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/technology/21986-all-you-can-eat-xero-data-buffet-comes-to-abrupt-end)), which adds cents per client and a certification step rather than a barrier. In parallel, start registering as a digital service provider so the product can read client account data live through Standard Business Reporting, including the ATO's own interest forecasts. Version three adds a super-guarantee module that flags charge assessments, SG-driven ICA debits and SG-related penalty-notice risk as Payday Super enforcement hardens after mid-2027, bringing BAS agents and bookkeepers in as buyers. Before building it, settle a conflict in the notes: one summary of the ATO's first-year guidance said late payments no longer reduce the charge, while the ATO's deadlines page says a late contribution can reduce it ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/super-for-employers/paying-super-on-payday/payment-deadlines-for-payday-super)). Launching as a debt-and-remission desk and adding super guarantee later is how the "ATO exceptions desk for practices" from the verification notes should grow, because the two halves only converge once payroll failures start appearing as ATO debt.

**Pricing follows what practices already pay.** GovReports' ATOInsights add-on costs about A$535 a year, roughly A$45 a month, and FYI charges A$30–70 per user a month with a five-user minimum ([FYI](https://fyi.app/pricing/)), so tiers of A$99, A$199 and A$349 sit above an add-on, because this product does work and produces outcomes, and below a workflow suite. Remission volume cannot carry a subscription on its own: 125,000 requests spread across a few thousand practices is fewer than ten per practice a year on average. That is why remission is a feature of the recurring register, with a A$39 pack for practices that want it only occasionally.

**The first ten customers come from public complaints rather than cold demos.** Start with three or four practitioners who asked Xero for this on the "Total ATO Debt", live-ATO-data and outstanding-activity-statement threads, who are named publicly and reachable directly ([Xero Product Ideas](https://productideas.xero.com/forums/967124-projects-tracking/suggestions/51675454-tax-manager-live-ato-data-in-dashboard-ato-acco)). Add two or three firms that market help with ATO debt and director penalties ([HOC](https://www.hoc.com.au/ato-debt-in-2026-director-penalty-notice-risks-for-business-owners/); [B&T Advisory](https://www.bandtadvisory.au/director-penalty-notices/)), two IPA small-practice members recruited through a free webinar on winning written remissions after the Ombudsman's review, and one or two tax-debt lawyers as power users and referrers, since Blackwattle Tax and Waterhouse already publish remission guides. Each gets the free health check, then a 30-day pilot, then a A$99 bill. After the first ten, the next channels are an IPA partnership, an opinion piece in Accountants Daily using anonymised remission-success data, accountant Facebook and LinkedIn groups, integrations with XPM, FYI or GovReports (partnering with GovReports is more realistic than competing with it), and the Xero App Store once ledger reconciliation exists.

**The Australian market is small but sufficient for a solo founder.** Sizing rests on counts the notes could not verify. The only national BAS-agent figure found is "just over 15,000" from 2017 ([Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/regulation/10485-tpb-reports-healthy-growth-in-bas-agents)), and the notes assume, without checking the Tax Practitioners Board register, about 10,000–15,000 practices serving small-business clients, of which 10–20% hold enough debt clients to care. That gives 1,500–3,000 serviceable practices and, at A$150–250 a month, **about A$2.7–9m ARR**. Even if every remission request in the country ran through a A$39 pack, it would add about A$4.9m a year, and in practice far less, which again shows that remission is a feature rather than the business. A realistic solo path is 50–150 paying practices in the first 18 months, roughly A$0.1–0.35m ARR (my estimate), with the super-guarantee module adding BAS-agent and bookkeeper buyers from 2027–28. New Zealand has a similar agent-debt problem on different systems, but it was not researched.

**Does your codebase give you a head start? Yes for the build, no for the moat, and not at all through the Lightspeed integration.** Version one reads ATO report exports, not POS data, so the Lightspeed OAuth connection and item-level sales sync are irrelevant. The CSV tables, SQL querying, pivot tables, dashboard builder and OpenAI integration, on the other hand, are close to exactly the shell a debt register and remission drafter needs, which is why eight to ten weeks is credible, and your experience building a resumable OAuth sync carries over to the Xero and MYOB connectors in version two. What the code cannot supply is the hard part: the semantics of ATO accounts, GIC arithmetic, remission criteria, the ATO's security framework for software that touches its systems, and the trust of practitioners who hold confidential client data. For comparison, the Lightspeed asset is a genuine advantage in only two places, the consented roll-up across separately owned merchants (verified down to 28/60) and a POS leakage watchdog (never verified, about 35/60 on the first pass), and neither beats the ATO desk.

## Incumbents, the ATO and upload fatigue can still kill it

**Incumbent extension is the first risk.** GovReports and MYOB come closest to a cross-client view, and HandiTax already pulls each client's account. ATOmate is the most dangerous neighbour: it already reads ATO correspondence, publishes penalty-notice content for accountants ([ATOmate](https://atomate.com.au/2025/07/11/how-accountants-can-protect-clients-from-ato-debt-and-director-penalty-notices/)) and is installed in XPM and APS practices, so a debt roll-up and remission tracking would be a short step for it. Xero could ship the "Total ATO Debt" report, and the ATO could add an all-debt report to its agent portal after the Ombudsman's review. If the ATO also restores fast phone remission or introduces automatic remission of small amounts, the remission half of the product loses most of its pain, so the register must be able to stand on its own.

**Licensing is manageable if the product stays a tool that agents operate.** Sold to registered agents who review and lodge, the product supports a tax agent service rather than providing one. But the Tax Practitioners Board's guidance for digital service providers, updated 30 April 2026, says registration is probably required where a provider configures a system to deliver a specific tax outcome or runs a help desk giving customised advice ([TPB](https://www.tpb.gov.au/node/192012)). The agent must therefore edit and sign every draft, the product must never sell remission directly to taxpayers, and a one-hour legal check should come before launch. The ATO's Operational Security Framework covers software that transmits tax data through ATO digital services and requires audit logging, encryption, hosting controls and annual review ([ATO](https://softwaredevelopers.ato.gov.au/scope-dsp-operational-security-framework)). A tool that only ingests reports agents have already downloaded appears to fall outside it, an inference the ATO's Digital Partnership Office should confirm, together with the time and cost of registering for live data. Practices owe clients confidentiality under the Board's code and must meet Privacy Act duties, so host client ATO data in Australia from day one: confirm that your Firebase and Vercel setup keeps data and functions in Australian regions, and offer a data-processing agreement. Do not build a scraper for the agent portal, because agents' own security obligations make that unacceptable.

**Data access and adoption are the quieter risks.** Version one depends on practices downloading and re-uploading exports every month; if they won't, the register goes stale, and live data depends on the registration path above. Remission decisions take more than 50 days, so a 90-day test cannot measure approval rates and must measure time saved and pack completeness instead. Practices also have a cheap alternative: outsourcing providers claim that 30% of accounting firms already use offshore staff to some degree, a vendor figure ([Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/business/21058-accounting-firms-turn-to-offshoring-amid-worker-shortages)), so the product has to make offshore staff faster or beat their cost.

Before committing, settle whether GovReports' ATOInsights, MYOB AE or Practice, HandiTax or ATOmate already provide a cross-client, all-account register; whether practices will pay A$99 or more after seeing their own health check; how many debt clients and remission requests a typical practice really has; whether practices will keep uploading exports; the TPB and security-framework scope; the ATO's post-Ombudsman roadmap; and that your hosting keeps data in Australia.

| Window | Test | Pass | Kill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Trial GovReports' ATOInsights for two weeks and walk through MYOB AE or Practice, HandiTax, ATOmate, LodgeiT, CCH iFirm, XPM, FYI and Karbon; get a one-hour legal read on TPB and security-framework scope; email the ATO's Digital Partnership Office | No product shows a cross-client, all-account debt register with ageing and export; the legal read confirms an agent-operated tool | A product already does it: switch to a remission-only add-on, or partner with that product |
| Weeks 1–4 | 15–20 discovery calls with practices holding 20+ debt clients | At least 8 rank cross-client debt and penalty-notice visibility a top-three problem; at least 5 share anonymised portal exports | Most say their software already shows it, or typical firms hold fewer than 10 debt clients |
| Weeks 3–8 | Build version one on design partners' exports; run the free 48-hour health check for 10 practices | At least 6 of 10 find debt or penalty-notice risk they had not been tracking | The health checks surface nothing new |
| Weeks 8–13 | Convert to A$99+ a month after a 30-day pilot; track re-uploads and time per remission pack | At least 3 of 10 pay; at least half re-upload in month two; pack time roughly halves against each practice's baseline | Fewer than 3 pay; fewer than half re-upload; firms average under 5 remissions and under 10 debt clients a year |

Three standing triggers apply throughout the test. If the ATO adds an all-debt report to the agent portal, narrow the product to remission only or to whatever the ATO leaves out. If it restores fast phone remission or automatic remission of small amounts, narrow it to the register. And if the Digital Partnership Office signals that registration for live data would take more than about nine months or A$30,000, stay CSV-only and reassess.

## A subcontractor deadline engine is the fallback

If the ATO desk fails its test, the runner-up is a **security-of-payment deadline engine for specialty trade subcontractors in Queensland and Victoria**, with a lawyer-signed adjudication pack added later. It edges out the aged-care desk, which scored a point higher, because it is the only finalist whose solution gap widened under verification, its losses are measured directly, it reuses the same data stack and the bookkeeper channel, and a cheap data-driven audit can test it. The aged-care desk is the better choice only if you would rather sell to care providers.

**The pain is concrete and rising.** In Queensland, 82 of 559 adjudication applications in FY2025–26 were ruled invalid, claiming $14.1m, with missed statutory deadlines the leading cause, and **61 of the 82 were small progress claims under about $56,700** ([The Good Builder](https://thegoodbuilder.com.au/14-1-million-in-queensland-adjudication-claims-ruled-invalid-missed-deadlines-topped-the-list/)). Volumes are climbing: Queensland went from 324 valid applications in FY2024–25 ([Lawyers Weekly](https://www.lawyersweekly.com.au/sme-law/44981-adjudication-is-won-on-site-not-in-the-registry)) to 477, and New South Wales handled roughly 940–1,060 in FY2023–24 ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/victoria-s-security-of-payment-regime-set-for-extensive-reform)). Victoria's reforms of 15 April 2026 widened what can be adjudicated and barred respondents from raising reasons not in their payment schedule ([MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/changes-to-victorias-security-of-payment-regime-have-now-commenced)), which rewards subcontractors who analyse a schedule fast. A 2026 joint survey led by Master Electricians Australia found that more than 60% of trade contractors had gone unpaid because a contractor became insolvent, that 65% of those recovered nothing, and that 35.5% did not know their rights ([MEA](https://www.masterelectricians.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/MEA-Survey-Results-Doc.pdf)).

**Existing tools stop at the claim.** PayClaim charges $69–149 per claim and says it does not handle adjudication or enforcement ([PayClaim](https://payclaim.com.au/adjudication-explained-australian-subcontractors/)), ClaimDone sells a $49 claim draft ([ClaimDone](https://claimdone.com.au/subcontractor-payment-dispute-australia-demand-vs-sop/)), and Payapps charges $65 per claim ([Payapps](https://www.payapps.com/pricing-subcontractors/)). Lawyers advertise fixed fees for adjudication applications but publish no prices, legal costs are generally not recoverable from the other side ([Odyssey Legal](https://www.odysseylegal.com.au/construction-payment-claims-queensland-adjudication-process-explained/)), and adjudicator fixed fees alone run about $1,000–3,410 for claims up to $40,000 ([RICS](https://rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/dispute-resolution-service/Adjudication-Application_Tasmania_April-2024.pdf)), which prices many small subcontractors out of help.

**The product, buyer and price.** The buyer is an electrical, plumbing, fire or HVAC subcontractor with $2–20m turnover, 5–60 staff, an office manager or bookkeeper, and Xero or MYOB. Version one syncs invoices and progress claims from Xero or MYOB, computes each payment deadline and adjudication window under the relevant state Act, including the 30-business-day window the Queensland regulator states for lodging after a disputed schedule ([QBCC](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/node/1366)), escalates by SMS and email before each deadline, shows each head contractor's payment behaviour on a dashboard, and uses AI to compare each payment schedule with the claim it answers. Version two adds a $1,500–3,500 adjudication pack reviewed and signed by a partner construction lawyer at a fixed fee. Pricing is $79–149 a month, or $990–1,490 a year through trade associations, for an estimated $0.35–1.2m a year in revenue within three years, sold through Master Electricians Australia, Master Plumbers Queensland, trade bookkeepers (overlapping the ATO desk's channel) and the Xero App Store, where Retention Track already lists a retention app ([Xero App Store](https://apps.xero.com/au/app/retention-track)).

**Its risks differ from the winner's, and so does its test.** A missed statutory deadline is fatal to a claim, so the engine must alert on the earliest plausible deadline and leave sign-off to a human. Whether a non-lawyer platform may draft adjudication submissions or charge outcome-linked fees is untested in Australia, and the Queensland Legal Services Commissioner does prosecute consultants for unlawful legal practice ([QLS Proctor](https://www.qlsproctor.com.au/?p=2778)). The test is a free 12-month deadline audit of Xero claims for Queensland and Victorian subcontractors. Upgrade the idea only if five subcontractors pay for the audit, one association agrees to co-market, one law firm agrees to sign off packs at a fixed $1,000–2,000, and legal advice confirms a compliant fee structure; kill it if no law firm will partner at a price that keeps packs under about $3,500.

**The aged-care admission finance pack is the third option.** Residential providers would pay about A$250–500 per new resident for a completed Services Australia means-assessment pack, a factual comparison of RAD, DAP and mixed payments, and a summary of the resident agreement, against a base of 77,400 permanent residential admissions in 2024–25 ([GEN](https://www.gen-agedcaredata.gov.au/getmedia/edfb1866-0384-411f-b957-6cb11e74d966/Admissions-into-aged-care-fact-sheet-2024-25.pdf?ext=.pdf)). Its willingness-to-pay case is weaker than the deadline engine's. Providers already pay placement consultants A$2,950 a resident ([Aged Care Decisions](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/faq/)), but the idea that they also have a cash-flow motive to speed up means assessments is an inference resting on an interim-period bill that had not passed ([Challenger](https://www.challenger.com.au/adviser/knowledge-hub/Articles/7-commonly-asked-aged-care-questions-in-2026)). Kill it if fewer than 2 of 6 providers will pay A$250 per admission.

## Conclusion

Across every Australian sector examined, the problems that clearly pass both tests — people pay today, and no good solution exists — share one shape: a government counterparty changed a process, but nobody built the cross-client view the intermediaries need. Tax agents hold dozens of clients' ATO relationships while the portal shows one at a time, and subcontractors' bookkeepers hold the claims while no tool watches the statutory clocks. Selling to the intermediary that already holds many clients' government relationships proved more valuable than any particular connector. The ideas that leaned on the founder's own data asset, POS sync for franchisors and card costs, lost the most under verification, because the platforms that own that data ship the same features natively.

Two implications follow for the winner. The ATO is both the source of the pain and its most likely competitor, since the Ombudsman's reforms could repair the remission half within a year or two, so the business should rest on the debt register and penalty-notice risk, which the ATO has no plan to fix. And the asset that compounds is outcome data — which remission narratives, mapped to which published criteria, actually win — something no template vendor or practice suite collects, and the thing that would make the desk hard to copy once the code itself is commonplace.
