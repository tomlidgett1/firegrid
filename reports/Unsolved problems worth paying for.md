# Medicaid Application Desk Survives the Cull

The single best opportunity in this research is a **done-for-you Medicaid long-term-care application desk for independent and small-chain US nursing homes**, launched in one state (Ohio) and priced at roughly **$1,000–$1,500 per approved application, paid by the facility and never billed to families**. It is the only finalist whose core thesis survived the verification round: facilities carry months of unpaid care while each resident's application sits "Medicaid pending", people already pay for this paperwork (about $3,000 per case to facility-partnered vendors, about $10,000 to elder-law firms, $60,000–75,000 a year for in-house coordinators), and while software that *tracks* these cases now exists, no product was found that *does* the work end to end. A dated trigger lands in about ten weeks: for applications made from **1 January 2027**, retroactive coverage for nursing-home applicants falls from three months to two, so every late or refiled application starts costing the facility money it used to recover. The realistic prize is a cash-generating **$2–5M ARR business across a few states**, drawn from a national pool the notes estimate at roughly $300–400M a year, which makes it an unglamorous services-plus-software company rather than a venture-scale category. Every other finalist fared worse under verification, and the high-ranking ideas that were never verified should be expected to lose ground the same way. Because all of this rests on search-result summaries and nobody in the target market was interviewed, treat it as a hypothesis and settle it with a 60-day, pay-on-approval pilot whose one deciding question is whether facilities will pay a new vendor when family-billed vendors look free to them. Geography is the main practical cost: a builder based in Australia needs a US entity and a US eligibility specialist from day one, and if that is unworkable, the best home-market alternative is a statement-and-claims checking layer for Australia's Support at Home aged-care providers, smaller but buildable immediately.

## Verification knocked out four of the five finalists

The notes came in two rounds. Eight first-sweep files covered SMBs and trades, healthcare, finance and legal, property and energy, industrial and logistics, consumer life admin, knowledge work, and cross-sector regulatory signals, producing more than 110 candidate problems and a scored shortlist for each sector; five verification files then re-tested the leaders. Evidence quality is uniform and limited. Page fetches failed throughout with DNS errors or proxy refusals, Reddit, Hacker News search and G2 reviews could not be read, most first-sweep researchers exhausted a shared search budget after roughly 15–45 queries, and every figure below is "as reported by" the search summary of the cited page. The cross-sector researcher compensated by keyword-scanning a public mirror of the YC directory, 6,306 companies with about 1,982 in 2024–2026 batches, to judge crowding ([yc-oss/api](https://github.com/yc-oss/api)). That proxy turned out to be the first sweep's biggest blind spot.

**ADA Title II accessibility remediation for US public entities** topped the cross-sector sweep at **43/60** on the strength of hard legal dates and zero recent YC entrants. Verification found a crowded market with a moving deadline. DOJ's interim final rule of 20 April 2026 pushed compliance to **26 April 2027** for entities serving 50,000+ people and **26 April 2028** for smaller entities and special districts ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/20/2026-07663/extension-of-compliance-dates-for-nondiscrimination-on-the-basis-of-disability-accessibility-of-web)), and DOJ's agenda lists a further cost-cutting re-examination with timetable "TBD" ([JD Supra](https://jdsupra.com/topics/title-ii/website-accessibility/websites/)). On the supply side, CivicPlus bought Streamline, a special-district website vendor serving 2,500+ agencies, along with its DocAccess PDF tool ([CivicPlus](https://www.civicplus.com/news/nn/civicplus-acquires-streamline/)); DocAccess was built into Diligent's BoardDocs and iCompass agenda platforms in September 2026 ([Diligent](https://www.diligent.com/company/newsroom/diligent-announces-integration-with-civicplus-docaccess)); and Siteimprove shipped a PDF remediation agent in June 2026 ([Siteimprove](https://www.siteimprove.com/launch/spring-2026-release/)). Prices are collapsing underneath them: an MIT-licensed ASU/AWS pipeline tags PDFs for about $0.015 per ten pages plus Adobe API fees ([AWS Marketplace](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-hvqjpfphsec6y)), a higher-education pilot put AI remediation at about **$0.06 a page against $4–5 by hand** ([CNI](https://www.cni.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/CNI_Project_Tressler.pdf)), and one special district already buys a bundled subscription for about **$4,800 a year** ([CivicIQ](https://civiciq.com/public-contract/907f93fe-b810-4d20-b973-0fa3babda110)). Buyers clearly pay; the trouble is that solutions exist and are being bundled, which leaves a $1–3M services niche in human-verified remediation for larger cities, universities and state agencies.

**Medicaid work-requirement retention sold to managed-care plans** (41/60) was killed in its original form, because the first sweep's "no YC company" finding was wrong. **Fortuna Health**, backed by YC and a16z with an $18M Series A in July 2025 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250721481890/en)), went live with a West Virginia Medicaid plan on 12 October 2026 ([News From The States](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/node/432252)). Findhelp bought Uno Health ([BuiltIn](https://www.builtinaustin.com/articles/findhelp-acquires-uno-health-to-modernize-public-benefits-20251023)) and reportedly supports social-care coordination for 190 of 282 Medicaid MCOs ([Unite Us](https://uniteus.com/medicaid-community-engagement/)), and CMS is building **Emmy**, a free open-source verification service ([CMS](https://cms.gov/medicaid-chip/community-engagement-support/eligibility-made-easy)). The rule also weakens the buyer: plans "cannot determine enrollee compliance" ([Avalere](https://avalerehealth.com/thought-leadership/how-can-managed-care-organizations-prepare-for-medicaid-community-engagement-work-requirements/)), and states may accept self-attestation through 2027, which pushes the documentation crunch out to 1 January 2028 ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/medicaid-work-rules-whats-in-whats-out-and-where-hospitals-stand/)). Only a provider-side exemption-paperwork tool for behavioural-health clinics and health centres survives, and it hangs on frailty litigation and the 2028 cliff.

**SMB vendor-payment fraud controls** (40/60) leaned on Nacha's 2026 fraud-monitoring rules, which since June 2026 cover every non-consumer ACH originator ([Nacha](https://www.nacha.org/rules/risk-management-topics-fraud-monitoring-phase-2)). Verification found the obligation light: written "risk-based processes and procedures… reasonably intended to identify" fraudulent entries, reviewed annually ([Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/aap-nacha/ach-risk-management-fraud/fraud-monitoring-rules-2026)), with no duty to screen each entry ([DefenseStorm](https://defensestorm.com/blogs/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-2026-nacha-operating-rules-and-regulations/)) and no reach over wires, which carry most of the **$3.05B** lost to business email compromise in 2025 ([McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/the-sobering-truth-of-the-fbis-2025-internet-crime-complaint-center-report)). The low end already has an A$9-a-month Xero bank-change alert ([Xero App Store](https://apps.xero.com/app/vendoralert)) and BILL's vendor-confirmation email ([BILL](https://vendorpayhelp.bill.com/hc/en-us/articles/360056648731)). Bank-run Confirmation of Payee has pushed UK invoice and mandate fraud to a record-low £41.3M ([IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/cfo-prevents-invoice-scam-1817723)) and ran 150M+ checks in Australia's first year ([eCommerceNews](https://ecommercenews.com.au/story/confirmation-of-payee-passes-150-million-checks-in-year)), while Eftsure has acquired its way past 4,000 customers ([Business News Australia](https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/eftsure-acquires-us-vendor-data-firm-relish-to-build-global-payment-assurance-platform.html)). The verifier re-scored it at **32–34/60**: a US-only product sold through accounting firms at $25–99 per client entity a month.

The Australian finalists split three ways. **Tranche 2 AML/CTF**, the finance sweep's #1, proved the most crowded market in the study: CompareAML lists **28 vetted vendors** priced from A$0 ([CompareAML](https://www.compareaml.com.au/compare)), easyAML's A$179-a-month plan already includes the risk assessment and AML program ([easyAML](https://easyaml.com/pricing)), and the channels are locked by InfoTrack with CA ANZ ([Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/technology/22457-infotrack-ca-anz-team-up-to-give-members-access-to-aml-ctf-support-tools)), First AML with CPA Australia ([First AML](https://www.firstaml.com/au/about/company/partners/cpa-australia/)) and PEXA Clear in property settlement ([IT Brief](https://itbrief.com.au/story/pexa-clear-launches-aml-compliance-tool-for-property)). Only 39,520 of an expected 89,557 entities had enrolled by 13 August 2026 ([Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/aml-ctf-reforms-why-austracs-first-enforcement-wave-may-arrive-sooner-than-expected/)), but that reflects firms not complying, not a gap in tools. **Award-wage auditing** fared worst, with no criminal prosecution in the 488 days after wage theft became a crime ([Newcastle Herald](https://www.newcastleherald.com.au/story/9238892/no-criminal-charges-despite-495m-wage-theft-blitz/)) and FairWork Mate auditing up to five employees free ([FairWork Mate](https://fairworkmate.com.au/for-business)). **Support at Home claims and statement checking** survived as a narrow home-market option, covered in the final section.

Only the **Medicaid long-term-care application** idea kept its thesis, downgraded from "open field" to "contested but real". Verification showed case *tracking* is taken, since Aidace and RevSuite sell "Medicaid pending" workflow on the MatrixCare marketplace, SNF Metrics has a pending report and PointClickCare sells a revenue-cycle service that includes applications, but found no product that does the paralegal work end to end. Its scores fell the least of any finalist.

## Nursing homes fund months of care on every Medicaid conversion

When a resident who entered paying privately or on a Medicare-covered stay runs through their savings, someone must file a Medicaid long-term-care application, and the facility usually keeps providing care while it is "Medicaid pending", expecting retroactive payment that is **not guaranteed** ([ExaCare](https://www.exacare.com/article/medicaid-pending-nursing-homes); [DHC Law](https://www.dhclaw.com/faqs/nursing-home-costs-while-medicaid-application-is-pending.cfm)). The application turns on a $2,000 individual asset limit and a five-year look-back on transfers ([Littman Krooks](https://www.littmankrooks.com/elder-law/elder-law-medicaid-planning/medicaid-faq-questions/)). Caseworkers' requests for information can grow from two months of statements to several years of them plus receipts for any expense over $200 ([caregivers.com](https://portal.prod.caregivers.com/questions/medicaid-what-happens-next-471681.htm)), and although state asset-verification systems return up to 60 months of month-start balances, caseworkers in New York and Washington must still chase paper when an account is missing or a transfer is flagged ([CBPP](https://www.cbpp.org/es/research/health/using-asset-verification-systems-to-streamline-medicaid-determinations); [NY DOH](https://healthweb-back.health.ny.gov/health_care/medicaid/publications/docs/adm/17adm02.pdf); [WA HCA](https://www.hca.wa.gov/node/19066)). Families do this once, in crisis; facilities do it every month.

The volume is structural. There are **14,742** certified nursing facilities ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/other/state-indicator/number-of-nursing-facilities/)) holding about 1.2 million residents ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1168843/number-residents-certified-nursing-facilities-state)), and Medicaid covers more than 60% of them ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/5-key-facts-about-nursing-facilities-and-medicaid/)). A JAMA Network Open cohort found that **16.4%** of residents who entered without Medicaid spent down and enrolled during their stay, typically after about six months, and that **61.8%** of residents who began as private pay were on Medicaid four years later ([Skilled Nursing News](https://skillednursingnews.com/2025/12/nearly-1-in-6-nursing-home-residents-spend-down-savings-to-qualify-for-medicaid/)). From those figures the verification notes estimate roughly **300,000–400,000 new nursing-facility applications a year, or 20–25 per facility**, plus annual renewals, an estimate built on an assumed 40–50% annual turnover rather than a published count. The money tied up is large but poorly measured. The only facility-level figure, a 2010 New York survey, found **$455,000** of Medicaid-pending receivables per facility, 17% of all receivables ([LeadingAge NY](https://leadingageny.org/providers/nursing-homes/reimbursement/medicaid-eligibility-determinations3)); filing reportedly took an average of 71 days in a 2018 Florida policy fight ([Georgetown CCF](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2018/06/18/doctors-patients-and-many-others-voice-strong-objections-to-floridas-plan-to-end-retroactive-medicaid-coverage/)); Illinois providers sued over a backlog of about 7,000 pending applications in an undated, probably older report ([Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/illinois-medicaid-backlog-spawns-multiple-lawsuits-behalf-providers-beneficiaries)); and asset-transfer denials averaged 7% across New York counties but ranged from under 1% to nearly half ([Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/study-ny-counties-vary-nursing-home-medicaid-denials)). With private-pay rates at about $8,000–12,000 a month ([ElderLawAnswers](https://attorney.elderlawanswers.com/how-much-will-medicaid-planning-and-application-cost-12408)), facilities price the risk by refusing pending status when they can ([caregivers.com](https://portal.prod.caregivers.com/questions/check-account-balance-barely-over-2k-nh-refusing-to-put-into-medicaid-pending-demanding-full-payment-491489.htm)).

The buyer is the **business office manager and administrator at an independent nursing home**, or the regional reimbursement or receivables director at a chain of 5–30 facilities, and they already spend on this. New York postings offer Medicaid coordinators up to **$75,000** ([Apploi](https://apply-jobs.apploi.com/job/1867923/hcnhealthcaresolutions/business-office-manager-medicaid-coordinator)) or $60,000–75,000 ([CMR Recruiting](https://jobs.crelate.com/portal/cmrrecruiting/job/z7uwp8okdiaqe8k7iyqm8oh6yo)), and one 150-bed facility's coordinator liaises with "families and Medicaid vendors", so staff and outside firms coexist ([breezy.hr](https://wealthy-recruiting.breezy.hr/p/3babb2f1833f-medicaid-coordinator-skilled-nursing-facility)). Large chains run in-house eligibility teams and lean on their record-system vendors, and assisted living is a weaker first buyer because only about 18% of residents use Medicaid for services ([NIC](https://www.nic.org/blog/assisted-living-medicaid-waivers-expand-access-and-choice/)).

Four kinds of solution exist, and none does the whole job. **Human outsourcing firms** do the work but are often free to the facility because they bill the family about **$2,950–3,000** ([caregivers.com](https://portal.prod.caregivers.com/questions/applying-for-medicaid-492850.htm); [caregivers.com](https://portal.prod.caregivers.com/questions/should-there-be-consumer-protection-laws-regarding-partnerships-between-different-business-entities--491223.htm)); they grow by hiring coordinators ([applicantstack](https://recruitsecure.applicantstack.com/x/detail/a2is35m6mqy5); [breezy.hr](https://wealthy-recruiting.breezy.hr/p/3babb2f1833f-medicaid-coordinator-skilled-nursing-facility)) and carry baggage, from Tennessee's 2020 settlement with Medicaid Done Right over implied government affiliation ([TN AG](https://tn.gov/attorneygeneral/news/2020/7/27/pr20-29.html)) to a "giant scam" lawsuit against a Medi-Cal firm ([Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/senior-care-advocates-called-giant-scam/)) and an elder-law critique that a facility handling its own residents' applications has a conflict of interest ([PA Elder Law](https://www.paelderlaw.net/uh-oh-nursing-home-handling-medicaid-application/)). **Tracking software**, namely Aidace and RevSuite on the MatrixCare marketplace ([MatrixCare](https://www.matrixcare.com/partner-marketplace/aidace/); [MatrixCare](https://www.matrixcare.com/partner-marketplace/revsuite-solutions/)) and SNF Metrics' pending report ([SNF Metrics](https://snfmetrics.com/ar-tracker-dashboard/)), shows status, missing documents and deadlines but leaves the reading, filing and answering to staff. **Records and admissions incumbents** sit next door: PointClickCare launched a revenue-cycle service with eligibility specialists in 2021 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210525005086/en/PointClickCare-Launches-Revenue-Cycle-Management-Services-to-Improve-Profitability-for-Skilled-Nursing-Facilities)), and ExaCare raised a $30M Series A in October 2025 for admissions agents that verify eligibility and flag financial risk, with no named application product ([Skilled Nursing News](https://skillednursingnews.com/2025/10/exacare-secures-30m-in-series-a-funding-for-ai-tools-streamlining-nursing-home-admissions/); [ExaCare](https://www.exacare.com/skilled-nursing)). **Family and attorney tools** are thin, with MedicaidSoft listing one employee ([Gust](https://gust.com/companies/medicaidsoft)) and NavigAid covering six states ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20211013005113/en/NavigAid-Supports-Caregivers-Through-the-Medicaid-Application-Process)). Elder-law firms charge flat fees "in the $10,000 range" yet say most application work "can be completed by an experienced paralegal" ([ElderLawAnswers](https://attorney.elderlawanswers.com/how-much-will-medicaid-planning-and-application-cost-12408)). That sentence is the opportunity: paralegal work priced like legal work, which agents can now do.

Three changes make this the moment. First, Section 71112 of the 2025 federal budget law limits retroactive coverage, for applications made on or after 1 January 2027, to **two months for non-expansion applicants, which includes nursing-home residents, and one month for expansion adults**, down from three ([CA DHCS](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/es/services/medi-cal/eligibility/letters/Documents/ACWDL-26-07.pdf); [WA HCA](https://www.hca.wa.gov/assets/free-or-low-cost/hr1-retro-fact-sheet.pdf); [Justice in Aging](https://justiceinaging.org/hr1-reduces-medicaid-retroactive-eligibility-starting-in-2027/); [Hall Booth Smith](https://hallboothsmith.com/medicaid-reforms-big-beautiful-bill/)). If filing really takes around 71 days, the old cushion is gone: files must open before the money runs out, and a denial followed by a refile now forfeits paid months. LeadingAge warned, about a harsher House draft, that cutting retroactive coverage would force more cautious admissions ([LeadingAge](https://leadingage.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/OBBB-Medicaid-Retroactive-Eligibility-June-2025-1.pdf)), and Minnesota says it will apply the change only from 2028 ([MN DHS](https://mn.gov/dhs/federalchanges/retroactive-coverage/index.jsp)), so each state's start date needs checking. Second, the same law froze parts of CMS's 2024 eligibility-streamlining rule until October 2034 ([NHeLP](https://healthlaw.org/resource/state-options-to-implement-provisions-of-the-eligibility-and-enrollment-final-rule-post-obbba/?author=lav)), so agency processing for aged and disabled applicants stays paper-heavy, and a $1M home-equity cap adds a screening step from 2028 ([Justice in Aging](https://justiceinaging.org/hr1-imposes-new-limit-on-home-equity-for-medicaid-ltss-effective-2028/)). Third, the work itself, reading five years of statements, building a ledger, flagging transfers, filling forms and tracking 10–15-day information-request deadlines, is the unstructured document labour that language models and browser agents have only recently made cheap. Searches found no change to the look-back or asset limits, though the statute itself was not read.

## An application desk paid per approval, launched in Ohio

| Element | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Buyer | Business office manager and administrator at independent nursing homes; regional receivables director at chains of 5–30 facilities |
| First market | Ohio (explicit non-lawyer permissions, large facility count); Illinois or Pennsylvania next; avoid New Jersey and New York at first |
| Wedge | Free audit of pending cases, then pay-on-approval rescue of the worst stuck cases |
| Product | Agent-prepared application files with human specialist sign-off, caseworker request handling and renewals |
| Price | $1,000–1,500 per approved new application (or $750 at submission plus $500 on approval); renewals $150–250; legacy stuck cases for a share of cash collected |
| Revenue per facility | About 20–25 applications a year, roughly $24,000–30,000, plus renewals (estimate) |
| Market | About $2.4–3.0M ARR from 100 facilities in one state; about $300–400M national pool (estimate) |
| Channels | Ohio Health Care Association and LeadingAge Ohio partner programmes; white-label work for elder-law firms; record-system marketplaces later |
| Kill test | Within about 60 days, at least 3 facilities pay $750 or more on live cases; kill if none will pay $500 |

The product is a desk the business office hands cases to, not software the office must operate. It starts by reading the facility's census to flag private-pay and Medicare residents likely to spend down, and because the JAMA data puts conversions about six months after admission, files can open months early. Families get a link to upload statements, deeds, life-insurance and annuity papers and pension letters. Agents turn up to 60 months of statements into a ledger, flag transfers and large withdrawals against state thresholds with an "explain this" checklist for the family, summarise countable assets and income under Ohio rules, fill the state forms, track every caseworker deadline, draft responses and chase status. A human eligibility specialist reviews and signs off each file before submission, and anything resembling eligibility *strategy*, such as annuities, trusts or reversing gifts, is routed to an elder-law attorney. Ledger-building and caseworker-request handling are where the value concentrates, because incomplete or late answers and refiles are exactly what the shorter retroactive window now punishes.

Law shapes the scope. Ohio's Supreme Court advisory opinion lets non-attorneys identify countable resources, prepare and file Medicaid applications and attend hearings, but not do "Medicaid planning" that needs legal training ([Supreme Court of Ohio](https://www.supremecourt.ohio.gov/Boards/UPL/advisory_opinions/UPLAdvOp_11_01.pdf)), whereas New Jersey treats advice on strategies to become eligible as the practice of law, which is a crime there ([NJ Courts](https://www.njcourts.gov/sites/default/files/notices/2016/05/n160518c.pdf)). The rule of thumb is "prepare and file, yes; advise how to qualify, no". A fraud claim against one application firm built on unauthorised-practice arguments was dismissed ([ElderLawAnswers](https://attorney.elderlawanswers.com/court-dismisses-fraud-claim-against-company-that-provides-assistance-to-medicaid-applicants-18050)), so the risk is survivable, but the positioning must stay clean: facility-paid, preparation only, no "guaranteed approval" claims and no fees charged to families.

Pricing follows how buyers already pay for outcomes. A facility pays **$1,000–1,500 per approved new application**, or $750 at submission plus $500 on approval to share risk, with annual renewals at $150–250 and legacy stuck cases taken for a share of pending cash collected. The anchors support it: family-billed vendors charge about $3,000, attorneys about $10,000, and the notes estimate a facility's in-house cost at roughly **$800–1,900 per new application** once a $60,000–75,000 coordinator's loaded salary is spread over 50–100 applications a year, which is an estimate. Delivery cost is the unproven half of the unit economics, and here I am adding my own working assumption rather than a finding: the business works if one specialist with agent support can carry a few hundred applications a year, so the pilot should measure specialist hours per file rather than assume it. Per facility, 20–25 applications at about $1,200 is roughly $24,000–30,000 a year before renewals, which makes renewals and legacy recovery the natural route to recurring revenue.

Sizing is estimated entirely from the cited resident counts. One state at 100 facilities × 20–25 applications × about $1,200 gives roughly **$2.4–3.0M ARR**; nationally, 300,000–400,000 applications at about $1,000 is a facility-side pool of **$300–400M a year** before renewals, assisted-living waivers and the elder-law channel. Ohio is a sensible start because it has one of the largest certified-facility counts ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/other/state-indicator/number-of-nursing-facilities/)) and explicit non-lawyer permissions. The verification notes judge this "a solid, unglamorous B2B business for a small team" at $2–5M ARR across a few states, and a weak venture story unless the agent layer becomes something an ExaCare-type player wants to buy.

The first ten customers come from proving recovered cash rather than giving demos. Offer every independent and small-chain facility in Ohio a free **pending-case audit**, ingesting its pending list and returning a triage of stuck cases within 48 hours, then convert by taking its five to ten worst cases on a pay-on-approval basis, which removes procurement friction and produces case studies. Prioritise facilities with open Medicaid-coordinator postings or recent business-office turnover, since those buyers are visibly short of capacity. Join the Ohio Health Care Association and LeadingAge Ohio business-partner programmes and speak at their reimbursement sessions; a New York outsourcing firm launched its application service the same way through LeadingAge New York ([LeadingAge NY](https://www.leadingageny.org/topics/coronavirus/supplier-resources1/comprehensive-ar-solutions/comprehensive-a-r-solutions-a-leadingage-ny-business-partner-announces-new-medicaid-applications-service/)). Use the 1 January 2027 retroactive cut as the reason to meet in the fourth quarter of 2026. In parallel, offer white-label file preparation to five to ten small elder-law firms at about $300–750 per case: attorneys supervise, so unauthorised-practice exposure disappears, deals close faster, and the firms become the referral path for families who need planning, the route MedicaidSoft took through Krause Financial ([Utah Business](https://www.utahbusiness.com/entrepreneurship/2025/01/27/medicaidsoft-wins-utah-tech-week-pitch-competition/)). Once a few logos exist, apply to the MatrixCare and PointClickCare marketplaces, as Aidace and RevSuite did.

Geography is the main practical cost for this user. If they are based in Australia, as their AUD-locale codebase suggests, the software can be built anywhere but sales and delivery cannot: buyers are Ohio business offices, files move between families, banks and county caseworkers, and handling health and financial records calls for a US entity, HIPAA business associate agreements and errors-and-omissions cover. The first hire or partner should therefore be an Ohio-based eligibility specialist, ideally a former business office manager or county caseworker, who brings the network, reviews files and owns caseworker relationships. Time zones are workable, since Ohio's business afternoon falls in eastern Australia's early morning.

## Facility willingness to pay is the question to settle first

The deciding risk is **facility willingness to pay**. Where a local vendor bills families, the facility's alternative costs it nothing, and in-house coordinators are already on payroll, so the pitch has to be pending dollars recovered and applications complete on first submission rather than labour saved. The notes could not establish what share of pending cases end in write-off rather than late payment, the ratio that sets the real value per facility. The second risk is **incumbent extension**: ExaCare already sits at admissions and reportedly runs in 1,500–2,000 facilities, an aggregator-sourced figure ([ExaCare](https://www.exacare.com/resources/news)), while PointClickCare owns the record system, and either could add an application agent within a year or two, which argues for building county-by-county request playbooks and outcome data quickly and for treating acquisition as a credible exit. Third, **agency backlogs** cap the promise; Illinois cases have waited more than a year ([Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/illinois-medicaid-backlog-spawns-multiple-lawsuits-behalf-providers-beneficiaries)), so the desk should sell "complete on first submission, requests answered within 48 hours, conversions started early" and never "approval in X days". Fourth, **document access** is unproven, because asset-verification systems do not remove paper, consumer bank feeds were not verified to reach five years back, and older statements may need archive requests made under the resident's or representative's authority. Fifth, **local variation** persists even inside one state where counties administer Medicaid, as New York's spread of denial rates shows ([Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/study-ny-counties-vary-nursing-home-medicaid-denials)). Finally, **reputation** matters in a sector that has drawn attorney-general action and scam allegations, which makes clean, facility-paid positioning a selling point rather than a nicety.

The evidence behind the recommendation is better than the first sweep's but still thin. Every citation is a search-result summary and no page was read in full; the most important cost figures are old, with pending receivables from 2010, denial rates from 2009 and filing time from 2018; the annual application count is estimated; no vendor or facility price list turned up; Ohio's county process and its retroactive start date were not checked; nobody in the target market was interviewed; and ExaCare's and PointClickCare's roadmaps are unknown. Before committing money or a hire, the user should confirm when Ohio applies the retroactive cut, have Ohio counsel confirm the preparation-only scope and the authorised-representative route, and gather real pending-receivable and write-off figures from facilities.

| Test (within about 60 days) | Pass | Kill |
|---|---|---|
| 15–20 discovery calls with business office managers and regional receivables directors in Ohio (or Illinois) | Most rank stuck pending cases and their cost as a top-three problem | Most say their vendor is free (family-billed) or PointClickCare already does it |
| Price test on live cases | At least 3 facilities agree to pay $750 or more per application | No facility will pay $500 or more |
| Pilot on 10–20 cases | Fewer caseworker requests and refiles, and shorter time to submit, than the facility's baseline | No measurable improvement |
| Delivery cost (my addition; set the target before the pilot) | Specialist hours per file consistent with a few hundred files per specialist a year | Hours per file make $1,000–1,500 unprofitable |

If the test fails, or a US operation is unrealistic, the best home-market alternative is a **Support at Home statement-and-claim checking layer** for Australian in-home aged-care providers. Its trigger is fresh and official. The Department's first payment-integrity review cycle found monthly-statement problems at **51 of 60** providers reviewed ([Department of Health, Disability and Ageing](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/support-at-home-payment-integrity-rolling-review-cycle-1-summary-report.pdf)), its pricing review traced most apparently high prices to incorrect claiming ([Department](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/support-at-home-payment-assurance-pricing-review-summary-report.pdf)), and providers told a Senate inquiry they spend about 31 minutes on admin and compliance for every hour of care ([The Weekly SOURCE](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/31-minutes-of-admin-for-every-hour-of-care-support-at-home-inquiry/)), with one citing a double-payment problem that cost about A$1.1M ([The Weekly SOURCE](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/pearl-home-care-takes-support-at-home-double-payment-nightmare-to-senate-inquiry/)). A read-only health check that ingests the provider portal's payment-statement CSV and the care-management export, then flags exceptions under the review's themes, needs no government certification to start. Its limits are just as clear. There were about **950 approved providers** in March 2026 ([GEN Aged Care Data](https://www.gen-agedcaredata.gov.au/getmedia/88363448-d30b-4958-8823-df697cb5f769/Support-at-Home-Program-Data-Report-Q3-2025-26-corrected)), exits are accelerating ([The Weekly SOURCE](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/ansell-strategic-flags-new-wave-of-exits-under-support-at-home/)), two in five home-care providers are unprofitable ([The Weekly SOURCE](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/two-in-five-home-care-providers-unprofitable-with-more-to-quit-sector-uarc/)), incumbents such as The Lookout Way and ShiftCare's QuickClaim integration already reconcile and validate claims ([Lookout](https://support.thelookoutway.com/en/articles/reconciling-your-support-at-home-claim); [ShiftCare](https://shiftcare.com/quickclaim-integration)), and a Senate report due 24 November 2026 could reshape the rules ([IGAC](https://www.igac.gov.au/news/our-submission-support-home-senate-inquiry)). The notes put serviceable revenue at roughly A$3–20M a year across all vendors, enough for a solo founder but not much more. If both fail, the next verification sprints should go to clinical-research-site charge recovery, HVAC warranty recovery and UK or Australian estate administration, in that order.

## Conclusion

The research changed the question. The first sweep asked where software is missing; verification showed that almost every regulated niche already has some software, usually a tracker, a checker or a bundle, and that what remains unbought is the *labour* on the far side of a counterparty with no API, whether a caseworker, a payer, a research sponsor or a manufacturer portal. The opportunities that survived are outcome desks, priced per approved application or per recovered dollar, where agents do the paralegal work and a human signs. Buyers in these markets already pay that way, which is why per-outcome pricing strips out most of the sales friction a solo founder would otherwise face.

Two further implications follow. Venture databases are poor maps of boring markets: the competitors that sank four finalists were bundling vendors, bootstrapped locals and labour-based service firms, visible only in trade press, association partner lists, record-system marketplaces and job boards, so any new idea should be hunted there before it is scored. And rules that govern money flows, such as retroactive payment windows, payment-integrity reviews and refunds, outlasted filing deadlines in 2026. The winner and its Australian runner-up share a shape, assembling evidence for a government payer whose rules just changed and selling it to the provider who gets paid, which suggests the user is really choosing a reusable playbook of rules engine, document agents and human sign-off that can move from Medicaid applications to renewals and exemption paperwork, or from Support at Home to other government-funded care programmes, once the first desk proves people will pay.
