# Launch brief: automated wellness concepts in Australia (robotic massage, robotic manicure, self-service IPL)

Prepared 13 October 2026. Scope: how to launch each idea in Australia. Whether the ideas already exist in Australia was checked separately and is not re-checked here. Method: 39 standard web searches, using search-result extracts only (WebFetch was unavailable). Every item under "Cited Findings" has an inline source. Everything under "Inferences" is the researcher's own reasoning or estimate and is labelled as such. Where an extract could not be tied to a single URL, the item is marked "attribution approximate". **FX assumptions used for conversions (not sourced; approximate, Oct 2026):** US$1 ≈ A$1.54 (A$1 = US$0.65); EUR1 ≈ A$1.75; JPY100 ≈ A$1.00; RMB1 ≈ A$0.21.

## 1. AI robotic massage studios/kiosks: suppliers, pricing, sites, approvals, risks, capital

### Takeaway
Hardware can be bought, but no supplier offers a turnkey Australian deal:
- **Aescape** now sells robots at US$125k (≈A$192k) plus a US$10k-a-year platform fee, but shows no distribution outside the US.
- **Capsix** leases the iYU at about EUR2,700 (≈A$4.7k) a month in France. Its only appointed distributor covers the Americas, so Asia-Pacific appears unallocated.
- **Chinese cobot packages** are the cheapest route but need local integration and safety validation.

On premium hardware, a bay needs about 7 sessions a day to break even (estimate). The best launch route is placing robots inside recovery clubs and gyms on a revenue share, priced just below a human remedial massage (A$49–59 for 30 minutes). Marketing should make relaxation and wellness claims only, which keeps the robots outside TGA medical-device rules.

### Inferences
**Supplier shortlist (inference)**
1. **Capsix (France): best premium option for Australia.**
   - Leasing caps capex, and the business-to-business model already targets gyms, hotels and offices.
   - Asia-Pacific appears to have no distributor, so negotiate an Australia/New Zealand distributorship or master licence.
   - Ask for 230V/50Hz units, local spare parts and remote-support service levels.
2. **Chinese six-axis cobot massage systems (Yiqi Future R2, Dobot/JAKA-based packages, ATEAGO-type units): lowest cost.**
   - They need an Australian integrator, English UI and content, force-limit and emergency-stop validation, and EESS/RCM work.
   - Yiqi has fresh capital and order momentum, but no export track record has been found.
   - Approach Yiqi as exclusive Australian distributor or OEM.
3. **Aescape Recovery: highest brand pull but highest counterparty risk.**
   - It is post-insolvency, has a US-only footprint, and its hardware is designed for the US.
   - Pursue only as a licence or partnership with performance protections (for example, software escrow and parts guarantees).

**Unit-economics sketch, one robot bay in a host site (ESTIMATE; all inputs are assumptions)**
- Price A$55 list for a 30-minute session; A$45 average realised after memberships and discounts.
- Host revenue share 25%; consumables, cleaning and payments A$4 a session; booking, insurance and maintenance A$600 a month.
- Premium hardware ≈A$4,700 a month lease (Capsix EUR2,700 or Aescape US$3k financing) plus ≈A$1,280 a month platform fee (Aescape-style).
- Chinese cobot ≈A$60k all-in (hardware plus integration) amortised over 36 months ≈A$1,670 a month.

| Scenario (A$/month) | Sessions/day | Revenue | Host 25% | Variable | Hardware + platform | Other | **Contribution** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premium, base | 6 | 8,100 | 2,025 | 720 | 5,980 | 600 | **−1,225** |
| Premium, upside | 10 | 13,500 | 3,375 | 1,200 | 5,980 | 600 | **+2,345** |
| Chinese cobot, base | 6 | 8,100 | 2,025 | 720 | 1,670 | 600 | **+3,085** |
| Chinese cobot, upside | 10 | 13,500 | 3,375 | 1,200 | 1,670 | 600 | **+6,655** |

- **Break-even.** About 7.4 sessions a day on premium hardware and about 2.5 a day on Chinese hardware (A$29.75 contribution per session).
- **Cross-check.** Aescape's illustrative US$7,280 a month at US$45 per 30 minutes implies about 162 sessions a month, roughly 5.4 a day (researcher's derivation). The premium break-even of about 7 a day is therefore above Aescape's own illustrative utilisation. Premium hardware only works with Aescape/Capsix-style pricing near A$70 per 30 minutes, or with high-traffic hosts.
- **A single-robot mall kiosk does not work.** Take casual mall rent of about A$3.5–4.3k a month (older Westfield pop-up rates, cross-cutting section) in place of the host share. At 6 sessions a day, premium hardware then loses about A$3.5k a month, and Chinese hardware makes only about A$0.8k before any staffing.

**Pricing recommendation (inference).**
- 15-minute express at A$25–29, 30 minutes at A$49–59, 60 minutes at A$89–99. This sits 30–40% below human remedial massage (A$85–175 for 60 minutes) and near Aescape's US$45 / US$80 (≈A$69 / A$123).
- Memberships, for example A$129 a month for 4 × 30 minutes, to drive repeat use.
- Accept no health-fund rebate positioning (no registered therapist).

**Beachhead sites (recommendation).**
1. **Self-service recovery clubs** (XtraClubs-style, P3 Recovery, O-Studio) and premium gyms. The audience is already paying for recovery and app access is already in place. This mirrors Aescape's Equinox route.
2. **CBD corporate offices and end-of-trip facilities** (Capsix's target segment), sold as an employee-wellness subscription.
3. **One flagship** in a high-footfall CBD centre (for example Westfield Sydney or Melbourne Central), once utilisation data exists.
4. **Airports later.** Dwell-time fit is good for 15-minute sessions, but airport leasing is slow and costly.

**Compliance checklist (inference unless cited above)**
1. **TGA claims.** Market for relaxation and wellness only. Claims such as pain relief or treating musculoskeletal conditions would pull the device into ARTG medical-device territory, as the Class IIa massagers show. Check this against TGA guidance.
2. **EESS/RCM.**
   - An Australian entity registers as responsible supplier.
   - Determine the device's risk level and whether it is "in-scope".
   - Obtain test reports to AS/NZS 3820 or the relevant IEC 60335 part (IEC 60335-2-32 covers massage appliances; to verify).
   - RCM marking.
   - Installation by a licensed electrician.
3. **Robot safety standards** (not verified here): ISO 13482 (personal care robots) / ISO 10218 and ISO/TS 15066 (collaborative robots). Use them as the safety-case basis for insurers and landlords.
4. **Council.** Planning and change-of-use for a "business premises" or "health services" use, plus signage.
5. **WHS.** Risk assessment covering cleaning and maintenance staff and remote monitoring.
6. **Privacy.** 3D body scans and preference profiles are personal information. Publish a privacy policy and collect consent.
7. **Insurance.** Public and products liability, equipment breakdown, and cyber cover (see Gaps).
8. **Australian Consumer Law.** Consumer guarantees and mandatory injury reporting for consumer goods and services (to verify).

**Top 3 risks and mitigations (inference)**
1. **Supplier and after-sales failure.** Aescape went insolvent; Capsix and Yiqi have no Australian presence. *Mitigation:* lease or pay-per-session rather than buy; contract for local spares, remote support and software escrow; dual-source (premium plus Chinese).
2. **Low utilisation and novelty fade.** Premium break-even of about 7 a day is above Aescape's own illustrative about 5.4 a day. *Mitigation:* host revenue share instead of fixed rent; memberships and corporate subscriptions; 15-minute express formats; place where recovery spend already exists.
3. **Injury and claims liability.** A robot applies pressure without a therapist present. *Mitigation:*
   - Force limits and an emergency stop, validated against ISO 13482.
   - Contraindication questionnaire in the app (pregnancy, recent surgery, anticoagulants).
   - Wellness-only marketing and incident logging.
   - Products-liability cover, ideally with a supplier indemnity.

**Indicative capital for the first 3–5 sites (ESTIMATE)**
- **Route A (recommended): host-site placements with Chinese cobot hardware, about 8 bays across 4 hosts.** Total ≈ **A$0.45–1.1m**:
  - Hardware A$80–360k.
  - One-off integration, safety and EESS work A$100–250k.
  - Bay fit-outs A$80–160k.
  - App and access A$30–80k.
  - Working capital and launch marketing A$150–250k.
- **Route B: premium hardware, 6 units bought.** Total ≈ **A$1.5–1.65m**:
  - Units 6 × ≈A$192k ≈ A$1.15m.
  - Fit-out A$90–150k.
  - Year-1 platform fees about A$92k.
  - Working capital and marketing A$150–250k.
- **Route B leased instead.** About **A$0.45–0.55m** upfront, but with about A$36k a month in fixed hardware and platform commitments.

## 2. Robotic manicure stations: suppliers, pricing, sites, approvals, risks, capital

### Takeaway
Supply is the main constraint:
- **10Beauty** is fully committed to US retail (about 850 machines agreed). Searches found no international partner, though per the brief it registered an Australian trade mark in August 2025.
- **Umia** sells hardware and takes marketplace commission, and is open to salons, cafes, spas and airports. It is the most licensable option.
- **Chinese products** are mainly nail-art printers (about US$800), not full manicure robots.

Australian express manicures are cheap (A$15–46), so a robot cannot win on price. It must win on convenience and placement: a polish-only express service at about A$29–35 inside beauty retailers, where retailer staff supervise. Victoria requires council registration of manicure premises. Imported polish pods need the importer to register with AICIS (the Australian Industrial Chemicals Introduction Scheme).

## 3. Self-service IPL hair-removal studios: machines, pricing, sites, approvals, risks, capital

### Takeaway
On regulation this is the most launchable of the three in most states: no operator licence for IPL (intense pulsed light) outside Tasmania, and IPL devices are readily available, including ARTG-included professional units from Australian suppliers. Two gates matter most:
1. **Insurance.** IPL is a standard exclusion that needs an endorsement, and endorsements normally assume qualified operators.
2. **Injury risk.** ARPANSA (the Australian Radiation Protection and Nuclear Safety Agency) found that 93% of media-reported laser/IPL injuries with a stated cause involved operator factors.

A Japan-style self-service room priced at about A$45 per 20 minutes or A$75 per 40 minutes undercuts Australian clinic list prices for large areas by a wide margin. That is a premium over ONESELF's regular JPY3,500 (≈A$35) per 20 minutes and JPY5,500 (≈A$55) per 40 minutes. A 4-room studio breaks even at about 20% room utilisation (estimate). The model depends on locked energy settings by skin type, a mandatory in-person first-visit skin check, and remote video supervision.

- **Licensing.**

**(e) Risk evidence**

### Inferences
**Machine selection for self-operation (inference).** Choose an ARTG-included professional IPL from an Australian supplier, even though ARTG inclusion may not be legally mandatory for cosmetic-only use. Inclusion supports insurance, landlord and consumer trust, and future-proofs against TGA reform. Require the following, through the supplier or a local engineering add-on:
1. **Locked presets by Fitzpatrick skin type and body area,** set by the therapist at the first visit and tied to the customer's app profile; no manual energy adjustment.
2. **A built-in skin-tone (melanin) sensor** that refuses to fire above the preset limit; exclude skin types V–VI and tanned skin at launch.
3. **Session-locked activation:** the device unlocks only for the booked slot via the app.
4. **Safety interlocks and eyewear:** door interlock, goggles dispenser, contact-cooling handpiece, pulse counter and usage logging for insurers.

Avoid cheap offshore SHR/OPT units (no ARTG, no verifiable output). This is exactly the Health Victoria concern.

**Self-service price point and membership (inference).**
- Pay-per-use: A$45 for 20 minutes, A$75 for 40 minutes, A$99 for 60 minutes (about ONESELF's JPY3,500 / 5,500 / 7,500 plus an Australian cost premium).
- Membership: A$99 a month for 2 × 40 minutes, or A$149 a month "unlimited off-peak" (fair-use cap).

**Safety and operating design (inference).**
1. **First visit in person** with a qualified therapist: Fitzpatrick typing, medication and contraindication screening (photosensitising drugs, pregnancy, tattoos and moles in the area), patch test with a 24–48-hour wait, then preset lock.
2. **Every later session:**
   - In-app pre-check (sun exposure in the last 2 weeks, new medications, area photo).
   - A live or on-call video supervisor who can pause any device remotely.
   - A two-way intercom in each room.
   - A post-session check-in.
3. **No minors.** Burns protocol and first-aid kit; incident reporting.
4. **Standards and reporting (to verify):** AS/NZS 4173 (safe use of lasers and intense light sources in health care) as the safety-management benchmark, and Australian Consumer Law mandatory injury reporting.

**Beachhead sites (recommendation).**
- **Brisbane/Gold Coast or Sydney first.** There is no IPL licence in either; Queensland's warm climate drives demand; NSW is the largest market.
- **Then Melbourne,** after scoping council beauty-therapy registration.
- **Avoid Tasmania** (licence required).
- **Formats:** upper-floor or fringe-CBD tenancies and suburban strip retail near gyms, universities and transport. Privacy and cheap rent matter more than footfall. Co-location inside 24/7 gyms or self-service recovery clubs is a strong option.

**Compliance checklist (inference)**
1. **State/territory.** No IPL operator licence in NSW, Victoria, Queensland, WA, SA, the ACT or the NT, per ARPANSA. Tasmania needs a licence. Victoria needs council registration of beauty-therapy premises that include epilation/hair removal (confirm category and fee). NSW and Queensland: confirm with council that IPL is not skin penetration or a higher-risk personal appearance service (it should not be). Planning and change-of-use everywhere.
2. **TGA.** Prefer an ARTG-included device. Make only cosmetic claims ("hair reduction"), never therapeutic ones (for example, hirsutism or PCOS treatment).
3. **EESS/RCM** for devices, and electrical installation.
4. **Insurance.** Public and products liability plus professional indemnity with an explicit IPL endorsement that covers *customer-operated* use under supervision. Get insurer sign-off on the operating protocol before signing leases.
5. **Privacy.** Skin photos, medications and conditions are health information. Store consented, encrypted data and publish a privacy policy.
6. **Australian Consumer Law.** Consumer guarantees, and clear claims about the number of sessions needed and results.

**Top 3 risks and mitigations (inference)**
1. **Burns and eye injuries leading to liability, media and regulatory backlash.** ARPANSA ties 93% of reported injuries to operator factors, and the customer is now the operator. *Mitigation:*
   - Locked presets, a melanin sensor and session-locked devices.
   - A mandatory in-person first visit and patch test.
   - Remote supervision, conservative energy settings and exclusion of higher-risk skin types at launch.
   - Publish the safety framework and invite ARPANSA or state comment.
2. **Insurance unavailable or unaffordable.** Endorsements usually assume qualified operators. *Mitigation:* treat insurability as a go/no-go gate; use a specialist broker (Lloyd's market); offer a "therapist-assisted express" fallback in which a supervisor is on site at peak times; collect usage logs.
3. **Regulatory tightening and demand risk.** TGA reform was left open; ARPANSA flagged regulatory gaps in October 2025; ONESELF's growth is slowing and Japanese salons have gone bankrupt (per the brief); home IPL devices are an alternative. *Mitigation:*
   - Design to exceed a likely future standard (qualified supervision, ARTG devices).
   - Keep sites small and leases short or flexible.
   - Use memberships for retention.
   - Avoid Tasmania; watch Victoria and NSW policy.

**Indicative capital (ESTIMATE)**
- **3 sites × 4 rooms:** total ≈ **A$0.96–1.82m**.
  - Fit-out A$120–220k a site (A$360–660k).
  - Devices 4 × A$15–40k a site (A$180–480k).
  - Pre-opening A$80–150k (clinical protocols, legal, insurance placement, app and access integration, training).
  - Ramp working capital A$80–110k a site (A$240–330k).
  - Launch marketing A$100–200k.
- **5 sites:** ≈ **A$1.5–2.9m**.

### Gaps
- No Australian quote for a professional IPL unit was found. The MedicalSearch tiers are for lasers, and the IPL price used is an assumption. Which Japanese self-service machines ONESELF uses, and whether they would export, is unknown.
- No Australian insurer was found offering cover for customer-operated IPL. This is the critical unknown.

## 4. Cross-cutting launch inputs: electrical compliance, retail space, access and booking tech

### Takeaway
All three concepts need an Australian entity to act as EESS "responsible supplier" for imported devices, with RCM marking. Australian retail rent makes single-unit mall kiosks uneconomic, which favours host revenue-share placements. App booking plus smart-lock access is proven in Australian unstaffed wellness venues (Reverie in Canberra, XtraClubs in Sydney).

### Cited Findings
- **EESS.**
  - Level 3 equipment needs registration, evidence of compliance, a certificate of conformity and RCM marking. Level 2 needs registration.
  - The responsible supplier must register first, declare the equipment safe, and be an Australian or New Zealand legal entity with an ABN or New Zealand IRD number.
  - Supplying unregistered Level 2/3 equipment attracts penalties.
  - Sources: [EESS registration](https://eess.gov.au/registration/registration-in-scope-electrical-equipment); [EESS compliance steps](https://www.eess.gov.au/equipment/selling-electrical-equipment-in-australia/steps-for-compliance-with-eess/); [EESS purpose](https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/eess-registrations-purpose/).
  - A third party says an "EESS Platform" replaced the database in October 2024 — [Access Communications](https://www.accesscomms.com.au/product-compliance/).
- **Retail space.**
  - Westfield Sydney CBD pop-ups (older report): A$1,000 a week on a 1-month term, A$900 on 3 months, A$800 on 6 months — [Ragtrader](https://www.ragtrader.com.au/archive/exclusive-insight-into-westfield-development).
  - Permanent kiosk leases: Westfield Carindale A$2,557 a week plus GST (7-year lease) — [BusinessSales](https://app.businesssales.com.au/?p=507537); a north-side Brisbane Westfield kiosk A$1,765 a week — [BusinessSales](https://app.businesssales.com.au/business/busy-premier-northside-shopping-centre-only-kiosk-available-3/).
  - JLL Specialty Mall Leasing quotes casual sites online by centre and dates — [JLL via Apartments.com.au (2016)](https://www.apartments.com.au/expert-insights/investing/number-of-australian-retail-centres-with-casual-leasing-increases-jll).
- **Access and booking precedents.**
  - **Reverie (Campbell, Canberra)** is described as Australia's first 24/7 self-guided wellness space. It offers infrared saunas, a dry float and an automated spray-tan booth, with app booking and Bluetooth access to the building and private suites. It opened in August 2025 — [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/australia/news/australias-first-24/7-self-guided-wellness-space-has-opened-but-not-where-youd-expect-100625).
  - XtraClubs uses an app "digital key" — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/bb/app/xtraclubs/id6479224485).

DoorBird provides a PIN intercom with video to remote staff (Kuuma Sauna, Netherlands) — [DoorBird](https://www.doorbird.com/en/kuuma-sauna).

### Inferences
- **Booking and access stack.** Off-the-shelf hardware and software (Arketa-type booking plus Bluetooth or PIN locks plus intercom with video) means no custom app build is needed for a pilot. Budget about A$30–80k for integration (estimate).
- **Retail space.** Casual mall space of about A$3.5–4.3k a month for a small pop-up (older rates) is roughly equal to one robot bay's whole contribution. Prefer revenue-share hosts (gyms, recovery clubs, beauty retailers), and reserve mall space for flagship or brand sites.
- **Electrical compliance budget.** About A$5–20k per device type for testing and certification (estimate; not sourced).

## 5. Combined "automated wellness studio" brand: shared costs, cross-sell, risks, verdict

### Takeaway
The synergy is real at the **platform** level: one app, membership wallet, smart-lock access, remote supervision hub, cleaning contracts, insurance programme and brand. It is weak at the **site** level, because manicure needs high-footfall retail while IPL and massage suit private rooms in cheaper space. Recommended sequence:
1. Launch robotic massage first (lightest regulation), placed with recovery and gym hosts.
2. Add IPL rooms after insurer sign-off and a supervised pilot, co-located with massage in a private-room studio.
3. Add manicure as a retail-kiosk channel once a hardware partner signs.

Ring-fence the IPL risk legally and in insurance terms.

### Cited Findings
- **Australian precedent.** Reverie (Canberra, August 2025) already combines unstaffed, app-booked, Bluetooth-access wellness services, including an *automated* spray-tan booth, under one brand — [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/australia/news/australias-first-24/7-self-guided-wellness-space-has-opened-but-not-where-youd-expect-100625).
- **Self-service recovery chains** with app keys are expanding in Sydney (XtraClubs: Marrickville, Manly) — [Men's Fitness](https://mensfitnessonline.com.au/xtralubs-marrickville-and-manly/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/bb/app/xtraclubs/id6479224485).
- **Victorian registration** groups beauty, nails and hair-removal premises in one beauty-therapy category (fee example "beauty premise, day spa, nails and hair removal premises"). Attribution approximate across Victorian council pages: [Mansfield](https://www.mansfield.vic.gov.au/Business/Business-Permits/Health-Businesses-Registration), [Greater Shepparton](https://greatershepparton.vic.gov.au/community/safety-and-wellbeing/starting-a-food-business/health-business-registration).
- **IPL insurance** needs specific endorsement (Idea 3 (d)), and ARPANSA ties most reported injuries to operators (Idea 3 (e)).

### Inferences
**Shared costs (estimate: 15–25% saving on overheads versus three separate businesses).**
- Booking, membership and access platform.
- Remote supervisor and customer-support hub. One qualified therapist on video can cover IPL first-visit consults and pauses plus massage exceptions across all sites.
- Cleaning and maintenance contractors; compliance (one Victorian beauty-therapy registration per premises; one EESS responsible-supplier entity; one privacy framework).
- Brand and customer-acquisition spend; a shared head office.
- Site rent is shared only where massage and IPL co-locate.

**Cross-sell.**
- A single "wellness credits" membership (for example A$149 a month spendable across a 30-minute massage, a 40-minute IPL session or an express manicure).
- Massage brings in a recovery and gym audience (more male); IPL and manicure bring a beauty audience (more female). Each channel cross-introduces the other.
- Retail manicure kiosks act as visible, low-commitment acquisition points that feed IPL and massage memberships.
- Corporate wellness subscriptions can bundle massage plus IPL.

**Combined capital (ESTIMATE).** 3 flagship studios, each with 3 IPL rooms, 2 massage bays (Chinese-cobot route) and 1–2 manicure machines up front: total ≈ **A$1.4–2.9m**.
- Fit-out A$540–900k.
- IPL devices A$135–360k.
- Massage robots A$60–270k plus integration A$100–250k.
- Manicure machines A$100–360k.
- Pre-opening and platform A$120–220k.
- Working capital and marketing A$300–500k.

**Risks of combining.**
1. **Brand contagion.** One IPL burn story damages the whole brand. *Mitigation:* separate operating entity and insurance for IPL; possibly a sub-brand until a safety record exists.
2. **Site-format mismatch.** *Mitigation:* a "hub and spokes" model, with private-room studios (IPL plus massage) and retail kiosks (manicure) and hosted bays (massage).
3. **Three foreign suppliers** with different service regimes and counterparty risk (the Aescape insolvency, no confirmed Australian distributors). *Mitigation:* stage the launches; secure one supplier per category with an Australian service-level agreement.
4. **Insurance.** Insurers may price the whole portfolio on IPL risk. *Mitigation:* separate policies.
5. **Regulatory overlap.** Victorian registration makes the whole premises subject to inspection.
6. **Unstaffed security and misuse.** *Mitigation:* identity-verified accounts, session-locked devices, CCTV in common areas only.

**Verdict (inference).** Worth pursuing as a *platform brand with modular formats*, not a single three-in-one shop:
- Start with robotic massage placements (fastest, least regulated).
- Validate IPL with insurers and a supervised pilot before scaling.
- Treat manicure as an optional retail channel, conditional on supplier access.

### Gaps
- No Australian operator yet combines robotic massage, IPL and manicure, so no data exists on cross-sell rates or shared-overhead savings. The 15–25% saving is an estimate.
- No information was found on how Australian insurers would price a multi-modality unstaffed studio.
