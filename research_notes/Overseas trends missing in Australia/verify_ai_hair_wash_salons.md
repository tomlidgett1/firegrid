# Verification: AI / automated hair-washing ("smart head-wash") salons — does it already exist in Australia?

Independent adversarial verification, 13 Oct 2026. 37 WebSearch calls, all in standard mode, out of a 38 cap. 29 were presence checks (78%), 5 covered overseas growth and quality, and 3 covered Australian industry and regulation facts. WebFetch was unavailable, so every finding below rests on search-result extracts and not on full-page reads. Where an extract merged several sources and a figure could not be pinned to one of them, this is flagged.

**VERDICT: ABSENT. Confidence: 70%.**

---

## Q1. Is any automated hair-washing service or machine operating in Australia today, or has one been piloted?

### Takeaway
I found no Australian salon, express bar, kiosk, gym or office pilot, distributor or importer offering automated machine hair washing. The searches covered English, Simplified Chinese, Korean and Japanese, plus Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth, Adelaide, the Gold Coast, Canberra and the Chinese-Australian hubs of Chatswood, Burwood, Hurstville and Box Hill. Everything found in Australia is one of four things: a staffed head spa, a staffed wash or blow-dry format, a therapist-operated head-spa basin, or a handheld home device. Confidence is capped at 70% for two reasons. First, the likeliest first mover is a small Chinese-Australian head spa quietly importing a machine, as already happened in Toronto. Second, such a venue would surface mainly on Xiaohongshu, Instagram, TikTok or Google Maps, which this search tool indexes poorly.

### Inferences
- A first "AI hair wash" store in Sydney or Melbourne would very likely have drawn novelty coverage, as Toronto's opening did in NOW Toronto (see Q3). Neither Australian English-language media nor Chinese-Australian media (Sydney Today, SBS Chinese) shows any.
- The import pathway is proven: a Toronto head-spa manager imported a Chinese machine after trying one in China. Australia already has the host venues, with staffed head spas spreading since 2019–2022 and multiple head-spa businesses changing hands. Absence could therefore end quickly, and the most likely entrant is an existing Chinese-Australian head spa.
- Chinese-made, plumbing-free head-spa beds already reach Australian buyers through a mainstream retail marketplace (Harvey Norman / The Online Beauty Warehouse). The supply chain for a fully automated model therefore exists.

## Q2. What is the closest thing found in Australia, and how does it differ?

### Takeaway
There are three closest analogues, and none automates the wash.
- **Staffed Japanese head spas** such as Tokyo Head Spa do the same "lie on a bed, get washed and scalp-massaged" job. They do it by hand, in long sessions, at premium prices; one example is $295 for 2 hours.
- **The Online Beauty Warehouse's plumbing-free Sakura/Serenity head-spa basins** on the Harvey Norman marketplace have jets and a head massager. They are still therapist-operated equipment.
- **Staffed express wash and blow-dry bars**, such as Saloon in Canberra at $50.

Nothing in Australia offers a cheap (under about A$40), short (under 30 minutes), low-staff machine wash.

### Cited Findings
- Tokyo Head Spa positions itself on authenticity: "fresh running water, hands-on technique and very little in the way of unnecessary gadgetry". It operates six locations across Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane — [Sitchu Sydney guide](https://sitchu.com.au/?p=425512); [Time Out Sydney](https://www.timeout.com/sydney/health-and-beauty/tokyo-headspa-bondi-junction)
- Australian price points:
  - The Head Spa Perth "Signature Head Spa": $295 for 2 hours — [Fresha](https://fresha.com/lp/en/tt/head-massages/in/au-perth)
  - Meridian Head Spa: a 90-minute session — [Atmosfy](https://atmosfy.io/listing/729929/victoria/melbourne/meridian-head-spa)
  - Saloon, Canberra: wash and blow-dry $50 — [Region Canberra](https://region.com.au/hair-beauty-and-drinks-a-winning-combination-for-canberras-first-blow-dry-bar/222815/)
- Head-spa equipment sold in Australia: the Sakura Deluxe "no plumbing" unit has a built-in 60L reservoir, a separate basin and table, a precision shower head, temple jets, an elliptical head massager and adjustable temperature, priced at A$4,200 — [Harvey Norman listing](https://www.harveynorman.com.au/the-online-beauty-warehouse-sakura-deluxe-japanese-head-spa-no-plumbing-black.html)
- An Australian hygiene concern already exists about this class of equipment: a Sydney head-spa guide notes that many head spas use portable, plumbing-free beds, and that this raises genuine hygiene concerns — [Sitchu Sydney guide](https://sitchu.com.au/?p=425512) (from a Chinese-language summary of the extract)
- The overseas machine format for comparison: a massage table with a helmet-shaped washing module. It positions the head with infrared and runs a 13-minute cycle of two shampoos, one conditioning and seven rinses, at about ¥19 — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3308621/robotic-hair-washer-enter-hong-kong-market-june-after-gaining-interest-mainland); [GDN Online](https://gdnonline.com/Details/1351196)

### Inferences
- The gap is format and price, not the service category. Australians already pay for lie-down scalp and wash experiences, but only in the staffed, premium format. A machine wash would sit between home washing and a $50 blow-dry bar or $150–300 head spa. Only the $295 and $50 data points are sourced; the $150–300 band is an indicative inference.
- Staffed head spas are a natural channel for an add-on or upsell, the Toronto model. Some Australian head spas, though, sell themselves on being hands-on and low-tech, as Tokyo Head Spa does.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether any Sakura/Serenity model offers an automatic wash program; the extract is ambiguous.
- No Australian price survey of staffed head spas beyond the single Perth data point.

## Q3. What are the strongest dated 2025–2026 overseas growth figures, and what are the quality and safety concerns?

### Takeaway
Growth evidence is real but concentrated in a spring-2025 burst in southern China:
- 500+ stores in Shenzhen by mid-April 2025.
- One chain (Faxiaoka) with about 40 stores, planning a June 2025 entry into Hong Kong.
- Machines at ¥21–30k.
- Washes at ¥19, or ¥9.9 on group-buy.

Early international spread is visible in Malaysia (MK AI Care) and Canada (Sakura Hours, Toronto). Coverage in 2026 is predominantly skeptical ("more gimmick than practical value"), and I found no 2026 store count. The trend may have plateaued, though this is unverified.

## Q4. Australian facts: industry size, labour shortages, electrical and water safety certification

### Takeaway
Australia's hairdressing and beauty sector has about $12.5bn revenue, about 38k businesses and about 107k workers, per IBISWorld; IBISWorld's own estimates conflict. Hairdressers have been on Jobs and Skills Australia's shortage lists. Any imported automated wash bed would be in-scope electrical equipment, so the importer would have to register as a Responsible Supplier and apply the RCM; Level 2 and 3 items must also be registered on the national database. The plumbing (WaterMark) requirement was not verified.

### Cited Findings (5)
1. **Industry size:** IBISWorld (July 2026 report) puts Hairdressing & Beauty Services revenue at about **$12.5bn in 2025-26**. It forecasts a 0.9% dip that year as households cut premium treatments, and counts **38,066 businesses** — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/australia/industry/hairdressing-beauty-services/677/). *Conflict:* a January 2025 IBISWorld page cited about $7.5bn and 26,664 businesses, and a September 2025 version gave $12.4bn and 40,346 businesses. Treat these as rough.
2. **Employment:** 106,897 people employed in 2025, up 6.4% a year on average over 2020–2025, with about 2.7 employees per business — [IBISWorld employment](https://www.ibisworld.com/australia/employment/hairdressing-beauty-services/677/)
3. **Shortage listing:** Jobs and Skills Australia's 2024 Occupation Shortage List ranked hairdresser 15th among the top 20 shortage occupations by employment size — [SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/industries/occupational-shortages-list-2024-australia-small-business-startup/). Hairdresser status on the 2025 list was not confirmed in my extracts.
4. **Severity of shortage:** industry body AHC says hairdressing and barbering is on the Core Skills Occupation List and shortages persist. A survey it cites found that 69% of hairdressing businesses advertised for a qualified hairdresser and only 19% filled the position. COSBOA cites apprenticeship requirements, a shift to sole traders and a shortage of job-ready workers — [JSA-hosted submission](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/media/3733/download); [JSA-hosted submission](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/media/3701/download); [SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/industries/retail/hairdresser-worker-skill-shortage-online-ad-shortage/). The extract did not say which document carries which figure.
5. **Electrical compliance (EESS/RCM):**
   - Importers, defined as the first Australian or NZ business in the supply chain, must register as Responsible Suppliers.
   - Responsible Suppliers declare that equipment is electrically safe and apply the RCM under AS/NZS 4417.1/.2.
   - Registration on the national database is mandatory for medium-risk (Level 2) and high-risk (Level 3) equipment and optional for Level 1.
   - Level 2 needs compliance evidence and a compliance folder; Level 3 also needs a Certificate of Conformity.
   - Sources: [EESS registration](https://eess.gov.au/registration); [EESS compliance steps](https://www.eess.gov.au/equipment/selling-electrical-equipment-in-australia/steps-for-compliance-with-eess/); [EESS responsible supplier](https://www.eess.gov.au/responsible-supplier/manufacturers-and-importers-responsible-suppliers/); [ERAC bulletin](https://www.erac.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Information-Notice-EESS-Commencement-General.pdf)
   - Energy Safe Victoria lists fees of $216.89 a year for supplier registration and $81.28 per Level 2 item; these may be outdated — [Energy Safe Victoria](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/industry-guidance/electrical/appliances-equipment-and-manufacurers/eess-legislation-and-regulations)

### Inferences
- The labour-shortage story holds up: owners struggle to fill qualified roles (69% advertised, 19% filled), and Chinese stores explicitly pitch the machines as staff-savers. A machine that takes over the shampoo step (traditionally junior or apprentice work) addresses a real pain point. The China evidence, though, shows stores still need 2–3 staff for five machines.
- Compliance cost is moderate but real. An automated wash bed combines water heating, pumps and possibly a dryer, all near water. That points to formal EESS registration and RCM marking, and possibly a Certificate of Conformity if it is classed as Level 3. This is a barrier to grey-market imports and an advantage for a compliant first mover.

- No ABS figures were retrieved; all industry-size numbers are from IBISWorld and are internally inconsistent.
- Hairdresser status on the 2025 Occupation Shortage List was not confirmed.
