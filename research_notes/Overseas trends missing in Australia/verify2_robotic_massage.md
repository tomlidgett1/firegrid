This is the second, independent, adversarial verification, dated 13 Oct 2026. The first verifier's notes were not read. ## Key Question 1: Is any robotic/AI massage service available in Australia or New Zealand today, or has one been piloted?

### Takeaway
**VERDICT: ABSENT. Confidence: 85%.**

About 29 presence searches used angles the first verifier did not cover:
- Chinese-language media and the Australian-Chinese portals

- a Victorian clinic that markets "AI-enhanced" massage delivered by human therapists

### Inferences
- **Two independent passes now agree.** Together they ran 38 + 39 searches, on different angles. There is no commercial robotic-arm or AI massage service in Australia or NZ as of October 2026.
- **ABSENT rather than PARTIAL.** Swinburne's system is a lab prototype, not a pilot service. Mornington's offering is human massage with AI marketing. Chairs, HydroMassage and Ceragem are excluded categories. A strict reader could treat the Swinburne announcement as "a one-off announcement", which would push the verdict towards PARTIAL. I judge it out of scope because it was research, not a consumer service.
- **Residual risk (why confidence is not above 90%):** a small Chinese-run massage or traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) shop in Sydney or Melbourne could have quietly imported a Chinese AI massage robot. Three things make this plausible:
  - Chinese makers report overseas orders and are raising money for overseas markets.
  - Such a shop would promote itself on Xiaohongshu, WeChat or Douyin, which web search indexes poorly.
  - Chinese robot makers are already building local channels (AGIBOT, July 2026).
- **Positioning:** a new entrant can safely claim "Australia's first commercial robotic massage service". It should not claim "first AI massage" (Mornington already uses that label) or "first massage robot" (Swinburne's research).
- **Hardware sourcing:** the Western leaders have no APAC distribution. Capsix's deal is limited to North America, and Aescape is restructuring. Chinese massage cobots (Dobot-based packages, the Yiqi R-series) are a realistic alternative. This is a supply-side inference only; TGA and electrical-compliance requirements were not researched.

## Key Question 2: Despite Aescape's failure, is robotic/AI massage still a growing category overseas in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
**GROWING overall, but the growth is China-led and the US model is contracting and restructuring.**
- **China:** Yiqi Future, a NetEase-incubated start-up, closed a Series A on 29 May 2026 and reported nearly RMB 40m of Q1 2026 orders. Cailian Press (CLS), a Chinese financial news service, reported on 31 Aug 2026 that the massage-robot sector is "rising", with funding rounds disclosed one after another. 36Kr reported AI massage robots moving into elderly-care centres in September 2026.
- **US:** Aescape's operating assets were sold in a January 2026 foreclosure to Aescape Recovery Inc., against a reported US$157m shortfall. The successor still trades, under a new CEO and business model.
- **Europe:** Capsix signed North American distribution with Earthlite in March 2026.
- **Others:** the remaining Western players are small or quiet.
- **Counter-signal:** mainland Chinese consumer reviews flag quality problems and doubts about whether "AI massage" shops can last.
