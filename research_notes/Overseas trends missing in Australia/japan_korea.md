## Which Japanese and Korean models grew fastest in 2024–2026, and which are the strongest transfer candidates?

### Takeaway
Two candidates have the best-documented growth in 2024–2026:
- Korea's unstaffed 24-hour stores and cafés: about 9,000 at end-2024, roughly 4x between 2020 and 2025.
- Japan's remote "avatar" staffing in convenience stores: Lawson and AVITA had avatars in 28 stores, and Lawson hired its first overseas-based remote worker in January 2025.

An AI companion doll for seniors living alone, funded by government (Korea's Hyodol, about 14,500 in use), is the strongest ageing-society candidate. Five more candidates are plausible but have weak or out-of-window evidence. None has a verified Australian absence yet.

#### 1. Remote "avatar" staffing for stores and service counters (Japan) — Grade A

**(1) How it works and makes money**
- Analysis: An animated avatar on a screen at the counter or self-checkout is driven by a remote worker. The worker greets customers, guides them through self-checkout, answers questions and calls on-site staff when needed. One remote worker can cover several stores.
- Analysis: An Australian startup could sell this two ways:
  - as a managed service billed per store-hour, where it recruits, trains and schedules the remote staff; or
  - as licensed software that retailers run with their own staff.
- AVITA's business lines, as summarised in search results: remote customer service ("AVACOM"), AI avatar training services, dispatching avatar workers, and creating avatars for businesses and municipalities. It was unclear which page this came from: [SMBC news release, 4 Sep 2023](https://www.smbc.co.jp/news_e/pdf/e20230904_01.pdf) or the [robotics.press profile](https://robotics.press/companies/avita/).

**(2) Overseas exemplars**
- Lawson (Japanese convenience-store chain) introduced avatars in 2022 with Tokyo-based AVITA. As of January 2025 they were active in **28 stores** across Tokyo, Osaka and Fukuoka. — [HR Grapevine, 2 Jan 2025](https://www.hrgrapevine.com/us/content/article/2025-01-02-japanese-store-pioneers-internationally-remote-digital-workers-in-store)
- In January 2025 Lawson hired its **first international remote store worker**, a Japanese resident living in Sweden.
  - The worker operates in-store avatars and guides shoppers through self-checkout during Japanese off-peak hours, using the 8-hour time difference. — [HR Grapevine, 2 Jan 2025](https://www.hrgrapevine.com/us/content/article/2025-01-02-japanese-store-pioneers-internationally-remote-digital-workers-in-store)
  - Nikkei originally reported this ([forum repost on JREF](https://jref.com/threads/lawson-hires-remote-worker-in-sweden-for-night-shift.738472/post-1542326)).
- Lawson President Sadanobu Takemasu outlined plans to extend remote avatar work to other countries, including Brazil and the US. — [HR Grapevine, 2 Jan 2025](https://www.hrgrapevine.com/us/content/article/2025-01-02-japanese-store-pioneers-internationally-remote-digital-workers-in-store)
- Conflicting source: a robotics.press profile says AVITA has no verified customer deployments. That contradicts the Lawson reporting, and the profile looks low-quality and unsourced, so weight it lightly. — [robotics.press](https://robotics.press/companies/avita/)
- German tech outlet t3n has covered Japanese shops staffed by avatars and robots. No figures were captured. — [t3n](https://t3n.de/news/japan-shop-bedienen-avatare-roboter-1517335/)
- [Unverified] Leads still to source:
  - AVITA was founded in 2021 by roboticist Hiroshi Ishiguro.
  - AVITA's 2024–26 funding and current store count were not retrieved.
  - Ory Laboratory's OriHime robots, and its "Avatar Robot Café DAWN" in Tokyo, employ people with severe disabilities as remote operators. This is a second exemplar that needs a source.

**(3) Why it is accelerating now**
- Lawson's president estimated that avatar-supported self-checkout could cut in-store staff time by **more than 1.5 hours per store per day**. This is a company projection, not an audited result. — [HR Grapevine, 2 Jan 2025](https://www.hrgrapevine.com/us/content/article/2025-01-02-japanese-store-pioneers-internationally-remote-digital-workers-in-store)
- Analysis: Hiring across borders turns a night shift into someone else's daytime shift. Lawson took that step in 2025 and plans to add more countries.

#### 2. Unstaffed and "hybrid" 24-hour stores and cafés (Korea) — Grade A

**(1) How it works and makes money**
- Analysis: Small-format shops (convenience, snack and ice-cream, coffee, ramen, flowers) run without staff, either around the clock or only overnight (the "hybrid" form).
  - Entry is gated by card or ID.
  - Customers pay at self-checkout kiosks.
  - AI CCTV, remote monitoring and restocking visits handle security and supply.
- Analysis: There are two revenue models:
  - **Operator**: retail margin across its own branded unstaffed cafés and stores.
  - **Enabler**: SaaS plus hardware leasing (access control, kiosks, monitoring) to independent convenience stores, grocers, campus shops and building managers.

**(2) Overseas exemplars**
- South Korea had about **9,000 unstaffed stores at end-2024**, according to the National Fire Agency. These include coffee shops, ramen restaurants and flower stands, and are usually open 24 hours. Samsung Card said their number **probably grew fourfold from 2020 to 2025**. — [Reuters via The Star, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/02/south-korean-shops-turn-to-robots-self-service-to-escape-labour-woes); [Reuters via GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/scitech/technology/994023/south-korean-shops-robots-self-service-labor-woes/story/)
- Samsung Card data cited by the Korea JoongAng Daily shows unmanned stores rose **314% between 2020 and 2025**. The data excludes some third-party platform payments. This is consistent with Reuters' "fourfold". — [Korea JoongAng Daily, "[WHY] Service without a smile: The popularity and problems of unstaffed stores"](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/kjd-special/why-service-without-a-smile-the-popularity-and-problems-of-unstaffed-stores/12614154). The publication date was not retrieved; the data runs to 2025.
- **Lounge X** runs **eight entirely unstaffed 24-hour coffee shops** in South Korea, most of them in Seoul. — [Reuters via The Star, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/02/south-korean-shops-turn-to-robots-self-service-to-escape-labour-woes)

  - In 2022 **emart24** had **1,600** crewless or hybrid stores, the most of any chain. — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1237029/south-korea-unmanned-and-hybrid-convenience-store-number-by-brand)

- Undated reports in the search results:

  - It was unclear which outlet said what among [Inside Retail Asia](https://insideretail.asia/?p=62070), [Inquirer Technology](https://technology.inquirer.net/?p=113360) and [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/fully-automated-high-tech-convenience-stores-shape-koreas-retail-landscape/11084433).
  - The dates are unconfirmed and probably before 2024, so treat these as background only. They also use different definitions (hybrid only vs crewless including hybrid).

**(3) Why it is accelerating now**
- Reuters (July 2026) frames robots and self-service as Korean shopkeepers' way "to escape labour woes". A follow-up report and a Malaysian op-ed made the same point. — [The Star, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/02/south-korean-shops-turn-to-robots-self-service-to-escape-labour-woes); [The Star, 4 Jul 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/04/shops-turning-to-automation-to-escape-labour-woes); [New Straits Times column, Jul 2026](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/opinion/columnists/2026/07/1479006/robots-serve-solution-s-korea-labour-crunch)

#### 3. AI companion doll for seniors living alone, funded by government (Korea) — Grade B

**(1) How it works and makes money**
- Analysis: A soft doll with touch sensors, voice and a companion app. It:
  - chats with the older person;
  - reminds them about medication and meals;
  - runs cognitive quizzes;
  - alerts family or caseworkers to inactivity or distress.
- Analysis: Revenue is device sales plus a monthly monitoring fee. Local governments and care providers pay most of it, with some direct sales to families.

**(2) Overseas exemplars**
- **Hyodol**: the startup estimates about **14,500** dolls are in use in South Korea. They are owned by individuals, rented out by governments or used in nursing homes. This is AFP reporting, republished widely; the date was not visible in the search snippets. — [Arab News/AFP](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2646720/offbeat); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/in-ageing-south-korea-ai-dolls-care-for-the-elderly-2); [Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/in-ageing-south-korea-ai-dolls-care-for-the-elderly-n384393)
- In February 2022 Hyodol was used by **96 of Korea's 228 local governments**, including Jongno-gu in Seoul. — [Korea Times, 15 Feb 2022](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/southkorea/society/20220215/local-municipalities-use-ai-robots-to-improve-care-for-elderly)
  - Functions: medication and meal management, dementia-prevention quizzes, touch and voice interaction, and alerts to families or caregivers.
- Competitor: Dasomi, from Wonderful Platform, was adopted by Haenam County. — [Korea Times, 15 Feb 2022](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/southkorea/society/20220215/local-municipalities-use-ai-robots-to-improve-care-for-elderly)
- Earlier coverage of the product: [Asia Economy, 2021](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2021050221353886796)
- Evidence caveat: studies show these robots help seniors overcome boredom, but two-way conversation was "still in infant stage". The article's date was not retrieved. — [Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/2842598)
- Not retrieved: founding year, funding, revenue and the 2025 municipal count (see Gaps).

**(3) Why it is accelerating now**
- Analysis: Large language models fix the weak two-way conversation the Korea Herald flagged.
- Analysis: Ageing and growing numbers of older people living alone push governments to buy remote monitoring.
- No 2024–26 growth series was retrieved, so the 14,500 figure is the only recent-looking datapoint.
