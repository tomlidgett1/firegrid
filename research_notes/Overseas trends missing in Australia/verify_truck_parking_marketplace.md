# Adversarial verification: bookable truck-parking marketplace in Australia (checked 13 Oct 2026)

**VERDICT: PARTIAL. Confidence: 75%.**
**Searches run:** 39 WebSearch calls, all "standard" mode. About 80% were presence checks.
**Method limits:** WebFetch was unavailable, so all evidence comes from search-result extracts, and no page was opened in full. No other research_notes files were read.

---

## 1. Can Australian truck drivers or fleets book third-party truck parking through any platform today?

### Takeaway
They can, but only in a thin, generic way, so the verdict is **PARTIAL**.
- **What already exists:** Parksy is a general parking marketplace. It carries a handful of third-party listings with "truck" in the title in NSW and Victoria, priced weekly or monthly, and one says "daily or long-term bookings". Storage operators also sell monthly truck and trailer bays that can be booked online.
- **What does not exist:** I found no dedicated Australian truck-parking marketplace and no SNAP-style truck-parking account network.
- **What nothing offers:** Nothing found offers nightly reservations along freight routes, standardised safety and amenity data, or Heavy Vehicle National Law (HVNL) fatigue and route integration.
- **Overseas players:** None of the overseas players (Truck Parking Club, SNAP, Rig Hut) serves Australia.

### Inferences
- **Why PARTIAL rather than ABSENT:** A new entrant could not credibly claim to be the first way for Australian truckies to book third-party truck parking online. Parksy already hosts and markets truck-specific listings, including one offering "daily or long-term bookings" in Richmond NSW. Storage operators already sell online-booked truck and trailer bays.
- **Why PARTIAL rather than PRESENT:** Parksy's truck supply is a handful of listings with no confirmed dedicated category. The rules class that as "occasional truck listings". Nothing found offers:
  - nightly or transient booking along freight corridors
  - standardised security and amenity data
  - HVNL fatigue or route integration
  - fleet accounts
  - SNAP-style payment at truck stops
- **The open niche** is a dedicated multi-site truck-parking network and marketplace on the Truck Parking Club or SNAP model, especially urban and peri-urban overnight bays for owner-drivers and small fleets, plus corridor reservations.
- **Weak inference:** The Parksy truck listing IDs (90029–90519) are much lower than recent Parksy listing IDs (164886, 173781). That suggests the truck listings are older and may be stale. Their activity is unverified.

### Gaps
- No pages could be opened, so I could not confirm:
  - Parksy's category filters
  - the total count of Australian truck listings on Parksy or Spacer
  - whether Parksy takes payment or commission on-platform
- Independent roadhouses, Puma and Viva/Shell were not checked brand by brand. Port and terminal truck-marshalling slot booking (e.g. Port Botany) was not checked; it is a different job anyway.

## 2. What is the closest thing found, and how does it differ?

### Takeaway
**The closest thing is Parksy's truck-titled third-party listings** (Richmond, Fairfield and Canowindra in NSW, Sydney Airport, and Melbourne CBD). The runner-up is **single-operator, online-bookable truck and trailer storage yards** (Safe Storage Solutions; Storage Land). Neither is a dedicated, multi-site truck-parking network with nightly reservations, safety and amenity standards, or HVNL route and fatigue integration.

### Cited Findings
- **Parksy pricing and terms:** Truck listings are priced weekly or monthly, from $350/week in Richmond to $280/month in Fairfield and $4,400/month in Canowindra. One mentions "daily or long-term bookings". Prices are "a guide only" and should be confirmed with the provider before paying. [Parksy Richmond](https://www.parksy.com/parking/au/nsw/richmond/secure-truck-parking-space-richmond-nsw-90348); [Parksy Fairfield](https://www.parksy.com/parking/au/nsw/fairfield/secure-private-truck-parking-fairfield-nsw-90519); [Parksy Canowindra](https://www.parksy.com/parking/au/nsw/canowindra/secure-truck-parking-space-canowindra-nsw-90497)
- **Parksy has unmet demand:** At least one Parksy post is a driver seeking secure 24/7 truck parking (Padstow). [Parksy Padstow](https://www.parksy.com/garages/padstow-or-surrounding-areas)
- **Storage-yard limits:** Storage Land's 6 m standard bays exclude prime movers, rigids and truck-and-dog combinations, and there is no power. [Storage Land](https://storageland.storeganise.com/truck-storage-brisbane) Safe Storage Solutions advertises instant online booking for truck and trailer storage but gives no combination-length detail. [Safe Storage Solutions](https://safestoragesolutions.storeganise.com/)
- **Truck Parking Club, for comparison:**
  - It is a dedicated network of 5,000+ locations and 80,000+ reservable spaces, run by small businesses (trucking companies, warehouses, repair shops, tow yards, hotels).
  - The company handles onboarding, customer service and quality control.
  - Drivers from 93 of the top 100 US fleets have used it.

  [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/truck-parking-club-5000-locations-nationwide)
- **SNAP, for comparison:** SNAP is a driver/fleet account used to pay for parking at all 53 UK Moto services and other sites. It is rolling out bookable parking across Europe. [Motor Transport](https://motortransport.co.uk/industry-news/snap-network-extends-to-cover-all-53-moto-services/17981.article); [trans.info](https://trans.info/en/snap-bookable-parking-410908)

### Inferences
How the closest Australian offering (Parksy) differs from the concept:
1. **Supply:** A handful of truck sites versus a dedicated network of thousands.
2. **Booking:** Mostly weekly or monthly leases, with on-platform booking and payment unclear, versus instant nightly, weekly or monthly reservations with platform commission.
3. **Data:** No standardised fencing, CCTV, lighting or amenity attributes for trucks.
4. **Integration:** No route, fatigue (HVNL) or fleet-account integration.
5. **Coverage:** No corridor or truck-stop coverage; the listings are urban or peri-urban yard spaces only.

The storage yards differ further. Each is a single operator, mostly sized for light commercial vehicles, and on monthly terms only.

### Gaps
- I could not verify whether Parksy takes a commission or processes payments for these truck listings, nor how many truck listings it has in total.

## 3. What are the strongest dated 2025–2026 overseas growth figures?

### Takeaway
The three strongest dated figures are:
1. **Truck Parking Club passed 5,000 locations and 80,000+ spaces**, announced on or before 28 Apr 2026.
2. **Truck Parking Club had 2,262 locations when CAT Scale invested on 11 Jul 2025.** It therefore grew about 2.2x in about 9.5 months.
3. **TRAVIS made 750+ secured parking sites in 16 countries bookable via Lkw.App** on 4 Mar 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Truck Parking Club, 5,000+ locations (most important):**
  - More than 5,000 locations and 80,000+ reservable spaces across 49 states.
  - The first 100 locations took 366 days; going from 4,000 to 5,000 took 81 days.
  - The target is 10,000 locations by the end of 2026, about 580 a month.
  - Drivers from 93 of the top 100 fleets have used it, and it was ranked No. 24 in the FreightTech 25 for 2026.

  [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/truck-parking-club-5000-locations-nationwide); [FleetOwner](https://www.fleetowner.com:443/for-the-driver/news/55373251/truck-parking-club-expands-reservable-truck-parking-network-to-5000-us-locations-to-ease-parking-shortage); [The Trucker](https://www.thetrucker.com/trucking-news/truck-driving-jobs-news/truck-parking-club-surpasses-5000-locations)

## 4. Australian demand facts

### Takeaway
Demand drivers are documented:
- national road rules bar heavy vehicles from parking more than an hour in built-up areas
- governments are funding new rest areas explicitly because of a shortage
- Western Sydney is only now getting its first dedicated truck rest stop
- trade press reports an acute shortage of legal off-street truck parking in Sydney.

### Cited Findings
The five strongest cited facts:
1. **One-hour limit in built-up areas (Australian Road Rule 200).** Drivers of heavy vehicles (GVM 4.5 t or more) or long vehicles (7.5 m or more) must not stop on a length of road in a built-up area for longer than one hour, except for loading or unloading and similar exemptions. [City of Salisbury guide](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/downloads/Heavy-Vehicle-Parking-Guide.pdf); [Fairfield City Council](https://www.fairfieldcity.nsw.gov.au/Your-Council/Roads-Transport-and-Parking) Campbelltown Council says it "receives many complaints regarding heavy vehicle parking in on-street locations within residential areas". [Campbelltown](https://www.campbelltown.nsw.gov.au/Your-Neighbourhood/Neighbourhood-Issues-Restrictions-and-Info/Roads-Management-Campbelltown-LGA/Heavy-vehicle-parking)
2. **Federal funding because of a shortage.** The HVRA initiative provides $140M over 10 years for new and upgraded heavy-vehicle rest areas, "addressing the shortage of heavy vehicle rest stops in locations of need". [Minister Chisholm](https://minister.infrastructure.gov.au/chisholm/media-release/new-rest-stops-set-keep-truckies-safe)
3. **Western Sydney's first dedicated truck rest stop.** Announced in Feb 2025 at Eastern Creek with $40M of initial funding. A survey of 800 drivers, mostly long-haul B-double and semi-trailer operators, ranked safety and security as the top priority. [NSW Gov](https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/western-sydney-gets-its-first-truck-rest-stop)
4. **Sydney truck-parking shortage (17 Sep 2025).** Big Rigs reports the pressure falls hardest on smaller operators without depots or legal off-street parking. Road Freight NSW has pitched sites to the City of Sydney and the NSW Government, including Pyrmont near the Fish Market. [Big Rigs](https://bigrigs.com.au/2025/09/17/sydneys-left-field-options-to-fix-truck-parking-shortage/)
5. **Fleet size (dated).** The ABS Survey of Motor Vehicle Use for the 12 months to June 2018 reported 521,255 rigid trucks and 104,442 articulated trucks. [ABS 9208.0](https://www.abs.gov.au/ausstats/abs@.nsf/Lookup/9208.0main+features112%20months%20ended%2030%20June%202018) The current replacement is BITRE's Road Vehicles Australia, January 2025, whose figures were not extracted. [BITRE](https://www.bitre.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/BITRE-Road-vehicles-Australia-January-2025.pdf)

Supporting context (secondary, not counted in the five):
- **Oversize loads on Sydney's outskirts:** A 2023 NSW parliamentary submission says there is almost nowhere safe for oversize loads to park there. [NSW Parliament submission](https://www.parliament.nsw.gov.au/lcdocs/submissions/81944/0008%20Name%20suppressed%20.pdf)
- **Closure of an informal bay:** A truckie complained in Mar 2024 about the closure of a "dust bowl" parking bay near Sydney. [Big Rigs](https://bigrigs.com.au/2024/03/02/truckie-angry-over-closure-of-dust-bowl-parking-bay-near-sydney/)
- **Melbourne service centres:** An interstate driver was quoted saying drivers are "struggling to find suitable parking at most service centres". This comes via a US newsletter. [Rig Chronicles](https://the-rig-chronicles.beehiiv.com/p/rig-hut-round-welcoming-new-lot-partners-australian-drivers)
- **Rest-area shortage:** The Australasian College of Road Safety (ACRS) cites a "recognised and confirmed lack of sufficient truck rest areas in Australia" (undated). [ACRS](https://archive.acrs.org.au/article/green-reflector-marking-of-informal-truck-bays)

### Inferences
- The binding constraints are urban and peri-urban: the one-hour residential rule and the loss of informal bays leave owner-drivers without legal overnight parking near home or depots. The marketplace model, which turns landowner yards into bookable bays, matches this most directly.
- Corridor rest-area demand is being addressed by government with free public rest areas, which limits willingness to pay on highways.

### Gaps
- No search was spent on HVNL fatigue rules (standard-hours rest requirements), so no cited figure is given.
- No current (2025–26) count of heavy vehicles or truck drivers was extracted.
- No dated Australian survey quantifying the truck-parking shortage in bays was found.
