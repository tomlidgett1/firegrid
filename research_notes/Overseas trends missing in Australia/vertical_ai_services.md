## Key Question 1 — Which vertical-AI / AI-native services companies grew fastest in 2024–2026, and in which industries?

### Takeaway
In the evidence gathered, the strongest 2024–2026 growth signals are in:
- claimant-side legal AI, led by US personal injury (EvenUp: valued over $1B around Oct 2024, then over $2B on 7 Oct 2025);
- regulator-authorised AI-native law firms in England & Wales (Garfield.Law in 2025, LawFairy in Feb 2026);
- AI-native insurance agencies (Harper: raised $46.8M in Feb 2026; 5,000+ customers);
- success-fee property-tax appeal services (Ownwell: raised $50M in Feb 2026; 1M+ appeals).

These share one model. Each is a licensed firm, or a tool for licensed firms, that sells an outcome in high-volume, document-heavy, rules-bound work. It is paid per case, by commission or on success.

### Inferences
- The model being proven overseas is the licensed outcome-seller: a law firm, insurance agency or appeals service that uses AI to handle volume and takes a per-case fee, commission or success fee. Software-only vertical AI (EvenUp) and AI-native firms (Garfield, LawFairy, Alma, Harper, Ownwell) are converging on it.
- Regulators are opening a path, but only for narrow, standardised work (SRA). An Australian version should therefore target one tightly scoped procedure rather than full-service practice.
- EvenUp's valuation doubled in about 12 months (Oct 2024 to Oct 2025), and RELX/LexisNexis invested. Legal-information incumbents appear to see claimant-side AI as strategic, so they could buy or partner with an Australian equivalent.
- Some Australian founders build AI-native services for the US first (Rosella, and OpenDebt exploring the US). That leaves local niches open, but it also shows founders see the Australian market as small.

## Key Question 2 — Which have no Australian equivalent, and is there an Australia-specific hook that makes a local version necessary and defensible?

### Takeaway
Of the concepts checked, only two have no Australian equivalent so far:
1. **AI claims preparation for injured and disabled claimants**, covering personal injury, CTP, workers' compensation, and TPD or income-protection insurance held in super (the EvenUp model).
2. **An AI-native land-value and land-tax objection service paid on success** (the Ownwell model).

Both rest on state-based Australian rules that a US tool does not encode. Eight other concepts already have Australian entrants. Both survivors had shortened checks and need the full verification pass.

### Cited Findings

#### Candidate 1 (rank 1): AI claims preparation for PI, CTP, workers' comp and TPD-in-super claims (a B2B tool for plaintiff firms, or an AI-native claims firm)

**(1) Concept and how it makes money.** The product ingests a claimant's medical records, employment and earnings evidence, and the relevant policy or scheme rules. It then produces:
- medical chronologies and injury summaries;
- eligibility assessments, such as whether a TPD definition is met;
- damages or benefit calculations;
- claim and settlement documents for insurers, super trustees or scheme agents.

There are two ways to earn revenue:
- a per-case fee or subscription sold to plaintiff firms, as EvenUp does;
- an AI-native incorporated legal practice that collects professional fees when a claim succeeds, following the Garfield.Law and LawFairy pattern.

*Whether Australian fee rules allow the second option was not checked (see Gaps).*

**(2) Overseas exemplars.**
- EvenUp (US): $150M Series E at a valuation above $2B on 7 Oct 2025; $385M raised in total; valued above $1B around Oct 2024; 2,000+ firms; 10,000 cases a week in Oct 2025. — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251007138957/en/EvenUp-Raises-150M-Series-E-at-2B-Valuation-to-Redefine-Personal-Injury-Law-and-Level-the-Playing-Field-for-Injury-Victims); [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/feed/evenup-raises-385m-valued-over-2b)

**(5) Australian fit, regulatory hook and tailwinds.**
- Law firms handle TPD claims made through superannuation, and they market the complexity of policy wording and strict criteria. — [Attwood Marshall](https://attwoodmarshall.com.au/melbourne/personal-injury/superannuation-insurance-tpd-claims/); [TGB](https://tgb.com.au/services/injured-people/superannuation-claims-total-permanent-disability)
- Demand is proven: Australia's largest plaintiff firms have built their own tools (Maurice Blackburn, 2020). — [Lawyers Weekly](https://www.lawyersweekly.com.au/sme-law/29102-ai-partnership-fast-tracks-superannuation-claims)
- Super funds are urging members to claim through the fund before hiring lawyers; Energy Super's CEO has said so publicly. That could open a channel for a cheaper AI-assisted claims service. — [Super Review](https://superreview.moneymanagement.com.au/comment/782)
- There may be a regulatory pathway for an AI-native firm. An Astris Law article from Jul 2026 argues Australia "built the corporate scaffolding" for a Garfield-style AI firm (incorporated legal practices) before England did. — [Astris Law](https://www.astrislaw.com/article/ai-law-firm-australia-garfield-question)
- *Possible moat (inference, to verify):* each state and territory runs its own CTP and workers' compensation scheme, with its own benefit formulas, thresholds and forms. **(6) Key risks.**
- There are few buyers, they are concentrated, and they can build their own tools (Maurice Blackburn did).
- Global legal-AI vendors already in Australia (Legora, NexLaw), or EvenUp itself, could add Australian workflows.
- Three regulatory limits were **not checked** (see Gaps): court and regulator rules on generative AI in evidence, health-privacy obligations, and the fee rules for an AI-native firm.
- If super funds steer members away from lawyers, plaintiff firms' volumes could shrink. That hurts a B2B tool but helps a model that sells direct to claimants.

**(5) Australian fit, regulatory hook and tailwinds.**
- **Queensland:** lodging an objection is free. The Act allows a fee, but none has been set, and objectors do not need formal representation. — [Qld Parliament petition response](https://apps.parliament.qld.gov.au/E-Petitions/Home/DownloadResponse/df444510-c7cd-4f40-af95-322346e1b0b8)

- **NSW:** objections go to the Valuer General and can cover the land value or the details on a Notice of Valuation or land-tax assessment. Only the person who received the notice, or someone they authorise, can lodge one. — [NSW Valuer General material via Wentworth Shire](https://www.wentworth.nsw.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/VG-Review-2020.pdf); [NSW Parliament VG newsletter](https://www.parliament.nsw.gov.au/ladocs/other/8537/Appendix%201%20VG%20Newsletter%202005.pdf) (attribution approximate)

- **Victoria:** over 59% of objections and over 40% of appeals received by the state revenue office related to land-tax assessments. The year is not given and the source attribution is uncertain. — [Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/australia/property-taxes/1431168/objecting-to-state-land-tax-assessments); [FVG](https://www.fvg.com.au/?p=6504)
- *Possible moat (inference):* each state values land on its own cycle and method, with its own objection window. A national engine that encodes these rules would be hard for both do-it-yourself objectors and local valuers to match.

**(6) Key risks.**
- The addressable market is probably smaller than in the US. Australian land tax falls mainly on investment and commercial land above state thresholds (*unverified*), and Ownwell's average US saving is only $774.
- Success rates and objection volumes by state are unknown (see Gaps).
- Lodging is free in some states, so owners may resist paying a fee.
- Valuers-General may push back on large numbers of AI-generated objections.
- Two licensing questions were not checked: valuer registration, and whether advising on state land tax counts as a regulated tax-agent service.
- Ownwell's complaints show the reputational risk. — [Trustpilot](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/ownwell.com)

## Key Question 3 — Which large, fragmented Australian professional-services industries remain unserved by AI-native entrants?

### Takeaway
In this session's evidence, two fragmented Australian professional services have no AI-native entrant:
- claimant-side injury and disability claims work (PI, CTP, workers' comp, TPD in super);
- advice on property-valuation and land-tax objections.

By contrast, AI entrants appeared in Australia between 2020 and 2026, mostly in 2025–2026, in:
- strata management;
- SME commercial insurance broking;
- debt collection;
- migration advice;
- R&D tax advisory;
- legal services for small businesses;
- hospital clinical coding.

### Inferences
