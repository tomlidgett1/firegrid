#### Candidate 1: Employer-paid eldercare / family-caregiver navigation benefit

**(1) Concept and revenue model.**
- **Who pays:** employers, with group-life insurers, super funds and private health insurers as possible distribution partners.
- **What employees get:** a named care navigator (social worker, nurse or aged-care specialist) plus an app.
- **What the navigator does:**
  - Triages the family's situation.
  - Handles My Aged Care registration and assessment preparation.
  - Explains Support at Home classifications, budgets and participant contributions.
  - Shortlists providers.
  - Supports residential-care placement and the accommodation-payment decision.
  - Plans for dementia, hospital discharge and the end-of-life pathway.
  - Coordinates siblings and other family members.
- **The app:** care plans, a document vault and toolkits for managers.
- **Revenue:** a per-employee-per-month platform fee across the whole workforce, or per-case pricing for smaller employers.
- **Upsells:** manager training, carer-friendly-workplace accreditation and anonymised workforce analytics.
- **What to avoid:** referral fees from care providers (a conflict of interest).

**(2) Overseas exemplars (unverified; no source URLs retrieved).**
- **Wellthy:** US, founded c.2014 [M]. A "care concierge" for employers and health plans that pairs human Care Coordinators with a platform [M]. 2024–26 funding and member metrics not recalled. Verify at wellthy.com newsroom and Crunchbase.
- **Cariloop:** US (Dallas), founded c.2013 [M]. A caregiving-coaching platform sold to employers [M]. 2024–26 metrics need verifying.
- **Homethrive** (US, c.2017) [L] and **ianacare** (US, c.2018) [L]: caregiver support sold to employers and health systems. Verify funding in 2024–26.
- **Bright Horizons:** includes elder-care support and back-up adult care in its employer contracts [M].
- **UK:** Carers UK's "Employers for Carers" employer membership network [M]; Mobilise (digital carer support) [L].
- **Demand indicators:**
  - AARP / National Alliance for Caregiving, *Caregiving in the US 2025*: about **63 million** US family caregivers, up from about 53 million in 2020 [M]. Verify at aarp.org.
  - **UK Carer's Leave Act 2023**, in force **6 April 2024**: one week of unpaid carer's leave a year, available from the first day of employment [H].
  - **Japan's amended Child Care and Family Care Leave Act**, from **1 April 2025**: employers must inform employees individually about caregiving-leave options and give early information, e.g. around age 40 [M].

**(3) Why it is accelerating now.**
- Baby boomers are reaching their late 70s and 80s, and employees in their 40s and 50s (the "sandwich generation") are cutting hours or leaving work to care.
- Labour shortages make retaining staff more valuable.
- The UK (2024) and Japan (2025) have put carer support on HR agendas by law.
- Benefit buyers are moving from health-insurance-only packages towards benefits tied to life events.

**(5) Australian fit, regulatory feasibility and funding flows.**
- **Need:**
  - **Support at Home** started **1 Nov 2025**, alongside the new Aged Care Act. It replaced Home Care Packages and Short-Term Restorative Care with:
    - 8 ongoing classifications with quarterly budgets
    - means-tested participant contributions
    - an Assistive Technology and Home Modifications (AT-HM) scheme
    - Restorative Care and End-of-Life pathways [M]
  - The new Act also changed residential accommodation rules; for example, providers may retain part of a refundable accommodation deposit (RAD) [M].
  - Together these sharply increase the complexity families face.
- **Scale:**
  - About **3.0 million carers** in Australia (ABS Survey of Disability, Ageing and Carers 2022) [M].
  - Informal care has a replacement value of about **$77.9bn a year** (Deloitte Access Economics for Carers Australia, 2020) [M].
- **Regulation:** non-clinical navigation needs no AHPRA or TGA approval. Watch-outs:
  - Advice on residential fees, the choice between a lump-sum deposit and daily payments, and means-testing can become personal financial product advice. Partner with AFSL-licensed aged-care financial advisers [M].
  - Privacy Act rules on health information [H].
  - Check the FBT treatment of the benefit for employers [L].
- **Funding flows:**
  - Employer HR, EAP and wellbeing budgets.
  - Group insurers and super funds (member retention, return to work).
  - Private health insurers (member value-adds).
  - WGEA reporting pressure on flexibility and carer support [M].
  - Not fundable directly from Support at Home budgets.

**(6) Key risks.**
- Free government alternatives.
- A thinner employer-benefits culture than the US (no employer-provided health insurance).
- Navigator labour costs.
- Long enterprise sales cycles.
- EAPs adding "good-enough" eldercare modules.
- Harm and reputational damage if navigation advice is wrong.

#### Candidate 2: AI voice check-in agents for older people living at home (sold to Support at Home providers)

**(1) Concept and revenue model.**
- AI voice agents built on large language models call older clients on a schedule (e.g. daily or weekly), or speak with them through a smart speaker or tablet.
- Each call checks mood, loneliness, falls, pain, appetite, hydration, medication adherence and upcoming appointments.
- The agent writes structured notes into the provider's care-management system and escalates red flags to a human care manager or nurse.
- **Extensions:** post-hospital-discharge calls, preparation for care-plan reviews, heatwave welfare checks, appointment reminders.
- **Revenue:** per-client-per-month software fees from Support at Home providers, plus contracts with Primary Health Networks (PHNs), state health services (post-discharge follow-up) and insurers. An optional family add-on is possible.

**(2) Overseas exemplars (unverified).**
- **Hippocratic AI:** US, founded 2023 [H]. "Safety-focused", non-diagnostic healthcare agents for chronic-care check-ins, post-discharge follow-up and pre-operative preparation, sold to health systems and payers [M].
  - Series A about $53M at about a $500M valuation (Mar 2024) [M].
  - **Series B $141M at about a $1.64bn valuation (Jan 2025, led by Kleiner Perkins)** [M].
  - A further round at a higher valuation was reported in late 2025 [L].
  - Verify at hippocraticai.com newsroom, TechCrunch, Fierce Healthcare.
- **Naver CLOVA CareCall:** South Korea, launched c.2021 [M]. AI phone calls to older people living alone, bought by local governments [M]. Current scale needs verifying.
- **ElliQ (Intuition Robotics):** Israel/US. A proactive AI companion device. The New York State Office for the Aging distributed hundreds of units from 2022 and reported large self-reported reductions in loneliness (a figure of about 95% circulated in 2023) [L]. Verify.
- **Ufonia:** UK. "Dora", an autonomous clinical voice assistant for routine follow-up such as after cataract surgery, used in NHS trusts [M].

**(3) Why it is accelerating now.**
- Real-time voice AI improved sharply in 2024–25 in latency, naturalness and cost per minute.
- Care workforces are short-staffed.
- Payers want proactive outreach at scale.
- Super-ageing societies (Korea, Japan) are driving government purchasing.

**(5) Australian fit, regulatory feasibility and funding flows.**

  - Support at Home providers must deliver care management, funded by roughly **10% of each participant's quarterly budget** [M], and must respond to changing needs. AI check-ins could stretch that budget further.
  - CEDA projected a shortfall of about **110,000 direct aged-care workers a year by 2030** (2021 report) [M].
  - Rural and remote reach; GP shortages mean fewer routine touchpoints.
- **Regulation:**
  - Non-diagnostic wellbeing calls with human escalation likely fall outside the TGA's medical-device definition.
  - Symptom triage or clinical decision support could make the product Software as a Medical Device, needing ARTG inclusion or an exemption [M].
  - Providers stay accountable under the new Act's strengthened Quality Standards [M].
  - Privacy Act (sensitive information and call recordings) [H] and state call-recording / listening-device laws [M].
- **Funding:**
  - Provider operating costs drawn from care-management funds.
  - PHN commissioning.
  - State hospital programs to avoid readmissions.
  - NDIS providers (possible extension).

**(6) Key risks.**
- Clinical safety and AI errors ("hallucinations").
- Older people may distrust automated calls given the scam epidemic.
- Dependence on foundation-model vendors.
- Global players entering Australia.
- A fragmented provider market, although Support at Home is driving consolidation.

#### Candidate 3: AI fall detection + remote clinical review in residential aged care (SafelyYou model)

**(1) Concept and revenue model.**
- With consent, privacy-preserving AI cameras (or radar) in residents' rooms detect falls and near-falls in real time and alert staff.
- For each event, a remote clinical team (nurses or occupational therapists) reviews the clip to work out how the fall happened and whether the head was struck. This avoids unnecessary emergency department transfers.
- The service then gives fall-prevention recommendations and automates quality-indicator reporting.
- **Revenue:** a per-bed-per-month subscription plus installation fees, possibly with outcome-linked pricing.

**(2) Overseas exemplars (unverified).**
- **SafelyYou:** US, founded c.2016 (UC Berkeley origins) [M]. AI video fall detection plus remote clinical review in memory care and assisted living [M]. A Series C was reported around 2024 [L]. Verify at safely-you.com and Senior Housing News.
- **Kepler Vision Technologies:** Netherlands. "Kepler Night Nurse" computer-vision monitoring in care homes [M].
- **Teton.ai:** Denmark. A computer-vision "AI nurse assistant" for care homes and hospitals [L].
- **Nobi:** Belgium. Smart ceiling lamps with fall detection [L–M].
- **Vayyar Care:** Israel. Radar-based fall detection [M].
- No 2024–26 adoption metrics recalled with confidence; all need verifying.

**(3) Why it is accelerating now.**
- Cheap edge-AI chips let images be anonymised on the device itself.
- Night shifts are short-staffed.
- Regulators and payers now measure falls.
- People accept in-room monitoring more readily than before.

**(6) Key risks.**
- Privacy backlash.
- Consent is complex where residents have dementia.
- Nurse-call incumbents adding AI themselves.

#### Candidate 4: DTC male fertility (mail-in semen analysis + home-collection sperm cryobanking)

**(1) Concept and revenue model.**
- The customer orders a kit online and collects a sample at home, using a kit with a stabilising medium.
- A courier takes it to an accredited andrology lab within about 24–72 hours for semen analysis: count, motility, morphology and optionally DNA fragmentation.
- Optional cryostorage is offered, with telehealth urology or andrology consultations.
- **Revenue:**
  - kit fees
  - annual storage subscriptions
  - consultations
  - B2B channels: employer fertility benefits, oncology (fertility preservation before chemotherapy), pre-vasectomy banking, and IVF back-up samples

**(2) Overseas exemplars (unverified).**
- **Legacy:** US, founded c.2018 [M].
  - Sells at-home semen analysis and sperm-freezing kits with storage plans.
  - Raised venture rounds 2019–2022, including a reported ~$25M Series B in 2022 [L].
  - Acquired competitor Dadi around 2023 [L–M].
  - Sold through fertility-benefit managers [L].
  - **No 2024–26 growth metrics recalled.** This is the weakest growth evidence of the top five. Verify at givelegacy.com and Crunchbase.
- **Fellow:** US. Mail-in semen analysis [M].
- **Posterity Health:** US. A virtual male-fertility clinic [L–M].
- **ExSeed Health:** Denmark/UK. A smartphone-based home sperm test [M].
- **Mojo:** Denmark. AI semen analysis [L].

**(3) Why it is accelerating now.**
- Evidence of falling sperm counts has raised awareness: Levine et al., *Human Reproduction Update* (Nov 2022) reported a decline of more than 50% from 1973 to 2018 [M].
- Men are becoming fathers later.
- Fertility benefits increasingly cover men.
- Demand for banking before vasectomy or oncology treatment.
- Home-collection stabilising media make mail-in samples workable.

**(5) Australian fit, regulatory feasibility and funding flows.**
- **Regulation:** storing sperm is an assisted reproductive technology (ART) activity, regulated by state law [H]:
  - NSW *Assisted Reproductive Technology Act 2007* (provider registration)
  - Victoria *Assisted Reproductive Treatment Act 2008*
  - WA *Human Reproductive Technology Act 1991* (licensing)
  - SA *Assisted Reproductive Treatment Act 1988*
  - Accreditation by the Reproductive Technology Accreditation Committee (RTAC) [H], NHMRC ART guidelines [H], and storage-time limits that vary by state [M].
- **Pathway:** become, or partner with, a registered ART provider and an accredited andrology lab; a telehealth doctor issues test requests.
- **Context:** the 2025 Monash IVF embryo mix-ups led health ministers to pursue national ART regulatory reform [M]. That raises the compliance bar but also creates an opening for a provider that builds trust.
- **Funding:**
  - Mostly out-of-pocket.
  - Employer fertility benefits.
  - Some publicly funded fertility preservation, e.g. Victoria's public fertility services [L–M].
  - Medicare rebates for semen analysis on referral [L–M].

**(6) Key risks.**
- Incumbents (Monash IVF, Virtus) could copy the model quickly.
- Samples may not survive transport across Australian distances (WA, regional areas).
- Licensing costs.
- A small market (about 27M people) [M].
- Sensitivity to reputational damage.

#### Candidate 8: Human composting / natural organic reduction (NOR, "terramation")

**(1) Concept and revenue model.**
- The body is placed in a vessel with organic material (woodchips, alfalfa, straw) for about 30–60 days.
- The result is about one cubic yard of soil, returned to the family or donated to conservation land.
- **Revenue:** consumer price of roughly US$5,000–7,000 [M], a facility plus funeral-home partner network, and pre-need (pre-paid) sales.
