## Key question 3: What Australian facts matter for launch (night-shift penalty rates under retail/hospitality awards, labour shortages, workplace/privacy rules for video, rules on remote age checks for alcohol/tobacco sales)?

### Takeaway
No regulatory or demand facts could be retrieved, so none can be cited: **0 of the up-to-5 requested cited facts**. The items below are prior-knowledge pointers. Each must be checked against the official source named before anyone relies on it.

### Cited Findings
- None.

### Inferences
- (Prior knowledge, unverified) The economics depend on two things. First, the evening and night loadings that awards require for on-site staff, which the remote model avoids or undercuts. Second, whether an offshore operator employed by a non-Australian entity sits outside the Fair Work system. That second point needs legal advice and carries reputational risk, and the "follow-the-sun" element of the pitch depends on it.
- (Prior knowledge, unverified) The hardest regulatory question is likely remote age verification for liquor and tobacco. Responsible-sale obligations are set state by state and are written around sighting evidence of age in person. As far as this verifier knows, nobody has tested whether a remote operator checking ID over video meets those obligations.
- (Prior knowledge, unverified) Privacy law pushes the design away from biometrics. Live video viewed by offshore operators is probably a cross-border disclosure under APP 8. Any automated face or ID matching runs into recent OAIC enforcement against retailers' use of facial recognition.
