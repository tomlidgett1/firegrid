# Verification: screen-free "landline for kids" in Australia (adversarial presence check, re-run)

**Re-run date:** 13 October 2026. This replaces the previous verifier's notes, which ran no searches.
**Evidence base:** 38 WebSearch calls in standard mode. 33 were presence checks (87%). One call was rejected because the search tool cannot access kidspot.com.au. The one permitted WebFetch (ciaobellaphone.com.au) failed with `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND`. All facts below therefore come from search-result extracts, not fetched pages.

## VERDICT: PRESENT. Confidence: 95%

- **Ciao Bella** ([ciaobellaphone.com.au](https://ciaobellaphone.com.au/)) is an Australian, family-owned start-up that already sells essentially this product:
  - A retro, screen-free kids' home phone that runs on home Wi-Fi with no SIM.
  - A parent app for approved contacts and quiet hours.
  - Free calls between Ciao Bella phones, which works as a kid-to-kid network.
  - 000 always works.
  - An optional **Bella Plus** plan at **A$14.99/month** for calls to ordinary Australian numbers.
  - Hardware at **A$149** (2 phones A$279, 3 phones A$415). Orders are stated to ship in about three weeks.
  - Press coverage in the Newcastle Herald and the St George & Sutherland Shire Leader.
- **Tin Can**, the US exemplar, has an Australian storefront ([tincan.kids/en-au](https://tincan.kids/en-au/products/tin-can)):
  - Taking **pre-orders at A$180**. Pre-orders for Australia, the UK and NZ opened on **21 May 2026**.
  - Shipping across Australia is targeted **by 30 Nov 2026**.
  - Its terms of service authorise use in Australia.
- **Why this is PRESENT.** Either fact alone meets the PRESENT rule: "an Australian business … or an overseas company serving Australian customers offers the same thing." A new entrant would be at best third. It could be fourth if Pinwheel brings its US "Pinwheel Home" kids' landline (launched July 2026) to Australia, where Pinwheel already sells kids' smartphones. That is unresolved.
- **Why not 100%:**
  - The Ciao Bella evidence shows a small operation: one Trustpilot review, two regional newspaper stories, and company pages seen only through search extracts.
  - Ciao Bella's launch date is not established, and one article calls it "a mobile".
  - Tin Can is pre-order only until its stated November 2026 ship date.
  - None of these caveats changes the verdict.

## 1. Can Australian parents buy a dedicated kids' approved-contacts landline from any provider today?

### Takeaway
Yes. Ciao Bella, an Australian start-up, sells a Wi-Fi kids' home phone with approved contacts, free phone-to-phone calls, an optional A$14.99/month plan and 000 access. It quotes roughly three-week shipping. Tin Can has taken Australian pre-orders since 21 May 2026 (A$180, ships by 30 Nov 2026). No Australian telco, crowdfunding campaign or major retailer offers a kid-specific landline. Telstra and Panasonic Australia are, however, actively marketing ordinary landlines to families as a screen-free option for kids.

## 2. What is the closest thing found, and how does it differ?

### Takeaway
The closest things are not substitutes. They are the same product. Ciao Bella matches the concept and is selling now. Tin Can is the exemplar itself and is on pre-order in Australia. The differences between them are minor: price, timing and industrial design.

Partial substitutes do a different job:
- **Spacetalk, Moochies, Garmin Bounce, the Pinwheel smartphone and generic 4G watches** are personal, screen-based, cellular devices with approved contacts.
- **Ordinary cordless phones** (Panasonic, VTech) and **DIY NBN VoIP lines** have no allow-list, no parent app and no kids' network.

Overseas, the category is crowding fast: Pinwheel Home, Waffle Home, Ring Ring, Ooma My Phone and YAPA in the UK.

## 3. Which Australian facts matter for launch?

### Takeaway
The under-16 ban has strong parental support (65–88% across polls) but leaks heavily (78% of under-16s still on covered platforms in June 2026), so parents still want screen-free social channels. Any kids' phone that reaches ordinary numbers or 000 falls under the Emergency Call Service rules. ACMA tightened those rules from 1 Nov 2025 and is under scrutiny after the September 2025 Optus Triple Zero failure. Both current players already advertise 000 access.

### Cited Findings

**Top 5 facts on demand and regulation**

### Inferences
- A kids' phone that calls ordinary Australian numbers (like Bella Plus or the Party Line Plan) very likely makes the operator, or its wholesale partner, a carriage service provider with obligations for 000, outages, the TIO and consumer codes. Both incumbents explicitly support 000, which sets the expectation for any new entrant. Regulatory scrutiny of 000 is unusually high after the Optus failure.
- Demand drivers are real: strong parental support for the ban, leakage showing children still want social connection, and phone-free schools. But at least two players are already positioned to capture that demand in Australia.
