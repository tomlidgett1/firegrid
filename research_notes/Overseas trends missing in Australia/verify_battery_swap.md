# Verification: battery-swapping network for delivery-rider light EVs (e-bikes, e-scooters, e-mopeds) — does it already exist in Australia?

**VERDICT: PARTIAL (borderline PRESENT). Confidence: 60%.**
The swing factor is one unverified Sydney operator, **eSwap (eswap.com.au)**. A Shopify store-directory listing describes it as "battery swapping for electric bikes and scooters across Sydney" for "commuters, delivery riders and fleets". If eSwap is actually running cabinets, the verdict becomes PRESENT. My rough split of outcomes: PARTIAL about 60%, PRESENT about 30%, ABSENT about 10%. Even if eSwap is ignored, adjacent offerings stop a new entrant claiming to be entirely first: Zoomo's two-battery bikes and in-store spare-battery rental, Australian-made fire-safe charging lockers, and a historical operator-side swap pilot in Adelaide.

Prepared 13 October 2026 by an independent verifier. Budget used: 37 WebSearch attempts, all in standard mode. Of these, 35 ran and 2 were rejected with API 400 errors because the domain filters included blocked domains. 33 of the 37 (89%) were presence checks. I also made 1 WebFetch attempt, on eswap.com.au, which failed with `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND`. Fetching is blocked across this environment, so the failure says nothing about whether the site is live.

## 1. Does any Australian business, pilot, or overseas entrant offer battery swapping for e-bikes, e-scooters or e-mopeds to riders in Australia?

### Takeaway
None of the named overseas swap operators serves Australia. That covers Popwheels, Swobbee, Gogoro, Oyika, Honda Power Pack Exchanger, ENYRING and Zoomo's PowerShelter swap. No government or council swap pilot exists either. One Sydney business, eSwap (eswap.com.au), presents itself online as doing exactly this concept, but I could not verify that it operates: I found no stations, pricing, ABN, media coverage or social activity. A new entrant therefore cannot safely claim to be "first in Australia".

### Inferences
- **Why not ABSENT.** eSwap's listing describes the exact concept in Sydney: e-bikes and e-scooters, delivery riders, fleets. That alone means a new entrant cannot claim to be "first" without first ruling eSwap out.
- **Why not PRESENT.** I found nothing showing an ongoing service: no cabinet locations, pricing, ABN, news coverage, LinkedIn page, social activity or rider discussion. The complete absence of media coverage is notable. NSW media and Fire and Rescue NSW are very focused on e-bike battery fires, and a firefighting official has publicly asked for a swap network. That suggests eSwap is pre-launch, very small or dormant. This is an inference, not a finding.
- **Zoomo is the biggest latent threat.** Zoomo already runs swaps in Berlin (through PowerShelter lockers), in Toronto (at its stores) and in France. In Australia it has three stores and two-battery bikes. It could plausibly copy the model locally, with a partner or alone, at low marginal cost.
- **Vmoto is a potential supplier or partner.** Vmoto (ASX-listed) sells swap-station hardware it already deploys abroad. Lit Energy Network Australia, if its ASX listing went ahead, gives local investors exposure to the model, though it operates only in Vietnam.
- **Rough probabilities:** PRESENT about 30% (if eSwap is running cabinets), PARTIAL about 60%, ABSENT about 10%. That gives 60% confidence in PARTIAL.

### Inferences
- **What a new entrant should still be able to claim.** It could reasonably call itself the first *verified, at-scale, multi-site* swap network for delivery riders in Australia. It should not claim to be the first swap service of any kind until eSwap is ruled out.
- **Zoomo's stores as a bridge.** Zoomo's two-battery design and in-store spare batteries meet the uptime need of Zoomo's own customers, at a cost of $10/week extra or so. A swap network has to beat that on convenience and on fire safety, since spare batteries are still charged at riders' homes.
- **Fire-safe lockers serve buildings, not riders.** Castledex-style lockers sell to building owners, not riders. A swap operator could instead partner with strata or end-of-trip facilities as cabinet host sites.

- No confirmation of Zoomo's Australian battery-ownership model (whether riders charge Zoomo-owned batteries at home), beyond the plan pages.

## 3. What Australian facts on demand or regulation matter?

### Takeaway
The regulatory and risk backdrop strongly favours certified, off-premises charging and swapping. Since 1 February 2026, NSW has required e-micromobility batteries, chargers and vehicles to be tested, certified and marked. Lithium-ion fires attended by Fire and Rescue NSW roughly doubled from 2022 to 2024. Strata bodies want charging out of apartments. Delivery platforms and regulators are engaged, but so far only through education. The size of the rider market is not reliably quantified.

### Cited Findings (5 key facts)
1. **NSW certification regime is in force from 1 Feb 2026.**
   - **What is covered.** E-bikes, e-scooters, e-skateboards and self-balancing devices, and the lithium-ion batteries that power them, are "declared electrical articles" under the Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Act 2017.
   - **Timeline.** Product safety standards applied from 1 Feb 2025. Testing, certification and marking were postponed from 1 Aug 2025 and took effect on 1 Feb 2026. They also cover second-hand devices and former hire fleets.
   - **What needs approval.** The battery, the charger and the vehicle each need their own Certificate of Approval and approval mark.
   - **Hire exemption.** Hire vehicles bought no later than 1 Feb 2026 and used only for hire are exempt from the testing and marking requirements.
   - Sources: [NSW Gov: new standards](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/safety-home/electrical-safety/lithium-ion-battery-safety/new-standards-for-lithium-ion-batteries-e-micromobility-devices); [NSW Gov: supplying e-micromobility products](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/selling-goods-and-services/supplying-e-micromobility-products)
   - **Penalties.** A 2024 article, written before the final rules, reported penalties of up to $825,000 for non-compliant suppliers. — [Council Magazine](https://councilmagazine.com.au/new-standards-for-e-bikes-and-e-scooters/)
   - **Train ban.** Separately, converted e-bikes were banned from NSW trains and the Metro from 1 Nov 2025, with fines up to $1,110. — [ABC, 22 Sep 2025](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-09-22/converted-e-bikes-ban-on-nsw-rail-metro-network-november-1/105799084)
2. **Fire trend.**
   - **Annual counts.** Reported suspected lithium-ion battery fires in NSW rose from 165 (2022) to 272 (2023) and 323 (2024). Fire and Rescue NSW's own February 2025 release says at least 318 for 2024, so sources vary between 317 and 323. — [ACS Information Age, 2025](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2025/sydney-man-dies-in-fire-as-battery-blazes-spike.html); [FRNSW, 10 Feb 2025](https://www.fire.nsw.gov.au/media/news/2025/20250210-spate-of-lithium-ion-fires-and-nsw-government-survey-sparks-community-wakeup-call-on-battery-risks)
   - **100th e-bike fire of the year.** Fire and Rescue NSW reported its 100th lithium-ion fire involving an e-bike "this year" after a Redfern terrace blaze. Per the search extract this was 28 Nov 2025. — [FRNSW](https://www.fire.nsw.gov.au/media/news/100th-e-bike-battery-fire-sparks-terrace-blaze)
3. **Strata demand to get charging out of apartments.** The Owners Corporation Network drafted a model by-law banning charging of e-bikes and e-scooters inside apartments, and banning storage of cheap imported, modified or damaged models. It acknowledged that few complexes have fire-safe charging stations in common areas. — [The Latz Report](https://thelatzreport.com.au/news/trade/australian-news-briefs-lusty-in-transition-threat-to-future-e-bike-sales/)
4. **Regulators and platforms are engaged, but only on education.**
   - **2024 NSW sessions.** Fire and Rescue NSW and SafeWork NSW worked with Uber Eats, DoorDash and Zoomo. The platforms agreed to educate delivery riders on safe storage and charging. — [Zoomo blog](https://www.ridezoomo.com/blog/keeping-food-delivery-riders-safe-in-nsw)
   - **Appetite for swapping.** A firefighting official told the ABC in 2023 that he wants a swappable battery network that charges batteries outside people's apartments. — [ABC, 18 Jan 2023](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023-01-18/e-scooter-lithium-battery-fire-risk-fears-/101863902)
5. **Market structure.**
   - **Menulog exit.** Menulog closed in Australia in November 2025, and the Transport Workers Union said "thousands of food delivery riders" were affected. That leaves Uber Eats and DoorDash as the main platforms. — [The New Daily, 12 Nov 2025](https://www.thenewdaily.com.au/news/2025/11/12/menulog-closes-in-australia)
   - **No reliable rider count.** I found no authoritative national count of delivery riders. IBISWorld's 1,040 "employment" figure for 2025 excludes contractor riders. — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/australia/employment/online-food-ordering-delivery-platforms/5538/)

### Inferences
- **Certification shapes a swap operator's costs and pitch.** Packs bought after 1 Feb 2026 would almost certainly need NSW-certified batteries and chargers. The hire exemption appears to cover only vehicles bought by that date. This is an inference from the rules as summarised; it needs legal confirmation. Certification also strengthens the pitch to strata bodies and platforms: riders get certified packs and never charge at home.
- **Platform consolidation simplifies partnerships.** With Menulog gone, most of the rider base sits with two platforms (Uber Eats and DoorDash) plus Zoomo as the main fleet provider. Those are the obvious partners, and also the obvious fast followers.

### Gaps
- No authoritative count of delivery riders in Australia or NSW. A figure would need to come from the ABS, the Fair Work Commission's gig-worker minimum-standards proceedings or a TWU publication.
- I did not verify which TWU campaigns concern e-bike battery safety. The search found only general TWU rider advocacy and the Menulog comment.
- Queensland's 2026 e-mobility bill, which may require a minimum age of 16, a driver's licence and a 10 km/h limit in cycle lanes, surfaced only through an Uber submission and a low-quality news brief. Its details and status are unverified. — [Qld Parliament submission](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/com/SDIWC-1AF9/TOLMEUPOCA-AB90/submissions/00002458.pdf)
- No official full-year 2025 Fire and Rescue NSW total was found.
