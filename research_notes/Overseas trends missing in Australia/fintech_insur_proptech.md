## Key Question 1: Which models grew fastest overseas in 2024–2026?

### Takeaway
I could find 2024–2026 evidence for five models with strong momentum:
- **Bereavement and estate-settlement platforms sold through insurers.** Empathy raised a US$47M Series B in March 2024 and a US$72M Series C in 2025, for US$162M in total.
- **Rent reporting for renters' credit.** Esusu was valued at US$1.2B in December 2025 and signed a Zillow distribution deal in October 2025.
- **Home warranties.** Frontdoor paid US$580M for 2-10 Home Buyers Warranty in December 2024 and guided 2025 revenue to about US$2.08B.
- **AI agents that handle consumer admin.** Pine raised a US$25M Series A in December 2025.
- **Parametric cover for homeowners' deductibles.** Sola's total funding grew from US$3.7M in December 2024 to US$11.7M in August 2025.

Two models showed no momentum. Leisure co-ownership (Pacaso) shrank in 2025, and I found no 2024–2026 growth data for deposit-replacement insurance (Rhino).

### Inferences
- **Winners sell through incumbents.** The in-window winners all reach customers through an incumbent partner:
  - Empathy has insurers as strategic investors and channels.
  - Esusu is distributed by Zillow.
  - Sola writes cover with Beazley and Spinnaker.
  - 2-10 sells through about 20,000 builders.
  - An Australian entrant should therefore build around an incumbent channel from day one, such as a super fund, life insurer, general insurer, REA/Domain, an energy retailer or builders.
- **Home warranties are large but consolidating, not hyper-growth.** Frontdoor's 2025 growth came mostly from the acquisition. The US$580M paid for 2-10 is the strongest validation signal in this set.
- **AI admin agents and parametric deductible cover are early.** Both are at funding rounds below US$30M, so they are validated as hot categories rather than as proven unit economics.
- **Discretionary property models are cyclical.** Pacaso contracted in 2025 and should be deprioritised.

## Key Question 2: Which are absent from Australia and legally feasible? Licensing barriers and the realistic pathway

### Takeaway
On one query each, two models turned up no Australian equivalent:
- AI agents that negotiate bills and cancel subscriptions.
- Warranties covering wear and tear on home systems and appliances.

Two more found only adjacent or partial players:
- Bereavement and estate concierge: estateXchange runs institution-facing rails, but no consumer concierge surfaced.
- Parametric household cover: Redicova covers cyclones only.

Three ideas are rejected because they already exist in Australia or were tried there: renter bond alternatives (also blocked by state laws), co-buying platforms, and rent reporting (an Equifax–Occubuy pilot). All verdicts are preliminary.

### Cited Findings

#### Ranked candidates, best first

##### 1. AI agent for consumer admin: bill negotiation, subscription cancellation, complaints and refunds
1. **How it works and makes money.** An app, or a white-label version embedded in a bank or insurer, whose AI agent phones and emails the user's providers. It negotiates lower telco, broadband, energy and insurance-renewal prices, cancels unwanted subscriptions, chases refunds and lodges complaints. Revenue would come from a subscription, a success fee or B2B licensing (**[inference]**). The function list mirrors what Pine says its agent does — [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/12/03/pine-launches-ai-agent-as-it-secures-25m-series-a/)
2. **Overseas exemplar.** Pine (US, Palo Alto; founding year not captured): US$25M Series A reported 3 Dec 2025; self-reported 93% negotiation success and more than US$3M saved — [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/12/03/pine-launches-ai-agent-as-it-secures-25m-series-a/). Its Consumer Friction Index of January 2026 found lowering bills was the top reason for use — [Business Wire, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260114758958/en)
3. **Why now (**[inference]**).** Voice-capable AI agents can now complete calls to retention desks end to end, which makes the labour-heavy model Rocket Money pioneered cheap to run.
4. **Australia check.**
   - Query run: "bill negotiation service Australia negotiate your bills for you".
   - Result: no Australian service that negotiates with a consumer's existing providers surfaced.
   - Closest found:
     - Easy Bill Pay, which only organises and tracks bills — [FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/fintech-companies/easy-bill-pay)
     - Centrepay, a government bill-payment service — [Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/sites/default/files/media/audio/2022/09/13744/15865-2208ne.pdf)
     - ProBills, whose offering I did not verify — [ProBills](https://www.probills.com.au/)
   - Queries attempted but blocked by the budget: `"Rocket Money" OR "Pine AI" Australia launch subscription cancellation app Australians`; "Australian startup AI agent calls telco energy retailer to lower bill".
   - Likely substitutes not yet checked (**[unverified]**): comparison and switching services (iSelect, Compare Club, Compare the Market, Energy Watch) and banks' in-app subscription controls.
   - **Verdict: Absent (preliminary, low confidence)** for agents that negotiate with your existing providers. Comparison and switching services are probably present.
5. **Fit and licensing (**[unverified]**).**
   - Negotiating telco, energy, gym or streaming bills is probably not a financial service, so no Australian Financial Services Licence (AFSL) should be needed.
   - Negotiating or switching insurance or credit products would probably count as dealing, arranging or credit assistance. That needs an AFSL, an Australian Credit Licence, or an authorised-representative or credit-representative arrangement.
   - Reading bank transactions would need Consumer Data Right (CDR) accreditation or a CDR-representative arrangement.
   - Energy-retail rules on explicit informed consent apply to switching; telcos require "authorised representative" status for account changes; consent to call recording is governed state by state.
   - Tailwinds (per project brief): CDR and open banking, PayTo, cost-of-living pressure.
6. **Key risks (**[inference]**).** Banks may add cancellation features; providers may refuse AI or third-party callers; Australia's smaller bill pool limits success fees; privacy and trust; scrutiny of AI "robot lawyer"-style claims.

##### 2. Home-systems and appliance warranty plans (home-service plans)
1. **How it works and makes money.** An annual or monthly plan with a per-call-out fee. It covers repair or replacement of household systems (hot water, split-system air-conditioning, electrical, plumbing) and appliances that fail through wear and tear, with app-dispatched vetted trades. It could be sold direct, gifted at settlement by agents or conveyancers, bundled by builders for new homes, or sold to landlords through property managers. The business earns the plan price minus claims and trade costs, with commissions paid to channels (**[inference]**). The builder channel is proven overseas, with about 20,000 builder partners at 2-10 — [Frontdoor FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1727263/000119312526076548/ftdr-20251231.htm)
2. **Overseas exemplar.** Frontdoor, Inc. (US):
   - Paid US$580M for 2-10 HBW, closing 19 Dec 2024 — [10-Q Q3 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1727263/000119312525267020/ftdr-20250930.htm)
   - Q3 2025 revenue of US$618M (+14%); 2025 revenue guidance raised to US$2.075–2.085B — [Q3 2025 earnings exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1727263/000119312525265709/ftdr-ex99_1.htm)
3. **Why now (**[inference]**).** Consolidation shows a profitable recurring category, and the builder channel adds scale.
4. **Australia check.**
   - Query run: "home warranty plan Australia appliances systems breakdown cover subscription".
   - Result: no Australian whole-of-home warranty provider surfaced.
   - Closest found: retailer extended "2 Year Replacement Plans", which the retailer states are in addition to Australian Consumer Law (ACL) rights and start when the manufacturer warranty ends — [Appliances Online](https://www.appliancesonline.com.au/product/2-year-replacement-plan-for-home-std-re-150)
   - Query attempted but blocked: "HomeServe Australia home emergency cover plumbing electrical breakdown plan RACV Home Assist".
   - Likely substitutes not yet checked (**[unverified]**): motoring clubs' emergency home-assistance memberships (RACV, NRMA, RACQ, RAA), insurers' home-assist add-ons, and statutory home-building warranty insurance for builder defects (NSW HBCF, Victorian DBI). The statutory schemes would overlap with the structural new-home warranty element of the 2-10 model.
   - **Verdict: Unclear**, leaning Absent for systems-and-appliance wear-and-tear plans.
5. **Fit and licensing (**[unverified]**).**
   - A third-party warranty may be "insurance business" under the Insurance Act 1973. If so, the realistic route is a managing general agent (MGA) or coverholder model with an APRA-authorised insurer or Lloyd's.
   - Distribution would need an AFSL or an authorised-representative arrangement, plus Design and Distribution Obligations (DDO) target-market determinations.
   - Marketing must not misstate ACL consumer-guarantee rights.
   - Tailwinds: renters are about a third of households (per brief), and state rental reforms (per brief) raise landlords' repair obligations, making landlords a buyer segment. Ageing housing stock and high trades costs also help.
6. **Key risks (**[inference]**).** ACL guarantees overlap for newer appliances; reputational risk from claim denials; shortages of trades; insurer appetite; Australia has no US-style habit of buying a warranty at settlement.

##### 3. Bereavement and estate-settlement concierge distributed by life insurers, super funds and employers (the Empathy model)
1. **How it works and makes money.** Software plus human case managers guide families after a death. Services include task lists, notifying institutions, help with super and life-insurance claims, probate preparation, finding assets and grief support. Life insurers, super funds and employers would pay for it as a member benefit, or families would pay per estate (**[inference]**, modelled on Empathy's insurer and employer channel) — [Startupim](https://startupim.com/company/empathy)
2. **Overseas exemplar.** Empathy (US; launched April 2021): US$47M Series B in March 2024 with MetLife, New York Life, MassMutual Ventures, Securian and Sumitomo — [Entrée Capital](https://entreecap.com/?p=6298). US$72M Series C in 2025, for US$162M in total — [Multiples.vc](https://multiples.vc/private-comps/empathy)
3. **Why now.** Insurers investing strategically gives built-in distribution (cited above). Ageing populations and the "great wealth transfer" are **[inference]**.
4. **Australia check.**
   - Query run: "estate administration probate concierge platform Australia startup".
   - Closest found:
     - **estateXchange** (Melbourne, founded 2023): a digital platform linking estate representatives such as lawyers with banks, share registries and super funds. It raised a A$12.5M seed with Macquarie Capital among the backers (funding date not captured) — [Business News Australia](https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/deceased-estate-admin-platform-estatexchange-secures-backing--from-macquarie-and-rich-listers.html)
     - **Yodal "Concierge":** estate-planning admin for financial advisers — [Financial Newswire](https://financialnewswire.com.au/fintech/aussie-fintech-unveils-automated-estate-planning-admin-service/)
     - **National Probate and Estates Group:** online help for executors to prepare and lodge probate, launched in NSW in 2016. Current status is unknown and the source attribution is uncertain — [benjoffe.com](https://www.benjoffe.com/probate)
     - **Smokeball:** estate-administration software for law firms — [Smokeball](https://www.smokeball.com.au/practice-areas/estate-administration)
   - The brief already excludes the government death-notification service (ADNS) and Safewill.
   - **Verdict: Unclear (partially present).** Institution-facing rails and practitioner tools exist, but I found no consumer concierge distributed through insurers or super funds. estateXchange looks like a partner rather than a blocker.
5. **Fit and licensing (**[unverified]**).**
   - Admin help on its own probably needs no AFSL.
   - Helping families make insurance claims may count as a regulated "claims handling" service under reforms that took effect in 2022.
   - Advice about super or insurance needs an AFSL. The route is to operate as an outsourced provider under the super fund's or insurer's licence and avoid personal advice.
   - Probate is state-based and some states restrict legal services by non-lawyers, so partnering with law firms would be needed.
   - Possible tailwind: super funds' handling of death-benefit claims is under regulatory scrutiny (needs a source).
6. **Key risks (**[inference]**).** A crowded adjacent space (estateXchange, ADNS, Safewill, public trustees); long enterprise sales cycles; legal-practice rules; the sensitivity of the moment.

##### 4. Parametric cover that tops up the home-insurance excess for hail, storm, flood and bushfire
1. **How it works and makes money.** A low-cost policy pays a fixed cash sum when an objective trigger is met at the insured address, such as radar hail size, flood depth, a bushfire perimeter or wind speed. The payment funds the home-insurance excess or provides emergency cash, including for uninsured renters. The business would earn MGA commission from the capacity provider (**[inference]**, modelled on Sola) — [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/parametric-insurtech-sola-raises-8m-series-a-to-expand-property-coverage/)
2. **Overseas exemplar.** Sola (US): funding grew from US$3.7M (December 2024) to US$11.7M after an US$8M Series A (August 2025). ## Key Question 3: Which line up best with Australia's housing and insurance affordability pressures?

### Takeaway
On the evidence gathered:
- **Parametric excess top-up cover** fits insurance affordability most directly.
- **Gradual homeownership** fits housing affordability most directly, but is capital-heavy and overlaps Help to Buy.
- **Home-systems warranties sold to landlords** fit the renter-heavy market and state rental reforms.
- **The AI admin agent** fits cost-of-living rather than housing.
- **The bereavement concierge** has little to do with affordability, but addresses a super-fund operations problem.

The Australian affordability statistics behind these judgements could not be sourced in this session.

### Cited Findings
- **Sola** targets high deductibles and out-of-pocket costs after wind, hail and tornado damage. That is the design pattern for lowering premiums with a higher excess and topping it up — [Coverager](https://coverager.com/sola-raises-8-million/); [Beazley](https://www.beazley.com/en-us/articles/beazley-and-sola-technologies-introduce-innovative-tornado-crisis-insurance-partnership-spinnaker?pdf=1)
- **Redicova** was pitched on faster payments than traditional insurance, with claims paid without physical assessment — [Lloyd's press release](https://www.lloyds.com/insights/media-centre/press-releases/lloyds-disaster-risk-facility-launches-a-parametric-cyclone-insurance-product-in-northern-australia)
- **Wayhome** lets households start with at least 5% and no mortgage, and charges customers no fees — [Corporate Adviser](https://corporate-adviser.com/?p=41797)
- **The Equifax–Occubuy pilot** targets reliable renters with thin credit files seeking home loans, though it doesn't replace a deposit — [Regulation Asia](https://www.regulationasia.com/articles/australia-to-pilot-use-of-rent-payments-in-mortgage-credit-scoring)
- **Kohab's** weak uptake suggests co-buying is a hard sell as an affordability answer in Australia — [NAB Business](https://business.nab.com.au/kohab-launches-social-network-for-homebuyers-32334)
- **2-10 HBW's** builder channel reached about 20,000 builder partners by 31 Dec 2025, showing warranties can be bundled into new-home purchases — [Frontdoor FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1727263/000119312526076548/ftdr-20251231.htm)

### Inferences
- **Insurance affordability.** A parametric excess top-up for hail, flood or bushfire lets households raise their excess to cut premiums while capping out-of-pocket risk. It also gives uninsured renters a cash payout. This is the closest match to the brief's "insurance affordability in disaster-prone areas" tailwind. Cyclone is already served by Redicova.
- **Housing affordability.** Gradual ownership is the closest overseas model. A private, super-funded version could complement Help to Buy, for example for households above its income caps, but this is unverified and capital-heavy.
- **Renters and rental reforms.** Landlords facing stricter repair and minimum-standard rules (per brief) are a natural buyer of home-systems warranties with fast dispatch.
- **Cost of living.** The AI admin agent attacks household bills (telco, energy, insurance renewals, subscriptions) and could use CDR data (per brief) to find savings.

### Gaps
- **Australian statistics not sourced:**
  - Renters' share of households (the brief says about a third).
  - Households in home-insurance affordability stress, and premium growth in northern Australia and flood zones.
  - Help to Buy start date and caps.
  - Excess-raising behaviour among policyholders.
  - Super funds' death-benefit processing delays and any enforcement.
  - Uptake of the battery subsidy (relevant to the untested solar/battery warranty idea).
- **No demand data:** I found no Australian demand evidence (surveys, waitlists, uptake) for any candidate. Redicova's uptake would be a useful demand proxy for parametric cover.
