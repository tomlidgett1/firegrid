# Verification: Mobile EV-charging robots for apartment buildings and shared car parks (Australia presence check and overseas growth)

Prepared 13 October 2026 by an independent, adversarial verifier. 40 WebSearch calls were run, all in standard mode, which is the hard cap. WebFetch was unavailable, so every finding rests on search-result extracts rather than full-page reads. Single-source and undated items are flagged.

**VERDICT: PARTIAL. Confidence: 75%.**
- **The robot itself is ABSENT in Australia and NZ.** That means autonomous robots, or battery carts moved by robots, charging parked EVs in strata, office or shopping-centre car parks. I found no deployment, pilot, distributor, announced launch or local start-up. My confidence on this narrower point is about 85%.
- **Why PARTIAL rather than ABSENT.** Vans with a driver already bring charging to parked cars on subscription, including scheduled charging:
  - Mobile EV Charging in Melbourne has operated since 2023.
  - EV Charging Australia in Brisbane launched in 2021. Its current status is unknown.
  - Melbourne start-up Re:Start announced in 2022 an app service that would deliver a battery module to the car.
  - The decision rules class all of these as PARTIAL. A new entrant could credibly claim to be the "first robotic car-park charging service in Australia", but not the first to bring charging to parked cars.
- **Where the remaining 25% sits.** Mostly in whether the van services still trade, because the sources are single or dated. There is also one on-demand charging app I could not identify (EVC EZY), and some of the evidence comes from search-extract summaries only.
- **Corrections to the proposal's exemplars:**
  - Mob-Energy's robots are named "Charles" and "Bolt" in the sources. No source mentions a "Charlotte".
  - Hyundai's ACR plugs into a *fixed* charger, and Rocsys targets port and fleet equipment. Neither avoids a switchboard upgrade, so they are weaker analogues for this concept.

## 1. Is any robotic or mobile EV-charging service for parked cars operating, trialled or offered in Australia (or NZ) today?

### Takeaway
I found no robotic or autonomous EV-charging service, pilot, distributor or start-up in Australia or NZ. Australian media coverage of charging robots is limited to overseas concepts, mainly Volkswagen's 2019–2020 prototype. What exists instead are mobile charging vans with drivers:
- a Melbourne subscription business that includes scheduled charging (PARTIAL);
- a 2021 Brisbane subscription service of unknown status (PARTIAL);
- Re:Start's 2022 announcement of on-demand battery-module delivery, with no launch found (PARTIAL);
- motoring-club breakdown vans (ADJACENT, excluded by the brief).

### Inferences
- The robotic core of the concept appears to be genuinely absent in Australia and NZ. Forty searches of Australian EV, energy and start-up media, robot-maker names plus "Australia", car-park operators, universities and NZ turned up only coverage of overseas concepts.
- The "first to bring charging to the parked car" claim is not available to a new entrant, because subscription vans exist (Mobile EV Charging; historically EV Charging Australia). "First robotic or autonomous car-park charging service" and "first strata-partnered mobile charging" do appear to be available.
- The truck and van services are built for emergencies and kerbside convenience. Diesel trucks are unlikely to fit basement clearances. This is an inference; I did not search clearance rules.
- The most serious competitive threat to the robot pitch in Australia is not another mobile service. It is cheap fixed Level 1 charging with load management for strata (ReadySteadyPlug, NOX Energy), which ARENA funded in 2026 to solve the same "no switchboard upgrade" problem.

## 2. What is the closest thing found, and how does it differ?

### Takeaway
No single Australian offer matches the concept. The closest match depends on which dimension you compare:
- **Delivery model:** Mobile EV Charging in Melbourne, a subscription van that includes scheduled charging.
- **Form factor:** Re:Start, a battery module delivered to the parked car. It was announced in 2022 and apparently never launched.
- **Customer and value proposition:** ReadySteadyPlug and NOX Energy, which offer cheap fixed Level 1 charging with load management for strata. ARENA funded them in 2026, and they compete directly with the robot's "no switchboard upgrade" pitch.

### Cited Findings
- **Mobile EV Charging.** A 60 kW DC charger on a converted diesel truck (2023); 24/7; subscription from $5.50 a week with scheduled charging depending on the plan; $380 one-off; many customers are drivers avoiding public-charger queues — [Drive / CarAdvice](https://www.caradvice.com.au/caradvice/mobile-ev-charging-stations-melbourne/)
  - How it differs: a human operator and a truck rather than a robot or cart. It is roadside or kerbside rather than based inside a building's car park, has no strata or car-park operator partnership, and the per-call-out economics are emergency-led.
- **Re:Start.** A battery module of 50–100 km range was to be delivered via an app, charge the car and be collected — [Compare the Market](https://www.comparethemarket.com.au/news/roadside-assistance-for-electric-vehicles-australia/?amp); [EPRI Techportal](https://techportal.sf.epri.com/taxonomy/term/20151?page=5)
  - How it differs: no autonomy, no evidence of launch, and the publicly confirmed use is RACV roadside (2022) — [The Driven](https://thedriven.io/2022/02/01/melbourne-start-up-launches-charging-units-for-stranded-electric-cars/)
- **ReadySteadyPlug** — [The Driven, 29 Apr 2026](https://thedriven.io/2026/04/29/arena-backs-new-model-for-low-cost-ev-charging-in-apartment-buildings/); [pv magazine Australia, 29 Apr 2026](https://www.pv-magazine-australia.com/2026/04/29/1-5-million-grant-accelerates-ev-charger-access-for-apartments/)
  - Founded 2024 in Sydney. ARENA granted $1.51m in April 2026 towards a $3.49m project for up to 428 charge points.
  - Level 1 charging through standard outlets, with smartphone access, cloud metering, automated billing and dynamic load management. It is sold as charging-as-a-service.
- **NOX Energy** plans about 2,000 Level 1 Intelligent Power Sockets across 16 apartment buildings — [ESD News](https://esdnews.com.au/nox-energy-to-deliver-strata-based-ev-charging-rollout/)
  - How #8 and #9 differ from the robot concept: the charging point is fixed at each bay and slow (Level 1). Each bay needs a power point, and one public submission says most strata car spaces lack one — [DCCEEW submission](https://consult.dcceew.gov.au/national-electric-vehicle-strategy/submission/view/239)

### Inferences
- A robot or cart operator in Australia would be positioned against two incumbents:
  - **Van services** on the "comes to you" claim. These are weak in basements and costly per visit.
  - **Fixed Level 1 with load management** on the "no switchboard upgrade" claim. This is cheap and ARENA-backed.
- The robot's credible edge would be faster per-session charging (Mob-Energy's robot is rated 22 kW AC/DC) and no per-bay wiring. That matters most where bays have no outlets at all, or for car parks with high turnover such as offices and shopping centres.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the current trading status of any of the three PARTIAL entities from a 2026 source.
- I found no Australian pricing benchmark per kWh for mobile charging, other than Mobile EV Charging's subscription and call-out fees.

## 4. What Australian facts matter?

### Takeaway
- **The segment is large and underserved:** at least 1 in 6 Australians live in strata.
- **Demand is rising:** EV sales set a record in 2025, at a 13.1% share.
- **Policy support has lapsed:** the NSW strata EV grant closed in December 2023.
- **Fire safety is the biggest regulatory risk.** Fire services treat EVs in basements as "special hazards", which is a headwind for robots carrying 13–30 kWh lithium packs underground.
- **Cheap fixed substitutes have funding:** ARENA is now backing fixed Level 1 charging with load management for strata.

### Cited Findings
1. **Strata scale and barriers.** At least one in six Australians live in strata-titled homes. Owners corporations and bodies corporate have refused EV charger installations, often on fire-safety grounds. The Australian Electric Vehicle Association is calling for "right to charge" laws — [SolarQuotes on AEVA](https://www.solarquotes.com.au/blog/ev-charging-strata-mb3171/)
2. **EV sales trend** — [Electrical Connection](https://electricalconnection.com.au/ev-sales-surge-38-in-2025-as-december-sets-record-market-share/); [AFMA](https://afma.org.au/australia-records-38-growth-in-ev-sales-in-2025/)
   - 2025 was a record: 156,753 EVs (battery-electric plus plug-in hybrid), 13.1% of new-car sales, up from 9.6% in 2024.
   - Battery-electric sales were about 103,300 (8.3% share, up from 7.4%). Plug-in hybrids were 53,484.
   - December 2025 hit 16.7%.
   - The FCAI called battery-electric share growth "anaemic", at +1.1 percentage points over two years.
3. **NSW EV Ready Buildings grant** — [NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/grants-and-funding/electric-vehicle-ready-buildings-grant); [The Driven, 23 Oct 2023](https://thedriven.io/2023/10/23/nsw-government-to-spend-10m-to-add-ev-chargers-at-100-apartment-buildings/)
   - A $10m program for strata buildings with 10 or more apartments. It co-funded up to 80% of costs, to a maximum of $80,000, including up to 4 shared visitor chargers, in two stages (feasibility, then installation).
   - Applications ran from 17 Oct to 15 Dec 2023. The program is listed as closed, and I found no 2026 reopening.
4. **Fire safety in basement car parks** — [The Driven, 19 May 2024](https://thedriven.io/2024/05/19/vehicle-or-hazard-electric-car-charge-rules-under-fire/amp); [International Fire & Safety Journal](https://internationalfireandsafetyjournal.com/new-fire-safety-rules-for-ev-chargers-create-installation-challenges-in-queensland/)
   - In 2024 Fire and Rescue NSW classed EVs as "special hazards". It "does not recommend EV parking and/or charging within any building not protected by an AS 2118 fire sprinkler system".
   - Queensland Fire and Emergency Services gave similar advice, including air-handling systems.
   - The CEO of the Australian Building Codes Board countered that EVs are now "expected" and "usual" in car parks.
   - Queensland's new fire-safety rules for EV chargers reportedly create installation challenges.
5. **Funded low-cost fixed alternatives for the same switchboard problem** — [The Driven, 29 Apr 2026](https://thedriven.io/2026/04/29/arena-backs-new-model-for-low-cost-ev-charging-in-apartment-buildings/); [ESD News](https://esdnews.com.au/nox-energy-to-deliver-strata-based-ev-charging-rollout/); [The Driven, 18 Mar 2026](https://thedriven.io/2026/03/18/it-can-be-done-australias-biggest-strata-fit-out-proves-ev-charging-in-apartments-is-possible/)
   - ARENA gave $1.51m to ReadySteadyPlug in April 2026 for up to 428 Level 1 points.
   - NOX Energy plans about 2,000 Level 1 sockets in 16 buildings.
   - A Melbourne basement fit-out of 251 charge points (March 2026) used software load-sharing to avoid a new substation and interlocked charging with the fire alarms.

### Inferences
- Fire safety is likely the gating issue for a robot business. A robot or cart carries 13–30 kWh of lithium (Mob-Energy's Bolt and Charles; VW's 25 kWh wagon) through basements that fire services already treat as special-hazard environments. Expect strata committees, insurers and fire engineers to ask for AS 2118 sprinklers, battery-safety certification and storage and charging protocols for the robot's dock.
- Fixed Level 1 with load management, now subsidised by ARENA, undercuts the robot on cost per bay. A robot would need to win on:
  - bays with no outlet at all;
  - faster sessions;
  - car parks with high turnover or shared use;
  - "zero capex" for owners corporations now that the NSW grant has closed.
- The 2025 sales record means demand for charging in multi-unit dwellings is growing. But the FCAI's "anaemic" comment shows battery-electric share growth is slow, so the base of strata EV owners is still small.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the ABS Census share of households in flats or apartments, so the AEVA "1 in 6 in strata" figure stands in.
- I found no Victorian, or other non-NSW, strata EV-charging grant. The budget did not allow a dedicated search.
- I found no Australian electrical or fire rule that specifically covers *mobile* battery units, or their charging docks, in enclosed car parks. For example, I did not check whether stationary-battery standards such as AS/NZS 5139 apply.
- I did not research insurer positions on robots in strata car parks.
- An unattributed search extract said EV FireSafe had verified only six Australian EV fires as of 2024, none related to batteries. I have not verified this, so it is not cited above.
