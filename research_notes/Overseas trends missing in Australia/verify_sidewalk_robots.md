# Verification: Autonomous sidewalk delivery robots — does it already exist in Australia?

Concept tested: small wheeled autonomous robots delivering food, groceries or parcels along footpaths and through campuses, paid per delivery by restaurants, delivery platforms, universities, hospitals, master-planned estates or retailers (overseas exemplars: Serve Robotics, Starship, Coco, Kiwibot, Avride, Cartken).
Verifier role: independent and adversarial (as of 13 Oct 2026). Evidence comes from WebSearch result extracts only. WebFetch was tried once and failed with a DNS error, so no full article was read.

## 1. Does any Australian business, pilot, or overseas entrant operate sidewalk delivery robots in Australia today, or has anyone recently?

### Takeaway
**VERDICT: PARTIAL, at the strong end and close to PRESENT. Confidence: 85% that PARTIAL is the right classification; at least 95% that "absent from Australia" is wrong.**

The concept is not absent from Australia:
1. **Serve Robotics is entering Australia.** It is the main overseas exemplar, and it holds a City of Gold Coast permit for "early-stage scoping works". ABC News (30 Sep 2026) reports it is expected to begin testing in Australia "in the coming months".
2. **A live commercial pilot exists.** Since March 2026, Ottonomy (US) has run a robot food-delivery pilot for Sodexo Australia at Rio Tinto's Gudai-Darri mining village in the Pilbara, WA.
3. **There were earlier footpath trials.** Domino's DRU robot ran in Brisbane in 2016, and Australia Post trialled a parcel robot in New Farm, Brisbane, in 2017. The Sydney company Marathon Targets built the robots for both.
4. **There is a university prototype.** Monash University students built the "Ari" campus delivery robot in 2025.

I found no ongoing commercial service on public footpaths, which is why the verdict is PARTIAL rather than PRESENT. However, the decision rules call a "commercial pilot" PRESENT. A strict reader could therefore classify the Sodexo/Ottonomy pilot that way, since it is the same Sodexo-plus-robot model used on US campuses. A new entrant cannot claim to be first, and would face the global market leader (Serve) arriving at the same time.

## 3. What is the current legal status of delivery robots on Australian footpaths and campuses (by state), and which pathways exist?

### Takeaway
**No Australian state or territory has a dedicated "personal delivery device" law.** Searches covered Queensland, NSW, Victoria and national bodies, with nothing found. That contrasts with the US, where many states have passed such laws since 2017. Under the Australian Road Rules (ARR), only a motorised wheelchair limited to 10 km/h is treated as a "pedestrian", and the 2019 NTC review of "innovative vehicles and motorised mobility devices" did not address delivery robots directly.

In practice, every Australian deployment so far has used one of three pathways:
1. **A state transport-department permit or exemption.** Queensland TMR gave Domino's a "special permit" in 2016, and Australia Post cited "regulator support" in 2017.
2. **A local council permit.** The City of Gold Coast gave Serve a scoping permit in 2026.
3. **Private land.** Examples are Rio Tinto's Gudai-Darri village and Monash's Clayton campus.

The national Automated Vehicle Safety Law targets automated road vehicles. No result showed it covering footpath robots.

### Cited Findings
- **Road Rules "pedestrian" test:** under the ARR, a motorised wheelchair that cannot exceed 10 km/h on level ground is treated as a pedestrian. The ARR are model laws with no legal force of their own: each state and territory adopts them, and no jurisdiction has adopted every provision — [Parliament of Australia Senate committee report on motorised mobility devices](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Senate/Rural_and_Regional_Affairs_and_Transport/MobilityScooters/Report/c03)
- **Queensland:**
  - The Road Rules regulation lists a motorised wheelchair that can exceed 10 km/h on level ground as a vehicle — [Qld Transport Operations (Road Use Management—Road Rules) Regulation 2009](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/2017-10-01/sl-2009-0194)
  - A 2018 amendment redefined personal mobility devices (PMDs) by maximum dimensions, mass and speed instead of device type, let them use footpaths and nature strips, and added offences to protect pedestrians — [Qld Subordinate Legislation 2018 No. 217](https://legislation.qld.gov.au/view/pdf/asmade/sl-2018-0217)
  - No result showed whether a riderless delivery robot can qualify as a PMD.
- **South Australia:** a motorised wheelchair may use a footpath only if it stays at or under 10 km/h, weighs no more than 170 kg unladen, and its user has a physical condition that reasonably requires it — [SA Government: motorised wheelchairs](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/driving-and-transport/vehicles/vehicle-standards-and-modifications/motorised-wheelchairs)
- **Tasmania:** the Road Rules 2019 use the same 10 km/h pedestrian threshold — [Tasmanian Road Rules 2019](https://www.legislation.tas.gov.au/view/whole/html/asmade/sr-2019-061)
- **NSW and Victoria:** searches for delivery-robot rules from Transport for NSW or Victoria's transport department returned only overseas material (Virginia, North Carolina, the UK). No NSW or Victorian rule for robots was found.
- **National Transport Commission (NTC):**
  - In May 2018 the Transport and Infrastructure Council asked the NTC to review the ARR for barriers to innovative vehicles and motorised mobility devices. The January 2019 issues paper did not directly discuss delivery robots — [NTC issues paper (PDF)](https://www.ntc.gov.au/sites/default/files/assets/files/NTC%20Issues%20paper%20-Barriers%20to%20the%20safe%20use%20of%20innovative%20vehicles%20and%20motorised%20mobility%20devices.pdf)
  - Wyndham City Council's submission argued the review should cover commercial delivery uses — [Wyndham submission (PDF)](https://www.ntc.gov.au/system/files/webform/submission_iv_ip/143/Barriers-safe-use-innovative-vehicles-motorised-mobility-devices-submission-wyndham-city-council.pdf)
  - The NTC's preferred option for PMDs was footpath use with a notional 10 km/h limit, which Victoria Walks opposed — [Victoria Walks submission (PDF)](https://www.ntc.gov.au/system/files/webform/submission_cris_pmd/787/VW%20submission%20on%20NTC%20personal%20mobility%20devices%20paper_final.pdf)
- **Automated Vehicle Safety Law (AVSL):**
  - The NTC says "current laws do not allow for the general use of automated vehicles – including automated cars, trucks and buses – on our roads". The AVSL is to be a Commonwealth law that regulates Automated Driving System Entities — [NTC: AV safety reform](https://ntc.gov.au/transport-reform/ntc-projects/av-safety-reform)
  - Public consultation ran from April to June 2024 — [Department of Infrastructure](https://infrastructure.gov.au/department/media/news/have-your-say-australias-automated-vehicle-safety-reforms)
  - No result tied the AVSL to footpath robots.
- **Pathway 1, state permit (Queensland, 2016):** Domino's worked with Queensland TMR. ### Inferences
- A driverless robot carries no person, so it cannot meet the ARR test for a "pedestrian" device (a person in a motorised wheelchair at 10 km/h or less). It also does not obviously fit Queensland's PMD category, which appears designed for ridden devices. On public footpaths it therefore most likely needs a case-by-case exemption or permit. This is inferred from the definitions above, not confirmed by any regulator statement.
- Both state and local approval appear to matter. The 2016 Domino's and 2017 Australia Post trials went through the state transport regulator, while the 2026 Serve step went through a council. A national rollout would mean negotiating approvals council by council and state by state, a barrier for a small local entrant but surmountable for well-funded overseas operators.
- Campuses, mine villages and private estates are the pathway with the least friction, which matches where activity is happening.
- Australia currently lags the US. US states have legislated sidewalk-robot rules, while Australia appears to rely on exemptions and permits.

### Gaps
- No current rules were found for WA, SA, Victoria, NSW, the ACT, NT or Tasmania that specifically address riderless footpath robots.
- The WA Department of Transport has an "automated vehicles" page, which surfaced but was not reviewed — [WA DoT](https://transport.wa.gov.au/projects/automated-vehicles.asp)
- From background knowledge only, **not verified in this session**: NSW (2017), South Australia (2016) and Victoria (2018) each passed laws enabling automated-vehicle trials. Whether those trial-permit schemes extend to footpath delivery robots should be checked against the primary legislation.
- The exact legal instrument behind Domino's 2016 "special permit" and Australia Post's 2017 "regulator support" was not found.
- The conditions of the Gold Coast permit, and whether Queensland TMR also approved it, are unknown.
- No NTC or Austroads output specific to delivery robots was found. It is unclear whether the 2019 review led to any rule changes affecting robots.
