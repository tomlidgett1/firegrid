## Key Question 1: Which robotics/mobility/logistics service companies scaled fastest overseas in 2024–2026, and which ones are the best candidates for Australia? (Ranked candidate profiles)

### Takeaway
Ten service models have documented 2024–2026 growth overseas and showed **no commercial operation in Australia** in preliminary checks. The strongest are (1) **robotic truck and container unloading-as-a-service** (UPS buying ~400 Pickle robots for ~US$120M; DHL MoU for 1,000+ Boston Dynamics Stretch units) and (2) **AI meal-assembly robots-as-a-service** for food manufacturers (Chef Robotics: US$43.1M Series A, 44M+ servings). Both are legal today and replace costly manual labour. The fastest-scaling category overall is (3) **sidewalk delivery robots** (Serve: 2,000 robots in 20 cities by end-2025). It has had no Australian activity since the 2016–17 trials, but its legal status on Australian footpaths is unclear.

#### Candidate 1 — Robotic truck and container unloading-as-a-service

**(1) Concept and business model.** AI-vision mobile robots drive into floor-loaded trailers and shipping containers and move cartons onto conveyors. Pickle's robot pairs a mobile base with a suction arm that lifts boxes of up to ~50 lb, unloads a typical truck in about 2 hours, and pays back in ~18 months through labour savings (Pickle-published figures, reported Dec 2025) ([TT News](https://www.ttnews.com/article/ups-robots-unload-trucks); [Automated Warehouse](https://www.automatedwarehouseonline.com/ups-to-spend-120m-on-400-pickle-truck-unloading-robots/)). Boston Dynamics' Stretch is rated at 600–800 cases/hour with up to 16 hours per charge (2026 brochure) ([Boston Dynamics brochure 2026](https://bostondynamics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Stretch-Brochure-2026-Final-1.pdf)), and it installs into existing warehouses within days ([Stretch brochure 2024](https://bostondynamics.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Stretch-Brochure_2024.pdf)). *Inference:* an Australian startup would buy or lease robots from OEMs (Pickle, Boston Dynamics, Dexterity). It would then sell "unloading-as-a-service" to 3PLs, importers and retail DCs, charged as a monthly robot subscription or a per-container fee, and bundle integration, maintenance, remote monitoring and exception-handling staff.

**(2) Overseas exemplars.**
- **Pickle Robot (US):** UPS will spend ~US$120M on ~400 Pickle robots (Bloomberg, reported Dec 2025). Deployment runs across multiple facilities from 2H 2026 into 2027. Pickle had raised ~US$97M since 2018 (PitchBook) ([TT News](https://www.ttnews.com/article/ups-robots-unload-trucks); [SupplyChain247](https://www.supplychain247.com/article/ups-invests-120m-on-truck-unloading-robots)). The purchase sits inside a UPS automation programme, launched in 2024, that targets US$3B in savings by end-2028 alongside US$9B of automation investment (same sources). A low-quality profile lists Pickle's customers as Yusen Logistics, UPS, Puma, BJ's and Ryobi ([SP-Edge](https://sp-edge.com/companies/755583)).
- **Boston Dynamics Stretch (US):** DHL Group signed an MoU on 13 May 2025 for 1,000+ additional Stretch units, by 2030 according to one outlet. DHL Supply Chain has used Stretch commercially in North America since 2023 and has expanded it to the UK and Europe. Stretch unloads up to 700 cases/hour, and DHL runs 7,500+ robots globally ([Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/dhl_group_signs_mou_with_boston_dynamics_for_additional_1k_robot_deployment); [Automated Warehouse](https://www.automatedwarehouseonline.com/dhl-buying-1000-stretch-robots-from-boston-dynamics/); [Supply Chain Digital](https://supplychaindigital.com/news/dhl-deploy-1-000-boston-dynamics-robots-warehouses)). The sources do not say whether the MoU is binding.
- **Competitors forming a category:** Dexterity is ranked a leader in automated truck loading ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/dexterity-1-vs-pickle-robot)), and Honeywell has announced an autonomous trailer and container unloader ([Manufacturing.net](https://www.manufacturing.net/operations/article/13196369/distribution-center-robotics-come-of-age)).

**(3) Why it is accelerating now.** DHL reports investing over €1B in contract-logistics automation over three years ([Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/dhl_group_signs_mou_with_boston_dynamics_for_additional_1k_robot_deployment)). In the 2026 Stretch brochure, DHL says the robot reduced injury rates and turnover ([Boston Dynamics](https://bostondynamics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Stretch-Brochure-2026-Final-1.pdf)). UPS is buying after "years of testing" ([TT News](https://www.ttnews.com/article/ups-robots-unload-trucks)). *Inference:* AI vision now handles mixed carton sizes, and equipment financing makes RaaS viable.

**(4) Preliminary Australia check.**
- Queries run: "container unloading robot Australia warehouse Boston Dynamics Stretch"; "truck unloading robot Australia distribution centre automated trailer unloader"; "Pickle Robot OR Dexterity OR "Stretch robot" Australia logistics".
- Closest found: **Young Guns** (a family-owned Queensland company) and **ARM Hub** are developing an AI container packing and unpacking system with A$1.5M from Advance Queensland. The source does not say it has been deployed ([Advance Queensland](https://advance.qld.gov.au/innovation-in-queensland/innovation-stories/ai-robotics-revolutionises-queensland-logistics)). No Australian DC with an installed trailer unloader was found, and the Stretch/Pickle/Dexterity results named no Australian customers ([Boston Dynamics 2024 brochure](https://bostondynamics.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Stretch-Brochure_2024.pdf)).
- **Verdict: Absent** (no commercial deployment; one Queensland R&D project).

**(5) Australian fit, regulation, tailwinds.** Amazon is building a robotics-heavy fulfilment centre south of Brisbane (~150,000 m², due 2028), which shows Australian appetite for warehouse automation ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/de/news/amazon-robot-powered-warehouse-australia/)). *Inference:* Australia imports most consumer goods in containers, many floor-loaded. Manual devanning is injury-prone, and its cost is driven by high wages and WHS manual-handling obligations. The robots work on private premises, so no special transport regulation applies beyond machinery and WHS compliance; no Australia-specific rule was found.

**(6) Key risks.** *Inference:* DHL Supply Chain or the OEMs could enter Australia directly. Capital intensity is high: the UPS deal implies roughly US$300k per robot (US$120M ÷ 400, arithmetic only). Australian DCs are smaller than US hubs, which creates a utilisation risk. Proof at scale is still pending, since the UPS rollout only starts 2H 2026 ([TT News](https://www.ttnews.com/article/ups-robots-unload-trucks)).

#### Candidate 2 — Sidewalk delivery robots-as-a-service

**(1) Concept and business model.** Small electric robots carry food, groceries or parcels along footpaths for the last 1–3 km, supervised remotely. Revenue is per delivery, from delivery platforms and merchants, plus campus contracts. Serve runs on both Uber Eats and DoorDash ([Serve IR](https://investors.serverobotics.com/news-releases/news-release-details/serve-robotics-announces-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025)). Restaurants opt into Coco bots through DoorDash and Uber Eats ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/coco-robotics-raises-80m-to-scale-sidewalk-delivery-robots/)). Starship serves 60+ US university campuses and has partnered with Grubhub since 2022 ([Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/starship-technologies-raises-50-million-us-robotics-delivery/802863/)).

**(2) Overseas exemplars.**
- **Serve Robotics (US; Uber spin-off ([Man of Many](https://manofmany.com/tech/uber-robotic-delivery-units))):** ended 2025 with 2,000 deployed robots across 20 cities in 6 metros. FY2025 revenue was US$2.7M against US$2.5M guidance, and Q4 revenue rose ~400% YoY to US$0.9M. Merchant partners grew to 4,500+ from ~400 a year earlier, and the 2026 revenue outlook is ~US$26M. These are company-reported figures from 11 Mar 2026 ([Serve IR](https://investors.serverobotics.com/news-releases/news-release-details/serve-robotics-announces-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025); [8-K exhibit](https://www.boardroomalpha.com/sec/serv-8-k-2026-03-11-0001832483-26-000004/serv-20251231xex991earning.htm)).
- **Starship Technologies (Estonia/US):** raised a US$50M Series C in Oct 2025 led by Plural, taking total funding above US$280M. It plans to grow the fleet from 2,700 to 12,000+ robots by 2027. It has completed 9M+ deliveries and operates in 30+ European cities and on 60+ US campuses ([Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/starship-technologies-raises-50-million-us-robotics-delivery/802863/); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/starship-technologies-50-million-series-c/amp/)). Its Uber partnership (Nov 2025) starts in the UK from Dec 2025, extends to European countries in 2026 and reaches the US in 2027 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251120298420/en)).
- **Coco Robotics (US):** raised US$80M in Jun 2025. Fleet estimates range from ~1,000 robots (LA, Dallas, Miami, Helsinki) to ~1,300 (a Bloomberg-sourced figure). It has made 500,000+ deliveries, and total funding is reported at >US$110–120M; the sources conflict ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/coco-robotics-raises-80m-to-scale-sidewalk-delivery-robots/); [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/coco-robotics-raises-80m-scale-autonomous-delivery-fleet); [SupplyChain247](https://www.supplychain247.com/article/coco-raises-80m-to-grow-delivery-robot-fleet-and-expand-ai-tech)). A search extract dated Oct 2026 reported an exclusive Coco–Deliveroo UK partnership starting at Canary Wharf; the source page was not confirmed ([The Robot Report tag page](https://www.therobotreport.com/tag/coco-robotics/)).

**(3) Why it is accelerating now.** Serve now covers both of the largest US delivery platforms, which it says reach 80%+ of the US food-delivery market (company claim) ([Serve IR](https://investors.serverobotics.com/news-releases/news-release-details/serve-robotics-announces-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025)). US states have passed many delivery-robot bills, creating explicit (though patchwork) legal pathways ([Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/delivery-robot-bills-laws-proliferate-state-legislatures/648303/)). *Inference:* falling robot costs and better autonomy reduce the number of remote supervisors needed per robot.

The NTC's 2019–2020 reform work covered *personal mobility devices*, not delivery devices ([NTC Decision RIS](https://www.ntc.gov.au/sites/default/files/assets/files/NTC-Decision-RIS-PMDs.pdf)). A legal review is required.

**(6) Key risks.** *Inference:* regulatory uncertainty, plus global players (Serve, Starship via Uber) arriving once rules are clarified. Revenue per robot is still low; Serve's FY2025 revenue was US$2.7M against 2,000 robots, many deployed late in the year, so this ratio is indicative only. Public acceptance and footpath quality are further risks.

#### Candidate 3 — AI meal-assembly robots-as-a-service for food manufacturers

**(1) Concept and business model.** AI-vision robot arms that occupy a worker's footprint on conveyor lines pick ingredients and deposit them into meal trays. Customers lease them under robotics-as-a-service, with the robots funded by equipment debt, so they avoid capex ([Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/chef-robotics-raises-43m-series-a-to-scale-ai-enabled-robots); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/chef-robotics-43-million-series-a-raised-for-ai-enabled-robotic-systems-for-meal-assembly)).

**(2) Overseas exemplars.** **Chef Robotics (US)** announced a US$43.1M Series A in Apr 2025: US$20.6M equity led by Avataar Ventures plus US$22.5M equipment financing. Total raised is ~US$65.6M including US$26.75M of SVB equipment financing. It reports 44M+ servings produced, with customers including Amy's Kitchen, Sunbasket, Chef Bombay and Cafe Spice, and planned a UK entry in 2025 ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/chef-robotics-43-million-series-a-raised-for-ai-enabled-robotic-systems-for-meal-assembly); [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/chef-robotics-raises-431-mn-led-by-avataar-ventures-8911803); [AgFunderNews](https://agfundernews.com/breaking-chef-robotics-raises-43m-series-a-to-scale-ai-enabled-robotics-in-meal-assembly)). Sources conflict on total equity: Pulse 2.0 gives US$38.8M. Chef announced partnerships with JBT Marel's Proseal (Oct 2025), ILPRA and Packline for end-to-end meal lines ([Food Industry Executive](https://foodindustryexecutive.com/2025/10/chef-robotics-and-jbt-marels-proseal-partner-on-joint-solution-for-meal-assembly/); [Chef/ILPRA](https://chefrobotics.ai/post/chef-robotics-and-ilpra-partner-to-deliver-flexible-automation-for-meal-assembly-and-packaging); [Chef/Packline](https://chefrobotics.ai/post/chef-robotics-and-packline-partner-on-end-to-end-meal-production-automation)).

**(3) Why it is accelerating now.** Equipment-financed RaaS removes the capex barrier ([Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/chef-robotics-raises-43m-series-a-to-scale-ai-enabled-robots)). Packaging-OEM partnerships (Proseal, ILPRA) widen distribution (sources above). *Inference:* AI now handles high-mix, short-run menus that fixed automation could not.

**(4) Preliminary Australia check.**
- Queries run: "robotic meal assembly Australia food manufacturer robot ready meals"; "Chef Robotics Australia". **Only 2 searches were run** because the search budget ran out; a third is needed.
- Closest found:
  - **B&J (Manildra Group)** opened high-speed automated ready-meal and pastry lines in Western Sydney in late 2022; they are not described as robotic pick-and-place ([Manildra](https://manildra.com.au/?p=2511)).
  - **GMEX Robotics** received an A$4.2M order (Mar 2026) from an Australian food and beverage group for ≥50 all-in-one *cooking* robots. These are kitchen robots, not tray assembly ([AOL/GMEX release](https://lite.aol.com/tech/story/0022/20260323/9676662.htm)).
  - **Placer Robotics** (Bondi) supplies Aldi's pizza vending robot ([SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/industries/retail/aldi-pizza-robot/)).
  - No Chef Robotics customer or Australian equivalent was found.

**(5) Australian fit, regulation, tailwinds.** The GMEX order shows Australian food groups are buying automation in 2026 ([AOL/GMEX](https://lite.aol.com/tech/story/0022/20260323/9676662.htm)). *Inference:* ready-meal, meal-kit and airline-catering production is labour-intensive. Regulation is ordinary food-safety and machinery compliance, with no barrier found.

**(6) Key risks.** *Inference:* Chef may enter directly or through Proseal/JBT Marel. The Australian customer base is smaller than the US one. Integration with legacy lines is a further risk.

#### Candidate 5 — Autonomous wheelchair / passenger-mobility service at airports (WHILL model)

**(1) Concept and business model.** Self-driving personal mobility devices carry passengers with reduced mobility from security or check-in to the gate and then return to base on their own. #### Candidate 7 — Teledriven car rental and delivery (Vay model)

### Inferences
- Across the top candidates, the strongest Australian opportunities are **B2B robots-as-a-service on private premises** (unloading, meal assembly, vehicle scanning, airport mobility). ## Key Question 3: Which candidates fit Australia's high labour costs and labour shortages best?

### Takeaway
On labour substitution, the best fits are **(1) truck/container unloading** (heavy, injury-prone manual handling), **(2) meal assembly** (repetitive line work), **(3) high-rise window cleaning** (scarce, high-risk rope access) and **(4) airport mobility assistance**. These are followed by vehicle inspection and sidewalk delivery. Vendor evidence on labour savings is strong but entirely company-reported, and this session retrieved **no Australian wage or shortage statistics**.

### Cited Findings
- Pickle robots are estimated to pay for themselves in ~18 months through labour savings (Pickle-published) ([TT News](https://www.ttnews.com/article/ups-robots-unload-trucks)).
- DHL says Stretch reduced injury rates and turnover and improved inbound flow (2026 brochure) ([Boston Dynamics](https://bostondynamics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Stretch-Brochure-2026-Final-1.pdf)).
- Chef robots occupy the same footprint as a worker and operate alongside humans on existing conveyor lines ([Food Industry Executive](https://foodindustryexecutive.com/2025/10/chef-robotics-and-jbt-marels-proseal-partner-on-joint-solution-for-meal-assembly/), attribution unconfirmed).
- Skyline claims Ozmo cuts on-site labour cost by 75% and is ~3x faster (company claim) ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2024/08/29/first-window-washing-robot-debuts-in-manhattan-with-more-on-the-way/)).
- Civ Robotics claims one operator can mark 1,000–5,000 survey points per day versus 200–450 for a manual crew (vendor claim) ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/civ-robotics-secures-7-5m-series-a-to-advance-the-future-of-construction-automation/), attribution unconfirmed).
- Australian automation demand signals:
  - Amazon's robotics-heavy Brisbane fulfilment centre (due 2028) ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/de/news/amazon-robot-powered-warehouse-australia/)).
  - The GMEX A$4.2M kitchen-robot order (Mar 2026) ([AOL](https://lite.aol.com/tech/story/0022/20260323/9676662.htm)).
  - ARENA-funded solar robots that reached manual-crew speeds after ramp-up ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/10/03/robots-speed-installation-of-500000-panels-at-solar-farm-in-australia/)).
- A vendor blog claims a 70,000-nurse shortfall in Australia by 2035; this is not verified against the cited government source ([Exaptec](https://exaptec.com.au/blog/hospital-delivery-robots-australia-solving-the-nursing-workload-gap-with-logistics-amrs)).

### Inferences
- **Labour-fit ranking:** (1) container/truck unloading; (2) meal assembly; (3) BMU window cleaning; (4) airport wheelchair service; (5) drive-through vehicle inspection, which replaces manual walk-around checks; (6) sidewalk delivery, which replaces gig couriers who are lower-cost per hour but whose minimum pay is being regulated (unverified, see Gaps); (7) e-bike battery swapping, whose case is safety and rider uptime rather than labour; (8–10) teledriving, robovans and shelf scanning.
- Australia-specific tailwinds where evidence was found:
  - Lithium-battery fires in NSW for battery swapping ([FRNSW](https://www.fire.nsw.gov.au/media/news/100th-e-bike-battery-fire-sparks-terrace-blaze)).
  - The renewables build-out, for survey and solar robotics (ARENA trials).
  - Strong warehouse investment (Amazon Brisbane).
- RaaS pricing matters especially in Australia because mid-sized operators may resist capex. Chef's equipment-financed model is the template ([Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/chef-robotics-raises-43m-series-a-to-scale-ai-enabled-robots)).

### Gaps
- No Australian wage or shortage data was retrieved; the search budget ran out. These need sourcing:
  - Fair Work Commission national minimum wage. Background recall, *unverified*: A$24.95/hr from 1 Jul 2025.
  - Jobs and Skills Australia Occupation Shortage List entries for storepersons, process workers, food trades and cleaners.
  - Award rates for warehouse and food-manufacturing workers.
  - Safe Work Australia manual-handling injury statistics for transport and warehousing.
- Whether the Fair Work Commission's 2024–2026 "employee-like" minimum standards apply to food-delivery gig workers was not verified. If they do, they would strengthen the sidewalk-robot case.
- No independent (non-vendor) productivity data was found for any of the robots above.
