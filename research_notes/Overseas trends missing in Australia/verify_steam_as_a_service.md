# Verification: Industrial heat-pump steam / heat-as-a-service in Australia

**VERDICT: PARTIAL, possibly PRESENT. Confidence: 60% in the PARTIAL label, and about 85% that the answer is PARTIAL or PRESENT (that is, not ABSENT).** At least one overseas heat-as-a-service provider, Newheat (France), publicly says it offers heat-as-a-service to industrial customers in Australia and New Zealand. A Sydney start-up, Good Heat, is building a thermal battery to supply industrial heat to a Victorian factory. The concept is therefore not "absent in any form". No signed or operating Australian heat-supply contract could be confirmed, so the label is not yet PRESENT.

## Q1. Does any Australian business, pilot, or overseas entrant offer industrial process heat/steam as a service from electric heat pumps or electric boilers in Australia?

### Takeaway
Yes, at least as a public offer. Newheat, a French renewable-heat company, describes itself as offering heat-as-a-service and turnkey construction (EPC) to industrial customers, district heating and large greenhouses in Australia and New Zealand. Its technology mix is solar thermal, heat pumps, waste-heat recovery and thermal storage. Good Heat, a Sydney start-up, is building thermal storage to supply heat to a Victorian factory, but its contract model is unconfirmed. Because no operating or signed Australian contract was confirmed, the label is PARTIAL. A new entrant could not claim to be the first to offer industrial heat-as-a-service in Australia.

### Cited Findings
- **Newheat (France; offer covers Australia and New Zealand).** A "new start-up member" welcome notice from the French Australian Chamber of Commerce and Industry (FACCI) describes Newheat as a French renewable heat provider that "offers Heat-as-a-Service and turnkey EPC solutions for industrial customers, district heating and large-scale greenhouses in Australia and New-Zealand". Its model combines solar thermal, heat pumps, waste-heat recovery and thermal storage, positioned as "decarbonized heat solutions that are local, sustainable, and competitive over the long term". The extract shows no date and no named Australian customer or project. — [FACCI, "Welcome to our new start-up member Newheat"](https://www.facci.com.au/news/n/news/welcome-to-our-new-start-up-member-newheat.html)
- **Good Heat (Sydney start-up).** Good Heat has raised more than $2 million "to build large-scale thermal energy storage that supplies industrial heat". It is planning "Australia's largest heat battery development for a significant industrial customer in Victoria", which it expects to cut that factory's energy bill. The extract does not give a date, the customer's name, the project size, or whether Good Heat owns the battery and sells heat or sells it as equipment. — [Startup Daily](https://www.startupdaily.net/?p=130075)
- **SmartConsult (Australia).** SmartConsult "has launched a cooling and heating as a service scheme". Customers pay each month for the heating and cooling units that an energy centre (a heating and air-conditioning plant) produces. — [HVACR News, SmartConsult tag page](https://hvacrnews.com.au/tag/smartconsult/)
- **"Steam as a service" in Australia: nothing found in one query.** The query `"steam as a service" Australia` returned only noise about Valve's Steam gaming platform, such as [MCV UK, "Valve loses Australian refund lawsuit"](https://www.mcvuk.com/business/valve-loses-australian-refund-lawsuit). It also returned one Startup Daily link with no description, which could not be opened: [Startup Daily ?p=131096](https://www.startupdaily.net/?p=131096). No Australian business marketing industrial "steam as a service" turned up.

## Q2. What is the closest thing found, and how does it differ?

### Takeaway
The closest is Newheat's stated heat-as-a-service offer for industrial customers in Australia and New Zealand. It uses the same commercial model, but its technology leads with solar thermal and no Australian contract is confirmed. Next is Good Heat's planned heat battery supplying heat to a Victorian factory, whose contract structure is unknown. SmartConsult sells heat on the same pay-per-unit model, but for building heating and cooling.

### Cited Findings
- **Newheat** uses the same commercial model: a third party supplies heat as a service to industrial customers in Australia and New Zealand, with heat pumps and thermal storage in the mix. It differs in two ways: its lead technology is solar thermal plus waste heat rather than steam-raising high-temperature heat pumps, and there is no evidence of an operating Australian contract. — [FACCI](https://www.facci.com.au/news/n/news/welcome-to-our-new-start-up-member-newheat.html)
- **Good Heat** plans thermal storage that supplies industrial heat to a Victorian factory and is expected to cut its energy bill. It differs in using a heat battery rather than a heat pump, and its contract structure is unconfirmed. — [Startup Daily](https://www.startupdaily.net/?p=130075)
- **SmartConsult** uses the same model of paying per unit of heat from an energy centre run by the operator. It differs in apparently serving building heating and cooling rather than process steam. — [HVACR News](https://hvacrnews.com.au/tag/smartconsult/)

### Inferences
- The space that may still be open is narrower than the full concept. It would be steam-raising high-temperature heat pumps (roughly 120–200 °C) with steam sold per tonne below the gas price, possibly paired with thermal storage to run on cheap midday electricity. As described in the extracts, neither Newheat nor Good Heat mentions steam sold per tonne or a price below gas. This is unconfirmed either way.
- A new entrant would have to compete on steam temperature, the heat-pump technology and price. It could not compete on the as-a-service model itself, which at least one competitor already markets in Australia.

### Gaps
- Newheat's and Good Heat's pricing (per MWh or per tonne), contract length and price indexation could not be checked.
- No evidence was found either way on whether any Australian heat-as-a-service offer is explicitly priced below gas.

## Q3. What Australian facts matter for launch (gas prices and east-coast shortfall, process-heat share of gas, ARENA and state grants, Safeguard Mechanism)?

### Takeaway
Before the search budget ran out, only three demand-side facts could be sourced: industry's large share of final energy use, with about half going to process heat; a renewable-heat accelerator that funds manufacturers moving off gas; and a Victorian manufacturer signing up for a planned heat battery. Facts on gas prices and the shortfall, the Safeguard Mechanism and grants could not be verified, and are listed under Gaps.

### Cited Findings
- Industrial energy demand is put at 42% of Australia's total final energy consumption, of which about half is used for process heating. **Attribution caveat:** the search extract credited this to "one source" among the results without naming it. It is probably one of the [pv magazine Australia industrial-heat article](https://www.pv-magazine-australia.com/?p=14112), [Future Heat](https://www.futureheat.info/copy-of-tools) or the [Australian Energy Council "Electrification and heat" PDF](https://www.energycouncil.com.au/media/qn3cwx4m/electrification-and-heat.pdf). Check the figure and its year before quoting it.
- The Australian Industry Renewable Heat Accelerator aims to help manufacturers move off gas. It supports pre-feasibility and options studies, small-scale pilots and appliance switching. Its owner, funding amounts and dates were not visible. — [Future Heat, "New funding opportunity to support industrial heat decarbonisation"](https://www.futureheat.info/post/new-funding-opportunity-to-support-industrial-heat-decarbonisation) (probable source; the extract did not attribute it explicitly)
- Demand signal: a "significant industrial customer in Victoria" is the planned host of what Good Heat calls "Australia's largest heat battery development", which it expects to cut the factory's energy bill. — [Startup Daily](https://www.startupdaily.net/?p=130075)

### Inferences
- Pilot and appliance-switching funding could lower a heat-as-a-service provider's first-project cost. It is unknown whether a third-party asset owner is eligible, or only the manufacturer.

### Gaps
The points below come from prior knowledge and were **not verified in this session**. Do not cite them without checking.
- **Safeguard Mechanism:** since 1 July 2023, baselines for facilities emitting more than 100,000 tCO2e a year decline by 4.9% a year to 2030. This pressures large food, paper and chemical plants, but many mid-sized factories fall below the threshold.
- **East-coast gas:** AEMO's Gas Statement of Opportunities has repeatedly forecast east-coast supply gaps later this decade. The Commonwealth has intervened in wholesale prices through a $12/GJ cap under the mandatory gas code of conduct. The 2026 outlook and current industrial contract prices ($/GJ) are unverified.
- **Programs to check:** ARENA's industrial transformation funding; the Powering the Regions Fund (Industrial Transformation and Safeguard Transformation streams); the NSW Net Zero Industry and Innovation Program; heat-pump activities under the NSW Energy Savings Scheme and Victorian Energy Upgrades; and Victoria's Gas Substitution Roadmap.
- The share of east-coast gas used for industrial process heat is unverified.
