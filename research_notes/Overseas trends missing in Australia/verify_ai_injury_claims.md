# Verification (re-run): AI claims preparation for compensation claims (PI / CTP / workers' comp / TPD) in Australia

*Independent adversarial re-run, 13 October 2026. This replaces the earlier notes in this file, which recorded no searches. Budget used: 37 WebSearch calls, all in standard mode (34 presence checks, 3 demand/regulation checks), plus 1 WebFetch, which failed with DNS error `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND a.storyblok.com`. ## Does any Australian business, pilot, or overseas entrant provide AI claim preparation for PI/CTP/workers' comp/TPD claims in Australia?

### Takeaway
**VERDICT: PRESENT. Confidence 75% that PRESENT rather than PARTIAL is the right label, and at least 95% that ABSENT is wrong.** Two Australian products already sell the core job to Australian claimant personal-injury (PI) firms:
- **LEAP Legal Software.** LEAP's Australian practice-management platform markets its in-built *Matter AI* on its Australian personal-injury page. The pitch is pre-built PI prompt guides that work from the accident circumstances and medical evidence to produce first drafts. A LEAP "Personal Injury Prompt Library" dated September 2026 adds prompts for medical negligence and common-law/expert-report work.
- **Mary Technology.** A Sydney start-up founded in 2023, Mary sells an AI fact-extraction and chronology platform. Reported users include Shine Lawyers, Maurice Blackburn and Zaparas Lawyers. In August 2025 it launched a pilot with Maurice Blackburn.

Both match the brief's own definition of "counts as the same or similar":
- LEAP is an AI feature in Australian practice software marketed for PI work.
- Mary is an Australian product, used by Australian plaintiff firms, that automates chronologies.

What was **not** found is an EvenUp-style, done-for-you, per-case claim pack built for Australian statutory schemes: chronology plus damages/entitlement calculation plus claim forms or demand letter plus evidence bundle, for CTP, workers' comp or TPD. A new entrant can therefore differentiate but cannot claim to be first. On a strict "end-to-end service only" reading the label would drop to PARTIAL, but no reading supports ABSENT.

### Inferences
- **Why PRESENT.** The brief counts "AI features in Australian legal practice software marketed for PI claim preparation" and "any Australian … product used by Australian firms that automates medical chronologies … for PI … claims" as the same thing. LEAP meets the first test on its own Australian PI page, and Mary appears to meet the second. Each alone would justify PRESENT under the brief; together they make the call robust. LEAP is a dominant practice-management system among small and mid-size Australian firms, and Mary reportedly serves the two largest national plaintiff firms. So the target customers already have AI chronologies and AI drafting from medical evidence.
- **Why confidence is 75%, not higher.** Neither product shown does EvenUp's full job: a finished, human-checked claim or demand package with damages valuation, sold per case. Mary's medical-chronology use is inferred from its customers and Lawyers Weekly's PI coverage, not stated in a source found. A strict reader who requires an end-to-end, done-for-you service could call this PARTIAL. Either way, a new entrant could not claim to be first in Australia.
- **Remaining white space.** No Australian player was found offering scheme-specific, end-to-end claim packs, for example:
  - TPD claim packs that map medical evidence to policy disability definitions (Maurice Blackburn's in-house tool does only the policy-matching triage);
  - CTP and workers' comp entitlement or damages calculations, prescribed claim forms and evidence bundles for disputes;
  - per-case pricing.

  This is room to differentiate, not a first-mover gap.
- **Competitive threat.** LEAP already has NSW work-injury-damages form automation and an economic loss calculator next to Matter AI, so it could plausibly add claim-pack workflows. Mary is well funded and embedded in the largest plaintiff firms. Insurer-side AI (EML, EvolutionIQ via CALI, CLARA/OPEX) is already deployed or arriving.
- **Failed or defunct attempts.** Shine's Claimify (about 2018–19) is the only earlier Australian attempt found at automated or AI PI claims. Its current status is unknown, and no evidence of closure was found.

## What is the closest thing found, and how does it differ?

### Takeaway
The closest substitutes are **LEAP Matter AI with LEAP's PI prompt guides** (closest in claim-preparation scope and distribution) and **Mary Technology** (closest in core AI capability, with the biggest plaintiff firms as customers). Both are tools that lawyers drive themselves, not a done-for-you, per-case claim-pack service. Neither was shown to produce damages or entitlement valuations with AI, scheme claim forms, or finished demand or claim packages for CTP, workers' comp or TPD.

### Cited Findings
- **LEAP:**
  - Matter AI drafts doctor and expert instruction letters, client statements and expert-report summary letters from the medical evidence on a PI matter — [LEAP AU PI](https://leap.com.au/area-of-law/personal-injury).
  - LEAP's NSW work injury damages matter type adds automated pre-filing statements, notices to insurers and an economic loss calculator — [LEAP Community](https://community.leap.com.au/s/blog-article/a63Tg0000002O4fIAE/nsw-work-injury-damages-wid-matter).
  - An Irish LEAP blog notes that Matter AI works only from documents already uploaded to the matter — [LEAP Ireland](https://www.leaplegalsoftware.com/ie/blog/matter-ai-prompts-irish-solicitors/).
- **Mary:**
  - Extracts facts into discrete, source-linked records and builds chronologies and indexes exportable to Word — [GetApp AU](https://www.getapp.com.au/software/2245337/mary-technology).
  - Its earlier funding was described as focused on AI-drafted chronologies — [Lawyers Weekly](https://lawyersweekly.com.au/newlaw/40657-legal-tech-startup-raises-1-35m-in-funding-round).
- **Maurice Blackburn's TPD tool** matches policy-document clauses to a client's situation at intake. It is in-house and "in the early stages of adoption" — [Microsoft AU](https://news.microsoft.com/en-au/features/hyperspeed-law-firm-uses-ai-to-slice-months-off-insurance-claims/).
- **Quantyx Global** uses EvenUp's operating model (automated chronologies plus clinician review) and claims coverage of Australia. It is new (2026) and India-based — [Legal Technology Hub](https://www.legaltechnologyhub.com/alsps/quantyx-global/).
- **EvenUp's product**, by contrast, is "AI-powered demand packages" sent to the liable party to seek settlement — [Capterra AU listing](https://www.capterra.com.au/software/1074839/EvenUp).
- **Insurer side:** EML reports an 80% cut in medical document review time using in-house AI for workers' comp claims — [EML](https://www.eml.com.au/latest-news/how-eml-is-using-ai-to-improve-claims-outcomes/).

### Inferences
- **Remaining room to differentiate:**
  - (a) A done-for-you service with clinician or paralegal QA, priced per case.
  - (b) Built around Australian schemes rather than US demand letters, for example:
    - TPD: evidence mapped to "unlikely ever to work" policy definitions;
    - CTP and workers' comp: whole person impairment thresholds, pre-injury earnings and entitlement calculations, prescribed forms;
    - evidence bundles for dispute bodies.
  - (c) Sold to firms that do not use LEAP.
- **Practical substitution risk.** A small PI firm on LEAP may see little reason to buy a separate chronology tool unless the output is clearly a finished claim pack. Large plaintiff firms are already piloting Mary and building in-house tools (Maurice Blackburn).

### Gaps
- No source was found comparing output quality, pricing or adoption of LEAP Matter AI or Mary on PI medical files.
- Whether Mary or LEAP plan scheme-specific claim packs is unknown.

## Which Australian facts matter for launch (plaintiff PI market size, TPD claim volumes via APRA, CTP/workers' comp claim volumes, practice rules on AI and fee arrangements)?

### Takeaway
Five cited facts were gathered:
- workers' comp volumes are large and rising in mental-health claims;
- TPD claims are mostly admitted, but disputes are concentrated in retail policies;
- NSW courts restrict generative AI in expert reports and affidavits;
- insurers have widely adopted generative AI in claims;
- one firm reports average TPD payouts of about $160,000.

Plaintiff PI market size, CTP claim volumes and fee-arrangement rules were not obtained.

### Cited Findings
1. **AI rules in court.** NSW Supreme Court Practice Note SC Gen 23 was issued on 21 November 2024 and took effect on 3 February 2025; the District Court adopted the same provisions.
   - Generative AI must not be used to draft or prepare the content of an expert report, or any part of one, without prior leave of the Court.
   - Affidavits and witness statements must disclose that generative AI was not used to generate their content.

   [NSW Government Gazette](https://gazette.nsw.gov.au/gazette/2024/11/2024-11_470-gazette.pdf); [Piper Alderman](https://piperalderman.com.au/insight/generative-ai-and-a-new-era-of-legal-technology-governance-in-the-supreme-court-of-nsw/)
2. **Workers' comp volume.**
   - There were 146,700 serious workers' compensation claims (at least one week off work) in 2023-24 (preliminary), more than 400 a day.
   - Mental-health claims made up 12% of serious claims, up 14.7% on the previous year, with median time off work almost five times that of other claims.

   [Safe Work Australia, Key WHS Statistics 2025](https://data.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/insights/key-whs-statistics-australia/latest-release)
3. **TPD outcomes (APRA/ASIC).**
   - Admittance rates for the 12 months to December 2020: individual advised 81%, individual non-advised 67%, group super 89%, group ordinary 82% — APRA/ASIC media releases, [release A](https://www.apra.gov.au/news-and-publications/apra-and-asic-publish-latest-data-on-life-insurance-claims-and-disputes-1) and [release B](https://www.apra.gov.au/news-and-publications/apra-and-asic-publish-latest-data-on-life-insurance-claims-and-disputes-0). Which release carries the figures is uncertain. For the 12 months to December 2019 the same channels were 81%, 67%, 91% and 86%.
   - TPD dispute-lodgement ratios per 100,000 lives insured (June 2022 publication): 68 individual advised, 63 individual non-advised, 27 group super, 8 group ordinary — [APRA, June 2022](https://www.apra.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-10/Life%20Insurance%20Claims%20and%20Disputes%20Statistics%20June%202022.pdf).
   - The latest release covers the 12 months to December 2024 across 17 insurers — [Insurance Business AU](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/claims/australias-life-insurance-claims-disputes-show-sharp-shifts-532281.aspx).
4. **Insurer AI adoption.** A Gallagher Bassett whitepaper reports that nearly nine in 10 insurers in Australia use generative AI somewhere in claims, mainly intake, triage, fraud detection and communication — [Insurance Business AU](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/technology/nearly-nine-in-10-insurers-in-australia-use-genai-in-claims--gb-531797.aspx).
5. **TPD claim value.** Maurice Blackburn's TPD pages cite an average of about $160,000 per TPD claim over the last three years. This is the firm's own marketing figure, and which of its two TPD pages carries it is uncertain — [Maurice Blackburn TPD claims](https://www.mauriceblackburn.com.au/tpd-claims/).

### Inferences
- **SC Gen 23** means an AI chronology or claim-pack product in NSW must be positioned as the lawyer's internal work product. It must not draft the content of expert reports, affidavits or witness statements. Source-linked outputs (which LEAP and Mary already advertise) are a minimum requirement. Other jurisdictions were not checked.
- **Volumes** of about 147,000 serious workers' comp claims a year, plus CTP and group-super TPD claims, mean a large, document-heavy claim flow. Rising mental-health claims mean longer, more complex medical files, which suits AI chronologies.
- **Insurer adoption** of generative AI is high (about nine in 10), which supports an "arms race" pitch to claimant firms. It also means insurer-side tools (EML, EvolutionIQ, CLARA) set the bar for review speed.

### Gaps

- **Fee-arrangement rules** affecting per-case pricing: not verified. The verifier recalls the following, all unverified:

  - uplift-fee caps;

  - regulated NSW CTP legal costs.

- **Law-society or regulator guidance** on lawyers' use of AI, beyond SC Gen 23: not searched.
