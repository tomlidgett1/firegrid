# Verification: Automated gold-buying kiosks / "smart gold stores" — presence in Australia and legal feasibility

Independent adversarial verification, 13 October 2026. 39 WebSearch calls (all "standard"; one `allowed_domains` call limited to austrac.gov.au). WebFetch was unavailable, so every finding below comes from search-result extracts, not full page reads. Treat exact wording of statutes as unconfirmed where noted.

**VERDICT: ABSENT. Confidence: 80%.** (See Q1 for reasoning and residual risk.)

---

## Q1. Does any automated/self-service gold-buying kiosk or machine operate, or has one been trialled, in Australia?

### Takeaway
**ABSENT (80% confidence).** About 25 presence-oriented searches covered English and Chinese terms, brand names, shopping centres, mail-in buyers, bullion vending and phone-kiosk analogues. None found an automated or self-service gold-buying machine, gold-buyback ATM, pilot or announcement in Australia. Nor did they find evidence that Kinghood, GOLDEX GLOBAL or Goldsikka serve Australian customers. Every Australian gold-buying channel found is staffed, so a new entrant could credibly claim "first automated gold-buying kiosk in Australia", but not "first fast, spot-linked gold buyer" (see Q2).

### Inferences
- The absence of any Australian media coverage is informative. Novelty gold-ATM stories syndicate widely: the Feb 2026 AFP piece ran in Malay Mail, NST, The Peninsula (Qatar), eNCA and BSS. An Australian installation would probably have drawn similar coverage.
- The melt-on-the-spot design breaches state holding periods (Q3). That makes a quiet, unpublicised deployment of a Chinese-style machine in Australia less likely.
- Residual risk (why confidence is 80% rather than 90%+): a privately imported unit at a Chinese-Australian jeweller (e.g., Chatswood, Hurstville, Box Hill), promoted only on WeChat or Xiaohongshu, would not appear in web search. Neither would a B2B sale by Kinghood or GOLDEX to an Australian distributor.
- By the decision rules, staffed buyers and non-automated mail-in services do not trigger PARTIAL. No one-off Australian trial or announcement was found, so the verdict is ABSENT rather than PARTIAL.

### Gaps
- Chinese social platforms (WeChat public accounts, Xiaohongshu/RED, Douyin) are not indexed by the search tool. This is the most likely place an unpublicised Australian unit would appear.
- No IP Australia trade-mark search ("Kinghood", "Smart Gold Store", "Goldomat", "gold ATM") or ASIC business-name search was possible (no fetch tool).
- A US reissue patent exists, USRE47273E1 "Gold and precious metal buying machine method" ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/USRE47273)). Australian patent equivalents were not checked (freedom-to-operate gap).

## Q2. What is the closest thing found, and how does it differ?

### Takeaway
The closest Australian equivalents are staffed buyers that already use XRF assay and spot-linked pricing, such as Gold Exchange Universe (Sydney) and the Perth Mint's jewellery desk. The mail-in options found (Ainslie Bullion, Jaggards) are bullion-oriented, human-assayed and not instant. None is automated, unattended or extended-hours, or pays within minutes by design. Australian staffed cash-for-gold kiosks have a documented history of poor offers, which suggests transparent spot-linked pricing could differentiate a new entrant.

### Inferences
- Staffed XRF buyers in Australia already do the core job: turning jewellery into money at a spot-linked price, often quickly. A kiosk's real differentiation is (a) price transparency (displayed assay, weight and spread), (b) convenience and extended hours in high-traffic sites, (c) lower labour cost per site, and (d) instant NPP/PayID settlement as a designed-in feature.
- The CHOICE finding (39–89% of gold value; kiosks "a rip-off") is dated (2010) but indicates a trust gap that transparent automated pricing could address.
- Mail-in services do not meet the brief's PARTIAL threshold ("fully automated and instant"). Each involves human receipt, assay and approval.

### Gaps
- Whether any Australian staffed buyer advertises PayID/instant payment could not be confirmed (search 32 found none explicitly).
- The nature of Gold Exchange Universe's "portal version 24K" (an online instant-quote tool or an automated process?) is unverified, as is its 95% vs 98% rate claim.
- No jewellery-focused Australian postal "cash for gold" service was clearly identified. Smaller unindexed operators may exist but would not be automated or instant.

## Q3. Is an automated (or one-attendant) gold-buying kiosk legally workable in Australia? What design changes would the law force?

### Takeaway
**Workable only as an "assisted kiosk" run by a licensed/registered second-hand dealer.** Every state checked requires identity verification and transaction records available or reported to police. Every state also requires purchased goods to be kept unchanged for a period: 7 days (Vic), 7 working days for precious metals (Qld) or 14 days (NSW, WA). **The Chinese "melt on the spot" feature is therefore unlawful as standard practice** unless a specific exemption is obtained. Nothing found prohibits **instant payment** to a bank account; the law restricts disposal of the goods, not the timing of payment. Paying only by bank transfer keeps a jewellery-only kiosk outside AUSTRAC's new dealers-in-precious-metals regime, which only covers cash or virtual-asset deals of A$10,000+. Accepting bullion engages the separate bullion-dealing designated service.

### Cited Findings
**New South Wales: Pawnbrokers and Second-hand Dealers Act 1996**
- Licensees must keep electronic records, check the identity of customers and display signs, and "most businesses also need to hold goods for at least 14 days before selling them" — [NSW Government: pawnbroker and second-hand dealer obligations](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/industry-specific-business-requirements/pawnbrokers-and-second-hand-dealers)
- NSW exemption instruments refer to "section 21 (1) of the Act, to the extent that it requires a licensee to retain second-hand goods purchased or received for a period of 14 days". Section 16 covers transaction records and sending particulars to the Commissioner of Police. Contract numbers must be displayed on goods by marking or label — [NSW exemption regulation, e.g. sl-2015-108](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/pdf/asmade/sl-2015-108); [sl-2020-558](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/pdf/asmade/sl-2020-558) (the extract did not isolate which instrument contains the quoted wording)
- The NSW licence covers dealing in items of jewellery that include gemstones or precious metals, including watches. ABLIS lists a "Pawnbrokers and Second-hand Dealers Regulation 2026" — [ABLIS NSW licence](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/nsw/pawnbroker-or-second-hand-dealers-licence/16617)
- The Pawnbrokers and Second-hand Dealers Regulation 2021 was due for automatic repeal on 1 September 2026 — [NSW legislation, sl-2021-0044](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/sl-2021-0044)
- NSW Fair Trading grants **limited, business-specific exemptions**. For example, a mobile-phone trade-in business was exempted subject to tracking devices by serial number/IMEI (list current as at 27 Feb 2025) — [NSW Government: limited exemptions](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/industry-specific-business-requirements/pawnbrokers-and-second-hand-dealers/limited-exemptions-for-pawnbrokers-and-second-hand-dealers)

**Victoria: Second-Hand Dealers and Pawnbrokers Act 1989 (registration with Consumer Affairs Victoria)**
- Registration is required — [ABLIS Vic registration](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/vic/second-hand-dealers-and-pawnbrokers-registration/23960)
- Dealers must keep goods for at least 7 days in their received form before selling. They must keep complete records of every receipt of second-hand goods (electronic or bound book). ### Inferences
**Holding-period summary** (from the cited sources above):

| State | Holding rule | On-site melting |
|---|---|---|
| NSW | Retain 14 days (s 21(1)) | Conflicts unless a Fair Trading exemption is granted |
| Vic | ≥7 days "in received form", plus possible police s 26 freezes of 21 + 21 days | Conflicts |
| Qld | Precious metals are nominated goods: 7 working days | Conflicts |
| WA | Goods "kept unchanged at least 14 days" (s 61) | Directly conflicts |

**Design changes the law would force:**
1. **Licence/registration in each state** where kiosks operate (NSW licence, Vic registration, Qld and WA licences). The kiosk network effectively becomes a licensed second-hand dealer with multiple places of business.
2. **No on-site melting.** Use non-destructive testing (XRF plus weight, and density/ultrasonic checks to catch plated or filled items). Seal each item in a numbered, tamper-evident bag inside an internal vault, and keep it unchanged for 7 days (Vic), 7 working days (Qld) or 14 days (NSW, WA). Then batch the gold to a refiner. Sites need vault capacity for about 2 weeks of intake and must accommodate police inspection and hold notices (Vic: up to 42 days).
3. **ID verification plus a human in the loop.** Kiosks would scan ID documents with a liveness/face match, but the duty to check identity, refuse under-18s, and (WA s 38) refuse persons affected by alcohol or drugs points to an **assisted kiosk with one attendant** or, subject to regulator approval, a remote video attendant. A fully unattended machine would probably need a regulator-specific exemption. The NSW phone trade-in exemption shows such exemptions exist, but gold has no serial number or IMEI, so police may resist.
4. **Electronic records and police reporting** for every transaction (NSW s 16 particulars to the Commissioner of Police; Vic complete records available to police; WA s 43). Capture item photos, weight, assay, seller ID and payee bank account; the kiosk's digital data trail is an advantage here.
5. **Payment by bank transfer only (NPP/PayID).** Instant payment remains possible because the holding rules apply to the goods, not to payment. Avoiding cash and crypto keeps a jewellery/scrap-only kiosk outside the DPMSP designated service. Accepting coins or bars engages the separate bullion designated service, so either exclude bullion or enrol and run an AML/CTF program. Paying a bank account in the seller's verified name also adds traceability.
6. **Fraud and pricing risk.** Without melting, surface XRF can be fooled by heavy plating or filled items. The operator either prices this into the spread or uses a hybrid model: an instant part-payment, with the balance after the hold-period fire assay.
7. **Regulatory pathway.** Before launch, seek written positions or exemptions from NSW Fair Trading (precedent: limited exemptions), CAV, Qld OFT and WA, and liaise with state police on a holding and inspection protocol.

**Overall:** a one-attendant, no-melt, bank-transfer-only kiosk appears legally workable. A fully unattended melt-on-the-spot Chinese clone does not.

### Gaps

- The content of the NSW Pawnbrokers and Second-hand Dealers Regulation 2026 (listed on ABLIS) is unknown. It could change ID, record-keeping or holding rules.

## Q4. How strong is the overseas proof, and what is the Australian demand context?

### Takeaway
Strong overseas proof comes mainly from one operator, Kinghood (mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau). It was in about 40 cities by Sept 2024 and had 40+ locations with 60+ planned by 2025; in Feb 2026, at record prices, queues ran over an hour. Most other "gold ATMs" worldwide sell gold rather than buy it, or are pre-launch (Goldsikka). Australian demand drivers are real: gold set records above A$6,000/oz in Oct 2025 and traded around A$6,460 at end-Dec 2025, and Australia has about 710k India-born and 596k China-born residents.
