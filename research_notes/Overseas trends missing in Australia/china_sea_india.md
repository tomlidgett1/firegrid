## 1. Which models are growing fastest in these markets in 2024–2026, and which could transfer to Australia? (candidate profiles, best first)

### Takeaway
Three models best combine fast growth overseas, likely absence from Australia and a labour-saving fit:
- **Two-wheeler battery-swap networks.** India's Battery Smart grew from 1,000 stations (June 2024) to 1,600+ (July 2026). Taiwan's Gogoro had 665,000 subscribers and US$59.9M adjusted EBITDA in 2025.
- **Unstaffed, app-booked 24/7 leisure venues** (billiards first, then mahjong, karaoke and board-game rooms). China's 24-hour unmanned pool halls grew 25% year on year in April 2024. A diaspora-run copy already operates in Auckland, but none was found in Australia.
- **AI self-study centres.** China has more than 50,000.

Robotic massage, autonomous delivery vans and AI companion toys are also growing fast but fit Australia less well. Four candidates were rejected because they already exist in Australia: shared power-bank rental, fresh-orange-juice vending, robotic-wok kitchens and hot-meal robotic vending.

#### Candidate 1: Battery-swap (and managed-charging) network for e-bike and e-moped delivery riders and commuters
**(1) How it works and makes money.** The operator owns standardised batteries and puts smart swap cabinets at kerbside sites, convenience stores, depots and apartment buildings. A rider swaps a flat battery for a charged one in under a minute and pays a monthly subscription or a fee per swap. The operator also sells fleet plans to delivery platforms and logistics firms. Zoomo's Berlin "PowerShelter" is a live reference: a swap-locker add-on at €70/month — [Zoomo PowerShelter](https://www.ridezoomo.com/de-en/powershelter).

  - **September 2025:** US$21M round led by Rising Tide Energy; 1,518 stations, including 321 in Delhi; 36% share of India's battery-swapping segment — [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/battery-smart-raises-21m-in-series-b)

#### Candidate 2: Unstaffed, app-booked 24/7 billiards halls
**(1) How it works and makes money.** These are small pool and snooker halls run with no staff. Customers book and prepay a table by the hour in an app and get in with a QR code or facial recognition. Internet-connected controls switch table lights and timers on automatically, and CCTV monitors the room. Revenue comes from hourly table fees, memberships, vending machines for drinks and snacks, and add-ons such as AI coaching or refereeing — [iiMedia](https://report.iimedia.cn/tag/台球行业); [Asiae, 3 Sep 2026](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026090315401419775).

**(2) Overseas exemplars**
- **China market data (iiMedia)** — [iiMedia](https://report.iimedia.cn/tag/台球行业):
  - The number of 24-hour unmanned self-service pool halls grew 25.0% year on year in April 2024
  - 106,641 new billiards-related businesses were registered in 2023, up 54.1%
  - China's billiards market was ¥87.2B in 2024, up 20.5%, with a forecast of ¥192.03B by 2030
  - Some halls run around the clock with "whole-house intelligent hardware and software instead of manual management", and some add AI coaching and refereeing

  iiMedia is a commercial research firm, and its figures are not independently verified.
- **China, September 2026 coverage.** Unmanned 24/7 venues are spreading through China's leisure sector. Players book in an app and enter by QR code or facial recognition. Young people like having no social contact, and vending machines keep visits cheap — [Asiae, 3 Sep 2026](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026090315401419775)
- **New Zealand, diaspora-run copy.** 98 Billiards in Browns Bay, Auckland, is described as "New Zealand's first in-app 24/7 self-service billiards venue". A QR-code deposit unlocks the table, and the lights and session start automatically once paid — [98 Billiards app listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/98-billiards/6760736804); [Browns Bay business directory](https://brownsbay.org.nz/directory-bbba/listing/11375/). Launch date not confirmed.

**(3) Why it is accelerating now**

#### Candidate 3: AI self-study centres (tablet-based adaptive learning rooms with light supervision)
**(1) How it works and makes money.** Small study centres where children work through adaptive AI courseware on tablets. One supervisor or "learning coach" oversees many students instead of a teacher instructing them. Revenue comes from monthly or term memberships plus device sales, often under a franchise — [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1016555); [Squirrel AI franchise listing](https://www.businessbroker.net/franchises/franchise/squirrel-ai-learning).

**(2) Overseas exemplars**
- **China scale and format.** More than 50,000 AI study rooms operate nationwide. They are branded as "self-study" spaces to get around restrictions on off-campus tutoring for grades 1–9. Students use tablets with preloaded courses and AI-generated study plans. Some centres sell the tablet with a free month; others bundle it into membership — [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1016555). Publication date not confirmed, probably late 2024 or 2025.
- **Coverage and criticism.** China Daily reported the boom on 27 Dec 2024, along with concerns — [China Daily](https://mobile.chinadaily.com.cn/cn/html5/2024-12/27/content_005_676dab88ed50ccabe150a658.htm). People's Daily Online (25 Dec 2024) reported mixed reactions, with some commentators calling the devices a marketing gimmick — [People's Daily Online](https://en.people.cn/n3/2024/1225/c90000-20258414.html).
- **Evidence on outcomes.** A 2026 quasi-experimental study of 383 primary-school students over 10 weeks found students in AI study rooms reported more motivation, engagement, enjoyment and self-regulation than those in traditional self-study — [Frontiers in Psychology, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1768389)
- **Already franchising abroad.** Squirrel AI Learning sells a US franchise: an AI tablet system plus a human data analyst, with a first-year investment of US$110k–137k — [BusinessBroker.net](https://www.businessbroker.net/franchises/franchise/squirrel-ai-learning)
- **Market size.** Rest of World estimates China's educational-device market could reach US$20B by 2026 — [WizCase citing Rest of World](https://www.wizcase.com/news/ai-self-study-rooms-gain-popularity-in-china-as-affordable-alternatives-to-private-tutors/)

**(3) Why it is accelerating now**
- China's tutoring restrictions push demand into "self-study" formats — [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1016555)
- Rooms are marketed as cheaper than private tutors — [WizCase](https://www.wizcase.com/news/ai-self-study-rooms-gain-popularity-in-china-as-affordable-alternatives-to-private-tutors/)
- Better AI courseware (inference).

## 2. Which models save labour or use technology in ways that suit Australia's high wages?

### Takeaway
The candidates that take out the most labour are unstaffed leisure venues (no staff at all), battery swap (no charging downtime, and more orders per rider), AI study centres (one supervisor for many students) and RoboVans (no driver). Robotic massage and robot kitchens cut labour only partly, because people still assess customers or load ingredients.

### Cited Findings
- Traditional mahjong parlours, board-game rooms and billiard halls struggled with labour and rent costs, and unmanned systems fixed that — [Asiae, 3 Sep 2026](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026090315401419775)
- Unmanned pool halls replace manual management with whole-venue smart hardware and software — [iiMedia](https://report.iimedia.cn/tag/台球行业)
- Battery swap for delivery e-bikes raised daily orders per rider by more than 20% and cut wasted mileage by 6 km — [SMU InK](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/sis_research/10408)
- Singapore's vending-machine cafés were part of a food-industry plan to ease manpower shortages — [Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/story/511128/Singapore-to-expand-vending-machine-cafes)
- In Chinese robot kitchens, a few staff only load ingredients, and key robot components have become much cheaper — [Jiemian](https://en.jiemian.com/article/14092875.html)
- AI study rooms replace live instruction with tablet courseware — [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1016555)
- The Shanghai "AI Tui Na" model splits work: the robot does standard techniques and a human does the pre-assessment — [Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-AU/activity/227251-soothing-ai-robot-massage-shanghai-bfc-ai-tui-na-experience/)
- Consumer trust is the main barrier for automated food vending — [FoodNavigator-Asia, 25 Nov 2025](https://www.foodnavigator-asia.com/Article/2025/11/25/ai-powered-human-led-kitchens-redefine-rte-vending-in-asia)

### Inferences
- Ranked by how much labour they replace: unstaffed venues (all of it) > RoboVans (driver) > AI study centres (most tutor hours) > battery swap (rider downtime and depot charging) > robotic massage (therapist time) > AI toys (none).
- None of the seven candidates depends on a super-app or on cheap labour. Instant home-help models such as Snabbit and Pronto in India were left out because they do.

### Gaps
- I did not capture Australian wage benchmarks. Background knowledge, unverified this session: the national minimum wage was set at A$24.95 an hour from 1 July 2025. Check the current figure at fairwork.gov.au.
- No per-unit economics (machine or vehicle cost, payback period) were captured for any candidate.
