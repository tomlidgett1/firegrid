## Key question 1: Which European/UK/Israeli models grew fastest in 2024–2026 and are NOT on the exclusion list?

### Takeaway
The exclusion list already removes almost every headline European consumer winner. What remains with documented 2024–2026 growth and a need for local operations is a set of niche, asset-light networks and platforms:
- **Freetrailer** (Denmark): retailer-hosted free trailer and e-cargo-bike rental. DKK 144.1m revenue in 2025, up about 24%, with 6,381 units across about 1,600 locations in 5 countries.
- **Cineville** (Netherlands): an unlimited subscription across independent cinemas. 141,000 members and 222 cinemas in 5 countries (Nov 2025), adding one country a year.
- **Kayakomat** (Sweden): app-unlocked kayak/SUP stations. About 200 stations in 2023 rising to about 300 in 12–13 countries by 2026 (self-reported).
- **ME Group's Revolution / Wash.ME** (UK-listed): outdoor self-service laundries. A record 1,326 machines installed in FY2025.
- **Felfel** (Switzerland): smart-fridge workplace meals. 1,000+ client organisations.
- **BattleKart** (Belgium): mixed-reality karting. 50+ sites.
- **Switchee** (UK only): damp/mould and energy IoT for social landlords.

Israel's 2025 "most promising" list is entirely B2B or deep tech, so no Israeli local-operations consumer model qualified.

### Inferences
- **Where the remaining white space is:** with the exclusion list removing marketplaces, quick commerce, energy and the big leisure formats, the European white space is mostly **asset-light networks on host land with revenue shares** (Freetrailer, Kayakomat, Revolution laundry), **platform subscriptions across independent operators** (Cineville) and **regulation-triggered B2B IoT** (Switchee and its UK peers).
- **Freetrailer's economics:** its audited figures give an EBIT margin of about 18.7% (26.9 / 144.1) in 2025. Unattended rental networks hosted by retailers can therefore be profitable at scale. This is the strongest financial evidence in this set.
- **Cineville's spread:** it entered a new country every year from 2022 to 2025 (BE, AT, DE, SE, plus France via Cinessentiel). This is the clearest sign in this set that a model transfers across markets.
- **Israel:** fast growers are global B2B or deep tech. Nothing in this run suggests an Israeli local-operations consumer model that is absent from Australia.

- No audited 2025–26 revenue was found for Cineville, Switchee, Kayakomat or Pirate. Switchee's current home count and any second-country launch (it has a Dutch-language page, [switchee.com/nl](https://switchee.com/nl/switchee-awaabs-law)) are unconfirmed.

### Takeaway

- **LIKELY ABSENT (2):** Cineville-style unlimited multi-cinema subscriptions, and Switchee-style damp/mould IoT for social landlords.

### Inferences

#### Australian fit and risks, per surviving candidate (inference; facts marked [unverified] were not searched this run)
- **1. Multi-cinema subscription (Cineville):**
  - **Fit:** it is software plus partnerships, so it can be built with seed capital. Australia has a recognisable independent and arthouse circuit (e.g., Palace, Dendy, Lido/Classic/Cameo, Cinema Nova, Ritz, Hayden Orpheum, Golden Age) [unverified list]. Cineville's yearly entry into a new country shows the model travels.
  - **Risks:**
    - Earlier Australian aggregator attempts (Sinemia, CinemaClub) failed or never launched. These were MoviePass-style resellers, not cinema-owned collectives, but cinema owners may remember them.
    - Indie screens per city are fewer and more spread out than in the Netherlands.
    - Chains (Hoyts, Event/Village) could copy the UK chain-level unlimited cards.
    - The payout formula to cinemas is the crux and was not verified.
    - Subscription-trap scrutiny under Australian consumer law [unverified].
- **2. Damp/mould IoT for social landlords (Switchee):**
  - **Fit:** tech-enabled B2B with recurring revenue. Australian state housing agencies and community housing providers are concentrated buyers. Humid coastal climates and state rental minimum standards that cover mould (e.g., Victoria [unverified]) create demand.
  - **Risks:**
    - There is no Awaab's-Law-style statutory deadline, so the urgency that drives UK sales is missing.
    - Long government procurement cycles.
    - Tenant consent and privacy, including surveillance-device laws [unverified].
    - Switchee's thermostat form factor suits UK central heating, not typical Australian homes, so a standalone sensor plus analytics product is likely needed.
    - Evidence is single-country, and UK vendors could license into Australia.
- **3. Kayak/SUP stations (Kayakomat):**
  - **Fit:** Australia's waterways and tourism suit it. The Lane Cove pilot shows councils will permit it. Kayakomat sells franchises, so an Australian master franchise is a realistic path [inference from the franchise pages].
  - **Risks:**
    - The founder cannot claim to be first; they would be first at scale.
    - State maritime rules on PFDs and craft [unverified], drowning liability and insurance.
    - Council or land-manager permits, theft and vandalism.
    - Northern waters carry crocodile and stinger zones [unverified].
- **4. Free retailer-hosted trailers and e-cargo bikes (Freetrailer):**
  - **Fit:** these are the best economics in the set (about 18.7% EBIT margin). Australia has a strong trailer culture and big-box hosts (e.g., Bunnings, IKEA) [unverified].
  - **Risks:**
    - Incumbents are entrenched (U-Haul with 1,000+ locations), and smart-lock hire software is already sold locally (Lockii).
    - Hosts could build it themselves.
    - Trailer registration, roadworthiness and towing liability [unverified].
- **5. Shared e-cargo bikes:**
  - **Fit:** inner-city family niche; councils as co-funders.
  - **Risks:**
    - Low cycling mode share and helmet laws [unverified].
    - Theft and vandalism.
    - Overseas growth evidence is weak, and Cargoroo's 2025–26 trajectory is unknown.
    - Under-screened (2 queries).
- **6. 24/7 unstaffed studios (Pirate):**
  - **Fit:** asset-light conversions of light-industrial space; music and podcast creators.
  - **Risks:**
    - Independent 24/7 rooms already exist.
    - Overseas growth evidence is undated or third-party.
    - Noise and permitted-use planning rules [unverified].
- **Pattern:** a three-query screen is not enough for unattended or self-service formats. Councils and micro-operators run pilots that only appear under council-news or listing-site searches (Lane Cove, Creative Spaces).
