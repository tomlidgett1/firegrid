# Adversarial verification: Robotic/AI manicure stations — does anything like it exist in Australia? (checked 13 Oct 2026)

Concept tested: unattended or lightly attended machines that scan nails with AI/computer vision and paint polish (some also prep, top-coat or dry) in roughly 10–45 minutes without a nail technician, placed in retailers, malls, salons, airports, offices or gyms, and paid per service via app or kiosk. Overseas exemplars: 10Beauty, Clockwork, Nimble and Umia.

## 1. Is any robotic/AI manicure service or nail-painting robot available in Australia today, or has one been piloted?

### Takeaway
**VERDICT: ABSENT. Confidence: 78%.** I ran 40 searches, about 30 of them presence checks. They found no robotic or AI nail-painting service in Australia that is operating, piloted or announced. They also found no Australian distributor or reseller and no Australian retail listing of a nail-painting robot. The two Australia-linked items found are not services:
- A 2014 Arbor Australia prototype kiosk that *printed designs* onto nails. It did not paint polish, and there is no evidence it was ever deployed.
- A registered Australian trade mark "10BEAUTY" held by the US leader 10Beauty, Inc. It was filed 1 Nov 2024 and registered 18 Aug 2025. This signals possible future intent but is not a launch.

Two unresolved leads could move the verdict to PARTIAL: (a) whether Nimble's home robot has ever shipped to Australian buyers, and (b) what 10Beauty plans to do with its Australian trade mark(s).

## 2. What is the closest thing found, and how does it differ?

### Takeaway
The closest Australian analogue is Arbor Australia's 2014 "Nail Designs Kiosk" prototype for Glamour Nail. It was an unattended, mall-targeted machine that printed images onto nails. It differs because it applied printed art rather than polish, did no manicure steps (prep, base, colour, top coat, dry), and shows no sign of having gone beyond a prototype. The closest *corporate* signal is 10Beauty's registered "10BEAUTY" trade mark in Australia, which is not a service.

### Cited Findings
- **Arbor/Glamour Nail kiosk:** users pick or upload artwork (camera photos, band images, abstract art) that is "printed onto a nail". It targeted shopping centres, arcades and cinemas, and was shown as a prototype at Microsoft Australia's "Internet of Your Things" event on 29 Apr 2014. The key challenge was a direct-to-nail printing method compliant with health and safety rules in several countries. Source: [Microsoft News Centre Australia](https://news.microsoft.com/en-au/2014/04/29/the-internet-of-your-things-the-internet-of-things-nailed-arbor-australia/)
- **Nail-art printers in general** often have difficulty printing onto natural nails because nails vary so much between users. This is a known limitation of the printer category. Source: [Justia patent 9962955](https://patents.justia.com/patent/9962955)
- **Fingernails2Go,** a comparable nail-art kiosk from Belfast, uses HP print technology and FDA/EU cosmetic-compliant inks, has a footprint under one square metre, and is aimed at fashion stores, airports and leisure venues. It is overseas only. Source: [Cosmetics Business](https://www.cosmeticsbusiness.com/fingernails2go-and-hp-join-digital-nail-art-revolution-113305)
- **The real concept, for contrast:**
  - Clockwork's robot used a structured-light 3D scanner and an AI model to guide a plastic-tipped polish cartridge nail by nail, charging US$8 per manicure. It planned placements in offices, retail stores and apartment buildings. Source: [The Batch / DeepLearning.AI](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/every-problem-looks-like-a-nail)
  - 10Beauty's Ulta system is a five-step robotic manicure taking 25–45 minutes for about US$30. Source: [ProspyrMed blog](https://www.prospyrmed.com/blog/post/ulta-beauty-five-step-robotic-manicure-10beauty)
- **Consumer substitutes in Australia** are press-on nails and DIY kits, which Australian media frame as cheaper alternatives to salon visits. Example headline: "I swapped my $70 a month nails for a $5 hack". Source: [Mamamia](https://www.mamamia.com.au/best-press-on-nails/)

### Inferences
- A new Australian entrant could truthfully claim to be the first *robotic polish-painting* manicure service in Australia, based on everything found. It should not claim to be the first *automated nail kiosk*, because Arbor's 2014 printing prototype predates it.
- Nail-art printers and press-ons compete for the same "quick nails without a technician" spend, but deliver a different product: printed art on a prepared nail, or glue-on tips.

### Gaps
- Whether any Arbor/Glamour Nail kiosk ever operated commercially in Australia, and when it stopped.
- Whether any Australian salon uses imported AI nail-art printers that also lay a polish base coat. None was found, but social media was not searchable.

## 4. Australian demand facts: industry size, labour and wages, and regulation

### Takeaway
Australia's waxing and nail salon industry is about A$1.9bn a year and growing slowly (about 2.3–2.4%). It is highly fragmented. Beauty therapists earn a median of about A$1,200 a week. In the two states checked, NSW and Queensland, nail services are regulated at premises/infection-control level by councils, not by individual licences. Polish-only application appears to fall outside NSW's "skin penetration" rules and Queensland's licensable "higher risk" services.

### Cited Findings (the 5 strongest Australian facts)
1. **Industry size.** IBISWorld's Nov 2025 report expects the Personal Waxing and Nail Salons industry to reach A$1.9bn in revenue in 2025-26. That is a 2.3% rise for the year and 2.4% annualised growth over the five years to 2025-26. The industry is highly fragmented, with no company holding more than 5% share. Source: [IBISWorld industry report](https://www.ibisworld.com/australia/industry/personal-waxing-and-nail-salons-in-australia/5126/)
   - Partly conflicting: IBISWorld's market-size page shows A$1.9bn for 2025, 1.7% growth in 2025 and a −0.7% CAGR for 2020–2025. Source: [IBISWorld market size](https://www.ibisworld.com/australia/market-size/personal-waxing-nail-salons/5126/)
2. **Workforce and pay.** Median weekly earnings for beauty therapists are A$1,200, per Jobs and Skills Australia as of Jan 2025. Source: [Victorian Government – Beauty therapist](https://vic.gov.au/beauty-therapist)
   - Another source lists 33,400 employed, A$1,196 a week and "strong growth". Source: [SkillsRoad](https://skillsroad.com.au/careers/industries/trades-services/beauty-therapists)
   - Advertised nail-technician pay is high in some markets: A$65k–$78k in Brisbane, and A$80k–$85k plus free accommodation in Roma, Qld. A Brunswick West (Melbourne) salon advertises A$26.44–$51.72 an hour under the Hair and Beauty Industry Award. Sources: [SEEK](https://au.seek.com/nail-jobs); [Indeed](https://au.indeed.com/Nail-jobs-in-Melbourne-VIC)
3. **Consumer prices (benchmark for a robot service).** A directory lists manicures from A$30 across 192 Australian clinics. Source: [WhatClinic](https://www.whatclinic.com/beauty-salons/australia/manicure?page=7)
   - Individual listings show manicures from A$25 in Perth and A$30 in Adelaide. Sources: [WhatClinic](https://whatclinic.com/beauty-salons/australia/manicure?page=5); [Fresha](https://www.fresha.com/lp/pt/tt/manicure/au-brisbane/red-hill)
   - In Brisbane, one listing shows a shellac manicure at A$43 and a BIAB manicure at A$70, and a BIAB infill with removal is A$80. These are snapshots and may be dated. Source: [Fresha Brisbane](https://www.fresha.com/lp/pt/tt/manicure/au-brisbane/red-hill)
4. **NSW regulation.** Skin penetration businesses are regulated under the Public Health Act 2010 and Public Health Regulation 2022. Councils keep a register of these premises and can inspect them. Sources: [NSW Health](https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/environment/factsheets/Pages/cont-skin-pen-procedures.aspx); [City of Canterbury Bankstown](https://www.cbcity.nsw.gov.au/index.php/environment/public-health/skin-penetration)
   - Council guidance lists "nail treatments (cuticle cutting and acrylic nail application)" as skin penetration procedures. Sources: [City of Canterbury Bankstown](https://www.cbcity.nsw.gov.au/index.php/environment/public-health/skin-penetration); [City of Canada Bay](https://www.canadabay.nsw.gov.au/business/compliance/health-and-food-safety/beauty-treatments)
   - Hairdressers and beauty salons that do no skin penetration are covered by the standards of the Local Government Act 1993. Sources: [Lane Cove Council](https://www.lanecove.nsw.gov.au/Development/Compliance%E2%80%8B/Health-Premises); [Kiama Council](https://www.kiama.nsw.gov.au/Services/Public-health-and-safety/Hairdressing-beauty-and-skin-penetration-premises)
5. **Queensland regulation.** Under the Public Health (Infection Control for Personal Appearance Services) Act 2003, hairdressing and beauty therapy are "non-higher risk" and need no licence. They must still comply with Queensland Health's infection control guidelines, and councils may inspect. Only higher-risk services that release blood, such as tattooing and piercing, need a licence. Sources: [Brisbane City Council](https://www.brisbane.qld.gov.au/laws-and-permits/licences-permits-regulations/personal-appearance-services-licence); [Queensland legislation](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/2014-07-01/act-2003-081)

### Inferences
- **Licensing.** On the NSW and Queensland rules found, a robot that only removes polish and paints/dries nails does not appear to need a licensed operator or skin-penetration registration. If it cuts cuticles, it would trigger NSW skin-penetration registration with the council. In both states the obligations attach to premises and infection control, not to individual technician licences. This contrasts with US cosmetology licensing, which robotic manicures partly sidestep. It should be confirmed with councils.
- **Demand.** The headline argument is labour cost and availability: technicians are advertised at A$65k–$85k in regional and high-demand markets.
  - But budget salons charging A$25–$30 already undercut 10Beauty's US price of about US$30 (roughly A$45). The Australian pitch would therefore rest on speed, no appointment, and placement (malls, airports, offices) rather than price, unless the machines can match the cheapest walk-in salons.
- **Market size.** The A$1.9bn industry also includes waxing. The nail-only share was not available.

### Gaps
- **Victoria:** registration rules for nail/beauty businesses under the Public Health and Wellbeing Act were not covered by the search results.
- **Product safety and compliance not checked** (no searches left):
  - ACCC product safety.
  - Electrical safety and Regulatory Compliance Mark (RCM) requirements for importing a mains-powered robot.
  - AICIS/cosmetics rules for imported polish pods.
- **Labour shortage status:** no formal Jobs and Skills Australia shortage-list rating for beauty therapists or nail technicians was found. The wage evidence is a job-ad snapshot.
- **Nail-only market size:** no nail-only revenue split, and no count of nail salons, was available beyond IBISWorld's paywalled summary.
