# Verifier 2 (adversarial): Automated gold-buying kiosks ("smart gold stores") in Australia and NZ

**VERDICT: ABSENT. Confidence: 80%.** Second, independent verifier. I did not read verifier 1's notes. I ran 38 WebSearch calls (the hard cap), all in standard mode, and did not use WebFetch. Every finding below comes from search-result extracts. Notes written 13 Oct 2026.

## Key Question 1: After resolving the leads, is any automated gold-buying kiosk or machine operating, piloted or announced in Australia or NZ?

### Takeaway
No. I searched in English and Chinese across Chinese-Australian portals, Indian-Australian precincts, web extracts of IP Australia and ABN registers, Australian jewellery trade press, the footprints of the overseas machine makers, and New Zealand. None of it turned up an automated or self-service gold-buying kiosk, a gold-buyback ATM, an Australian or NZ distributor or reseller of such machines, or an announced pilot. I also found no sell-only bullion vending machine in Australia. Every Australian or NZ result was a staffed counter, a staffed shopping-centre kiosk, an online portal, or a mail-in service.

### Inferences
- Two independent passes found nothing: verifier 1 with 39 searches and this pass with 38, using different angles (Chinese and Indian diaspora channels, register extracts, trade press, supplier footprints, NZ). That is fairly strong evidence that no web-visible automated gold-buying kiosk, pilot or distributor exists in Australia or NZ as of Oct 2026.
- The most plausible thing a web search could miss is a single imported Chinese machine in a Chinese-run jewellery shop (e.g. Box Hill, Chatswood, Hurstville, Sunnybank), promoted only on WeChat. That residual risk is why confidence is 80% rather than higher.
- Kinghood's reported financial distress from December 2025 makes a Kinghood-supplied Australian rollout in 2026 less likely. It also means an Australian entrant should treat Kinghood as a high-risk supplier and consider GOLDEX (Singapore) or other makers. This rests on Chinese press extracts and still needs verifying.
- The main supply sources are trying to export: Kinghood's international edition and Canton Fair push, GOLDEX's Singapore base, and Goldsikka's overseas plans. A competitor could appear quickly, so being first would likely be a short-lived advantage.

## Key Question 2: What is the closest thing found, and how does it differ?

### Takeaway
Every closest match needs a person to do the assay. These are staffed "cash for gold" kiosks in shopping centres, counter buyers with XRF, Gold Exchange Universe's relaunched website and Pitt St counter, mint buyback desks that pay by bank transfer within 1–5 days, and mail-in or door-to-door buyers. None of them has a machine that weighs, assays, quotes and pays on its own. All the true machines (Kinghood, GoldSmart, GOLDEX, Goldsikka's melting ATM) are overseas, with no AU or NZ presence found.

### Inferences
- The service itself (turning gold jewellery into cash) is well covered in Australia by staffed buyers. What is missing is the format: an automated, transparent, fast-paying machine. A new entrant would be "first to automate", not "first to buy gold". Its pitch would rest on price transparency, against CHOICE's finding of 39–89% of gold value, and on speed, against the Perth Mint's 2–5 day payout.
- The nearest format is the staffed shopping-centre kiosk. An automated kiosk would compete for the same mall spaces and customers, and might inherit that format's reputation for low payouts and for fraud.
- Because Australian rules would require an attendant for ID checks and a holding period (per the brief), an Australian version would be semi-automated: XRF, weighing and pricing done by machine, with an attendant for KYC (customer ID checks). That narrows the gap to XRF-equipped staffed counters such as Logan Gold Buyers, so differentiation would come from software, transparency and payout speed rather than from removing staff.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether any staffed Australian buyer uses an automated quote screen or XRF output shown to customers (a "semi-kiosk"); store-level detail needs WebFetch or site visits.
- The current number and operators of shopping-centre cash-for-gold kiosks in Australia are unknown; CHOICE's data is from 2010.
- I did not verify how quickly Gold Exchange Universe pays or whether its portal offers instant online quotes.
