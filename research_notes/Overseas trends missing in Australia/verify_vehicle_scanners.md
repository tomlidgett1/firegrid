# Verification: AI drive-through vehicle inspection scanners (UVeye/DeGould-style) in Australia

## Is any automated drive-through vehicle-inspection scanner operating commercially in Australia today, or has one been piloted?

### Takeaway
**VERDICT: PARTIAL (provisional). Confidence: 60% that PARTIAL is the right label. About 85% that ABSENT is wrong. About 30% that a fuller search would show PRESENT.**

The deciding find is in Australian collision-repair trade press (Paint & Panel). It reports that Car Solutions International's Australian arm is building a demonstration and training centre at Warwick Farm in Sydney, due to open in early 2026. The centrepiece is an AI-powered vehicle scanning system: vehicles drive through a scanner that maps dents and scratches. It is aimed mainly at insurers and assessors.

That announcement alone means a new entrant cannot claim to be first in Australia with a drive-through AI damage scanner. I could not confirm whether the centre opened or now runs commercially. If it does, the insurer and claims segment would arguably be PRESENT.

### Inferences
- The only Australian drive-through AI scanner found is aimed at the **insurance and claims** side (insurers and assessors), not remarketing or rental. This suggests, without proof, that the dealer, auction, rental, fleet-return and port segments may not yet be served by a drive-through scanner. A fuller search is needed to confirm.
- App-based AI inspection (NextFleet smartInspect) is already standard at one Australian fleet manager. A scanner-as-a-service seller would compete against cheaper app tools at fleet return points, and would need to win on speed, consistency and undercarriage and tyre coverage.
- Tchek's reported sale of its scanner hardware to a German firm suggests the hardware arch segment is consolidating in Europe. An Australian entrant could become a distributor or licensee of a consolidated vendor rather than build from scratch. This is speculative.
- On the evidence gathered, the PARTIAL rating rests mainly on one trade-press report. If Car Solutions' Warwick Farm centre is operating and offering scans to insurers, the verdict for the claims segment moves toward PRESENT.

## What is the closest thing found, and how does it differ?

### Takeaway
The closest thing found is Car Solutions International's planned Warwick Farm (Sydney) centre with a drive-through AI scanner for dents and scratches, aimed at insurers and assessors. It differs from the proposed concept in four ways:
- the customer segment (claims, not remarketing or rental)
- scope (body damage only, with no evidence of tyre or undercarriage scanning)
- scale (a single demonstration and training site)
- unconfirmed status (it was due to open in early 2026)

The next closest is NextFleet's app-based smartInspect, which is adjacent but has no fixed hardware.

### Inferences
- An entrant positioned on **remarketing and returns** (dealer trade-ins, auction intake, rental and lease returns, port handover) with full body, tyre and undercarriage coverage would be differentiated from the only Australian precedent found, which is insurer-focused body-damage scanning. The entrant still could not claim "first drive-through AI vehicle scanner in Australia".
