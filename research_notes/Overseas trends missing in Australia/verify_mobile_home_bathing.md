# Verification: mobile home bathing for bed-bound and frail people (portable full-immersion tub or bath van). Australian presence, overseas growth and funding

**VERDICT: ABSENT.** **Confidence: 75%** that no Australian business, commercial pilot, trial, announcement, or overseas company serving Australian customers brings a portable full-immersion bathtub or a bath van into clients' homes.

- Checked on 13 Oct 2026 by an independent, adversarial verifier.
- Searches: 38 WebSearch calls in "standard" mode, in English, Chinese, Japanese and Korean. 25 were presence checks, 9 covered overseas growth, and 4 covered funding and WHS.
- WebFetch was not used (environment constraint), so no full page was opened. Every finding below comes from search-result extracts, and snippet-level details should be read with that in mind.
- Why confidence is not higher than 75%:
  - Very small operators are poorly indexed. These include sole-trader NDIS workers on Mable or Hireup, and community-language businesses that only advertise on WeChat, Xiaohongshu, KakaoTalk or Facebook.
  - One entity on the NDIS register, "Portable Mobility Access Bathroom" (Prestons NSW), could not be resolved.
  - The search tool is US-centric. It returned almost no pages at all from Bolton Clarke, Silverchain, Feros Care or Five Good Friends, so their silence is an absence of evidence, not proof that they offer nothing.
- Why the verdict is ABSENT and not PARTIAL: every adjacent offering found falls in a category the brief says does not count:
  - standard personal care using existing facilities
  - bathroom modification or bathroom-pod hire
  - product sales with no service
  - homeless shower vans in a different segment
- The two offerings closest to PARTIAL are portable accessible-bathroom hire and the Aquabuddy in-bed shower, which is sold to private clients in Australia. Neither is a staffed visiting full-bath service.

## 1. Does any Australian provider bring a portable full bathtub or bath van into clients' homes today, or has one been piloted?

### Takeaway
No. Across 25 presence searches, I found no Australian business, pilot, trial or announcement, and no overseas operator serving Australians, that brings a portable full-immersion tub or a bath van to a client's home. Every hit fell into one of these groups:
- standard personal care (assisted shower, bath or bed-bath using the client's own bathroom)
- bathroom products or modifications
- portable accessible-bathroom (pod) hire
- shower vans for people experiencing homelessness

### Inferences
- Vehicle-based hygiene services do run in Australia, but only for people experiencing homelessness (Orange Sky; the 2016 Melbourne shower bus). The know-how for mobile water heating and drainage exists locally, but nobody found has applied it to home-bound frail or disabled people.
- The Australian market for "bathing for frail people" is split between hourly personal-care visits (no tub), bathroom modification or pod hire (no staff), and equipment (no service). A new entrant offering the Japanese or Chinese team-plus-portable-tub model could, on this evidence, claim to be first. This is subject to the micro-operator caveat in the preamble.
- Bed-based products already reach Australian private clients and nursing homes (Aquabuddy) and are sold online (EZ-ACCESS, Vikus). Equipment supply therefore does not block launch; the gap is the staffed service.

## 2. What is the closest thing found, and how does it differ?

### Takeaway
The closest Australian offerings each cover only one part of the job:
- hourly personal care, with bed baths or an assisted shower or bath using the home's own bathroom
- portable accessible-bathroom (pod) hire for NDIS participants
- in-bed shower products sold into Australia (Aquabuddy)
- shower vans for people experiencing homelessness (Orange Sky)
- a 2018 spa-bath pilot for palliative patients in hospital

None brings a full-immersion tub and a team to a bed-bound person's home.

### Cited Findings
- **Personal care (closest by customer and funding).** Right at Home offers "assistance with bathing and showering, bed baths" and bed or chair transfers ([Right at Home](https://www.rightathome.com.au/clinical-services/personal-care)). Uniting AgeWell's CHSP personal care covers "showering, bathing, drying and dressing" ([My Aged Care](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/find-a-provider/search/3y738vhqft-uniting-agewell-limited/help-at-home/uniting-agewell-mildura-chsp-hmnhzl5007)).
  - How it differs: usually one worker, the client's existing bathroom, or a bed bath (washing without immersion). There is no tub or hot-water supply, and no nurse vital-sign check.
- **Portable bathroom hire (closest by "bring the facility to the home").** Australian Ramp & Access Solutions, an NDIS-registered provider, offers "hire of compliant portable disability bathrooms, short and long term" nationally ([listing](https://www.infoqore.com.au/directory/info-by-ndis-registration-groups/australian-ramp-and-access-solutions)). Careport is an "NDIS-registered portable bathroom available to hire" ([Leapin](https://www.leapin.com.au/?p=13424)).
  - How it differs: a static pod for wheelchair users, often used during renovations. It has no staff, is not a full-immersion tub, and is not suitable for bed-bound clients.
- **In-bed bathing products (closest by technology).** Aquabuddy's portable in-bed shower system has sold to nursing homes and private clients in Australia ([CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/article/id/223805-no-more-bathtime-anxiety-for-athome-bedridden-patients)). EZ-ACCESS sells an inflatable tub for "full submersion bathing in bed" ([EZ-ACCESS](https://www.ezaccess.com/post/in-bed-bathing-accessories-guide-for-caregivers)).
  - How it differs: these are products bought or used by families or facilities. No trained visiting team comes with them.
- **Mobile hygiene vans (closest by vehicle model).** Orange Sky provides "free laundry and shower access to anyone in need", with vans that operate "in parks, and alongside partner organisations all across the country" ([Orange Sky](https://orangesky.org.au/what-we-do/); [Orange Sky Sydney](https://orangesky.org.au/locations/sydney/)).
  - How it differs: serves people experiencing homelessness, offers showers rather than baths, and operates at fixed sites rather than in clients' homes.
- **Clinical immersion bathing (closest by clinical intent).** A 2018 Austin Health pilot tested spa baths for pain and anxiety in acute palliative inpatients ([Austin Health](https://ahro.austin.org.au/austinjspui/handle/1/18774)).
  - How it differs: hospital-based with a fixed spa, and a research pilot rather than a service.

### Inferences
- The gap is specifically a **staffed, equipment-carrying, full-immersion service for people who cannot get to a bathroom.** Personal-care providers already have the customers and the funding, and pod-hire firms already have NDIS registration. Either group could become a fast follower, which is a competitive risk for a first entrant.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether any personal-care provider occasionally uses an inflatable in-bed tub informally. No search result showed it.

## 3. What are the strongest dated 2024–2026 overseas growth figures?

### Takeaway
China is where the growth is:
- one operator's monthly orders grew about tenfold from 2022 to 2024
- Meituan reported bathing orders up 442% year on year, but this is most likely September 2023, which is before 2024
- Shanghai issued a dedicated support policy on 17 March 2025
- July 2026 reports show continuing demand, but also price resistance

Japan's 訪問入浴介護 (home-visit bathing care) is entrenched but **shrinking**: 1,708 establishments in 2020 against 1,641 in 2024. Taiwan standardised training for bath-van staff in January 2025. Korea is adding severe-case payment loadings ("add-ons") for visiting bathing in 2026.

### Cited Findings
**China**
- **Meituan.** Commercial orders for bathing services on Meituan rose **442% year on year "in September"**, and merchants offering the service rose **936%**. The snippet gives no year; the surrounding policy context points to **2023**, so this is not a 2024–26 figure. Source: [Sixth Tone, "In Shanghai, a Splash of Comfort in Elderly Care: Home Bathing Services"](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1014251)
- **Operator growth.** Industry insider Kong Dandan reported monthly orders of **about 10 (2022), 30–50 (2023) and more than 100 (2024)**, with the Spring Festival as the peak season. The price per order stayed at **298–358 yuan**. This was reported by Workers' Daily and carried by [China News Service, 23 Aug 2024](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2024/08-23/10273321.shtml). The same figures appear in [gmw.cn, 30 Jul 2026](https://m.gmw.cn/2026-07/30/content_1304540211.htm); the search extracts disagree on which article is the original.
- **Shanghai policy.** The Shanghai Civil Affairs Bureau issued 《关于支持发展老年助浴服务的若干意见（试行）》 ("Several Opinions on Supporting the Development of Elderly Bathing Services, Trial"), document number 沪民养老发〔2025〕4号, on **17 Mar 2025**. Its main points:
  - priority is disabled older people living at home, with a horizon to 2027
  - districts and sub-districts are to raise funds for disabled older people in financial hardship
  - bathing services are added to the subsidised comprehensive liability insurance for aged-care providers
  - community spaces are to be provided free or at low cost for bathing-service outlets
  - organisations already delivering aged-care subsidies, long-term-care insurance and family care beds are encouraged to offer home bathing
  - market-based pilots are encouraged

## 4. Is it fundable under Support at Home (from 1 Nov 2025) or the NDIS? Clinical and WHS considerations

### Takeaway
Fundable in principle:
- **Support at Home.** Bathing sits within "personal care". **From 1 Oct 2026 participants pay no contribution for personal care**, but budgets do not grow.
- **CHSP.** Personal care includes bathing.
- **NDIS.** Core "Assistance with Self-Care Activities" covers personal care, at roughly A$70–74 per hour standard weekday.

No source covers how a 2–3 person team (or a nurse) would be billed per bath, or what price caps apply.

WHS: Victorian and WA regulators name bathing people in their homes as a high-risk manual task. I found no Australian infection-control guidance for portable tubs shared between clients.

### Cited Findings
- **Support at Home basics.** It replaced Home Care Packages on 1 Nov 2025. Personal care is one of 14 service categories and covers "showering, bathing, sponge baths and personal hygiene". At launch, personal care was an "independence" service with contributions from 5% (full pensioners) up to 50% (self-funded retirees). Sources: [Aged Care Decisions, service list](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/complete-support-at-home-service-list/); [Aged Care Decisions](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/support-at-home-showers-dressing-continence-fully-funded/) (a provider website, not official)
- **Official change from 1 October 2026.** "Participants will no longer pay contributions for personal care services delivered from 1 October, if they are approved for the personal care service type and have available Support at Home funding." Personal care includes showering, non-clinical continence management, dressing, eating, personal hygiene, and help with self-administering medication, and "the definition of personal care will remain the same". The 2026 year is confirmed by the law-firm and provider sources. Sources: [Department of Health, Disability and Ageing](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/support-at-home/charging-for-support-at-home-services/personal-care-contribution-change); [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/contribution-payments-removed-for-personal-care); [CatholicCare Sydney](https://www.catholiccare.org/catholiccare-sydney-blog/support-at-home-personal-care-fees-update)
- **Limits of the change.** No new assessment is needed, but personal care must be in the support plan and budget must be available. The budget does not increase. Other non-clinical services, such as cleaning and transport, still attract contributions. Sources: [Aged Care Decisions](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/support-at-home-personal-care-fees/); [CatholicCare Sydney](https://www.catholiccare.org/catholiccare-sydney-blog/support-at-home-personal-care-fees-update)
- **CHSP.** Personal care includes "support with showering, bathing, drying and dressing" (Uniting AgeWell Mildura CHSP listing). Source: [My Aged Care](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/find-a-provider/search/3y738vhqft-uniting-agewell-limited/help-at-home/uniting-agewell-mildura-chsp-hmnhzl5007)
- **NDIS pricing.**
  - The 2025–26 Pricing Arrangements are listed as effective 24 Nov 2025 to 30 Jun 2026 ([NDIS](https://ndis.gov.au/providers/pricing-and-payments/pricing/pricing-arrangements)).
  - Assistance with Self-Care Activities, standard weekday daytime (item 01_011_0107_1_1): 2026–27 cap **$73.58 per hour**, according to an unofficial aggregator ([Carevo](https://carevo.com.au/tools/ndis-price-guide?category=1)). Another source says $70.23 was held over, so the figures conflict.
  - High-intensity self-care, weekday evening: 2025–26 rate $83.72 per hour ([DAAR](https://daar.com.au/resources/ndis/ndis-pricing-guide/01_401_0104_1_1)).
  - How two-worker support is claimed was not found.
- **NDIS bathing equipment.** Supplier guidance says an occupational therapist normally applies for NDIS or state funding for bathing aids, using a supplier quote. Source: [Mobility Care](https://mobilitycare.net.au/?p=2281) (exact page uncertain; a supplier source)
- **WHS (Australian regulators).**
  - WorkSafe Victoria's home-care OHS compliance kit lists "showering or bathing people in the home" among **seven high-risk tasks** ([WorkSafe Victoria](https://worksafe.vic.gov.au/resources/home-care-occupational-health-and-safety-compliance-kit)).
  - WorkSafe WA lists "assisting with transferring, bathing and dressing clients" among common manual tasks in people's homes ([WorkSafe WA](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/manual-tasks-healthcare-and-social-assistance)). Its December 2024 guide for carers links showering and bathing to musculoskeletal injuries ([WorkSafe WA carers guide](https://worksafe.wa.gov.au/system/files/documents/2024-12/GL_ManualTask_Carers.pdf)).
  - Safe Work Australia classes people handling as a hazardous manual task. Businesses (PCBUs) must eliminate risks where reasonably practicable, or otherwise minimise them using the hierarchy of controls ([Safe Work Australia](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/book/health-care-and-social-assistance/hazards-risks-and-controls/hazardous-manual-tasks)).
  - SafeWork SA uses bathing a patient as an example of a manual task ([SafeWork SA](https://www.safework.sa.gov.au/workplaces/work-tasks-and-projects/hazardous-manual-tasks/overview)).
  - Homes "are not designed for healthcare purposes" and may have confined spaces ([Ausmed](https://www.ausmed.com/cpd/articles/lone-working-safety)).
- **Clinical considerations.** Victorian dementia guidance suggests a basin wash as an alternative to a bath or shower, plus a hand-held shower, rails and a seat ([Better Health Channel](https://betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/conditionsandtreatments/dementia-hygiene)). A review of dementia bathing recommends considering a towel or bed bath instead of a shower or tub ([Health Plexus review](https://healthplexus.net/files/content/2009/October/1209dementia.pdf)). Immersion bathing is therefore not the default recommendation for people with dementia.
- **Overseas staffing benchmark.** Japan uses 2 care workers plus 1 nurse per visit ([Aichi Prefecture](https://www.pref.aichi.jp/korei/kaigo-net/katsuyaku/volume02/)). Taiwan requires 20 hours of standardised training for bath-van staff ([MOHW](https://mohw.gov.tw/dl-93112-e78219b0-99c8-4739-995a-58d50ef425ee.html)).

### Up to 5 cited Australian facts
1. From 1 October 2026, Support at Home participants pay **no contributions for personal care services**, including showering and personal hygiene, if they are approved for the service and have funding available. Source: [Department of Health, Disability and Ageing](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/support-at-home/charging-for-support-at-home-services/personal-care-contribution-change)
2. Support at Home replaced Home Care Packages from 1 November 2025. Its personal care category covers showering, bathing, sponge baths and personal hygiene. Source: [Aged Care Decisions](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/complete-support-at-home-service-list/)
3. WorkSafe Victoria lists "showering or bathing people in the home" among seven high-risk tasks in home care. Source: [WorkSafe Victoria](https://worksafe.vic.gov.au/resources/home-care-occupational-health-and-safety-compliance-kit)
4. Older Australians face "significant barriers to safe and independent bathing" because of household features such as steps and shower thresholds. Source: [Flinders University "Safe Steps"](https://www.flinders.edu.au/factory-of-the-future/case-studies/safe-steps)
5. Australia's only van-based hygiene service found, Orange Sky, offers free shower and laundry access in parks and at partner sites for people in need. It does not offer in-home bathing for frail people. Source: [Orange Sky](https://orangesky.org.au/what-we-do/)
- Further demand context: a Japanese embassy article notes that many Australian homes and hotels have only showers ([Embassy of Japan in Australia](https://www.au.emb-japan.go.jp/files/100423943.pdf); search extract).

### Inferences
- **Support at Home.** A registered provider could most likely deliver a mobile full bath as "personal care" (bathing), and from 1 Oct 2026 the participant would pay nothing. This removes the main consumer price barrier, but the cost comes out of fixed budgets: 2–3 staff for about an hour uses 2–3 hours of personal-care budget per bath. Actual Support at Home price caps and multi-worker billing were not verified. A nurse's assessment component might be billed as clinical care (nursing) only where clinically justified; also unverified.
- **NDIS.** The service could plausibly be claimed as Assistance with Self-Care Activities, possibly at the high-intensity rate or with two workers where the plan supports it. NDIS assistive-technology funding is for the participant's own equipment, assessed by an OT, so it would not fund a provider's shared tub or van.
- **Private pay.** This is a likely complementary channel, for example respite or palliative "bath days" paid for by families.
- **WHS.** Transfers into a floor-level portable tub are a high-risk people-handling task. Expect to need:
  - a documented risk assessment under the hazardous manual tasks Code of Practice
  - mechanical aids such as hoists and slide sheets
  - two-person transfers

  Japan's three-person team fits this. Small Australian homes may limit where a tub can be set up.
- **Infection control.** Shared tubs will need single-use liners or validated disinfection between clients. No Australian standard specific to this was found. Water supply and drainage, including the client's hot-water capacity, is an operational issue for this model.

### Gaps
- Support at Home price caps and unit prices for personal care, and rules for billing multiple workers, were not verified.
- How the NDIS prices and claims two-worker bathing support was not found.
- Whether the 1 Oct 2026 personal-care change took effect as planned was confirmed only through the official page snippet ("from 1 October"); the full text was not read.
- No Australian infection-control guidance specific to portable bathing equipment was found.
- No specific licensing or registration category for mobile bathing was identified. Registration under the new Aged Care Act (Support at Home) or the NDIS would apply, but this was not verified.
