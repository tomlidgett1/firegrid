# Verification: pay-per-use soundproof work/call booths in public places. Is it already in Australia, and how fast is it growing overseas?

Adversarial verifier notes. Today is 13 Oct 2026. 39 WebSearch calls were run, all in "standard" mode (28 presence checks, 9 overseas, 2 Australian context). WebFetch was not used. All findings come from search-result extracts, so the exact wording of the underlying articles could not be checked.

## Key Question 1: Is any pay-per-use public work/call booth operating or trialled in Australia (or NZ) today?

### Verdict and confidence
- **VERDICT: PRESENT. Confidence: 80%.**
- An Adelaide startup, **TaskPod** (TASKPOD PTY LTD), launched the same concept in Australia in 2022:
  - soundproof 1–4 person pods in airports, Westfield shopping centres and CBD malls;
  - booked and unlocked through an app;
  - A$3.75 per 15 minutes (A$15 per hour), with business subscriptions for hybrid workers.
- It ran across at least three states, with 10 pods in Nov 2022 and 13 by Jan 2023.
- The proposal's premise ("not yet existing in Australia in any form") is false.
- **Most of the remaining 20% uncertainty is about whether TaskPod still trades in Oct 2026.** I found no dated 2024–2026 coverage. If it has wound down, a strict reading of the rules would make the verdict PARTIAL: a commercial launch with no ongoing service. Even then, a new entrant could not claim to be first.

### Takeaway
The concept was launched in Australia as a commercial, multi-site, multi-state network: TaskPod (2022–23). It used the same model (per-15-minute pricing, app unlock, corporate subscriptions) in the same kinds of venues (Adelaide and Perth airports; Westfield Parramatta, Bondi Junction and Miranda; Pitt Street Mall; World Square). TaskPod's co-founders also co-founded the booth maker **Bureau**. In April–May 2026 Bureau became the hardware partner for **WeWork Go**, a pay-per-use public pod network that is US-only for now and marketed as "worldwide". I found no transport-authority pod trial (Sydney Metro, Metro Tunnel, Cross River Rail) and no NZ operator.

### Inferences
- **Fidelity to the concept.** TaskPod matches the concept on nearly every axis:
  - enclosed 1–4 person soundproof pods;
  - app booking and unlock, billed per 15 minutes;
  - corporate memberships for hybrid workers;
  - sites in airports, shopping centres and CBD malls, with office and hotel lobbies and train stations targeted.
- **Points not evidenced for TaskPod:**
  - **Monitors.** Wi-Fi and power are confirmed, but monitors are not.
  - **Station placements.** Train-station pods were planned but never shown to exist.
  - **Venue revenue share.** It is not confirmed that TaskPod owned the pods and shared revenue with hosts; this is likely but unverified.
- **Signs of a stall after early 2023:**
  - The planned "+25 in four months" (Nov 2022) became "13 installed, +8 coming" (Jan 2023).
  - No dated coverage after Jan 2023 was found.
  - App version notes were last seen dated 2022.
  - LinkedIn shows 2–10 staff.
  - The founders' visible focus in 2026 is Bureau, a B2B booth maker and WeWork Go's supplier.
  - Together these suggest TaskPod may be dormant or scaled back, but this is not confirmed.
- **Strategic implications for an entrant:**
  - The model has been tried in Australia at small scale, and the people who tried it moved on to supplying hardware.
  - Australian-founded Bureau is now the pod supplier for a global WeWork pay-per-use network. WeWork operates in Australia.
  - A local entrant could therefore face a well-capitalised incumbent arriving later.

### Inferences
- Excluding TaskPod, nothing in Australia offers pay-per-use public booths. Libraries meet part of the need (somewhere private to take a call) for free, but are limited to opening hours and membership. Coworking meets it at a higher price, in private premises, with more friction. So if TaskPod is dormant, the *live* market may be empty. The "first mover" claim is still unavailable.
- Booth hardware is plentiful in Australia (Framery, Hushoffice, Focuspod at Bunnings, Envoy, Bureau). Supply is not a barrier; the constraints are site access and utilisation.

### Gaps
- No register of TaskPod's live sites was found, so it is unknown whether any of the 13+ pods remain in place.
- The search found no council or state library that charges per use, but not every library was checked. State Libraries NSW, Victoria and Queensland were not specifically confirmed.

## Key Question 3: How fast is the category growing overseas (dated booth counts, revenue, funding)?

### Takeaway
- **Japan** is the deepest market:
  - JR East's STATION WORK grew from 300+ sites (Nov 2021) to 815 (Jun 2023) against a 1,000-site target.
  - Telecube's public units passed 200 (Mar 2022) and then 300 (Feb 2023).
  - CocoDesk went from 7 booths (Feb 2020) to 100 (Sep 2021).
  - Toei Subway added booths from Mar 2024 and reached 5 stations by Feb 2026.
- **The West is earlier but accelerating in 2026:** WeWork Go launched in the US (13 Apr 2026, hardware by Australian-founded Bureau), and Silentbox began pay-per-use in Sept 2026, reporting 78% occupancy at a Lisbon site.
- I found **no 2024–2026 network-wide counts** for the Japanese leaders, and some Western pilots failed (BookAPod UK).

### Inferences
- Growth is strongest where a transport operator or landlord owns the floor space and sponsors the network (JR East, Tokyo Metro, Toei). Japan's networks grew 2–10× over 2020–23.
- Japanese suppliers' revenue growth has shifted toward selling booths to companies (V-cube FY2025). This suggests public pay-per-use networks plateaued in the hundreds of units per operator, while B2B booth sales scaled.
- 2026 shows renewed Western momentum (WeWork Go, Silentbox SPaaS), but utilisation is site-specific: 78% occupancy in Lisbon versus BookAPod's removal for low usage.

## Key Question 4: What Australian facts matter?

### Takeaway
About a third of Australian workers usually work from home. Melbourne's Metro Tunnel opened five large new CBD stations (Nov 2025 to Feb 2026) with heavy early traffic. Australian retail landlords have already hosted work formats: Westfield hosted TaskPods, and Vicinity partnered with Hub Australia. Demand and sites exist, but the one local operator does not appear to have scaled.

### Cited Findings
1. **Hybrid work.** 36% of employed Australians **usually** worked from home in Aug 2025 (released 12 Dec 2025). In Aug 2023, 37% **regularly** worked from home; the ABS wording differs between the two releases. — [ABS Working arrangements, Aug 2025](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/labour/earnings-and-working-conditions/working-arrangements/aug-2025); [ABS Aug 2023](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/labour/earnings-and-working-conditions/working-arrangements/aug-2023)
2. **Metro Tunnel opening.** It opened to passengers on **30 Nov 2025** ("Summer Start"), with 5 new stations: Arden, Parkville, State Library, Town Hall and Anzac. More than 70,000 people visited on opening day. — [Big Build Victoria](https://bigbuild.vic.gov.au/news/metro-tunnel-project/the-metro-tunnel-is-open); [Premier of Victoria](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/victorians-love-metro-tunnel)
3. **Opening-weekend patronage by station:**
   - State Library more than 22,000
   - Town Hall more than 16,000
   - Anzac about 13,000
   - Parkville about 12,000
   - Arden more than 8,000
   - Source: [Beat](https://beat.com.au/metro-tunnel-draws-70000-as-five-new-stations-open/)
4. **Full service.** The "Big Switch" began full timetable service through the tunnel on **1 Feb 2026**. One report gives 800,000+ station visits by then, but that figure is undated and unconfirmed. — [Railway News](https://railway-news.com/melbourne-welcomes-big-switch-on-metro-tunnel/)
5. **Retail landlords and work formats.**
   - Vicinity partnered with Hub Australia on a 4,100 sqm coworking space at Box Hill Central to serve "work near home" demand (2021): [Hub Australia](https://www.hubaustralia.com/news/hub-australia-partners-with-vicinity-to-support-work-near-home/)
   - Scentre's Westfield Parramatta, Bondi Junction and Miranda hosted TaskPods in 2022–23: [eGlobal Travel Media](https://eglobaltravelmedia.com.au/2023/01/25/the-new-on-the-go-workplaces-of-the-future/)

### Inferences
- Persistent hybrid work (about 36%) plus new high-footfall underground stations fit the concept well. However, the Australian test case (TaskPod) suggests that winning sites from landlords is feasible, while utilisation, reliability (SmartCompany's unlock and connectivity problems) and scaling beyond about 13 units were the hard parts.

- No Sydney Metro City & Southwest patronage figure was gathered (out of budget).
- No Cross River Rail opening date was confirmed.
- No 2025–26 statement was found from Scentre or Vicinity about adding new work formats.
