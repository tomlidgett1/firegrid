# Verification 2 (adversarial): Unstaffed, app/QR-booked billiards, mahjong and board-game rooms in Australia

**Verifier:** second, independent, adversarial verifier. I did not read verifier 1's notes or any other file in research_notes.
**Date:** 13 October 2026.
**Method:** 40 WebSearch calls, all in "standard" mode, which hit the hard cap. WebFetch was not used because it fails in this environment. Every finding below therefore comes from search-result extracts, so hours, prices and status may be out of date.

**VERDICT: PARTIAL. Confidence: 65%.**

**What was not found.** No Australian business lets the public book and pay by app, QR code or WeChat for a billiards, mahjong or board-game room and then play with no staff on site. No Chinese unmanned-venue brand (such as Xiaotie 小铁) shows any Australian presence. 98 Billiards (Auckland) has no Australian site or announced plan.

**Why the verdict is PARTIAL rather than ABSENT.** At least two Australian member-only cue-sport clubs already run billiards rooms with no staff, open at any hour:
- **Kedron Wavell Snooker Club** (Chermside, Brisbane): "Members gain 24/7 access to our secure, self-managed billiards room".
- **Buderim Billiards and Snooker Club** (Sunshine Coast): "Members are provided with a door key, enabling them to come and play at any time".

These clubs cover a narrow segment of the job. Access is limited to vetted members, with no app or QR booking, no pay-per-hour pricing and a single site each. A new entrant could still claim to be first with public, app- or QR-booked, pay-per-hour rooms that run with no staff. It could not claim to be first with 24/7 billiards rooms that run with no staff in Australia.

**If member clubs are judged too different.** The verdict would then fall to ABSENT for the commercial model. I put about 80% confidence on that version. The remaining risk is an operator that advertises only on WeChat, Xiaohongshu or Dianping, which web search cannot index.

## Key Question 1: Is any unstaffed, app/QR-booked billiards, mahjong or board-game room operating in Australia today?

### Takeaway
No. Across 40 searches in English and Chinese, I found no public venue that runs with no staff and is booked by app, QR code or WeChat. The searches included site-restricted searches of Yeeyi, Sydney Today, 1688.com.au and Xiaohongshu. The only billiards rooms found that run with no staff are member-only community clubs in Queensland that give members key or 24/7 access.

### Inferences
- **The full model is absent.** The public commercial model is app or QR booking and payment, smart-lock entry, tables and lights switched on by IoT, and no staff. It does not appear to operate in Australia. The visible market is staffed halls, some trading until 2am to 4am (Cityheroes, Pot Black, Hangar 8).
- **Members' key clubs are the only precedent for running with no staff.** That model is old and small-scale. It shows that 24/7 billiards rooms with no staff are workable in Australia, but nobody has commercialised it for casual public use.
- **Chinese-run heyball venues are the most likely early adopters** of Chinese self-service systems. Recent examples are HeyCan (September 2024), Heyball Nation (June, probably 2026) and JOY Billiards heyball events in Brisbane, Sydney and Melbourne in 2025 ([Macau Business](https://macaubusiness.com/joy-billiards-group-to-host-heyball-events-in-september-and-october-2025/)). HeyCan's per-minute "start, then pay at finish" billing is one step from QR self-start. This is speculative.
- **98 Billiards proves the exact model is live in New Zealand.** That makes a fast entry into Australia by it or a copycat a realistic competitive risk. This is speculative.

### Gaps
- **Unindexed Chinese platforms.** WeChat mini-programs, Dianping and most Xiaohongshu posts are not web-indexed. A small self-service mahjong room in Hurstville, Box Hill or Sunnybank that advertises only on WeChat would be invisible to this method. This is the main residual risk for a PRESENT finding.

- **Operating details not confirmed:** Q Billiard's model, whether HeyCan starts tables by QR, and the outcome of Cityheroes George St's 24-hour application.

### Inferences
- **What the Australian precedents lack.** The closest precedents are membership clubs, using physical keys and board oversight. They lack every element that makes the overseas model scale: on-demand public access, pay-per-use pricing, app, QR or WeChat booking, smart-lock entry, tables started by IoT, remote CCTV support and multi-site platform economics.

- **HeyCan is the most likely domestic fast-follower.** It already combines billiards and automatic mahjong with per-minute billing. 98 Billiards (NZ) is the most likely trans-Tasman entrant. Both points are speculative.

## Key Question 3 (Lead 7): What legal and approval issues apply?

### Takeaway
The search extracts did not show mahjong-specific rules. NSW's Unlawful Gambling Act 1998 regulates "unlawful games" and premises used for gambling, including single rooms, and Victoria penalises unauthorised gambling heavily. Queensland law was not reached. For any site, the practical constraints are trading hours under planning consent, the need for staff to serve alcohol, and verifying the age of minors.

### Cited Findings
- **NSW law:** the long title of the Unlawful Gambling Act 1998 (NSW) is "An Act to impose prohibitions on certain gambling activities and in relation to premises used for gambling". [NSW legislation](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/2012-03-01/act-1998-113); [NSW legislation (in force 2024-08-13)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/pdf/inforce/2024-08-13/act-1998-113)
  - It contains offences for organising an unlawful game, selling tickets in one, participating in one and possessing prohibited gaming devices.
  - "Premises" includes part of a building, such as a room.
  - "Unlawful game" is defined in s 5, and lawful exceptions are in s 7.
  - The extracts did not show how a mahjong game with a time-based room or table fee is treated.
- **Victorian law:** obligations sit in the Gambling Regulation Act 2003 and the Gambling Regulations 2015, administered by the VGCCC. No mahjong or private-gaming guidance surfaced. [VGCCC](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/venue-operators-and-poker-machines/venue-operator-obligations/legislative-and-regulatory-requirements/gambling-regulation-act-2003)
- **Victorian enforcement:** conducting unauthorised gambling carries up to two years' jail or a $192,000 fine. This was reported after the August 2023 raid on an alleged illegal casino in a Truganina factory, where 21 people were arrested and VGCCC staff took part. [The New Daily](https://www.thenewdaily.com.au/news/2023/08/25/illegal-casino-melbourne); [The Drop](https://thedrop.com.au/police-arrest-21-people-in-raid-on-illegal-casino/)
- **Mahjong-specific raids:** none were found in Sydney or Melbourne. The raids found were on casino-style operations.
- **Planning and trading hours:** Cityheroes' 2013 application to extend George St to 24-hour trading shows that a Sydney pool hall's hours are fixed in its development consent. Its 10am–2am hours were already approved. An unstaffed 24/7 site would need consent that allows 24-hour use. [PlanningAlerts](https://www.planningalerts.org.au/applications/609336)
- **Liquor and minors:**
  - BB8 is licensed; under-18s must be supervised and only over-18s may enter after 7pm. [OzBargain](https://www.ozbargain.com.au/node/802276)
  - In November 2024 HeyCan sold only soft drinks while its liquor licence was pending. [Region](https://region.com.au/inside-canberras-only-dedicated-pool-and-billiard-club/823958)
  - Pot Black Northbridge is licensed. [Visit Perth](https://visitperth.com/venues/pot-black-northbridge)
  - An undated AZBilliards forum post says Sydney pool rooms are "dry" while Melbourne halls serve alcohol. This may be out of date and conflicts with Cityheroes-style late trading. [AZBilliards](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/looking-for-pool-halls-in-oz.236265/post-3080898)
- **Chinese regulatory precedent:** the Nandu undercover report found minors staying overnight in unmanned venues and ID checks that did not work (query 35 extract). [Huxiu](https://www.huxiu.com/article/4885435.html?type=text); [NetEase](https://c.m.163.com/news/a/L4PT5JLE05129QAF.html)
- **Precedent for unstaffed venues in Australia:** 24/7 gyms already run staff-free hours using key cards linked to billing and cameras (Anytime Fitness), and phone-tap entry (Viva Leisure). [Dynamic Business](https://dynamicbusiness.com/franchising/open-for-business-247-02092013.html); [Region](https://region.com.au/viva-leisure-launches-australian-first-keyless-entry-app/317193/)

### Inferences
- **Billiards and board games are not gambling in themselves.** The gambling-law risk sits with mahjong played for money.
  - Charging only a flat, time-based room or table fee, taking no cut of stakes and holding no bank looks like the structure least likely to make the operator an "organiser" of an "unlawful game" in NSW, or an unauthorised gambling provider in Victoria.
  - This is unverified. NSW ss 5 and 7, the Victorian Act's private-gaming provisions and Queensland's Criminal Code or gaming Acts need legal review.
- **Running with no staff means selling no alcohol,** because responsible-service rules need staff on site. These sites would therefore be unlicensed.
- **24-hour use probably needs explicit development consent.** Councils may attach conditions such as CCTV, after-hours management plans and noise limits, as they do for 24/7 gyms. That last point is an analogy and was not searched directly.
- **Age verification needs designing in.** The Chinese experience with minors staying overnight is a reputational and regulatory risk an Australian operator should design for, for example with ID-verified app accounts. This is speculative.

### Gaps
- **Queensland:** gaming law on mahjong rooms was not researched because of the search budget. This matters because the closest precedents are in Queensland.
- **Exact wording:** the definitions of "unlawful game" in NSW s 5 and the exceptions in s 7 were not retrieved, and neither were Victoria's private-gaming provisions. No regulator guidance on mahjong parlours was found in any state.
- **Council rules for unstaffed sites:** no council policy specific to unstaffed leisure venues was found. The Cityheroes 2013 24-hour application outcome is also unknown.
