# Verification 2 (adversarial, independent): Robotic/AI manicure stations in Australia and New Zealand

**VERDICT: ABSENT. Confidence: 75%.** Searches run: 38 WebSearch calls in "standard" mode, which is the hard cap (listed under Key Question 1). Research date: 13 October 2026. I did not read the first verifier's notes or any other file in research_notes.

Across all seven leads and several new angles, I found no robotic or AI nail-painting service, pilot, distributor, reseller, retailer listing, salon installation or Australian/NZ start-up. That held in English, Vietnamese, Korean and Chinese. The strongest signals are two things that are not presence:
- 10Beauty, Inc. holds a **registered Australian trade mark** (TM 2551881, entered on the Register 18 Aug 2025) covering its manicure device and polish pods. No partner, launch or local activity is attached to it.
- **Nimble's 2021 Kickstarter** had backers in more than 25 countries and promised worldwide shipping. Whether any units reached Australian backers, or whether Nimble ships to Australia now, could not be verified. This is the main way the verdict could become PARTIAL.

Method caveats:
- WebFetch is unavailable, so every finding comes from search-result extracts, which are machine-summarised and may misattribute.
- The search tool is US-centric.
- Two domain-restricted searches of Australian trade, tech and NZ media returned only USPTO patents, so the filter apparently did not apply. Coverage of Startup Daily, SmartCompany, Inside Retail and similar outlets is therefore **not directly confirmed**. "No result" there is weak evidence, not proof of absence.

## Key Question 1: After resolving the leads, is any robotic/AI nail-painting service or device available in Australia or NZ?

### Takeaway
No. None of the 38 queries found any operating, piloted or announced robotic/AI nail-painting service, or any device sold or shipped into Australia or NZ. Every robotic manicure deployment found is in the US (10Beauty, Clockwork), China (Umia at Disney Shanghai) or Ireland/UK (Umia). Under the decision rules the verdict is **ABSENT**. Two caveats are a watch item and an unresolved PARTIAL risk: 10Beauty's Australian trade mark, and the unverified question of whether Nimble shipped to Australian Kickstarter backers.

### Inferences
- Two independent verifiers found nothing (the first with 40 searches, this one with 38 using different angles and four languages). That makes it very unlikely (my estimate: under 5%) that a commercial robotic manicure **service** is operating or piloted in Australia or NZ as of 13 October 2026.
- The 10BEAUTY Australian registration came through the Madrid route, with a May 2024 priority date and a 1 Nov 2024 renewal anniversary. That is consistent with a broad international filing that designated Australia, not with an imminent local launch. It is still a **competitive-entry risk**: 10Beauty has secured its brand and device and pod classes in Australia and could enter directly or through a partner. An Australian entrant should treat 10Beauty as a likely future competitor, or as a potential partner or licensor.
- Most of the residual PARTIAL risk (about 20%) is Nimble. Backers came from 25+ countries and worldwide shipping was promised, but nothing confirms fulfilment outside the US. Even if a few Kickstarter units reached Australians, that would be a one-off home-device delivery, not an ongoing retail service.
- A further small risk (about 5%) is undocumented: a Chinese-Australian or Vietnamese-Australian salon quietly importing a Chinese AI gel-polish machine, publicised only on Xiaohongshu, WeChat or Instagram, which this tool does not index.
- The likely Australian commercial gatekeepers are the services-heavy beauty retailers (Mecca's Melbourne flagship already gives over a third of its floor to services) and the shopping-centre landlords. None has announced anything.

### Inferences
- The closest real-world substitutes in Australia are staffed express nail bars and nail-art printers. Neither delivers the concept's value proposition: an unattended, AI-vision robot applying polish, priced per service through an app or kiosk, in high-footfall retail sites.
- The most consequential "closest thing" for strategy is 10Beauty's registered Australian mark, not any local operator. It shows the best-funded overseas player has at least taken the low-cost step of protecting its brand and its device and pod goods in Australia. A local first mover could face it later, or could approach it as a distributor or licensee.
- An entrant could credibly claim to be first in Australia for in-store robotic manicures, subject to the Nimble caveat for home devices. It should avoid naming that conflicts with the registered 10BEAUTY mark.
