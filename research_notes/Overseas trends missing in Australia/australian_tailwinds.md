## Q1. What are the most significant new Australian policy or market shifts of 2024–2027 that create demand for new kinds of businesses?

### Takeaway
The shifts that most clearly create *new* demand, as opposed to compliance work for incumbents, are:
- the under-16 social media minimum age (live 10 Dec 2025);
- the AI data-centre investment wave, which coincides with councils taking facilities off gas;
- the New Vehicle Efficiency Standard (2025) plus the first cohort of FBT-exempt novated-lease EVs reaching lease end (2025–27);
- the home-insurance affordability crisis in flood, cyclone and hail zones;
- the energy package (Cheaper Home Batteries from 1 Jul 2025; Solar Sharer from 1 Jul 2026);
- the aged-care overhaul (Aged Care Act 2024 and Support at Home, 1 Nov 2025).

Many other shifts in the brief are already absorbed by Australian incumbents or by excluded categories: payroll/super, batteries/VPPs, age assurance, NDIS and aged-care marketplaces.

#### Candidate 1 — Screen-free kids' home phone network ("landline for kids"; Tin Can model)

**(1) Australian shift.**
- The under-16 social media minimum age has been live since 10 Dec 2025. [UNVERIFIED, conf. H]
- It sits on top of public-school phone bans in every state and territory [UNVERIFIED, conf. M] and parent campaigns to delay smartphones ("36 Months", "Let Them Be Kids", Heads Up Alliance). [UNVERIFIED, conf. M]
- Messaging and gaming are exempt, so parents of 7–13-year-olds still face the first-smartphone decision. Delaying it is now a mainstream social norm.

**(2) Concept and business model.**
- **Product:** a simple, screen-free handset (target retail A$99–149) that connects over home Wi-Fi to a cloud VoIP service. Parents approve every contact in an app: classmates, grandparents.
- **Pricing:** calls between devices are free. A subscription (about A$8–15/month) adds calls to mobiles and landlines, voicemail, quiet hours and call logs.
- **Growth loop:** each school class becomes a local network, which lets P&C associations and sports clubs drive bulk adoption.
- **Revenue:** hardware margin plus recurring subscription; later a locked-down "starter phone"/MVNO tier and New Zealand expansion.
- **Australian build requirements:**
  - register as a carriage service provider and join the TIO;
  - comply with the TCP Code;
  - provide 000 access under the Emergency Call Service Determination (more scrutiny after the Sept 2025 Optus Triple Zero outage);
  - ACMA/RCM device labelling;
  - privacy-by-design ahead of the Children's Online Privacy Code (Dec 2026).

#### Candidate 2 — Data-centre waste heat for aquatic centres ("compute-heated pools"; Deep Green model)

**(1) Australian shift.**
- **AI/cloud data-centre boom:**
  - AirTrunk ~A$24b (Sept 2024);
  - AWS A$20b plan for 2025–29;
  - reported OpenAI–NEXTDC Western Sydney MoU (Dec 2025);
  - AEMO forecasts data-centre load roughly tripling by about 2030;
  - grid-connection queues for large sites.
  [UNVERIFIED, conf. H→L as above]
- **Demand side:** councils are moving aquatic and leisure centres off gas (net-zero targets; Victoria's gas transition) while pool heating stays a large operating cost.

**(2) Concept and business model.**
- **Product:** containerised, immersion- or liquid-cooled compute nodes (about 100 kW–1 MW) placed in a council aquatic centre's plant room. Server heat goes through a heat exchanger into pool and shower water, so the host gets heat free or at a deep discount.
- **Revenue:**
  - selling compute, positioned as sovereign onshore capacity for AI inference/fine-tuning, rendering and batch HPC, to enterprises, universities and government;
  - heat-supply or shared-savings contracts with councils;
  - any eligible energy-efficiency certificates.
- **Grid advantage:** nodes use the site's existing connection, avoiding multi-year big-data-centre connection queues.
- **Startup role:** site origination, engineering integration, workload-orchestration software and the financing structure. GPUs are funded via asset finance or infrastructure partners; seed money funds the first pilot site.

## Q3. Which shifts are so recent that the market has not yet responded?

### Takeaway
The least-served recent shifts are:
- the **under-16 social media minimum age** (Dec 2025): no known Australian kids-landline or "starter-network" provider;
- the **AI data-centre wave**: nobody known to be monetising its waste heat;
- the **used-EV wave** from NVES and FBT-exempt lease returns: battery-health tooling unclear;
- **insurance affordability**: no known household or SME parametric gap cover.

Solar Sharer, Payday Super, Cheaper Home Batteries and the 3-Day Guarantee are recent but were absorbed quickly by incumbents.
