## Q1. Which US consumer and local-services companies or categories grew fastest in 2024–2026?

### Takeaway
Two of the checked models have strong, dated 2024–2026 growth evidence and no Australian presence found so far:
- **AI-first private schools ("2-hour learning")**: Alpha School plans to go from about 12 campuses to about 50 for 2026–27.
- **Vaulted or tokenised collectibles marketplaces**: Courtyard went from about US$50k a month of sales in January 2024 to about US$50M a month by July 2025.

Live shopping (Whatnot) and kid-safe phones (Pinwheel) also grew fast, but both are already in Australia. Dog-park bars and peer-to-peer laundry show only mixed or weak growth evidence.

### Inferences
- **Pattern.** In this sample, three US consumer leaders running app or marketplace models (Whatnot, Pinwheel, Sniffspot) had already entered Australia. Australia is English-speaking and high-income, so it is an early export market. A purely digital model gives a local founder only a short window. Models that need local physical operations or local regulatory approval are more defensible.
- **Why each model may still be absent.** These are hypotheses, not verified:
  - **AI-first schools** need state school registration (or a home-education workaround), physical campuses, and parents willing to accept AI-led instruction. Alpha's own US expansion is slowed by real estate ([Axios](https://axios.com/2026/08/02/alpha-schools-ai-expansion-50-campuses)), so a near-term Australian entry looks unlikely.
  - **Vaulted collectibles** combine paid random "packs", tokenisation and buyback. That likely sits close to Australian gambling and loot-box rules and digital-asset/AML rules (model knowledge; verify). Both could deter local founders. A US leader can still serve Australia online, so "absence" may be fragile.
  - **Dog-park bars** compete with free council off-leash areas and dog-friendly pubs and cafés, which are plentiful in Australia (model knowledge), so customers may be unwilling to pay.
  - **Peer-to-peer laundry** may be absent simply because the model is weakly validated even in the US.

## Q3. Which models would be hard for the US leader to simply export, so a local founder could own the category? Ranked candidates

1. **AI-first private microschools** (best-evidenced).
2. **An Australian collectibles vault and instant-liquidity marketplace** (strong US evidence, but Australian absence is fragile).
3. **Provisional:** a home-chef meal marketplace or youth-sports streaming. Both US growth and Australian absence are unverified for these.

Everything ranked third or lower needs fresh verification of both US growth and Australian absence before it goes into a final report.
