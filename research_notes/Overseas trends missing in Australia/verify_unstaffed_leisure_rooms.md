# Verification: Unstaffed, app-booked 24/7 leisure rooms in Australia (billiards, mahjong, board-game, karaoke, tea rooms)

**Verdict: PARTIAL (borderline ABSENT). Confidence: 60%.** Checked 13 October 2026 with 37 WebSearch queries in English, Chinese and Korean, 33 of them presence checks. One WebFetch attempt failed with a DNS error.

- **Not found in Australia:** any unstaffed, app/QR-booked venue run by smart locks or IoT, in any of the five room types (billiards, mahjong, board-game, karaoke, tea).
- **Not found either:** any Australian site, franchise or announced plan from 98 Billiards (Auckland) or from a Chinese unmanned-venue brand.
- **Why PARTIAL, not ABSENT:** self-operated, coin-operated karaoke booths already trade in Melbourne's CBD (Kono Coin Karaoke; Kpop Coin Karaoke). A new entrant could not claim to be Australia's first "self-service karaoke booth" venue.
- **Narrower concept:** on web-indexed evidence, an entrant could still claim to be first with *unstaffed, app/QR-booked* billiards, mahjong or board-game rooms. For that narrower concept the verdict would be ABSENT, at about 65% confidence.
- **Main residual risk:** Chinese-diaspora venues promoted only on Xiaohongshu, WeChat or Dianping, which the search index does not reach.

## Q1. Is any unstaffed, app/QR-booked billiards, mahjong, board-game, karaoke or tea-room venue operating in Australia today?

### Takeaway
None found.

### Inferences
- **Fastest incumbents to convert.** HeyCan already serves Chinese-Australian heyball players, bills by the minute and uses automated mahjong tables, so it is best placed to switch to an unstaffed model. Coin-karaoke operators already run self-operated booths.
- **Where the gap is.** It is not the room categories, which staffed venues already serve. The open space is the operating model: unstaffed, booked and paid by app or QR, smart lock plus IoT control of tables and rooms, open 24/7, and many small low-rent sites.

### Gaps
- Staffing and app use at Kono, Kpop Coin Karaoke and HeyCan are unconfirmed (see Q1 unresolved leads).
- Most entity dates come from listings, and several may be stale; for example, the 2013 City Heroes approval and the 2022 小雀馆 post.

## Q3. Which Australian facts matter for launch (gambling, liquor, council/fire, insurance, demand)?

### Takeaway
- **Demand:** the core community is large; Mandarin is Australia's most common home language other than English.
- **Mahjong:** the main regulatory risk. NSW bans named Chinese games of chance and "any similar game of chance", with heavy penalties for running unlawful gambling.
- **Council approval:** NSW councils approve 24/7 unstaffed venues (gyms) with trial periods and plans of management.
- **Liquor:** venues need a licence to serve alcohol.
- **Not found:** rules on fire safety and insurance for unstaffed rooms.

### Cited Findings
#### Five key Australian facts (demand and regulation)
1. **Demand (Census 2021).**
   - Mandarin is the most common language other than English spoken at home: 685,274 people (2.7%), up almost 90,000 since 2016. Cantonese is spoken by a further 295,281.
   - Chinese is the fifth most reported ancestry, at 5.5%.
   - Sources: [SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/chinese/english/mandarin-remains-as-most-spoken-language-other-than-english-in-australia-2021-census-data-shows); [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/2021-census-nearly-half-australians-have-parent-born-overseas)
2. **Mahjong and gambling law.**
   - NSW's Unlawful Gambling Act 1998 lists unlawful games including fan-tan, pak-a-pu, two-up, hazard, baccarat, faro and roulette, plus "any similar game of chance". Mahjong is not named.
   - Organising or taking part in unlawful gambling: penalties are cited as up to $5,500 and 12 months' jail.
   - Conducting an unlawful gambling operation under the Crimes Act: up to $110,000 and/or 7 years.
   - NSW sources: [Sydney Criminal Lawyers](https://www.sydneycriminallawyers.com.au/?p=6045); [Sydney Criminal Lawyers](https://www.sydneycriminallawyers.com.au/?p=64266)
   - Victoria regulates gambling under the Gambling Regulation Act 2003, with the Victorian Gambling and Casino Control Commission (VGCCC) as licensing and compliance authority. — [Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/guides/chapter/australia-gambling-law/); [Senet Legal (Legal 500 chapter PDF)](https://senetlegal.com/content/insights/the-legal-500-australian-gambling-laws-and-regulat-194ebbe9.pdf?export-pdf)
3. **Mahjong for money is an established practice.** A Macquarie University study measured how common mahjong gambling is in Sydney's Chinese community; the search extract dates it to 2008. — [Macquarie University](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/mahjong-gambling-in-sydney-a-prevalence-study-in-an-australian-ch/)
4. **Council rules for unstaffed 24/7 venues (gym precedent).**
   - North Sydney Council made a Crows Nest 24/7 gym subject to a 12-month trial period by condition (2022).
   - It earlier moved a Neutral Bay gym from trial to permanent 24-hour hours under an approved plan of management (2019). — [North Sydney Council](https://www.northsydney.nsw.gov.au/ecm/download/document-10250715); [North Sydney Council](https://www.northsydney.nsw.gov.au/ecm/download/document-10090849)
   - Camden's planning panel required an Operational Plan of Management before an Occupation Certificate for a 24/7 gym (Nov 2024). — [Camden LPP minutes](https://www.camden.nsw.gov.au/assets/Uploads/Camden-Local-Planning-Panel-Minutes-19-November-2024.pdf)
   - A Ryde case (2018) involved noise and vibration complaints from residents living above a 24/7 gym. — [City of Ryde LPP agenda](https://www.ryde.nsw.gov.au/files/assets/public/committees/lpp/local-planning-panel-13-dec-2018-agenda.pdf)
5. **Liquor.**
   - HeyCan (Canberra) sold only soft drinks and snacks while its liquor licence application was in progress (late 2024). — [Region Canberra](https://region.com.au/inside-canberras-only-dedicated-pool-and-billiard-club/823958)

### Inferences
- **Mahjong is the riskiest category.** Hourly room fees for groups who then play for stakes could draw unlawful-gambling or gaming-house scrutiny, especially in NSW given its list of Chinese games. Mahjong rooms need a no-stakes policy, terms of use and legal advice before launch. Billiards, karaoke and board-game rooms carry less gambling risk.
- **Alcohol.** An unstaffed model would most likely have to be alcohol-free, because a liquor licence brings supervision obligations. RSA rules were not researched, so this is unverified.
- **Council approval.** The gym precedent points to approval on a trial-period basis with a plan of management covering CCTV, duress and remote monitoring, and noise. Residential neighbours are the main source of objections.

### Gaps
- International student numbers were not gathered because of the search budget; they would strengthen the demand case.
- No fire-safety or Building Code requirements for unattended assembly-type rooms were found.
- No insurance terms for unstaffed leisure venues were found.
- No regulator guidance (Liquor & Gaming NSW, VGCCC) on mahjong specifically was found. Whether hourly-hire mahjong rooms are lawful in NSW or Victoria is unresolved and needs legal advice.
- No Victorian council conditions for 24/7 unstaffed venues were retrieved.
