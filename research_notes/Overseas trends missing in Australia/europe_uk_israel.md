#### Candidate 1: Staffless or hybrid 24/7 micro-supermarkets ("smart village stores")

**(1) Concept and how it makes money**
- **Format:** a 50–150 m² shop or prefabricated "box" stocking about 800–1,500 everyday items (groceries, fresh food, household goods, regional produce), open 24/7.
- **Access and checkout:** customers enter with an app, bank card or ID card and pay at self-checkouts. Cameras and sensors deter theft.
- **Staffing:** staff attend only a few hours a day to restock, manage fresh food and help customers. This is the "hybrid" model.
- **Variants:**
  - corporate chain (tegut's teo);
  - community co-owned (Tante Enso: residents buy shares and a store opens once enough pledges come in);
  - fully autonomous (Żabka Nano).
- **Revenue:** grocery gross margin, plus ancillary fees (parcel pick-up, container returns, ticketing), listing fees from local producers, in-store retail-media screens, and possibly licensing the operating platform to councils, co-ops or property developers.

#### Candidate 2: Salary-sacrifice employee bike and e-bike leasing (the JobRad / Cycle-to-Work model)

**(1) Concept and how it makes money**
- **How it works:**
  - An employer signs a free or low-cost framework agreement with a leasing platform.
  - Employees choose a bike or e-bike (plus gear) at partner dealers.
  - A 36-month lease is paid from gross salary, with insurance, servicing and breakdown cover bundled in.
  - At the end the employee can buy the bike at its residual value. Returned bikes are refurbished and resold, which links to Candidate 4.
- **Platform revenue:** lease or financing margin, insurance and service commissions, dealer commissions, residual-value sales, and sometimes employer fees.
- **Precondition:** the model works only where tax rules make a salary-sacrificed bike cheaper than one bought from post-tax income.

**(2) Overseas exemplars [UNVERIFIED]**
- **Germany:** JobRad (Freiburg; founded 2008), the market leader, also active in Austria. Other providers are Bikeleasing-Service, Business Bike, Lease a Bike (Pon Holdings, also in the Netherlands, Belgium, Austria and other markets), Deutsche Dienstrad and mein-dienstrad.de.
- **UK:** Cyclescheme, Cycle Solutions, Bike2Work Scheme, Green Commute Initiative, Halfords Cycle2Work.
- **Ireland:** Bike to Work scheme providers.
- **Belgium:** Cyclis and Lease a Bike.
- **France:** Zenride and others.
- **Tax bases** (as recalled in KQ1): Germany's 0.25% monthly rule (to 2030), the UK's 1999 exemption, the Netherlands' 7% addition (2020), Ireland's €1,250/€1,500 caps (2020) and Belgium's exemptions.
- **Verify:** JobRad's 2024–2026 employer count, bike count and revenue; BVZF market size; the UK Cycle to Work Alliance's annual figures.
- **Multi-country signal:** about seven countries, which is very strong.

**(3) Why it is accelerating now (inference)**
- E-bikes cost roughly A$3–8k, so a 20–40% tax saving is meaningful.
- Employers want low-cost perks and commuting-emissions programmes.
- Leasing became a key sales channel during the 2023–25 bike-industry downturn.

  - `JobRad Australia`, `Lease a Bike Australia` and `Cyclescheme Australia`

- **Closest Australian things the model knows of (unverified):**

- Credit and residual-value risk.
- E-bike battery-safety compliance, such as NSW's standards [UNVERIFIED].
