## 1. History and post-mortems of text-a-number concierges (Magic, GoButler, Operator, Facebook M, Fancy Hands, Path Talk, etc.). What changes with 2026 AI agents?

### Takeaway
The 2015–2018 wave proved that "text a number for anything" creates fast, cheap demand (press, virality, hundreds of thousands of beta users), but none of these products survived as a general consumer concierge. Three things killed them: human operator cost on unbounded requests, low automation, and low-value or low-frequency requests that couldn't carry the cost. Survivors narrowed hard: GoButler went to flights only, Magic moved to B2B assistant staffing, and Facebook M kept only automated suggestions. In 2026, AI voice and LLM costs remove most of the operator cost. Narrow scope and reliable supply are still unsolved, and these are the things this product has to get right.

### Cited Findings
**Magic (2015, US)**
- Launched Feb 2015 as an SMS concierge: text a number to request "literally any (legal) item or service", no app, with all fulfilment outsourced (Magic did no deliveries itself). It drew national press within weeks of a Hacker News post — [CNN Money, Feb 2015](https://money.cnn.com/2015/02/25/smallbusiness/magic-startup/); [yespress.io profile (promotional, treat cautiously)](https://yespress.io/magic)
- Unit economics at launch: founder Mike Chen estimated Magic **broke even on 50% of orders, lost money on 25%, and profited on 25%**. TechCrunch noted that taking losses on orders "doesn't scale". Pricing was a per-job quote plus a "small markup for their time", with fees "case by case" — [TechCrunch, "No, Magic Can't Deliver A Tiger", Feb 27 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/02/27/catch-a-tiger-by-the-toe/)
- Raised US$12M at a US$40M valuation led by Sequoia. Added a **US$100/hour tier** for large requests because "multiple people [are] working on behalf of a client", and sometimes waived fees on expensive requests. Fortune described Magic as "more of a middle man" between users and other on-demand services — [Fortune, Jan 5 2016](https://fortune.com/2016/01/05/magic-assistant/)
- Later pivoted to a B2B platform placing full-time and part-time executive, admin, support and bookkeeping assistants, with AI tools on top. A promotional profile claims about 350 employees and about US$128M in revenue (unverified) — [yespress.io](https://yespress.io/magic)

**GoButler (2015–2016, Berlin/US, Rocket Internet-backed)**
- Free SMS concierge. Raised US$8M. At that round it had **100,000+ beta users and over 1 million requests across six countries** — [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/brief/rocket-internet-gobutler-funding); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1189645)
- In March 2016 it dropped the general concierge to become a "24/7 on-demand flight assistant" only. It had failed to establish the service in Germany, and travel had been "a popular segment" of requests from the start — [Deutsche Startups, Mar 21 2016 (German)](https://www.deutsche-startups.de/2016/03/21/gobutler-ist-kein-full-service-butler-mehr/)

**Facebook M (2015–2018)**
- A human-plus-AI concierge inside Messenger. It was only ever available to about **2,000 people**, and the human-assisted service ended **Jan 19, 2018** after about 2.5 years. Facebook called it "an experiment" and gave no detailed reason. Staff were offered other roles — [Engadget, Jan 8 2018](https://www.engadget.com/2018-01-08-facebook-m-ai-assistant-closure.html); [BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/alexkantrowitz/facebook-scraps-plans-for-ai-concierge-in-messenger)
- Commentators reported that M "allegedly never surpassed **30% automation**", so it needed human trainers. Separately, Messenger chatbots generally failed on 70% of requests, which led Facebook to scale them back in early 2017. That 70% figure is not M-specific — [Motley Fool, Jan 2018](https://www.fool.com/investing/2018/01/12/facebook-kills-its-answer-to-siri-and-alexa.aspx); [Fox Business, "Facebook's M Was Always Doomed"](https://foxbusiness.com/markets/facebooks-m-was-always-doomed)
- The surviving piece was automated "M suggestions" in Messenger — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/facebook-m-beta-discontinued/)

**Operator (2015, Garrett Camp / Robin Chan)**
- A concierge commerce app. Users texted requests and a (human) sales rep replied with product options and pictures. It was in testing for over a year before its April 2015 launch — [TechCrunch, Apr 7 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/04/07/operator/); [Engadget, Apr 23 2015](https://www.engadget.com/2015-04-23-operator-shopping-app.html)

**Path Talk (2014–2015)**
- Path launched Path Talk to connect users and businesses. Its "Places" feature (message any local business) was disabled after Path's sale to Kakao in 2015. Path itself closed in October 2018 — [TechCrunch, May 29 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/05/29/path-talk-places-may-life-on-in-a-new-form); [TechCrunch, Sep 17 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/09/17/rip-path/)

**Fancy Hands (2010–present)**
- A human-powered virtual assistant for to-do list tasks. In 2011 the founder said "this is going to be a make-or-break year for us… we're either killing it this time next year or we probably don't exist." Trustpilot reviews show it still operating (undated) — [Fast Company via Fancy Hands press page](https://legacy.fancyhands.com/press/fast-company2); [netted.net, Jan 2011](https://www.netted.net/2011/01/21/hired-help/)

**Later human+AI concierges (2023–2026)**
- **Duckbill** (Boston, CEO Meghan Joyce, ex-Uber/Oscar) pairs AI with human "co-pilots" who make calls and handle scheduling. Requests come by app, text, email or voice memo. It has raised about US$33M (seed + Series A, Sept 2023 round led by Forerunner) — [Forerunner](https://forerunnerventures.substack.com/p/the-cq-delivering-on-one-of-techs); [citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/?p=467208). **What changes in 2026 (cost and capability)**
- All-in AI voice agents now cost about **US$0.07–0.30/min**: Vapi platform fee US$0.05/min (orchestration only), Retell US$0.07–0.31/min, Bland about US$0.09–0.11/min, and Twilio US outbound carrier about US$0.014/min. These are secondary comparison blogs, not vendor pages — [MorphLLM](https://www.morphllm.com/best-ai-voice-agent-platforms); [Techsy](https://techsy.io/en/blog/ai-voice-agent-pricing); [AI Agent Rank](https://aiagentrank.io/blog/vapi-review-2026)
- Big platforms now routinely run AI agents that phone local businesses: Google "Ask for Me" (2025), Gemini "Call for Me" (Sept 2026), and Yelp Host (1M+ calls). See Section 2.

### Inferences
- **Demand was never the problem.** GoButler reached 1M+ requests and 100k+ users from a free SMS number, and Magic went viral off one Hacker News post. "Text a number" is a strong demand-generation hook. Retention and unit economics were the problem.
- **The fatal cost was humans on open-ended requests.** Magic lost money on 25% of orders, M stayed under roughly 30% automated, and GoButler narrowed to the one category users asked for most. In 2026 the work these companies paid humans for (searching, calling around, following up) costs cents to a couple of dollars in AI voice and LLM time (see the Section 5 model).
- **This product's structure is better than 2015's.** The deliverable is "a human provider phones you", so fulfilment (the conversation, quote and job) is done and paid for by the provider, not by the concierge. That is closer to Jim's Group's central-number lead model (Section 3) than to Magic's model of doing the task yourself.
- **What 2026 AI does not fix:** (a) unbounded or weird requests that fall outside the provider network, (b) low purchase frequency for household services, and (c) supply reliability, meaning tradies who don't answer or don't call back (Section 6). The scope has to be designed around these.
- GoButler's pivot is a signal for category choice: start where request logs cluster. For this product that is likely urgent or small home jobs (handyman, locksmith, plumber, electrician) plus quote callbacks (painter, cleaner).

## 2. Current competitors and adjacent players (2026). Where is the open space?

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 the "AI calls local businesses for you" capability is commoditising fast in the US: Google Ask for Me, Gemini Call for Me, Thumbtack inside Claude and ChatGPT, Yelp Assistant, and Angi's homeowner agent. Almost all of it is **US-only**, **web or app-based** (not a saved iMessage contact), and returns **information** (quotes, availability) rather than **a human provider phoning you within minutes**. In Australia, hipages sells shared leads and is pivoting to tradie software, and Airtasker is a post-and-wait bid marketplace that is now inside ChatGPT. The open space is an **Australian, iMessage-native, outcome-guaranteed, urgent/small-job "human callback within X minutes" service**. That window is probably 12–24 months before Google or Airtasker close it.

### Inferences
- **Open space 1 (geography):** Google's consumer calling agents and Thumbtack's in-LLM integrations are US-only as of Oct 2026, and Gemini Call for Me only dials US numbers. Australia has no Google-scale "AI calls tradies for you" product yet. Expect Google to expand eventually, so plan for a 12–24 month window (no source gives a timeline).
- **Open space 2 (outcome):** Google, Gemini and Yelp return a summary or quote. Google's own test found nearly half of businesses won't quote by phone, which supports the founder's premise that the useful output is **a human conversation**, not a price. "A tradie will call you within 30 minutes" is a different, more concrete promise.
- **Open space 3 (interface):** Everyone else lives in apps, web pages or LLM chat UIs. A saved contact in iMessage, with no download, form or account, is distinctive for urgent or casual requests ("by 3pm today"). Poke is general-purpose and not home-services or supply-network focused.
- **Open space 4 (exclusive, not shared):** hipages shares each lead with up to 3 tradies and its volumes are falling. Airtasker is bid-based and asynchronous. A "one tradie, already briefed, calls you now" flow is a better consumer experience. On the supply side it can be sold as an *exclusive, live* lead, which commands a premium over shared leads.
- **Main competitive threat in AU:** Airtasker, since it is already in ChatGPT with an MCP and has the payments and Tasker supply to add "call me" flows. Next is Google expanding Ask for Me / Call for Me to AU. Positioning should therefore rely on things big players won't do quickly: guaranteed callback SLAs, a curated local roster, an iMessage-native experience, and B2B relationships with property and strata managers.
- Do not compete head-on with Tapi for property-management software. Either integrate as a tradie-sourcing provider or target **self-managing landlords, small agencies, strata committees and Airbnb hosts**, who lack a system.

## 3. Marketplace cold-start playbooks and liquidity metrics/thresholds

### Takeaway
The consistent pattern is: **constrain the market (geography, category, time), go supply-first or make supply "virtual", and do it by hand first.** Thumbtack is the important exception: low-frequency home services pushed it to go broad. This product can partly escape cold start because the AI can cold-contact any business on Google Maps, giving it "virtual supply" from day one. Reliability inside a ≤30-minute SLA still needs a curated, pre-committed roster per suburb and category. There are no authoritative numeric expansion thresholds. Use per-cell (suburb × category × hour) scorecards with self-set gates.

### Cited Findings
**General playbooks (Lenny Rachitsky / NFX-style research)**
- In Rachitsky's interviews with operators of 17 marketplaces, **16 of 17 initially constrained** their market. **Thumbtack was the exception** because low-frequency, high-value transactions forced it to "cast a wide net". **14 of 17 went supply-first**, and "almost 80%" focused on supply. These counts come via secondary summaries, so verify them against the originals — [Greylock excerpt](https://greylock.com/blog/lessons-learned-growing-successful-marketplaces-2/); [echai.ventures summary](https://echai.ventures/startingup/validate-idea/can-i-validate-a-marketplace-idea-before-i-have-supply-or-demand)
- "Liquidity is basically all a marketplace should focus on… the marketplace who hits liquidity first typically wins." Supply can drive demand, as when restaurants marketed DoorDash and Caviar — [Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/lessons-learned-growing-successful-marketplaces-2/)
- Of many supply-growth levers, most of each marketplace's gains came from just 2–3 — [Lenny Rachitsky, "28 ways to grow supply" (Andrew Chen repost)](https://andrewchen.com/grow-marketplace-supply/)
- Fill rate ranges from under 5% (e-commerce) to over 80% (bottom-of-funnel), so benchmark against context. Track liquidity per segment and geography, because global averages hide thin local markets. Search-to-fill, time-to-first-booking, repeat rates and category density are the core seed-stage liquidity metrics (low-authority source) — [Lenny's Newsletter, "The most important marketplace metrics"](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-most-important-marketplace-metrics); [Point Nine, "WTF is Marketplace Liquidity?"](https://writing.pointnine.com/p/wtf-is-marketplace-liquidity); [Causo seed benchmarks](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/marketplace-liquidity-seed)

**Thumbtack (US)**
- The founders hand-collected local service pros' email addresses and emailed them to build early supply, reaching "the first 1,000 users". They recruited pros before consumers and launched every category in every US region. Thumbtack began as a bulletin-board-style listing site, then pivoted to instant matching, shutting down "millions in recurring revenue" to do so — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/thumbtack-doing-things-that-dont-scale); [Sharetribe](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/why-thumbtack-succeeded/); [Deal Lift](https://dealift.beehiiv.com/p/from-42-rejections-to-a-3-2b-home-services-powerhouse-how-thumbtack-beat-the-odds)

**Airtasker (Sydney, 2012)**
- Launched v1.0 in Sydney and waited for first tasks (e.g., car washing, cleaning a 2-bedroom apartment) — [Airtasker blog](https://blog-content.airtasker.com/hi-and-welcome-to-airtasker/)
- The founders did tasks themselves, including driving around Sydney at 2am delivering KFC, to learn the pain points — [NAB, "Task masters"](https://business.nab.com.au/task-masters-24975); [Content Sherpa](https://contentsherpa.com.au/turning-small-tasks-big-money/)

Fung later called the response an "embarrassing and costly mistake" — [Pulse 2.0 interview](https://pulse2.com/airtasker-profile-tim-fung-interview/); [Content Sherpa](https://contentsherpa.com.au/turning-small-tasks-big-money/)
- The founders chose a marketplace model over TaskRabbit's employee model for capital efficiency — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/airtasker-profile-tim-fung-interview/)

**Jim's Group (AU): the "central number" precedent**
- Leads come from a **central call centre** (Mooroolbark, Victoria) and are routed to franchisees. Franchisees pay a flat monthly fee (about **A$700–750/month**, varying by division) plus a **per-lead fee of about A$9–18** depending on state. Jim's claims roughly 200k–240k "unserviced" leads a year (claims vary up to "the best part of a million"; self-reported marketing) — [Jim's FAQ](https://jims.net/faq/); [Jim's franchise FAQs](https://jims.net/jims-franchise-faqs-answered/); [How a Jim's franchise works](https://jims.net/?p=277858)

**Supply responsiveness (AU tradies)**
- A founder test of 180 AU tradies called during business hours found **60% unanswered** (vendor, no methodology) — [Product Hunt, AIssie](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/1215531). A vendor blog claims a typical tradie misses **1 in 3** inbound calls and 55% of callers who hit voicemail leave no message — [VOCPhone](https://vocphone.com/blog/missed-calls-costing-tradies-after-hours-fix-australia)
- hipages reports an 84% "connection rate" on its shared leads (third-party summary) — [WebBlend](https://webblend.au/is-hipages-worth-it/)

**DoorDash and Uber**
- No specific sources were retrieved in this session (budget). See Gaps.

### Inferences
- **"Virtual supply" is this product's cold-start edge.** Like Magic outsourcing fulfilment and GoButler routing to existing services, the agent can contact *any* listed business on demand. This gives "single-player mode" for demand: the service works for request #1 without a recruited network. Tradie non-answer rates of 33–60% mean cold calls alone won't hit a 5–30 minute SLA reliably, so blend two kinds of supply: (a) a **pre-committed roster** (tradies who opted in, set hours and categories, and promise to call back within N minutes) and (b) **cold outreach** as overflow. Each responsive cold contact should be converted into a roster member.
- **Avoid Airtasker's mistake.** Don't over-recruit supply ahead of demand. Recruit just enough roster per *cell* (a launch zone × category) to cover the SLA. A starting assumption is 3–5 committed providers per cell.
- **Jim's Group is the closest proven model:** one memorable number, a central dispatcher, and providers paying a small per-lead fee. The AI agent replaces the Mooroolbark call centre. Jim's A$9–18 per lead shows AU tradies accept small per-lead fees when leads are pre-qualified and routed.
- **Constrain by time as well as place and category.** Launch "business hours, weekdays plus Saturday morning" when tradies answer phones. The "by 3pm today" promise is only credible inside staffed hours.
- **Suggested liquidity metrics and gates** (self-set, because no authoritative thresholds exist; measured per suburb-cluster × category × time-band):
  - *In-scope rate* (requests the service is designed for): ≥80%.
  - *Fill rate* (in-scope requests where a real provider speaks to the user within SLA): launch ≥60%; **expansion gate ≥80% for 4 consecutive weeks**.
  - *Time-to-first-call* (request → provider phones user): median ≤15 min, p90 ≤45 min for "now" requests in staffed hours; same-day for "quote" requests.
  - *Provider acceptance rate* (contacted → agrees to call): cold ≥20–25%; roster ≥60%.
  - *Connect rate* (accepted → provider actually reaches user): ≥90% (benchmark: hipages' 84% on shared leads).
  - *Booked-job rate* (connections → job booked or quote visit): ≥40%, as reported by both sides via follow-up text.
  - *User repeat rate*: ≥25% make a 2nd request within 90 days. *User NPS/CSAT* after each call.
  - *Provider retention*: ≥70% of paying roster providers active month-over-month.
  - *Supply density*: ≥3–5 committed roster providers per cell before advertising that cell.
- Expand to an adjacent cell only once the current cell passes the fill-rate and time-to-first-call gates. Expand category before geography where request logs show demand, as GoButler did with flights.

Verify before citing.
- No authoritative published **numeric thresholds** for fill rate or time-to-fill were found. The gates above are recommendations, not benchmarks.

## 4. Demand acquisition and virality for an iMessage number in Australia; realistic CAC

### Takeaway
Evidence on AU-specific CAC was not found. What exists suggests novelty "text-a-number" products get large free press and word-of-mouth bursts (Magic, GoButler), and that AI-search referrals are a rising channel (Airtasker AI-referred traffic +200% YoY). The strongest structural channel for repeat volume in AU is **B2B intermediaries who constantly need tradies**: property managers, strata managers, real-estate agents and Airbnb hosts. Tapi already serves property managers with software, so the gap is self-managers and small agencies. Low-cost local channels (Facebook community groups, letterbox drops plus fridge magnets, contact-card sharing) suit a single-suburb launch. CAC numbers below are labelled assumptions.

### Cited Findings
- Magic got national press within weeks of a single Hacker News post — [yespress.io (promotional)](https://yespress.io/magic); [CNN Money, Feb 2015](https://money.cnn.com/2015/02/25/smallbusiness/magic-startup/)
- GoButler reached 100,000+ beta users and 1M+ requests across six countries as a free SMS concierge — [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/brief/rocket-internet-gobutler-funding)
- Airtasker reports AI-referred traffic up 200%+ YoY and has an MCP/ChatGPT app. Most users still use AI to describe and compare tasks rather than book end-to-end — [Investing.com FY26 transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-airtasker-lifts-fy-2026-growth-shares-jump-on-outlook-93CH-4876175); [iTWire](https://itwire.com/business-it-news/data/airtasker-has-entered-the-chat-ai-meets-real-world-help-with-new-chatgpt-launch)
- Thumbtack, Yelp and Airtasker are all distributing through LLM assistants (Claude, ChatGPT), and Yelp Assistant already drives about 15% of quote requests — [Business Wire (Thumbtack/Claude)](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260423884720/en/Thumbtack-Delivers-Home-Services-Experience-in-Anthropics-Claude); [Yelp Q1 2026 call](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/yelp-inc-nyseyelp-q1-2026-earnings-call-transcript-1758007/)

### Inferences
- **Make the product a contact card, not an app.** The core growth mechanic is "save this contact": a vCard with a name like "Get a Tradie" and a photo, sent after each successful call with "Send this to someone who needs a tradie". Users forwarding it into family and housemate group chats is the virality loop (mechanism inferred; no data found).
- **Hyper-local launch channels** (prioritise free and cheap):
  1. *Local Facebook community and "buy nothing" groups* in the launch suburbs. Post as a resident founder offering free "I'll get a tradie to call you in 30 min" for the first N requests.
  2. *Letterbox drops plus fridge magnets* with the number in a 2–5k home radius, matching Jim's "remember one number" brand logic. Magnets keep the number on hand when the leak happens.
  3. *Supply-driven demand:* give roster tradies cards and magnets to leave at jobs. Tradies benefit because the service sends them exclusive leads.
  4. *B2B intermediaries:* self-managing landlords, small real-estate agencies, strata committees and managers, Airbnb/short-stay hosts and cleaners, and building managers. One account generates recurring requests and fixes the low-frequency problem.
  5. *AI-search and SEO presence:* suburb × category landing pages ("Get a plumber to call you in 30 min — Marrickville"), a Google Business Profile, and an LLM-readable site. Later, an MCP/ChatGPT app so assistants can hand off ("text this number") as Airtasker and Thumbtack do.
  6. *Press and Product Hunt / AU tech media* for a novelty burst. This works (Magic, GoButler) but brings non-local, out-of-scope requests, so do it only once the service can decline gracefully or has wider coverage.
- **CAC estimates** (all [Assumption], not sourced; validate in-market):
  - Facebook groups / word of mouth / contact-card shares: about A$0–5 per activated user (founder time only).
  - Letterbox drop plus magnet: printing and distribution roughly A$0.15–0.60 per household, with an assumed 0.2–1% activation over 6 months. That implies roughly **A$30–150 per activated user**, so test small (2–5k homes).
  - Paid search on "plumber near me"-type keywords: high-intent AU home-services clicks are expensive (unverified, likely several to tens of A$ per click). Expect **A$20–80 per activated request**. Use it only for testing, not core growth.
  - B2B (agency or strata) accounts via founder sales: high time cost, but each account can generate dozens of requests a year, so per-request CAC is likely lowest.
- Retention design matters more than acquisition. Re-engage with seasonal check-ins only with consent (e.g., "gutter clean before storm season?"). Keep messaging consent-based, since Spam Act rules apply to commercial SMS/iMessage (see Section 6).

## 5. Monetization options and unit economics

### Takeaway
Comparable AU/US platforms charge providers roughly A$9–18 per lead (Jim's, routed and franchise-subsidised), about A$100–200 in effective credit cost per shared lead or won job (hipages, per competitor estimates), US$15–85+ per shared lead (Angi), or about 21.7% of GMV (Airtasker, with payments in the flow). Consumers pay about US$30/month for AI call agents (Pine). A simple per-request model shows AI, voice and messaging costs of roughly A$2.5–7 per request, which is very affordable against a provider fee of A$15–35 per *connected* (exclusive, live) call. The cost risk is unfilled requests and founder ops time, not AI. **Recommended:** the provider pays per connected call (first few free), consumers are free at launch, and paid B2B accounts come next. Avoid a GMV take rate and finance/insurance lead-gen early.

Revenue per fulfilled request:
- **Provider pays per connected call:**
  - Base price is A$20, with category bands of A$15 for small handyman jobs and A$35 for urgent plumbing, electrical or locksmith work, and A$40+ for big quote jobs (painting, roofing).
  - Positioning: "This customer is waiting for your call right now, and only you got it." That is more valuable than hipages' shared leads (up to 3 tradies, about A$100–200 effective) and more targeted than Jim's A$9–18 routed leads.
  - Charge only when the provider actually connects with the user, not per contact attempt. Refund for spam or unreachable users.
- **Contribution per fulfilled request:** early ≈ A$20 − A$11 = **≈ A$9 (45%)**; scaled ≈ A$20 − A$2.5 = **≈ A$17.5 (≈87%)**.
- **Break-even for one founder:** A$10k/month contribution ÷ A$17.5 ≈ **570 fulfilled requests/month ≈ 19/day**. Household home-service frequency is assumed (unsourced) at about 1–3 requests per user per year, so a consumer-only path needs roughly 3,000–7,000 active users. A B2B account (agency or strata) at ~5–30 requests/month each would need only about 30–100 accounts. This is the arithmetic case for adding B2B early.

**Options compared:**
1. *Provider per-connected-call fee* (**recommended primary**). It aligns with "a human calls you". It is cheap to bill, either card-on-file or prepaid credits as Jim's and hipages do. It needs no payments in the job flow and avoids disintermediation fights. Risk: tradies dislike paying for leads that don't convert, so price against the "exclusive, live, pre-qualified" value and give 3 free connections to try.
2. *Per-won-job fee or GMV take rate* (Airtasker 21.7%). The upside is higher, but it needs job-value tracking, payments, dispute handling and anti-bypass measures. Revisit in year 2 for high-value quote categories (painting, renovations), possibly as a flat "won job" fee confirmed by the customer.
3. *Provider subscription with priority routing* (hipages-style, A$2,475/yr ARPU). Add once demand per cell is reliable (month 4–6+). Sell "first right to accept in your suburb" and an SLA badge.
4. *Consumer fee.* Keep it free at launch to maximise trial and contact-card sharing. Later options: a "rush" fee (A$5–10 for a ≤15-minute guarantee, refunded if missed) or a household/landlord plan (about A$10–20/month, benchmarked loosely against Pine's US$30/month). Don't build the business on consumer subscriptions, because low frequency undermines them.
5. *B2B accounts* (self-managing landlords, small agencies, strata, short-stay hosts). Charge a monthly fee (assumption: A$49–199/month) plus per-job coordination. These are high-frequency, sticky accounts.
6. *Affiliate/lead-gen in high-value regulated categories* (insurance, finance, solar finance, energy switching). **Avoid**: licensing and the 2026–27 regulatory tightening (Section 6) carry high compliance risk for a solo founder.

## 6. Risks: unbounded requests, abuse, trust and safety, no-shows, platform risk, AU regulation; how to scope for reliability

### Takeaway
The biggest operating risks are supply unreliability (tradies miss many calls) and scope creep into unbounded requests, which killed the 2015 concierges. The biggest external risks are iMessage platform dependence (unofficial gateways vs. an Apple-gated, per-user-fee official channel with one approved agent) and big players entering AU. Regulatory exposure is manageable if the service stays out of finance/insurance lead-gen, keeps provider calls as genuine service inquiries rather than sales pitches, discloses AI, and gets consent before sharing the user's number.

  - One source says Australia has no statutory must-disclose-AI rule, while another claims a June 2026 change that could not be verified — [Waboom](https://www.waboom.ai/blog/australia-dnc-ai-voice-agent-telemarketing-law)
- **Gemini's guardrails** exclude telemarketing, financial transactions and sharing sensitive data (an industry norm signal) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/google-tests-letting-gemini-make-phone-calls-initially-for-us-pixel-owners/)

### Inferences
**Scoping for reliability:**
- Define the product as **"get a local home-service pro to phone you"** in a whitelist of 5–8 categories: handyman/odd jobs, plumber, electrician, locksmith, cleaner, painter, gardener/rubbish removal, and possibly appliance repair.
- Cover a defined zone during defined hours. Everything else gets a fast, polite decline with a suggestion. Do not fulfil shopping, travel, deliveries or bookings, where Magic, GoButler and Operator lost money.
- Separate **"now"** requests (urgent, 5–30 minute SLA, roster only) from **"quote"** requests (same-day callback, roster plus cold outreach).

**Abuse and trust & safety:**
- Risks include prank requests that dispatch tradies to fake addresses, harassment of providers, and users trying to source illegal services.
- Mitigations:
  - Verify the user's phone number via the iMessage handle.
  - Require explicit consent before sharing the user's number with a provider ("OK to give Dave from X Plumbing your number?").
  - Rate-limit per user.
  - Let providers flag bad requests, and refund the provider fee on flagged ones.
  - Block risky categories (weapons, drugs, medical, legal, finance).
  - Keep logs.

**Provider no-shows and missed calls:**
- Dial in parallel or cascade (2–3 roster providers at once, first to accept wins) with SMS plus call.
- Keep the user informed every few minutes ("Called 3 plumbers; Sam accepted, he'll call within 10 min").
- Run an automatic re-route timer if the accepted provider hasn't called within N minutes.
- Use a strike system and SLA-based ranking for roster providers.
- Send a post-call verification text to both sides, which also confirms the "connected" event for billing.

**Telemarketing/DNCR:**
- Calls that **ask a business about a specific customer job** are plausibly not telemarketing (inference, not legal advice; get AU legal review).
- Calls or texts **pitching providers to join or pay** likely are marketing. Keep recruiting pitches out of job calls, wash recruiting lists against the DNCR, respect calling hours, and identify as an AI assistant acting for a named customer, as Pine does.
- Commercial SMS/iMessage to users and providers needs consent and an unsubscribe under the Spam Act.

**Privacy:** Users' numbers and addresses are personal information under the Privacy Act. Share them with the chosen provider only, with consent, and publish a privacy policy. The ACCC lead-gen and data-broker findings make "selling" user data a reputational and soon a legal risk.

**Finance/insurance:** Don't route mortgage, insurance, loans, super or energy comparison requests for referral fees. ASIC treats insurance quoting and comparison as a financial service, and lead-gen is under active ASIC and ACCC scrutiny, with the new unfair-trading provisions from July 2027. If users ask, decline or send them to a non-commissioned public resource.

**Platform:**
- Treat the iMessage gateway as replaceable. Own an AU mobile number with SMS/RCS fallback, so the "saved contact" survives a gateway outage or Apple enforcement.
- Apply for Apple Messages for Business once traction justifies it. Expect per-user fees and human-support requirements, which roster ops already cover.
- Don't depend on a single gateway vendor. Sendblue caps inbound contacts and gates outbound behind Enterprise.

### Gaps
- No **ACMA primary guidance** on AI voice agents was found. DNCR and AI analysis comes from vendor blogs.
- No reporting was found on **Apple enforcement against unofficial iMessage gateways** (Sendblue, Linq, Blooio), so the probability of a crackdown is unknown.
- No data was found on **provider no-show rates** in AU home services.
- **Whether Apple Messages for Business is available to AU businesses for AI agents** on the same terms as Poke was not confirmed.
- **Tradie licensing verification** (state licensing registers for plumbers and electricians) was not researched. Consider it for trust: verify licences for regulated trades.

## 7. Synthesis: phased liquidity plan (week 1 → month 6 → year 2), recommended monetization model, top 5 risks with mitigations

### Takeaway
Launch as a hand-run "Jim's Group central number, but AI": one zone, 5–8 categories, staffed hours, a 30-minute callback promise, and a small pre-committed tradie roster backed by AI cold outreach. Charge tradies per *connected* call after free trials, keep consumers free, and add B2B landlord, agency and strata accounts early to fix low purchase frequency. Gate expansion on per-cell fill rate (≥80%) and time-to-first-call (median ≤15 min). This synthesises findings from Sections 1–6. All thresholds and volumes below are recommendations or assumptions, not sourced benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
- Constrain first and go supply-first (16/17 constrained, 14/17 supply-first; Thumbtack's low-frequency exception) — [Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/lessons-learned-growing-successful-marketplaces-2/); [echai.ventures](https://echai.ventures/startingup/validate-idea/can-i-validate-a-marketplace-idea-before-i-have-supply-or-demand)
- Founder hand-fulfilment and the risk of supply outrunning demand (Airtasker) — [Content Sherpa](https://contentsherpa.com.au/turning-small-tasks-big-money/); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/airtasker-profile-tim-fung-interview/)
- Central-number lead routing at A$9–18 per lead (Jim's) — [Jim's FAQ](https://jims.net/faq/)
- Shared-lead incumbents and pricing (hipages A$139–649/month plans; leads shared with up to 3) — [What's The Damage](https://whatsthedamage.com.au/tradies/hipages-cost/)
- Narrowing to the dominant request category (GoButler → flights) — [Deutsche Startups](https://www.deutsche-startups.de/2016/03/21/gobutler-ist-kein-full-service-butler-mehr/)
- AI voice at US$0.07–0.30/min — [MorphLLM](https://www.morphllm.com/best-ai-voice-agent-platforms)
- Google/Gemini calling agents are US-only as of Sept 2026 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/google-tests-letting-gemini-make-phone-calls-initially-for-us-pixel-owners/)

### Inferences

**Phased liquidity plan**

**Week 1: Wizard of Oz in one cell**
- Pick a 3–5 km launch zone where the founder lives (e.g., a cluster of inner-ring suburbs). Choose 5 categories: handyman/odd jobs (poster hanging, furniture assembly), plumber, electrician, locksmith, cleaner. Hours are Mon–Sat, 7am–6pm.
- Get the number live via an iMessage gateway with SMS fallback. **The founder manually does discovery and calls**, with AI drafting and dialling where ready. The promise: "A real local tradie will call you within 30 minutes, or we'll tell you we couldn't."
- **Recruit 15–25 roster providers** in person and by phone (Thumbtack-style hand recruiting). Pitch: "free, exclusive, live leads for the first month; just commit to call back within 15 minutes when you accept."
- Seed 20–50 users from friends, neighbours and 2–3 local Facebook groups.
- Log everything: request text, category, urgency, time-to-first-call, provider responses and outcome.

**Weeks 2–4: Semi-automate and measure**
- The AI agent handles intake triage, the scope check, a cascade to roster providers by SMS and call, cold outreach overflow, user status updates and follow-up verification. The founder approves edge cases.
- Hit **≥60% fill rate and median time-to-first-call ≤20 min** in-zone. Convert every responsive cold-contacted tradie into the roster.
- Run the first letterbox plus fridge-magnet drop (2–3k homes). Send the contact card after every successful call.
- Start 3–5 conversations with self-managing landlords, a small real-estate agency, a strata manager and short-stay hosts.

**Month 2–3: Monetize supply and widen the zone**
- Turn on the **per-connected-call fee** (A$15–35 by category) after each provider's first 3 free connections. Bill via a stored card or prepaid credits, and refund unreachable or spam connections.
- Add 1–2 adjacent zones and 1–2 categories based on request logs (e.g., painter quotes, gardener, rubbish removal).
- Gate each new cell on ≥3–5 roster providers and ≥60% fill at launch.
- Targets: **≥75% fill, median time-to-first-call ≤15 min, ≥90% connect rate, ≥25% provider acceptance on cold outreach**, and 5–10 fulfilled requests/day.
- Sign 2–5 B2B pilot accounts at free or low cost in exchange for volume and feedback.

**Month 4–6: Prove the repeatable cell and the B2B lane**
- Cover a metro sub-region (e.g., 10–20 suburbs), and expand only cells passing **≥80% fill for 4 consecutive weeks**.
- Launch paid B2B accounts (assumption: A$49–199/month plus per-job fees) and test a provider "priority" subscription for top performers.
- Targets: **~20 fulfilled requests/day (≈600/month, roughly founder break-even per the Section 5 model)**, ≥25% 90-day user repeat (including B2B), ≥70% month-over-month paying-provider retention.
- Publish suburb × category landing pages and an LLM-readable site. Consider PR or Product Hunt once out-of-scope requests can be declined gracefully.

**Months 6–12 (bridge)**
- Fill out the metro (Sydney *or* Melbourne). Extend hours (after-hours locksmith and emergency plumber at premium fees).
- Hire 1–2 part-time ops staff for exceptions.
- Apply to Apple Messages for Business and keep an SMS/RCS fallback.
- Build tradie-side value beyond leads (e.g., an AI "missed-call catcher" that turns a tradie's missed calls into callbacks). This increases supply loyalty and is suggested by the tradie missed-call data.

**Year 2**
- Launch a second and third city (Melbourne/Brisbane) using the cell playbook: recruit roster first, run a city-wide but category-constrained launch, and pass gates before marketing.
- Grow B2B to dozens or hundreds of agency, strata and host accounts. Add a won-job fee or GMV take for high-value quote categories (painting, renovations) only if payments are in-flow.
- Distribute through AI assistants (ChatGPT/Claude app or MCP handing off to "text this number"), as Airtasker and Thumbtack do.
- Watch for Google bringing Ask for Me / Call for Me to AU. The defence is roster SLAs, local density, B2B contracts and iMessage habit.

**Recommended monetization model**
- **Primary:** the provider pays per connected call. The connection is exclusive and live, and the customer is waiting for that call.
  - Pricing: A$15 small jobs, A$20 standard, A$35 urgent or after-hours, A$40+ large quote categories.
  - Terms: first 3 free, no lock-in contract (a deliberate contrast to hipages' 6-to-12-month terms), and automatic refunds for unreachable or spam users.
- **Secondary (month 4+):** B2B accounts for landlords, agencies, strata and short-stay hosts (monthly fee plus per-job fee), and an optional provider priority subscription.
- **Consumers:** free at launch. Later, an optional rush or after-hours fee with a money-back SLA.
- **Avoid early:** GMV take rate (needs payments and dispute handling), consumer subscriptions (low frequency), and finance/insurance/energy referral fees (regulatory risk).
- **Unit economics** (Section 5 model, assumptions): about A$11 cost per fulfilled request early, falling to about A$2.5 at scale, against about A$20 average revenue. That gives roughly 45% contribution early and about 85% at scale. Break-even for one founder is about 570–600 fulfilled requests/month.

**Top 5 risks and mitigations**
1. **Supply unreliability.** Tradies miss calls (vendor data: 33–60% unanswered), accept and then don't call, or no-show, so the SLA promise breaks.
   - Use a pre-committed roster with an explicit callback SLA, and cascade or parallel-dial 2–3 providers.
   - Re-route automatically when a provider goes silent, and send live status texts to the user.
   - Apply strikes and ranking, and only charge on verified connection.
   - Limit "now" requests to staffed hours and roster-covered cells.
2. **Scope creep and unbounded requests** (the Magic, M and GoButler failure mode).
   - Whitelist categories and zones, with an instant polite decline for everything else.
   - Fulfil only "a human provider calls you"; never buy, deliver or book on the user's behalf.
   - Review request logs weekly to add the next category only when it clusters (the GoButler lesson).
3. **Low frequency, weak retention and CAC payback.**
   - Lead with B2B accounts (landlords, agencies, strata, hosts) for recurring volume.
   - Grow through contact-card sharing after every success, plus fridge magnets.
   - Send consent-based seasonal nudges.
   - Keep paid acquisition to tests until cell fill rates are high.
4. **Platform and competitive risk.**
   - Apple: unofficial iMessage gateways could be disrupted, and the official Messages for Business route is gated (one approved agent) with per-user fees. Mitigate with a portable AU number and SMS/RCS fallback, multiple gateway vendors, and an application to Messages for Business once traction exists.
   - Google, Airtasker or hipages could launch "AI calls tradies" in AU. Mitigate by winning on SLA, local roster density, B2B contracts and brand habit, and by moving fast in the window before Google's US-only calling agents arrive.
5. **Regulatory, trust and safety exposure** (DNCR/telemarketing, Spam Act, privacy, AI disclosure, finance/insurance lead-gen rules tightening via the ACCC/ASIC and the 1 July 2027 unfair-trading provisions).
   - Keep job calls as genuine customer-specific inquiries, with recruiting pitches separate and DNCR-washed.
   - The AI identifies itself and the customer it acts for.
   - Get explicit consent before sharing user contact details, and verify user numbers with rate limits.
   - Let providers flag abuse with refunds, and verify licences for regulated trades.
   - Exclude finance, insurance and energy lead-gen.
   - Get a one-off AU legal review before scaling outbound calling.

### Gaps
- All thresholds, prices, volumes and CAC figures in this section are **recommendations or assumptions** derived from the cited benchmarks, not externally validated numbers.
- No AU-specific evidence was found on willingness to pay **per connected call** (vs. per shared lead). Validate with the first 20 roster tradies before fixing prices.
- B2B channel sizing (maintenance jobs per property per year; agency and strata buying behaviour) remains unquantified.
