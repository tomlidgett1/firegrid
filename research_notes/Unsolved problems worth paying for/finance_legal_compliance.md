# Unsolved, high-willingness-to-pay problems in financial services, accounting, tax, insurance, legal, compliance/regtech and government-facing work (as of 18 October 2026)

*How this was researched: about 29 web searches were run before the shared search budget ran out. WebFetch failed on every domain (DNS "ENOTFOUND"), so no page was read in full. Every citation below therefore comes from the search tool's own summary of the cited page. Many sources are vendor blogs or law-firm alerts, and these are flagged as such. Anything from background knowledge that could not be checked in this session is listed under Gaps, not under Cited Findings.*

## Objective: 8–15 candidate problems, each assessed on (a)–(h), and a ranked top 3

### Takeaway
Software built for a deadline is the strongest opportunity in this sector, and it pays most where a regulation newly applies to tens of thousands of small professionals or mid-market firms that have no compliance staff. The best example found is Australia's Tranche 2 AML/CTF regime. It has been in force since 1 July 2026, covers roughly 80–90k newly regulated businesses, and only 6% of accountants said they were "on top of" it. Two more candidates have hard deadlines and immature, fragmented tooling: the EU Pay Transparency Directive (first reports due June 2027 on 2026 pay data) and the UK's Making Tax Digital for Income Tax (MTD ITSA). Several areas look like good opportunities but are now crowded with well-funded AI startups and should be avoided or entered only through a narrow wedge: US tax preparation, accounting-firm "AI agents", plaintiff law, insurance brokerage/COIs, accounts receivable, permitting and SOC 2. Several deadlines that were expected to force spending have receded or disappeared: CMMC Phase 2 (suspended), EU AI Act high-risk rules (delayed), FinCEN's residential real estate rule (vacated) and CFPB Section 1071 (pushed to 2028).

#### C1. Australia Tranche 2 AML/CTF obligations for accountants, conveyancers, lawyers and real estate agents (AU)
- Tranche 2 took effect on 1 July 2026. Lawyers, conveyancers, accountants, real estate agents, property developers, dealers in precious stones and metals, and trust and company service providers are now reporting entities when they provide a "designated service". Coverage depends on the activity, not the job title. Examples are a real estate agency settling a sale, a law firm handling client funds in a transaction, or an accountant forming companies. — [Bradley Bray](https://www.bradleybray.com.au/news-blog/new-anti-money-laundering-laws-from-1-july-2026); [NameScan](https://namescan.io/insights/tranche-2-now-in-force/)
- Core obligations are enrolment, an AML/CTF programme, customer due diligence, sanctions and PEP screening, suspicious matter reports, and seven-year record keeping. AUSTRAC opened enrolment on 31 March 2026, and newly regulated firms had to enrol by 29 July 2026. Providing a designated service without enrolment and a compliant programme is a breach from the first service provided. — [Zyphe](https://www.zyphe.com/resources/news/austrac-tranche-2-aml-reforms-july-2026); [NameScan](https://namescan.io/insights/tranche-2-now-in-force/); [Cleardocs](https://www.cleardocs.com/clearlaw/professional-adviser-related/AML-CTF-reforms-for-Tranche-2-entities-what-accountants-and-lawyers-must-be-ready-for.html)

#### C2. EU Pay Transparency Directive: gap reporting and joint pay assessments for mid-market employers (EU)
- Member States had to transpose the directive by 7 June 2026. As of July 2026 most had not finished, and the Commission has said there will be "no pause, no extension and no carve-out". — [Lewis Silkin, Jul 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/07/01/eu-pay-transparency-directive-2026-employer-compliance); [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/eu-pay-transparency-directive-early-transposition-trends-watch)
- Reporting phases: employers with 250+ workers file their first report on 7 June 2027 and annually after that. Employers with 150–249 workers report every three years from the same date. Employers with 100–149 workers join in 2031. The first cycle covers 2026 pay data. — [Assessio](https://assessio.com/articles/eu-pay-transparency-directive-guide/); [Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2025/12/the-eu-pay-transparency-directive-what-it-means)
- A gap of 5% or more in any worker category that cannot be objectively justified and is not fixed within six months triggers a joint pay assessment with worker representatives. Salary ranges in job ads and workers' rights to pay information apply regardless of headcount. — [Assessio](https://assessio.com/articles/eu-pay-transparency-directive-guide/); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/07/01/eu-pay-transparency-directive-2026-employer-compliance)
- One May 2026 source says Slovakia was the first Member State to fully transpose. — [Remote Work Europe](https://remoteworkeurope.eu/news/2026/eu-pay-transparency-directive-disclosures-2026)
- An HR-focused guide argues employers with 150+ staff need the data infrastructure in place "now, not in 2027". A vendor blog (sota.io) frames this as an HR-software compliance obligation. — [Assessio](https://assessio.com/articles/eu-pay-transparency-directive-guide/); [sota.io (vendor)](https://sota.io/blog/eu-pay-transparency-directive-2023-970-eu-compliance-hr-software-june-2026)

- (a) Who: partners and practice managers at sole and small accounting practices (1–50 staff) doing company or trust formation, trustee or client-money work; small conveyancing firms; independent real estate agencies; and, secondarily, small law firms. None of these have a compliance function. [cited/inference]
- (b) Pain: a one-off build (enrolment, risk assessment, written AML/CTF programme, compliance officer appointment), followed by permanent per-client and per-matter work. Each engagement needs a check on whether it is a designated service, plus customer due diligence, beneficial-ownership checks on trusts and companies, PEP/sanctions screening, ongoing monitoring, SMR decisions and seven-year records. That work happens on every new client and every in-scope matter, so it is daily or weekly. [cited]
- (c) Willingness to pay: the obligation is legally mandated, with A$31.3m maximum civil penalties per contravention, and enforcement has started (s167 notices from 28 Aug 2026). An adviser estimates budgets of A$25–80k to implement plus A$12–40k a year for 20–100 seat firms. Readiness was very low (6% on top, 32% not started), and about half of accounting practices and about 60% of agencies had not enrolled by the July deadline, which leaves a large group of laggards who are now being forced to act. [cited]. No Reddit or forum "I'd pay" quotes were collected (search budget ran out).
- (d) Competitors: First AML, Kyckr and NameScan (screening and identity), Annature (e-signature with an AML module), easyAML, identity vendors (Persona, Zyphe), Legl (UK law-firm compliance, Tranche 2 content), and The Access Group (practice software, ran the survey). [cited]. Why they fall short [inference, no review evidence found]: most are identity/screening point tools. The hard parts for a two-partner practice are working out which of its services are designated services, writing and maintaining a programme that fits the firm, triaging each matter, judging source of funds and wealth, deciding on SMRs, and keeping audit-ready evidence. These sit outside the screening tools and outside practice-management systems (XPM, FYI, LEAP, Smokeball, PEXA [unverified integration targets]).
- (e) Why now: the obligation is new as of 1 July 2026 and enforcement started in August 2026. LLMs make it cheap to generate a tailored programme and risk assessment from a questionnaire, and to classify each engagement against the designated-services table. [inference]. Existing AU KYC/IDV APIs can be bought rather than built. [inference]
- (f) Market size [inference]: about 80–90k new reporting entities [cited]. At A$1.5–6k a year each (A$125–500 a month for small firms), the total is roughly A$120–540M a year. A realistic serviceable segment is accounting practices plus conveyancers plus agencies that actually provide designated services, perhaps 40–60k entities, or about A$60–250M. This market is Australia-only unless extended to NZ or the UK, where these professions are already regulated and incumbents exist [unverified].
- (g) Feasibility for a small team: high. The product is a workflow-plus-content SaaS. Identity and screening can be bought, and there is no licence requirement. Buyers are owner-operators with short sales cycles, reachable through CPA Australia, CA ANZ, IPA, REIA and state law societies [unverified channel names], plus accountant webinars. First paying customers within 3–6 months is plausible because the deadline has already passed and laggards are receiving notices. [inference]
- (h) Risks: (1) the first-wave buyers (March–July 2026) may already have chosen a vendor, and the remaining laggards are price-sensitive. (2) AUSTRAC's free starter kits put a ceiling on willingness to pay for "programme-only" tools [cited that starter kits exist]. (3) Practice-software incumbents can bundle a module (The Access Group and Annature already are) [cited]. (4) Being wrong about a regulatory interpretation carries liability. (5) Storing identity documents makes the company a breach target. (6) The market is a single country. [inference]

**C2. EU Pay Transparency gap reporting and joint pay assessment for mid-market employers with 150–1,000 staff (EU). Rank #2.**
- (a) Who: HR, reward or people-ops leads and CFOs at EU employers with 150–1,000 staff, especially those spread across several countries, who have no compensation analytics team. Small employers are hit by the job-ad pay ranges and information-request obligations. [cited]
- (b) Pain: classify jobs into "work of equal value" categories, compute gaps by category and pay component, justify or fix any gap of 5% or more within six months or run a joint pay assessment with worker representatives, publish pay ranges in every job ad, and answer individual information requests. This runs annually, or every three years for 150–249 staff, with ongoing hiring obligations. [cited]
- (c) Willingness to pay: the deadline is hard (first reports 7 June 2027 on 2026 data, which is already being generated), and the Commission has said "no pause". A joint pay assessment involving worker representatives is costly and reputationally sensitive. [cited]. National penalty levels were not collected (gap).
- (d) Competitors: no named reporting software was found this session (gap). Background knowledge [unverified] suggests specialist tools (e.g. Figures, Syndio, Trusaic, beqom) and HRIS modules (Personio, HiBob, Workday, SAP SuccessFactors). Weaknesses could not be verified from reviews.
- (e) Why now: transposition (from June 2026) is creating 27 different national variants, and most states are late [cited]. That complexity is the moat. LLMs can draft job-architecture and "equal value" classifications from job descriptions, which is the step that otherwise needs consultants. [inference]
- (f) Market size [inference, base count unverified]: if roughly 30–60k EU employers have 150+ staff, at €5–25k a year the total is about €150M–1.5B. Treat this as an order of magnitude only.
- (g) Feasibility: medium. It needs payroll/HRIS integrations and per-country rule packs. An MVP for one large market (e.g. Germany, the Netherlands or Ireland once transposed) plus a CSV-upload path is doable in 6–9 months. [inference]
- (h) Risks: transposition delays could postpone buying, HRIS incumbents could bundle a "good enough" report, sales cycles grow for 500+ employee firms, and getting an equal-value classification wrong in an employment dispute carries legal exposure. [inference]

**C3. Agent-side automation of quarterly MTD ITSA cycles for UK practices with many landlord and sole-trader clients (UK). Rank #3, with a crowding caveat.**
- (a) Who: small UK accounting practices and bookkeepers whose clients are landlords and sole traders, plus self-managing landlords. [cited/inference]
- (b) Pain: four quarterly updates plus a final declaration per client per year, compared with one return before. Each quarter means chasing records, categorising bank data and submitting. More than 864k people signed up in wave 1, and roughly half missed the first update. Waves at £30k (2027) and £20k (2028) will add more taxpayers. [cited]
- (c) Willingness to pay: the mandate is real but the penalty is soft in 2026–27 (no points for late quarterly updates) [cited], so urgency peaks from April 2027 onward. Pressure on accountants' capacity is implied by the missed updates. No fee data was collected (gap).
- (d) Competitors: the space is crowded with HMRC-recognised software (Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent, Sage, TaxCalc, Coconut, Hammock and others [unverified]). The remaining gap is agent-side bulk workflow for messy landlord data. [inference]
- (e) Why now: the mandate is new, UK open banking is mature, and LLMs can categorise bank transactions. [inference]
- (f) Market size [inference]: about 1–2M MTD taxpayers by 2028 at £20–60 a year through agents comes to roughly £20–120M a year, with low price points.
- (g) Feasibility: medium-high. HMRC API recognition is required but routine. [inference]
- (h) Risks: crowding, a price war, HMRC policy changes, and accounting-platform incumbents bundling the workflow. [inference]

### Inferences
- Funding has concentrated on US, high-volume, horizontal workflows (tax returns, PI demand letters, broker submissions, AR). The gaps sit in non-US, regulation-specific, fragmented niches (AU AML, EU pay transparency, FR e-invoicing for accounting firms), which large funds find too small or too local. A 1–5 person team can win there.
- Entry into a crowded category needs a jurisdictional wedge (e.g. AU/UK/CA tax and bookkeeping, where US-centric tools like Accrual and Juno do not apply) and its own evidence. That was not gathered here.
