# Verification: AI/software for US Medicaid long-term-care (LTC) eligibility applications (as of October 2026)

*Method note: I used 35 web searches, the full budget. WebFetch failed on every attempt (DNS errors; the proxy returned 403 on CONNECT), so every citation below comes from search-result summaries, not full-page reads. Treat exact figures as "reported by the cited page via search summary". Items marked **[BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED]** are from my prior knowledge and were not confirmed in this session. This file builds on `healthcare_life_sciences.md` (C1) and `consumer_life_admin.md` (C2) and does not repeat their findings: the 5-year lookback, the $2,000 asset limit, NavigAid's 6-state scope, the $6,000 NJ forum anecdote, and the Precision Medicaid marketing page.*

*Overall verdict (detail under the Kill Criteria question): **the opportunity survives, but it is downgraded from "open field" to "contested but real".** The pain is confirmed and gets sharper on 1 January 2027, when retroactive coverage for nursing-home applicants drops from 3 months to 2. But the earlier notes missed several direct software competitors: Aidace, RevSuite Insight, SNF Metrics, and PointClickCare's own RCM service. The most common incumbent model is also a "free to the facility, family pays about $3,000" outsourced vendor. The only defensible slot is agentic, done-for-you application preparation and caseworker follow-up, sold to facilities per application, in one or two states. A "Medicaid pending tracker" SaaS is already taken.*

## 1. Competitor landscape: who already does LTC Medicaid applications, and how they charge

### Takeaway
The market is more crowded than the earlier notes said. There are three layers: (a) many small human outsourcing firms that partner with facilities and often bill the **family** about $3,000 per case; (b) a 2024–2026 wave of SNF business-office software that already tracks "Medicaid pending" cases (Aidace, RevSuite, SNF Metrics), plus PointClickCare's RCM service and ExaCare ($30M Series A) next door; (c) thin family- and attorney-facing tools (MedicaidSoft, NavigAid, ElderDocx document assembly). I found no funded AI startup (YC or otherwise) that does end-to-end preparation, submission and caseworker follow-up of LTC Medicaid applications. That gap is the remaining opening.

### Inferences
- The earlier statement "I found no strong, multi-state software for this" (healthcare C1) is **wrong for case tracking**. At least four tools (Aidace, RevSuite, SNF Metrics, PointClickCare RCM) already sell "Medicaid pending" workflow to SNF business offices, and two are on the MatrixCare marketplace. The statement still roughly holds for **doing the work**: no product found reads 60 months of statements, flags transfers, fills state forms, and answers caseworker requests for information (RFIs) on its own.
- The dominant incumbent economics are odd. Many outsourced vendors are **free to the facility and billed to the family** (about $2,950–$3,000). That gives facilities a "free" alternative to any paid product. It also creates a regulatory and reputational target (TN AG versus MDR; SCA lawsuit; the conflict-of-interest critique). A facility-paid, outcome-priced product that never bills families is a clean way to position against it.
- ExaCare is the most dangerous adjacent player. It already sits at admissions (where Medicaid-pending risk is decided), has $30M, and runs about 1,500–2,000 facilities. Adding an "application agent" would be a natural extension. PointClickCare could do the same through its RCM service.
- Traction for MedicaidSoft (one employee) and the absence of a YC entrant suggest that pure software for attorneys or families has not broken out. That reads either as an open lane or as weak demand; this evidence cannot tell which.

### Gaps
- No public pricing for any vendor (Precision Medicaid, MDR, Medicaid Success, Aidace, RevSuite, Benefits Fast, PointClickCare RCM). Search turned up no RFPs or published fee schedules.
- No funding or customer counts for Aidace, RevSuite or Benefits Fast. No independent reviews (G2, Capterra) found for any of them.
- I could not query the YC directory directly (the site: operator failed). "No YC company found" is search-based, not exhaustive.
- Lawgical was not found. NavigAid's 2026 status and pricing were not re-checked.

## 2. Market size: applications per year, facilities, pending days and dollars, denial rates

### Takeaway
There is no national count of LTC Medicaid applications. A bottom-up estimate gives about 300,000–400,000 new nursing-facility LTC Medicaid applications a year, plus annual renewals, across about 14,700 nursing facilities (inference). Pending receivables were about $455k per facility in a 2010 New York survey, and spend-down to Medicaid is common: 16.4% of non-Medicaid admissions, rising to 62% of private-pay residents after four years. Denial data is sparse and old: asset-transfer denials averaged 7% in New York, ranging from under 1% to nearly half by county.

### Cited Findings
- **Nursing facilities: 14,742** certified nationwide (KFF 2025 state indicator; Texas and Ohio among the largest) — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/other/state-indicator/number-of-nursing-facilities/). About **1.2 million** residents in 2025 — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1168843/number-residents-certified-nursing-facilities-state). Medicaid covers **more than 60%** of the over 1 million nursing-home residents — [KFF, 5 Key Facts](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/5-key-facts-about-nursing-facilities-and-medicaid/)
- **Assisted living**: 32,231 assisted-living and other residential care communities (NCHS, year not stated) — [A Place for Mom](https://aplaceformom.com/senior-living-data/articles/assisted-living-statistics); NCAL says "more than 30,000" communities serving nearly 1 million residents — [AHCA/NCAL](https://ahcancal.org/News-and-Communications/Press-Releases/Pages/Just-The-Facts-Assisted-Living-Is-A-Safe,-High-Quality-Long-Term-Care-Option-For-America’s-Seniors.aspx). About **18%** of AL residents receive Medicaid for services, and 61% of AL communities were Medicaid-certified in 2025 (attributed to NCAL; original not confirmed) — [NIC](https://www.nic.org/blog/assisted-living-medicaid-waivers-expand-access-and-choice/). As of March 2025, 44 states' Medicaid programs covered AL services (29 through HCBS waivers); federal Medicaid AL spending was at least $3.5B in 2024 — [GAO-26-107884](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107884/index.html); [LeadingAge summary](https://leadingage.org/gao-releases-report-on-assisted-living-spending-and-coverage/)
- **Spend-down volume**: a JAMA Network Open cohort (191,416 traditional-Medicare first-time nursing-home entrants in 2018, reported Dec 2025) found **16.4%** of those entering without Medicaid spent down and enrolled during the stay, typically after about **6 months**. After four years, **61.8%** of residents who began as private pay had moved to Medicaid — [Skilled Nursing News, Dec 2025](https://skillednursingnews.com/2025/12/nearly-1-in-6-nursing-home-residents-spend-down-savings-to-qualify-for-medicaid/). Older Connecticut data: spend-down residents were about a quarter of the population but 52% of resident days, with a mean stay of 4.6 years versus 1.2 years for private pay — [CT OPM Spend-Down Study](https://portal.ct.gov/OPM/PDPD-HHS-Long-Term-Care/Researcher/Partnership-Researcher-Spend-Down-Study)
- **Pending dollars per facility (old data)**: a LeadingAge NY/NYSHFA survey (data as of Feb 28, **2010**) found **$85M** in Medicaid-pending receivables across 188 nursing homes, an average of **$455,000 per facility** (17% of total receivables), versus $467,000 (20%) in the 2009 survey. It cited wide variation in processing time across county social-services districts — [LeadingAge NY](https://leadingageny.org/providers/nursing-homes/reimbursement/medicaid-eligibility-determinations3). No later survey was found.
- **Collection days**: Maryland SNF collection periods averaged **56.65 days in 2022**, up from 48.02 in 2018, with a Medicaid mix of about 65.5% (all payers, not pending-only) — [GMA CPA](https://gma-cpa.com/blog/how-long-term-care-facilities-can-better-manage-receivables-and-collections)
- **Time to file**: critics of Florida's retroactive-coverage waiver said that for nursing-home residents, filing a Medicaid application "takes an average of 71 days" (2018 claim) — [Georgetown CCF](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2018/06/18/doctors-patients-and-many-others-voice-strong-objections-to-floridas-plan-to-end-retroactive-medicaid-coverage/)
- **Agency backlogs**: Illinois providers sued over a backlog of about **7,000** pending applications, some delayed more than a year. The suits blame in part the state's failure to use an electronic verification system (article date not shown; probably around 2018) — [Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/illinois-medicaid-backlog-spawns-multiple-lawsuits-behalf-providers-beneficiaries). Illinois publishes quarterly backlog reports (2024) — [IL HFS Q2 2024](https://hfs.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/hfs/sitecollectiondocuments/Q22024backlogreport08262024.pdf). New Hampshire LTC applicants wait months. DHHS blamed staffing and application complexity and hired an **outside contractor** to process applications, cutting the backlog 20% (Keene Sentinel series, after July 2025) — [Keene Sentinel](https://www.keenesentinel.com/eedition/keene-sentinel/edition_c38887bc-1867-5a6b-a6ce-46707fd16747.html)
- **Denials (old data)**: a 2009 Rockefeller Institute study of New York counties (excluding NYC) found asset transfers caused denial of an average **7%** of applications over 10 years. Some counties denied close to half of the applications examined and others under 1%, possibly reflecting how counties interpret the rules — [Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/study-ny-counties-vary-nursing-home-medicaid-denials); [NY Health Foundation](https://nyhealthfoundation.org/print-resource/?print=8500)
- **Common friction points (anecdotes and hearing decisions)**: requests for information that grow from 2 months of statements to several years plus receipts for expenses over $200; a bank fraud department freezing statement requests during a MassHealth case; an application left unreviewed for months after a NJ caseworker died; families asked for 5 years of statements (3 in another case) — [caregivers.com thread](https://portal.prod.caregivers.com/questions/medicaid-what-happens-next-471681.htm); [MassHealth appeal](https://www.mass.gov/doc/appeal-2301909/download); [NJ DMAHS decision](https://www.nj.gov/humanservices/dmahs/info/decisions/2021/EWvDMAHS_Union.pdf). A nursing home refused "Medicaid pending" status when the resident's account was barely over $2k and demanded full payment — [caregivers.com](https://portal.prod.caregivers.com/questions/check-account-balance-barely-over-2k-nh-refusing-to-put-into-medicaid-pending-demanding-full-payment-491489.htm)

### Inferences
- **Applications per year (bottom-up, inference)**: about 1.2M residents × more than 60% Medicaid gives roughly 700k+ Medicaid nursing-home residents. With typical Medicaid-resident turnover (my assumption: 40–50% a year, given multi-year stays and high mortality), that implies about **300k–400k new nursing-facility LTC Medicaid applications a year**, or **about 20–25 per facility a year** (an average facility has about 81 residents and about 49 on Medicaid). Each Medicaid resident also needs an annual renewal (about 700k a year). AL and HCBS waiver applications come on top. This is consistent with the earlier guess of about 500k all-setting applications but is **not a cited figure**.
- **Pending AR scale (inference)**: the 2010 NY figure ($455k per facility) is the only facility-level datapoint. Scaling it nationally (about 14.7k × roughly $300k–$450k) suggests **about $4–7B** tied up in pending receivables at any time. That is illustrative only; NY has high rates and county-level processing.
- The JAMA finding that spend-down typically happens about 6 months after admission means most applications are **predictable conversions** of current private-pay or Medicare residents. An agent can start the file months early. This is the core operational wedge.
- Assisted living is a weaker first buyer. Only about 18% of residents use Medicaid, waivers are capped or waitlisted in many states (**[BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED]**), and Medicaid pays for services only, not room and board.

### Gaps
- No national or state-level count of LTC Medicaid applications, approvals or denials. No current (post-2015) denial-rate data, and no breakdown of denial reasons (procedural "failure to verify" versus substantive asset or transfer denials).
- No current pending-days or pending-dollar benchmark (AHCA, LeadingAge national or cost reports). The NY survey is 16 years old.
- The Florida retroactive-waiver status (whether nursing-home applicants have had no retro coverage since about 2019) was not confirmed. If confirmed, Florida facilities would be a natural "already living with the problem" test market.

## 3. Regulatory status as of October 2026: what OBBBA (H.R. 1, Public Law 119-21) changed for LTC Medicaid

### Takeaway
Confirmed: **Section 71112 cuts retroactive coverage from 3 months to 2 months for non-expansion applicants, which includes nursing-home applicants, and to 1 month for expansion adults, for applications made on or after 1 January 2027.** A **$1,000,000 home-equity cap, not indexed to inflation**, applies to LTSS from 1 January 2028 (non-agricultural homes). Parts of the 2024 eligibility-streamlining rule are frozen until 1 October 2034. Searches found **no change to the 5-year lookback or the asset limits**. Net effect: a facility's cushion for late or refiled applications shrinks by a third about 10 weeks from now. That is a real, dated urgency trigger, though modest per case.

### Cited Findings
- **Retroactive coverage**: California DHCS letter ACWDL 26-07 says Section 71112 limits retroactive Medi-Cal to **1 month for the MAGI new-adult group and 2 months for other groups**, for applications dated **on or after January 1, 2027** — [CA DHCS ACWDL 26-07](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/es/services/medi-cal/eligibility/letters/Documents/ACWDL-26-07.pdf); also [Santa Clara County Medi-Cal Update 2026-4](https://stgenssa.sccgov.org/debs/program_handbooks/medi-cal/assets/00Updates/Updates2026/Medi-Cal_Update_2026-4.htm). Washington confirms the same 1-month and 2-month limits — [WA HCA fact sheet](https://www.hca.wa.gov/assets/free-or-low-cost/hr1-retro-fact-sheet.pdf); [Justice in Aging](https://justiceinaging.org/hr1-reduces-medicaid-retroactive-eligibility-starting-in-2027/). Nursing-home applicants fall in the 2-month (non-expansion) group — [Hall Booth Smith](https://hallboothsmith.com/medicaid-reforms-big-beautiful-bill/); [Skilled Nursing News, Jul 2025](https://skillednursingnews.com/2025/07/bad-situation-only-made-worse-sen-sanders-leadingage-and-ag-rhodes-explain-obbba-impact-on-nursing-homes/)
- The current federal baseline is 3 months (42 CFR 435.915) — [Brevy](https://brevy.com/medicaid/retroactive-eligibility)
- **State variation in timing**: Minnesota says it will keep 3 months through 2027 and apply the reduced periods from **January 1, 2028** — [MN DHS](https://mn.gov/dhs/federalchanges/retroactive-coverage/index.jsp). (How a state can defer a federal statutory change is not explained in the search result.)
- The House-passed version would have cut retroactive coverage to 30 days from Dec 31, 2026. LeadingAge warned the cut "would force providers to make more cautious admission decisions" — [LeadingAge, Jun 2025](https://leadingage.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/OBBB-Medicaid-Retroactive-Eligibility-June-2025-1.pdf). The enacted law is 2 months for this group.
- **Home equity**: from **January 1, 2028**, Medicaid may not cover LTSS for anyone with more than **$1,000,000** in home equity (non-agricultural homes). The cap is **not inflation-indexed** — [Justice in Aging](https://justiceinaging.org/hr1-imposes-new-limit-on-home-equity-for-medicaid-ltss-effective-2028/); [ElderLawAnswers](https://attorney.elderlawanswers.com/newsletter/link-to/52212/article/13153); [24/7 Wall St, Sep 2026](https://247wallst.com/retirement/2026/09/03/starting-in-2028-more-than-1-million-in-home-equity-disqualifies-you-from-medicaid-nursing-home-coverage-and-the-cap-never-adjusts-for-inflation/); [NHeLP, Feb 2026](https://healthlaw.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Retroactive-Coverage-and-Home-Equity-2122026.pdf). The 2026 range is **$752,000 (federal minimum) to $1,130,000 (state maximum)**, with 12 jurisdictions including DC at the maximum — [Medicaid Eligibility Calculator](https://medicaideligibilitycalculator.com/medicaid-home-equity-interest-limit/). Agricultural-zoned homes stay under prior law — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/medicaid-long-term-care-home-equity-cap/)
- **Eligibility & Enrollment rule moratorium**: OBBBA bars CMS from implementing specified provisions of the April 2024 "Streamlining ... Application, Eligibility Determination, Enrollment, and Renewal" final rule (89 FR 22780) until **Oct 1, 2034** — [NHeLP](https://healthlaw.org/resource/state-options-to-implement-provisions-of-the-eligibility-and-enrollment-final-rule-post-obbba/?author=lav); [Digital Government Hub summary](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025-08-20-EE-Provisions-in-OBBBA_Final-CLEAN.pdf)
- A New Hampshire report notes the Medicaid system "will soon have fewer resources and more requirements" after the July 2025 law, which bears on caseworker capacity — [Keene Sentinel](https://www.keenesentinel.com/eedition/keene-sentinel/edition_c38887bc-1867-5a6b-a6ce-46707fd16747.html)

### Inferences
- **What the retro cut does to facilities**: for applications filed on or after 1 Jan 2027, any month of eligibility more than 2 months before the application month is unpaid. An average filing time of about 71 days (2018 claim) already exceeds a 2-month window. So facilities must **start applications before private funds run out or the Medicare Part A stay ends**, not after. A denial-and-refile cycle now costs more, because the new application date carries only 2 months of retro. Per lost month, the exposure is roughly one month of the Medicaid per diem, which **[BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED]** is often about $7,000–$9,000 a month depending on state. The private-pay rate is about $8,000–$12,000 a month per cost guides — [ElderLawAnswers](https://attorney.elderlawanswers.com/how-much-will-medicaid-planning-and-application-cost-12408).
- The urgency is **real but moderate**. It is a one-month haircut on late cases, not a cliff. The stronger sales message is operational: "every likely conversion starts its file in week one, and every RFI gets answered in 48 hours." The deadline gives a reason to buy now (Q4 2026 to Q1 2027).
- The home-equity cap affects few people (equity above $1M) and mostly in high-cost states (CA, NY, NJ, MA). It adds a new screening step but no meaningful new volume. The E&E moratorium means states are **not** forced to streamline non-MAGI (aged/disabled) processing, so paper-heavy, slow agency processes persist. That is good for demand for application help and bad for turnaround times.
- Medicaid cuts to state budgets may squeeze facility margins and worsen caseworker staffing, which raises the value of getting applications right the first time.

### Gaps
- No CMS State Medicaid Director letter on Section 71112 was retrieved. The Minnesota 2028 deferral is unexplained.
- Searches found no OBBBA change to the 5-year lookback, the $2,000 asset limit or annuity rules, but I did not read the statute. **[BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED]** My understanding is that the lookback and asset limits are unchanged.
- **[BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED]** A search summary said CMS repealed the 2024 SNF minimum-staffing rule in December 2025 after OBBBA delayed it to 2034. The source was not pinned down, and it is tangential.

## 4. What is automatable: data access (open banking, AVS) and where it becomes unauthorized practice of law

### Takeaway
Most of the paralegal work is automatable now: collecting and parsing statements, flagging transfers, summarizing countable assets, filling state forms, tracking RFI deadlines and drafting caseworker follow-up. State Asset Verification Systems (AVS) already pull 60 months of month-start balances. But caseworkers still demand statements and explanations for flagged transfers, so the applicant side has to produce 60 months of documents, and consumer open-banking feeds usually cover far less history (background). Ohio explicitly lets non-attorneys identify countable resources and prepare and file applications. New Jersey treats eligibility *strategy* advice as practice of law. So the line is "prepare and file: yes; advise on how to qualify: no."

### Cited Findings
- **AVS**: Congress required state asset verification programs from 2008. For LTSS applicants, AVS can return balances for the **60 months** before application (institution name, address, first-of-month balance), for applicant and spouse, across large, regional and online banks and credit unions. Bank response times vary: some answer electronically in near real time, others manually or by fax, and requests often stay open 10–14 days — [CBPP](https://www.cbpp.org/es/research/health/using-asset-verification-systems-to-streamline-medicaid-determinations); [MassHealth EOM 16-08](https://www.mass.gov/doc/eom-16-08-asset-verification-system/download); [WA HCA](https://www.hca.wa.gov/node/19066)
- **AVS does not remove paper**: NJ caseworkers may still request statements when AVS finds no account or inconsistent data — [NJ DMAHS 17-16](https://nj.gov/humanservices/dmahs/info/resources/medicaid/2017/17-16_Implementation_of_the_Asset_Verification_System.pdf). New York requires paper documentation when a reported account gets no AVS response, or when AVS flags a possible transfer, for certain LTC applicants — [NY DOH 17 ADM-02](https://healthweb-back.health.ny.gov/health_care/medicaid/publications/docs/adm/17adm02.pdf). Washington staff cannot deny on AVS results alone and must request alternate verification — [WA HCA](https://www.hca.wa.gov/node/19066)
- Illinois provider lawsuits blamed the state's failure to use electronic verification for part of its backlog — [Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/illinois-medicaid-backlog-spawns-multiple-lawsuits-behalf-providers-beneficiaries)
- **UPL, Ohio (permissive)**: Advisory Opinion UPL 11-01 says a non-attorney may review documents to identify "countable" resources, and may prepare and file a Medicaid application and attend state hearings, to the extent federal law authorizes. A non-attorney may not do "Medicaid planning" that requires specialized legal training — [Supreme Court of Ohio, UPL Adv. Op. 11-01](https://www.supremecourt.ohio.gov/Boards/UPL/advisory_opinions/UPLAdvOp_11_01.pdf)
- **UPL, New Jersey (strict)**: UPL Committee Opinion 53 (2016) on "Application Assistors" holds that advising on strategies to become eligible is the practice of law. Unauthorized practice is a crime (N.J.S.A. 2C:21-22) and may violate the Consumer Fraud Act. The Committee acknowledged federal rules require states to allow non-lawyers to assist applicants — [NJ Courts notice, May 2016](https://www.njcourts.gov/sites/default/files/notices/2016/05/n160518c.pdf)
- **New York**: a 2012 report says non-attorneys may not handle some complex Medicaid applications — [Rochester Business Journal](https://rbj.net/2012/01/13/non-attorneys-may-not-handle-some-complex-medicaid-applications/)
- **Litigation risk is real but has been survivable**: the UPL-based fraud claim against Senior Planning Services was dismissed — [ElderLawAnswers](https://attorney.elderlawanswers.com/court-dismisses-fraud-claim-against-company-that-provides-assistance-to-medicaid-applicants-18050)

### Inferences
- **Automatable (high confidence)**: (1) document intake from families (photos and PDFs of statements, deeds, insurance, annuities, pension letters); (2) LLM parsing of 60 months of statements into a ledger; (3) flagging transfers and large withdrawals above state-specific thresholds, with an "explain this" checklist for the family; (4) a countable-asset and income summary by state rule; (5) filling state forms and portal entries; (6) tracking RFI and verification deadlines (often 10–15 days) with reminders; (7) drafting cover letters and RFI responses; (8) status checks and escalation; (9) annual renewals. Steps (2), (3) and (6) are the high-value ones: incomplete or late RFI responses and refiles are what the retro cut now punishes.
- **Data access is the main friction, and open banking only partly solves it.** **[BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED]** Consumer aggregators (Plaid and similar) typically return about 24 months of transactions, and many banks show only some years of statements online. Older statements usually need a bank archive request, sometimes with fees and mailed delivery. So a 60-month lookback still means agent-assisted requests to banks, made under the applicant's or POA's authority. Facility-side products also need access to the resident's records through a POA or authorized representative designation (**[BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED]**: 42 CFR 435.923 permits organizations to act as authorized representatives).
- **Safe product scope**: "application preparation and submission, document collection, and status follow-up" sits on the safe side in Ohio-style states. Spend-down strategy (annuities, trusts, caregiver agreements, gifting cures) must route to an attorney. That makes elder-law firms a referral partner, not an enemy. In NJ-style states, any copy that implies "we'll get you qualified" is a risk. A facility-paid model that never advises the family on strategy is the cleanest.

### Gaps
- Plaid and aggregator history limits, and bank e-statement retention, were not verified this session (search budget).
- No state found that publishes an API for LTC Medicaid application submission or status. Most are portal or paper/fax (**[BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED]**). Whether agencies accept agent-driven portal use or AI voice follow-up was not researched.
- UPL positions in other priority states (IL, PA, TX, FL) were not checked.

## 5. Willingness to pay: what facilities and families pay today

### Takeaway
Families pay about $3,000 to facility-partnered vendors and about $1,000–$10,000+ to elder-law attorneys, often benchmarked to about one month of nursing-home cost, and attorneys say paralegals can do most of the application work. Facilities pay $60–75k a year for in-house Medicaid coordinators (NY postings). Software prices for facilities are not public, and some facilities pay vendors nothing because families are billed. WTP clearly exists on the family side. Facility-side WTP for software is plausible but unproven.

### Cited Findings
- **Elder-law attorneys**: most have moved to **flat fees**; ElderLawAnswers puts planning-plus-application in "the $10,000 range" and notes most application work "can be completed by an experienced paralegal" — [ElderLawAnswers](https://attorney.elderlawanswers.com/how-much-will-medicaid-planning-and-application-cost-12408). One NJ elder-law firm says it charges **$6,500–$8,500** (a broader planning engagement) — [eldercarelawyer.com](https://www.eldercarelawyer.com/blog/applying-for-medicaid-cost-and-services/). Cost guides (weak sources) cite $1,000–$5,000 basic and $3,000–$15,000 complex, with flat fees often benchmarked to about one month of local nursing-home cost — [mashmagazine guide](https://mashmagazine.it.com/?p=15629); basic application help at $500–$3,000+ — [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-a-medicaid-lawyer-cost/)
- **Facility-partnered vendors billed to families**: ParentCare USA **$2,950** — [caregivers.com](https://portal.prod.caregivers.com/questions/applying-for-medicaid-492850.htm); MDR-type charge about **$3,000** — [caregivers.com](https://portal.prod.caregivers.com/questions/should-there-be-consumer-protection-laws-regarding-partnerships-between-different-business-entities--491223.htm); SCA (alleged) $5,000–$25,000 — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/senior-care-advocates-called-giant-scam/). PA/NJ "flat fee with payment plans" (amount not shown) — [LongTermCareLink](https://www.longtermcarelink.net/listingprofiles/2919-Paul-Lorrah-New-Jersey.htm)
- **Facility in-house cost**: NY postings show a Business Office Manager/Medicaid Coordinator at "up to **$75,000**" (Selden, NY) and an SNF care coordinator with Medicaid eligibility tracking at **$60,000–$75,000** (Staten Island). - Facilities protect themselves by refusing pending status or demanding payment, which shows they price the risk — [caregivers.com](https://portal.prod.caregivers.com/questions/check-account-balance-barely-over-2k-nh-refusing-to-put-into-medicaid-pending-demanding-full-payment-491489.htm)

### Inferences
- **Facility internal cost per application (inference)**: a $60–75k coordinator (about $80–95k loaded) handling about 50–100 new applications a year plus renewals costs roughly **$800–$1,900 per new application**, before counting pending-AR carrying cost and write-offs. A price of **$750–$1,500 per submitted or approved application**, facility-paid, undercuts the family-billed $2,950 vendors and fits within coordinator cost.
- **Attorney buyer (inference)**: if paralegal time is most of a $5–10k flat fee, a tool that cuts paralegal hours by half is worth perhaps $300–$1,000 per case to a firm. That is a smaller check, but quick decisions and no UPL exposure, since the attorney supervises.
- **Family direct-to-consumer (inference)**: WTP is high (about $3k anchor), but there is one transaction per family, crisis timing, trust barriers, and the most UPL exposure. It is better reached through facilities or attorneys than through paid ads.
- Risk to facility WTP: where a local vendor is "free" to the facility because families pay, the facility's alternative cost is zero. The pitch must be pending-AR dollars and approval speed, not labor savings alone.

## 6. Best wedge and go-to-market for a 1–5 person team

### Takeaway
Best wedge (inference): an **agentic, done-for-you "Medicaid application desk" for independent and small-chain nursing homes (5–30 facilities) in one permissive state, Ohio first**. It flags likely private-pay and Medicare-to-Medicaid conversions at admission, builds the 60-month file, files, and works RFIs to approval. Price it **per approved application (about $1,000–$1,500), facility-paid, never billed to families**. Sell it as protection against the 1 January 2027 retroactive cut. Run elder-law firms as a second channel (white-label preparation under attorney supervision). Do not lead with a pending-tracker SaaS; that slot is taken.

### Cited Findings
- Ohio is among the states with the most certified nursing facilities — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/other/state-indicator/number-of-nursing-facilities/) — and its Supreme Court UPL board expressly allows non-attorneys to identify countable resources and prepare and file applications — [Ohio UPL 11-01](https://www.supremecourt.ohio.gov/Boards/UPL/advisory_opinions/UPLAdvOp_11_01.pdf)
- Pain-heavy states with documented backlogs: Illinois (about 7,000 pending, provider lawsuits) — [Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/illinois-medicaid-backlog-spawns-multiple-lawsuits-behalf-providers-beneficiaries); New Hampshire (outside contractor hired) — [Keene Sentinel](https://www.keenesentinel.com/eedition/keene-sentinel/edition_c38887bc-1867-5a6b-a6ce-46707fd16747.html); New York (county-level variation in pending time and denials) — [LeadingAge NY](https://leadingageny.org/providers/nursing-homes/reimbursement/medicaid-eligibility-determinations3); [HFN](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/study-ny-counties-vary-nursing-home-medicaid-denials)
- Avoid New Jersey for anything advice-adjacent (Opinion 53; criminal UPL plus Consumer Fraud Act) — [NJ Courts](https://www.njcourts.gov/sites/default/files/notices/2016/05/n160518c.pdf)
- Channel precedents: state association business-partner programs (Comprehensive A/R Solutions through LeadingAge NY) — [LeadingAge NY](https://www.leadingageny.org/topics/coronavirus/supplier-resources1/comprehensive-ar-solutions/comprehensive-a-r-solutions-a-leadingage-ny-business-partner-announces-new-medicaid-applications-service/); EHR marketplaces (Aidace and RevSuite on MatrixCare) — [Aidace](https://www.matrixcare.com/partner-marketplace/aidace/), [RevSuite](https://www.matrixcare.com/partner-marketplace/revsuite-solutions/); billing outsourcers (RevSuite and NDC) — [RevSuite](https://www.findglocal.com/US/Clearwater/227067643823608/RevSuite-Software); attorney channel through annuity and planning firms (MedicaidSoft and Krause) — [Utah Business](https://www.utahbusiness.com/entrepreneurship/2025/01/27/medicaidsoft-wins-utah-tech-week-pitch-competition/)
- Spend-down typically happens about 6 months after admission, so conversions are visible well ahead of time — [Skilled Nursing News](https://skillednursingnews.com/2025/12/nearly-1-in-6-nursing-home-residents-spend-down-savings-to-qualify-for-medicaid/)
- Facilities post "Medicaid Coordinator" jobs, which marks them as buyers with an unfilled or costly role — [job postings](https://apply-jobs.apploi.com/job/1867923/hcnhealthcaresolutions/business-office-manager-medicaid-coordinator)

### Inferences
- **Buyer**: the business office manager plus the administrator at independent facilities, or the regional reimbursement or AR director at 5–30 facility chains. Large chains have in-house eligibility teams or PointClickCare/ExaCare relationships. Avoid AL as first buyer (only about 18% Medicaid; services-only coverage).
- **States**: Ohio first (legal clarity, large facility count, county-administered). Then Illinois (acute backlog pain, though the agency queue limits how fast you can show results) or Pennsylvania. Skip NJ and NY at first: crowded vendor ecosystems and stricter UPL culture.
- **First 10 customers (playbook, inference)**: (1) offer a free "pending AR audit" that ingests the facility's pending list and returns a stuck-case triage within 48 hours; (2) take 5–10 of the worst pending cases on a pay-on-approval basis to prove the result; (3) target facilities with open Medicaid-coordinator postings and BOM turnover; (4) join the Ohio Health Care Association and LeadingAge Ohio business-partner programs and speak at their reimbursement sessions; (5) use the "1 Jan 2027 retro cut" as the reason to meet now (Q4 2026); (6) once a few logos exist, apply to MatrixCare and PointClickCare marketplaces.
- **Price**: $1,000–$1,500 per approved new application (or $750 per submitted application plus a $500 approval bonus); renewals at about $150–$250 each; an optional pending-recovery tier at a percentage of collected pending dollars for legacy cases. Illustrative: one state, 100 facilities × about 20–25 applications a year × about $1,200 gives about **$2.4–3.0M ARR**. Nationally (about 300–400k applications a year × about $1,000) the facility-side pool is about **$300–400M**, plus renewals. Both figures are inference built on the estimates in section 2.
- **Delivery model**: start as a tech-enabled service, with a human eligibility specialist in the loop and agents doing the paralegal work. Gross margin rises as agents take over. This matches how incumbents sell (outcomes, not seats) and avoids the "yet another dashboard" objection.
- **Elder-law channel**: in parallel, offer white-label file preparation to 5–10 small elder-law firms at about $300–$750 per case. It is fast to close, removes UPL exposure, and gives a referral path for families who need planning (which the facility product must not provide).

## 7. Kill criteria and verdict

### Takeaway
No single finding kills the idea, but three findings shrink it. (1) Case-tracking software already exists (Aidace, RevSuite, SNF Metrics), and the EHR incumbent sells a Medicaid-application RCM service. (2) The incumbent outsourced model is often free to facilities because families pay, which caps facility WTP. (3) State agencies' own backlogs, not application quality, often set the pace. Liability is real but manageable with facility-paid, no-advice scope. **Verdict: proceed only as a narrow, outcome-priced, agent-powered application service in one permissive state. Kill if early customer discovery shows facilities won't pay per application because family-billed vendors or PointClickCare already cover them.**

### Cited Findings
- **Crowding and incumbent bundling**: PointClickCare RCM services include Medicaid application handling — [BusinessWire 2021](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210525005086/en/PointClickCare-Launches-Revenue-Cycle-Management-Services-to-Improve-Profitability-for-Skilled-Nursing-Facilities); Aidace, RevSuite, SNF Metrics sell Medicaid-pending workflow — [Aidace](https://www.matrixcare.com/partner-marketplace/aidace/), [RevSuite](https://www.matrixcare.com/partner-marketplace/revsuite-solutions/), [SNF Metrics](https://snfmetrics.com/ar-tracker-dashboard/); ExaCare has $30M and does pre-admission eligibility and financial-risk checks — [SNN](https://skillednursingnews.com/2025/10/exacare-secures-30m-in-series-a-funding-for-ai-tools-streamlining-nursing-home-admissions/), [ExaCare](https://www.exacare.com/skilled-nursing)

- **Tailwind (counter to kill)**: retro window cut to 2 months from 1 Jan 2027 — [CA DHCS](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/es/services/medi-cal/eligibility/letters/Documents/ACWDL-26-07.pdf); LeadingAge expects more cautious admissions — [LeadingAge](https://leadingage.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/OBBB-Medicaid-Retroactive-Eligibility-June-2025-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Kill-criteria scorecard (inference)**:
  - *Entrenched vendors*: **partly triggered.** Tracking SaaS and human services are entrenched, but no one found sells agentic end-to-end preparation at software cost. Risk: ExaCare or PointClickCare adds it within 12–24 months.
  - *Low WTP*: **unresolved, the key risk.** Family WTP is proven (about $3k). Facility WTP for a paid vendor competes with "free" family-billed vendors and in-house staff. Test within 60 days.
  - *State-by-state complexity*: **real but manageable** with a one-state start. Rules sit at county level in NY, OH and PA, so even one state has local variation.
  - *Liability*: **manageable** if facility-paid, preparation-only, with attorney referral for planning, HIPAA/financial-PII controls, and no "guaranteed approval" claims.
  - *Agency bottleneck*: **caps ROI claims.** Sell "complete on first submission, RFIs answered in 48 hours, conversions started early", not "approval in X days".
- **Verdict**: keep it on the shortlist as a **healthcare/consumer crossover, but no longer clearly #1**. It is a solid, unglamorous B2B business for a small team: plausibly $2–5M ARR in a few states. The upside comes from adding renewals, AL waivers and an elder-law channel. It is a weak venture-scale story unless the agent layer becomes the platform that ExaCare-type players want to buy.
- **Concrete go/no-go test (inference)**: within about 60 days, (a) 15–20 conversations with Ohio or Illinois BOMs and regional AR directors; (b) at least 3 facilities agree to pay at least $750 per application on live cases; (c) on a 10–20 case pilot, show fewer RFIs and refiles and a shorter time-to-submit than the facility baseline. **Kill** if most facilities say their vendor is free (family-billed) or PointClickCare already does it, or if no facility will pay at least $500 per application.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence on churn, switching or satisfaction with existing vendors (no G2/Capterra reviews; Reddit threads not surfaced by search).
- Whether ExaCare or PointClickCare has a 2026 roadmap item for Medicaid applications is unknown.
- How often pending applications end in write-off (versus late payment) is undocumented. That ratio determines the real dollar value per facility.
