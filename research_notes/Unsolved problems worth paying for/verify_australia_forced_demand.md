## Q1. Resolving the disagreement: is Tranche 2 underserved or "filling fast", and does Support at Home hold up as a #2?

### Takeaway
The cross-sector researcher was right about **supply**. By May 2026 at least 28 vetted vendors sold Tranche 2 software from A$0–49/month upward, with the AML/CTF programme and risk assessment included. Every major channel (CA ANZ, CPA Australia, REIA, PEXA, Rex, LEAP, Smokeball, Actionstep) has a partner or a native module. The finance researcher was right about **demand-side readiness**: only about 39,500 of roughly 89,600 expected entities had enrolled by mid-August 2026. But the claim that "existing tools only do ID checks" is false. Low enrolment reflects firms not complying, not a gap in tools. Support at Home holds up better than Tranche 2 as a small-team wedge, though only as a narrow product. A new government forcing function appeared in September 2026: payment-integrity reviews found monthly-statement problems at 51 of 60 providers. The buyer pool is small (about 950 providers, shrinking through consolidation), and incumbents already ship basic reconciliation features. Award-wage checking is the weakest of the three. No criminal prosecution has been brought yet, and independent checking tools already exist, one of them with a free tier.

### Inferences
- In Tranche 2 the remaining gap is non-compliant firms, not missing tools. About 50,000 expected entities had not enrolled by mid-August. Some are legitimately out of scope (tax-only accountants, for example), and the rest are price-sensitive laggards being pushed by s167 notices. Selling a new tool to laggards in a market already priced at A$49–179/month and A$5–20 per check is a weak position for a 1–5 person team without a channel.
- The finance researcher's #1 ranking should be downgraded from "best opportunity" to "avoid as a horizontal product; consider only a narrow channel-specific niche". The cross-sector "filling fast" view is closer to the evidence.
- Support at Home should move up relative to Tranche 2 as a small-team wedge. The case rests on the September 2026 payment-integrity findings, a new audit-driven reason to buy, rather than on cash-flow complaints alone. Its ceiling is low: under 1,000 buyers and a shrinking base.

### Gaps
- No primary AUSTRAC dashboard or media release with an October 2026 enrolment count was found. The figures conflict (see Q3).
- No independent user reviews of Tranche 2 tools were found, so tool quality, as opposed to tool existence, could not be checked.

## Q2. Tranche 2 competitor landscape: who covers what, at what price, and which obligations are well served vs unserved

### Takeaway
Every obligation that applies now has multiple vendors. Identity, KYB, PEP and sanctions checks are commoditised at A$5–40 per check. Risk assessment and programme authoring are bundled into A$49–449/month tools, and a free AUSTRAC starter kit sits underneath them. Training is a separate mature market. The thinnest coverage is in judgement-heavy work: source of funds and wealth, beneficial-ownership chains through trusts, and documenting risk decisions. Independent evaluation is not due until 2029–2030. Distribution is largely locked up by partnerships with professional bodies and platforms.

### Inferences
- The finance researcher's "existing tools only do ID checks" is falsified for 2026. At least six vendors bundle risk assessment and programme generation with CDD, at A$49–449/month.
- Price compression has already happened: free tiers, A$49/month plans and A$8 per check. A new entrant cannot win on price, and AUSTRAC's free kit caps what buyers will pay for "programme-only" tools.
- The remaining real gaps are source of funds and wealth judgements, beneficial-ownership chains through trusts and companies, and evidencing risk decisions. They are judgement-heavy and carry liability, which makes them hard to productise and easy for incumbents to add as fields. Independent evaluation is a 2029–2030 market.
- Distribution is the moat, and it is taken. CA ANZ has InfoTrack, CPA Australia and REIA have First AML, property settlement has PEXA Clear, and the leading legal and real-estate practice systems have native or partner AML. A small team would need an uncovered channel: possibly smaller real-estate CRMs such as VaultRE or Agentbox (no AML integration found, which is unverified, not proven absent), IPA or other bookkeeper bodies, or trust and company service providers.

### Gaps
- The search did not find First AML, InfoTrack, Kyckr, Persona, Didit, Zyphe or Ble pricing for Australian Tranche 2, and found no Tranche 2 product details for Didit, Persona, Ble or Kyckr. Zyphe and Persona appear only as guide publishers in prior notes.

- The search did not find whether the Law Council or state law societies endorse a specific vendor; the Law Society NSW and WA host guidance and regulator updates only.
- There are no independent reviews (G2 or Capterra) of the new Tranche 2 tools, so tool quality and churn are unknown.

## Q3. Latest Tranche 2 numbers: enrolments vs expected, enforcement, complaints

### Takeaway
By mid-August 2026 fewer than half of expected entities had enrolled: 39,520 against 89,557. Real estate was at about 17,800–18,000 of an expected 36,000–45,000. AUSTRAC began sending s167 information notices on 28 August 2026, but no civil penalty action against a Tranche 2 entity had been reported by mid-October 2026. One figure of about 62,000 enrolled, attributed to the AUSTRAC CEO, conflicts with the others and is unverified. Complaints are mostly about cost, beneficial-ownership work and staff shortages, and they are voiced mainly through vendor and law-firm commentary rather than forums.

### Inferences
- The remaining pool is about 50,000 unenrolled expected entities. Some will exit designated services or are out of scope, so the true pool of laggard buyers is probably well under 50,000. These are the least motivated, most price-sensitive buyers.
- Enforcement is still at the information-gathering stage. Until AUSTRAC publicises a penalty or undertaking against a small firm, urgency for laggards stays modest.

## Q4. Support at Home: claim failures, provider software, government fixes, provider count, willingness to pay

### Takeaway

A rough range of A$500–2,500/month per small or mid provider is an inference, not a market data point.
- The market is small: about 950 approved providers, shrinking, with large providers having in-house finance teams. About 400–700 small and mid providers at A$6–30k a year gives roughly A$3–20m a year of serviceable revenue (inference). That is enough for a 1–5 person business but not for venture scale. ## Q6. For each opportunity: buyer count, realistic price, best wedge for a 1–5 person Australian team, first 10 customers, kill criteria

### Takeaway
For a 1–5 person Australian team the ranking should be: (1) a Support at Home statement and claim assurance layer, which is narrow but has a fresh government audit trigger and less direct competition; (2) Tranche 2, only as a channel-specific niche and not as a horizontal compliance tool, because the market is crowded, prices are compressed and channels are locked; (3) award-wage checking, which has low urgency and existing independent tools. Everything below except the cited buyer counts is inference.

### Cited Findings
- Tranche 2 buyer pool: 89,557 expected new reporting entities (AGD) and 39,520 enrolled by 13 August 2026. Real estate: 36,408 expected and about 17,800–18,000 enrolled. — [Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/aml-ctf-reforms-why-austracs-first-enforcement-wave-may-arrive-sooner-than-expected/); [REB](https://www.realestatebusiness.com.au/industry/32386-aml-crackdown-begins-austrac-puts-real-estate-agencies-under-scrutiny)
- Tranche 2 price anchors: A$49–449/month self-serve, A$5–40 per check, AUSTRAC starter kit free or low-cost, outsourced A$650–2,500/month. — [CompareAML](https://www.compareaml.com.au/compare); [easyAML](https://easyaml.com/pricing); [FreeAML](https://freeaml.com.au/blog/best-aml-software-australia)
- Support at Home buyer pool: 950 approved providers (31 March 2026) and shrinking. — [GEN Aged Care Data](https://www.gen-agedcaredata.gov.au/getmedia/88363448-d30b-4958-8823-df697cb5f769/Support-at-Home-Program-Data-Report-Q3-2025-26-corrected); [The Weekly SOURCE, Ansell](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/ansell-strategic-flags-new-wave-of-exits-under-support-at-home/)
- Support at Home trigger: payment-integrity findings on monthly statements at 51 of 60 reviewed providers (September 2026). — [Department](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/support-at-home-payment-integrity-rolling-review-cycle-1-summary-report.pdf)
- Award-checking price anchors: FairWork Mate free for 5 or fewer employees and A$499+/month paid; Tanda about A$12.80 per user per month bundled. — [FairWork Mate](https://fairworkmate.com.au/for-business); [RosterElf](https://www.rosterelf.com/compare/deputy-vs-tanda)

### Inferences

**(1) Support at Home statement and claim assurance (recommended of the three)**
- Buyer count: about 950 approved providers. A realistic target is about 400–700 small and mid providers (roughly 50–1,500 clients) without in-house systems teams, because large providers build in-house.
- Price: A$500–2,500/month per provider, or about A$3–8 per active client per month, with an optional "pre-review health check" at A$2–5k one-off. Total serviceable revenue is roughly A$3–20m a year.
- Wedge: a read-only "statement and claim health check". The provider uploads the Aged Care Provider Portal payment-statement CSV and its client-management export. The product returns line-level exceptions mapped to the Department's payment-integrity review themes: statement content errors, contribution errors around the 1 October 2026 personal-care cut-over, brokered-service evidence gaps and duplicate or double payments. Start with the most common systems (Lookout, AlayaCare, CareMaster, ShiftCare and QuickClaim exports). No B2G certification is needed for v1.
- First 10 customers: (a) providers who complained publicly or in Senate submissions about claiming and statements (for example Pearl Home Care and Community Vision, from prior notes), offered a free 3-month health check; (b) members of Advance Ageing WA and Ageing Australia, through webinars on the September 2026 payment-integrity findings; (c) aged-care finance consultants (Enkindle, SAH Consulting) as resellers; (d) providers named in Department review cycles once they are notified.
- Kill criteria: the Senate report (24 November 2026) or the Department replaces monthly statements or claiming rules in a way that makes v1 obsolete; fewer than 3 of the first 15 health-check providers convert to paid within 60 days; two or more major client-management vendors (for example Lookout or AlayaCare) ship equivalent exception reports by mid-2027; payment-integrity reviews do not lead to recoveries or sanctions, so the audit fear fades.

**(2) Tranche 2 AML/CTF (do not build horizontally; niche only)**
- Buyer count: about 50,000 expected entities had not enrolled by mid-August 2026, many of them out of scope or exiting. About 40,000–62,000 had enrolled, most already on a tool, a kit or a partner offer.
- Price: the market has settled at A$49–449/month or A$5–40 per check. A new entrant would struggle above about A$150/month for small firms.
- Wedge, only if one is pursued:
  - (a) An AML add-on for a practice or CRM system with no partner yet. VaultRE, Agentbox, FYI and Karbon showed no AML integration in search, which is unverified rather than proven absent.
  - (b) A source of funds and wealth plus beneficial-ownership evidence workflow (trust and company chains) that sits on top of commodity ID checks, sold to firms already enrolled. This is the thinnest-covered obligation.
  - (c) A pre-evaluation readiness product timed for 2028–2029, when independent evaluations begin.
- First 10 customers: firms receiving s167 notices, reached through accountant and conveyancer Facebook and LinkedIn groups and through smaller bodies (IPA, Australian Institute of Conveyancers state divisions, buyer's agent associations), because CA ANZ, CPA Australia and REIA are already partnered.
- Kill criteria: in 10 discovery calls, at least 7 firms already use a tool, kit or partner offer and would not switch for under A$100/month; no uncovered practice-system or CRM channel will partner within 3 months; AUSTRAC brings no public enforcement against a small Tranche 2 firm by mid-2027, so laggards stay inert.

**(3) Award-wage checking (weakest)**
- Buyer count: tens of thousands of employing hospitality, retail and childcare businesses (unverified; see Q5 Gaps).
- Price: A$30–150/month for micro and small businesses, or A$300–1,000 per accountant-delivered annual check. Free tiers and bundled roster tools hold prices down.
- Wedge: an accountant- or bookkeeper-delivered annual "award health check plus Small Business Code evidence pack" for clients under 15 staff, generated from Xero, MYOB or Employment Hero payroll exports, priced per client.
- First 10 customers: 3–5 bookkeeping firms with hospitality-heavy client books, on a revenue-share basis, positioned after FWO sweep announcements in their city.
- Kill criteria: bookkeepers will not pay more than about A$100 per client check; FairWork Mate or WageSafe already hold the accountant channel; criminal prosecutions remain at zero through 2027 and no FWO sweep coincides with a sales push.

### Gaps
- No primary willingness-to-pay data (quotes or surveys) for any of the three. Every price above is inferred from competitor anchors.
- No interviews or forum posts from target buyers were captured within the search budget. The first step for any of the three should be 10–15 discovery calls.
