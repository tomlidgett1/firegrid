# Verify: Medicaid work-requirement and 6-month redetermination retention, sold to MCOs and providers (as of 18 Oct 2026)

**Bottom line: kill it as framed. Keep only a narrow provider-side version as a conditional candidate.**

- **As framed, it fails.** The pitch was an MCO-facing "help members keep coverage" platform, priced per retained member. That space is now crowded. Fortuna Health is backed by a16z and Y Combinator, has about $22M raised, and already has live MCO contracts. The earlier "0 YC companies" finding was wrong. Findhelp bought Uno Health, Unite Us is in the space, CMS is building a free federal verification tool (Emmy), and ten state-side vendors have pledged support.
- **The rule itself weakens demand in year one.** It limits what MCOs can do. It also lets states accept self-attestation through 2027, so the documentation crunch mostly arrives on 1 Jan 2028.
- **What could survive:** a provider-side tool that assembles exemption paperwork (medical frailty, substance-use treatment, behavioral health) for behavioral-health clinics, CCBHCs (Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics) and FQHCs. Even this faces litigation and timing risk.

**Method note:** Every WebFetch attempt failed with DNS errors (sellersdorsey.com, hklaw.com). All findings below therefore come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full text. Facts marked [UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND] are from model knowledge or earlier notes and have no source here. I used 35 of the 35 allowed web searches.

## 1. Regulatory status as of October 2026 (start date, early states, CMS rule, exemptions, verification, 6-month renewals, projected losses)

### Takeaway
The requirement is in force. Most states must start on 1 Jan 2027, and that date stands unless CMS grants a good-faith extension; extensions are capped at 31 Dec 2028.

Other key points:
- **The rule.** CMS issued its interim final rule (IFR) on 1 June 2026. It took effect 31 July 2026.
- **Verification order.** States must try ex parte data first. Ex parte means data the state already holds: payroll records, SNAP/TANF records, and 12 months of claims for medical frailty.
- **2027 vs. 2028.** In 2027, states may accept self-attestation when electronic data is unavailable. From 2028, documentation is generally required.
- **6-month renewals.** These start with renewals scheduled from 1 Jan 2027.
- **Projected losses.** CMS projects 2.3M fewer enrollees in FY2027, rising to 3.1–3.3M in later years.
- **Litigation and delay requests.** The IFR's medical-frailty provisions are being litigated, with a hearing on 20 Oct 2026. Five governors have asked for a delay.

### Cited Findings
**The interim final rule (CMS-2454-IFC)**
- CMS issued the IFR on 1 June 2026. It was published in the Federal Register on 3 June 2026 (91 FR 33348).
  - It took effect on 31 July 2026, and comments were due the same day.
  - States must implement by **1 January 2027**.
  - It covers adults aged 19–64 who must show 80 hours a month of work, education, job training or community service, or meet an equivalent income threshold.
  - Sources: [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/03/2026-11094/medicaid-program-community-engagement-requirement-for-certain-individuals); [AHA](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-06-01-cms-issues-interim-final-rule-medicaid-community-engagement-requirements); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/healthcare/1799182/cms-issues-interim-final-rule-implementing-medicaid-community-engagement-requirements)
- Federal documents call the law the "Working Families Tax Cut" legislation (H.R. 1 / OBBBA) — [AHA](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-06-01-cms-issues-interim-final-rule-medicaid-community-engagement-requirements)
- It applies to all 40 expansion states plus DC unless a state gets an extension — [HFMA](https://www.hfma.org/payment-reimbursement-and-managed-care/medicaid-work-requirement-rule/). Earlier notes said 41–43 states.

**Good-faith extensions**
- CMS may grant extensions to no later than **31 Dec 2028**. It expects to approve initial requests for **no more than six months**.
- A state's request must document:
  - the steps it has taken so far
  - the barriers it has met
  - a detailed implementation plan
  - any exigent circumstances
- Source: [McDermott+](https://www.mcdermottplus.com/?p=160419); [HFMA](https://www.hfma.org/payment-reimbursement-and-managed-care/medicaid-work-requirement-rule/)

**Exemptions**
- The IFR keeps the statutory exemptions, including:
  - pregnant or postpartum people
  - people who are medically frail or disabled
  - caregivers
- States may also grant short-term hardship exemptions.
- Counties with high unemployment can be exempted, but the state must apply for it.
- Sources: [AHA](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-06-01-cms-issues-interim-final-rule-medicaid-community-engagement-requirements); [Minnesota Reformer/KFF Health News, Oct 2025](https://minnesotareformer.com/2025/10/24/officials-show-little-proof-tht-new-tech-will-help-medicaid-enrollees-meet-work-rules/)

**Verification**
- **Ex parte first.** States must try to confirm compliance or exemption using reliable data before asking the enrollee. Examples include the federal data hub, SNAP/TANF records and payroll data.
  - For medical frailty, states must first check the **prior 12 months of claims and encounters**.
  - Source: [MACPAC Sept 2026 slides](https://www.macpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/01_September-Slides-Community-Engagement-Requirements-in-Medicaid.pdf)
- **2027 versus 2028.**
  - In 2027, states may accept self-attestation.
  - From 2028, self-attestation can be used only once per enrollment period, and only for medical-frailty or special-medical-needs exemptions.
  - From **1 Jan 2028**, states generally must require documentation when it is reasonably available.
  - Sources: [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/medicaid-work-rules-whats-in-whats-out-and-where-hospitals-stand/); [National Council fact sheet](https://hub.thenationalcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/H.R.1-Comm-Engagement-Requirements.pdf); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/healthcare/1799182/cms-issues-interim-final-rule-implementing-medicaid-community-engagement-requirements)
  - **Sources disagree on the 2027 scope:**
    - One comment letter says self-declaration is allowed in 2027 only "when reliable electronic data is unavailable" — [SpruceID comment](https://spruceid.com/public-comments/cms-2254-ifc-medicaid-program-interim-final-rule-comments.pdf).
    - An advocacy summary says self-attestation is accepted at all applications and renewals in 2027 — [CHRT](https://chrt.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Work-Requirements-8-6-26-clean.pdf).
- **Look-back at renewal.** At each six-month renewal, states must look back one or more months to verify compliance — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/issue-brief/a-closer-look-at-the-medicaid-work-requirement-provisions-in-the-big-beautiful-bill)

**Outreach and notice**
- States must send periodic outreach through at least two channels.
- Initial outreach must go out at least three months before the compliance review period starts. For states starting 1 Jan 2027, that means by 30 Sept 2026 — [MACPAC Sept 2026 slides](https://www.macpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/01_September-Slides-Community-Engagement-Requirements-in-Medicaid.pdf)
- The state attorneys general cite a notice deadline of **31 Aug 2026** — [Michigan AG](https://www.michigan.gov/ag/news/press-releases/2026/07/01/ag-nessel-sues-trump-administration-over-unlawful-implementation-of-medicaid-work-requirements)
- Members found non-compliant get a notice and 30 days to comply or claim an exemption before denial or disenrollment. This is Nebraska's reported process — [DHHS Nebraska / 1011now](https://www.1011now.com/2026/04/30/nebraska-medicaid-work-requirements-take-effect-friday-could-impact-up-40000-residents/)

**Federal tools and funding**
- CMS is building **"Emmy" (Eligibility Made Easy)**, an open-source, CMS-hosted service that uses consent-based verification across multiple data sources. States can use it in either of two ways:
  - the CMS-hosted app, where the enrollee gives consent
  - an API behind the state's own interface
  - Source: [CMS Emmy page](https://cms.gov/medicaid-chip/community-engagement-support/eligibility-made-easy)
- The law gave states **$200M for FY2026** to build their systems. Experts called this possibly insufficient — [Minnesota Reformer/KFF Health News, Oct 2025](https://minnesotareformer.com/2025/10/24/officials-show-little-proof-tht-new-tech-will-help-medicaid-enrollees-meet-work-rules/); [Bangor Daily News](https://www.bangordailynews.com/?p=3655260)

**Six-month renewals (CMS letter SMD #26-001, 6 Mar 2026)**
- Expansion adults move to six-month renewals for renewals "scheduled on or after the first day of the first quarter that begins after December 31, 2026."
- States may do either of the following:
  - move current enrollees onto the six-month cycle right away, with renewals starting around 4 Jan 2027
  - set the next eligibility period to six months
- Partial-expansion waiver states are excluded.
- CMS links readiness to eligibility-error disallowances starting in FY2030.
- Sources: [Georgetown CCF](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/03/06/cms-releases-guidance-on-6-month-medicaid-renewals-for-expansion-adults/); [HFMA](https://www.hfma.org/payment-reimbursement-and-managed-care/medicaid-six-month-eligibility-redeterminations-cms-guidance/)

**Early-start states**
- **Nebraska** (started 1 May 2026):
  - Compliance checks began 31 July 2026, starting with members due for renewal.
  - DHHS mailed notices to 70,000 expansion members in Dec 2025.
  - Estimates of people affected range from 20,000 to 40,000.
  - Sources: [1011now](https://www.1011now.com/2026/04/30/nebraska-medicaid-work-requirements-take-effect-friday-could-impact-up-40000-residents/); [Nebraska DHHS](https://dhhs.ne.gov/Pages/New-Medicaid-Work-Requirements-Take-Effect-for-Expansion-Members-Friday.aspx)
  - One official estimated **30–40%** of the 70,000 will need their hours verified manually — [1011now, Apr 2026](https://www.1011now.com/2026/04/13/hospitals-warn-nebraska-medicaid-work-rules-could-strain-staffing-disrupt-care/)
  - The state does not plan to add staff — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/a-closer-look-at-nebraska-the-first-state-planning-to-implement-a-medicaid-work-requirement/)
- **Montana** (started 1 July 2026):
  - A no-penalty grace period ran to **1 Oct 2026**.
  - The state hired 39 staff and planned 20 more.
  - It had no access to medical claims data for exemption checks.
  - It has not published ex parte clearance rates.
  - Sources: [Yellowstone Public Radio](https://www.ypradio.org/regional-news/2026-08-10/uncertain-effects-of-montana-medicaid-work-rules); [Flathead Beacon](https://flatheadbeacon.com/2026/04/30/state-health-department-begins-to-prepare-medicaid-patients-for-new-work-requirements/); [Montana Budget & Policy Center](https://www.montanabudget.org/post/montana-dphhs-to-roll-out-trump-s-medicaid-work-requirements-starting-july-1)
- **Georgia Pathways**:
  - CMS approved a temporary extension that added exemptions. The waiver now expires **31 Dec 2026**, after which Georgia must follow the federal rules — [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/medicaid-work-requirements-governors-ask-hhs-delay-letter/831992/)
  - A GAO review found **$54.2M in administrative spending versus $26.1M in benefits** from FY2021 to Q2 2025.
  - Only 3,500 people enrolled in the first year, against a projected 25,028.
  - Sources: [ProPublica](https://propublica.org/article/georgia-pathways-medicaid-work-requirement-gao-report); [Florida Phoenix](https://floridaphoenix.com/2025/09/26/repub/georgias-limited-medicaid-expansion-program-is-extended-through-2026-despite-concerns-about-cost/)
- As of 23 Jan 2026, waivers from Arizona, Arkansas, Iowa, Montana, Ohio, South Carolina and Utah were pending at CMS — [Ballotpedia via Healthcare Dive search result](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/01/23/mandatory-medicaid-work-requirements-are-coming-what-do-they-look-like-now/). Earlier notes give Iowa's start as 1 Dec 2026.

**Projected coverage losses**
- **CMS's own projection:** enrollment falls by **2.3M in FY2027**, rising to **3.1–3.3M** in later years — [AHA](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-06-01-cms-issues-interim-final-rule-medicaid-community-engagement-requirements); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/healthcare/1799182/cms-issues-interim-final-rule-implementing-medicaid-community-engagement-requirements)
- **CBO:** **5.3M** more uninsured in 2034 from these provisions, as cited by Harvard — [Harvard T.H. - **Urban Institute:** **4.9M–10M** lose coverage from work requirements plus six-month renewals. Of these, **2.0–3.1M** come from six-month renewals alone — [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/10-million-lose-medicaid-work-requirements-eligibility-checks-urban-institute-robert-wood-johnson/815886/)
- **Manatt Health:** the IFR will push losses beyond what the statute alone would cause — reported in the [Georgetown CCF Big Five Q2 2026](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/08/13/medicaid-managed-care-the-big-five-in-q2-2026/) context (search summary; I did not confirm the exact source page).
- **Montana's own figures conflict:**
  - The state health department estimated 65% of enrollees already comply or are exempt.
  - The state Department of Labor and Industry put it above 90%.
  - 72% of expansion enrollees already work.
  - Source: [Yellowstone Public Radio](https://www.ypradio.org/regional-news/2026-08-10/uncertain-effects-of-montana-medicaid-work-rules)

**Litigation and delay requests**
- **Multistate attorneys-general suit** (filed 29 June 2026) challenges the IFR's narrowed definition of "medically frail" — [Michigan AG](https://www.michigan.gov/ag/news/press-releases/2026/07/01/ag-nessel-sues-trump-administration-over-unlawful-implementation-of-medicaid-work-requirements); [California AG](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-sues-trump-administration-over-unlawful-implementation)
  - The count of states differs: "25 states plus DC" per [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/medicaid-work-requirements-governors-ask-hhs-delay-letter/831992/), or "26 states" in the Georgetown/Manatt summary.
  - **A hearing is scheduled for 20 Oct 2026** (search summary of the Georgetown CCF Q2 2026 context).
- **Private suit in the District of Maryland** (filed 18 Sept 2026) by enrollees and physician groups. It challenges:
  - the new frailty conditions
  - the 12-month limit on data
  - the short-term nature of self-attestation
  - Source: [LeadingAge](https://leadingage.org/?p=1173173)
- **Five Democratic governors** (OR, CA, ME, NM, VA) wrote on 1 Oct 2026 asking HHS to tie the compliance date to each state's readiness — [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/medicaid-work-requirements-governors-ask-hhs-delay-letter/831992/); [Becker's](https://www.beckerspayer.com/payer/medicaid/5-governors-push-hhs-to-delay-medicaid-work-requirements/)

### Inferences
- The core mandate comes from the statute, and the lawsuits target only the IFR's frailty and data rules. So the requirement will very likely start in most expansion states between Jan 2027 and Dec 2028.
- Repeal would need Congress and the President. [UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND] That makes repeal before 2029 unlikely regardless of the Nov 2026 midterms.
- **The real documentation cliff is 1 Jan 2028, not 1 Jan 2027.** In 2027, self-attestation and ex parte checks absorb much of the burden. This pushes the peak of paid demand for documentation tooling out by a year.
- **Six-month renewals are a separate, more certain source of churn.** They are not in litigation, and Urban attributes 2–3.1M losses to them. A retention product should be built around renewals at least as much as around work hours.
- Exemption rules, especially medical frailty, may change if the plaintiffs win an injunction. A product built mainly around frailty documentation carries direct legal-change risk.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Federal Register text. The exact scope of 2027 self-attestation and the precise MCO limits need to be checked against the rule itself (42 CFR 435.550 onward, per the SpruceID comment).
- No result reported the outcome of the 20 Oct 2026 hearing; it is two days after these notes.
- I found no Nebraska or Montana post-launch numbers: no counts of notices, people found non-compliant, terminations, or ex parte clearance rates.
- I found no list of states that have formally requested or received extensions as of Oct 2026.
- I did not confirm Iowa's 1 Dec 2026 start or any other early-start dates.

## 2. Who bears the cost of disenrollment, and what a retained member is worth

### Takeaway
MCOs lose capitation and get a sicker risk pool. Members who drop off for paperwork reasons tend to be healthier workers, so the members who remain cost more on average.

Other key points:
- **Per-member value.** In 2024, Medicaid spent about **$6,421 a year per expansion adult**, roughly **$535 a month**. That is the gross revenue at stake per member. An MCO's contribution margin on it is far smaller.
- **Hospitals.** They face lower margins and risk losing DSH and 340B eligibility. They have started hiring staff to help patients keep coverage.
- **FQHCs.** Their exposure is clear, but I found no hard per-patient figures.

### Cited Findings
**MCOs**
- **Industry-wide enrollment.** Medicaid enrollment at the five largest MCOs fell from **36.2M (30 June 2025) to 34.0M (30 June 2026), down 6.5%**, and declined in every quarter since H.R. 1 passed — [Georgetown CCF Q2 2026](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/08/13/medicaid-managed-care-the-big-five-in-q2-2026/)
  - As of Q1 2026, net declines ranged from 3.1% (Centene) to 5.8% (Molina) — [Georgetown CCF Q1 2026](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/05/21/medicaid-managed-care-the-big-five-in-q1-2026/)
  - Georgetown says Molina is the only company that has quantified its expected losses from H.R. 1; the figure was not in the search snippet.
- **Centene:**
  - Now expects Medicaid membership to fall **8–9%** for the year, up from the 6% it projected earlier, and ties the decline to work requirements — [Reuters via WDEZ](https://wdez.com/?p=972379)
  - Its 10-Q says OBBBA could raise "the overall morbidity of the Medicaid Expansion population largely beginning in 2027" — [Centene 10-Q, Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001071739/000107173926000153/cnc-20260630.htm)
  - It is exiting Arkansas's Medicaid expansion program in 2027.
- **Elevance** agreed to exit DC Medicaid (Q2 2026 call) — [Georgetown CCF Q2 2026](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/08/13/medicaid-managed-care-the-big-five-in-q2-2026/)
- **Unwinding precedent (2023):** Centene expected to lose more than 2M of its 16M Medicaid members during the post-pandemic unwinding. It ran member outreach and partnered with community organizations and health centers — [KFF Health News, 2023](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/medicaid-disenrollment-public-health-emergency-insurance-revenue-increase/)

**Per-member value**
- KFF lists about **$6,421.50 per enrollee for ACA expansion adults nationally in 2024** ($6,417 in 2023) — [KFF State Health Facts](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/state-indicator/medicaid-spending-per-enrollee/?state=ME)
- Average spending per enrollee is $8,444 in expansion states, across all groups — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/issue-brief/medicaid-per-enrollee-spending-variation-across-states)

**Hospitals**
- Hospitals have begun hiring care managers and social workers to find patients who will be subject to the requirement and help them keep coverage — [HFMA](https://www.hfma.org/cost-effectiveness-of-health/the-obbbs-medicaid-work-requirements-and-the-hidden-risks-for-hospitals/)
- Losing enough Medicaid patients could end a hospital's eligibility for DSH (Disproportionate Share Hospital) payments and 340B drug-pricing revenue — [HFMA](https://www.hfma.org/cost-effectiveness-of-health/the-obbbs-medicaid-work-requirements-and-the-hidden-risks-for-hospitals/)
- A 2025 HFMA analysis estimated average operating margins in expansion states could fall by **up to 13.3%**, and safety-net hospital margins by **up to 29.6%** — [HFMA](https://www.hfma.org/cost-effectiveness-of-health/the-obbbs-medicaid-work-requirements-and-the-hidden-risks-for-hospitals/)
- Fitch disagreed on revenue: it estimated a negligible revenue effect but a larger margin effect (older piece) — [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/medicaid-work-requirements-bring-uncertainty-to-hospital-bottom-lines-fitc/516184/)
- Bloomberg Government (July 2026) reported hospitals helping patients with work-requirement paperwork "to save their bottom lines" (paywalled) — [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bloomberg-government-news/hospitals-take-up-medicaid-work-rules-to-save-their-bottom-lines)
- Reviewing diagnosis codes to establish medical frailty "could prove onerous, as seen in Nebraska" — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/medicaid-work-rules-whats-in-whats-out-and-where-hospitals-stand/)
- America's Essential Hospitals noted that patients are unlikely to bring pay stubs to the hospital — [AEH letter, Mar 2026](https://essentialhospitals.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Work-Requirement-Letter-March26.pdf)
- A Missouri rural-health white paper (Feb 2026) claims OBBBA will cut hospital Medicaid revenue by "$25 billion annually." This is advocacy or vendor material — [MO Rural Health Info Center](https://ruralhealthinfocenter.health.mo.gov/whitepaper-the-25b-medicaid-threat-how-hospitals-are-fighting-back)

**FQHCs**
- The CEO of Bluestem Health, a Lincoln, Nebraska FQHC, expects **10–15%** of the roughly 28,000 people who must report to be disenrolled. He says the financial effect won't be known until 2027 — [Nebraska coverage via search](https://www.1011now.com/2026/04/13/hospitals-warn-nebraska-medicaid-work-rules-could-strain-staffing-disrupt-care/)
- Neighborhood Health asked CMS to clarify how MCOs may contract with community health centers for outreach and navigation, and whether those costs count toward the medical loss ratio (MLR) — [InsuranceNewsNet](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/neighborhood-healthcare-urges-cms-to-revise-medicaid-community-engagement-rules-to-protect-vulnerable-patients-and-support-health-centers)

### Inferences
- **Value to an MCO per retained member.** Gross capitation is roughly $535 a member per month. [UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND] Medicaid MCO operating margins are typically low single digits, with administrative load around 8–12% of premium. On that basis, a retained member is worth roughly **$40–80 a month in contribution**, or about **$250–500 per six-month cycle**.
  - Members who drop for paperwork reasons skew healthy and low-utilizing (consistent with Centene's morbidity warning), so retaining them is worth more than the average margin implies.
  - That supports a fee of roughly **$25–100 per member retained per cycle**. This is an estimate only; I found no comparable price.
- **Hospitals.** Value is concentrated in high-acuity encounters, such as an uninsured inpatient stay. Most retained members generate little hospital revenue, so hospitals will pay for retention aimed at patients they already see, not for broad retention.
  - [UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND, from earlier notes] OBBBA reportedly shortens retroactive coverage starting in 2027. If true, hospitals can no longer fix coverage gaps after the fact, which makes keeping coverage continuous more valuable.
- **FQHCs.** [UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND] FQHCs are paid a per-visit PPS rate, often around $200 or more. A patient with three or four visits a year means roughly $600–1,000 in revenue at risk if the patient becomes uninsured or sliding-fee. FQHC budgets are thin, and their willingness to pay is likely in the hundreds of dollars a month per site, not per member.

### Gaps
- I found no actual state capitation rates for the expansion adult group in 2026.
- I found no MCO disclosure of a dollar value per retained member, or of retention spending.
- I found no Molina figure and no NACHC estimate of FQHC revenue at risk.
- I found no hospital-specific dollar losses from Nebraska or Montana so far.

## 3. Competitor landscape beyond YC

### Takeaway
The space is crowded on every side:
- **State side.** Ten large vendors, a free CMS tool (Emmy), and open-source kits from Nava and Code for America.
- **Member and MCO side.** Fortuna Health (YC- and a16z-backed, $22M, live MCO contracts), Findhelp with Uno Health (Findhelp already works with about 190 of 282 Medicaid MCOs), and Unite Us.
- **Smaller players.** Sky Island AI, Care Angel, mPulse, Drips, Genesys, Activate Care and Wider Circle.

The earlier finding of "no YC companies" was wrong: Fortuna is YC-backed.

## 4. Evidence of willingness to pay (MCO procurements, health-system budgets, unwinding-era comparables)

### Takeaway
Willingness to pay is real but indirect:
- **MCOs.** Fortuna's Highmark WV deal shows MCOs buy member-facing tools, but no prices are public. MCO involvement is optional under the rule, and the MLR treatment of these costs is unresolved.
- **Hospitals.** They are spending on staff (care managers and social workers) rather than on software, and they already pay contingency fees to Medicaid-eligibility vendors.
- **Unwinding comparables.** I found no 2023–2024 vendor retention prices or results.

### Cited Findings
**MCO purchases and the rule's limits on MCOs**
- The Highmark Health Options WV–Fortuna deal (Oct 2026) is an MCO buying member-facing work-requirement help — [News From The States](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/node/432252)
- **No direct MCO duties.** The IFR imposes no direct operational duties on MCOs. It sets guardrails on what MCOs may and may not do — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/cms-announces-new-medicaid-eligibility-requirements-implications-for-managed-care-plans)

- **Plan role is optional.** Plans "may be encouraged to support outreach" under future guidance — [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2146/2026-06-24-what-do-medicaid-community-engagement-requirements-mean)

### Inferences
- **The best pricing anchor is the hospital contingency model**: the hospital pays only when a patient's coverage is secured. That is a model providers already buy. A "per retained member" price fits hospitals and FQHCs more naturally than MCOs, because MCOs already hold platform contracts.

## 5. The open wedge for a 1–5 person team, the path to the first 10 customers, and price; plus kill criteria and verdict

### Takeaway
The broad MCO play should be killed:
- It is crowded, and the incumbents already have distribution.
- MCOs' role is optional under the rule.
- The 2027 self-attestation period softens year-one demand.
- Most MCOs have already chosen partners, with go-live about 10 weeks away.

The only plausible wedge is a provider-side "exemption packet" tool:
- **What it does:** draws on EHR and claims evidence to build state-specific exemption documentation (medical frailty, SUD treatment, serious mental illness, caregivers), plus six-month renewal alerts and a member SMS or voice agent.
- **Who buys it:** behavioral-health clinics, CCBHCs, SUD programs and FQHCs, starting in Nebraska, Montana, Iowa and Georgia.
- **How it is priced:** SaaS per site or per completed packet.

Even this wedge is conditional on the frailty litigation, the 2028 documentation cliff, and whether Findhelp or Fortuna add a provider module.

### Cited Findings
**Why providers are the opening**
- Montana's state agency lacked medical-claims access for exemption checks — [Flathead Beacon](https://flatheadbeacon.com/2026/04/30/state-health-department-begins-to-prepare-medicaid-patients-for-new-work-requirements/)
- The IFR limits frailty ex parte checks to 12 months of claims and allows frailty self-attestation only once per enrollment period after 2027 — [MACPAC](https://www.macpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/01_September-Slides-Community-Engagement-Requirements-in-Medicaid.pdf); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/healthcare/1799182/cms-issues-interim-final-rule-implementing-medicaid-community-engagement-requirements)
- The frailty code review is "onerous" (Nebraska) — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/medicaid-work-rules-whats-in-whats-out-and-where-hospitals-stand/)
- Nebraska expects 30–40% of cases to need manual verification and is not adding staff — [1011now](https://www.1011now.com/2026/04/13/hospitals-warn-nebraska-medicaid-work-rules-could-strain-staffing-disrupt-care/); [KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/a-closer-look-at-nebraska-the-first-state-planning-to-implement-a-medicaid-work-requirement/)

**Who is already organizing providers**
- The National Council for Mental Wellbeing (the behavioral-health association) has published H.R. 1 community-engagement and redetermination fact sheets and webinars, 2026 — [National Council](https://hub.thenationalcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Eligibility-Redeterminations-Fact-Sheet_v1.pdf); [National Council](https://hub.thenationalcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/H.R.1-Comm-Engagement-Requirements.pdf)
- Bluestem Health, a Nebraska FQHC, is publicly worried about disenrollment — [1011now](https://www.1011now.com/2026/04/13/hospitals-warn-nebraska-medicaid-work-rules-could-strain-staffing-disrupt-care/)

**Kill criteria checked**
- **Political and legal risk:** moderate. The statute stands, and the lawsuits target frailty provisions. The hearing is on 20 Oct 2026. Governors want delays, and extensions run up to 31 Dec 2028 in six-month steps — see Q1 sources.
- **Ex parte checks removing the need:** partial.
  - Ex parte checks come first by rule; Montana's labor department says more than 90% comply or are exempt; Emmy is free; and self-attestation is allowed in 2027 — see Q1 sources.
  - Against that, 30–40% of Nebraska cases are manual, and Louisiana got only 7% engagement — see above.
- **Crowding:** high at the MCO and state levels — see Q3.
- **Long MCO sales cycles and weak MCO mandate:**
  - MCOs have no direct duties and cannot determine compliance — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/cms-announces-new-medicaid-eligibility-requirements-implications-for-managed-care-plans); [Avalere](https://avalerehealth.com/thought-leadership/how-can-managed-care-organizations-prepare-for-medicaid-community-engagement-work-requirements/)
  - MCOs are exiting markets (Elevance DC, Centene Arkansas expansion) — [Georgetown CCF](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/08/13/medicaid-managed-care-the-big-five-in-q2-2026/); [Reuters via WDEZ](https://wdez.com/?p=972379)

### Inferences
**Verdict**
- **Downgrade the earlier #2 score** (a total of 41 out of a possible 60, across six criteria).
- The MCO-facing, per-retained-member version **fails the kill criteria on crowding and on the buyer's mandate**.
- A provider-side exemption-documentation wedge survives only as a **conditional, lower-ranked candidate**.
- The LTC Medicaid application idea in healthcare_life_sciences.md (C1) shares the same skills (state eligibility rules plus document assembly) and faces less funded competition. The report writer may want to compare the two.

**Precise wedge (if pursued)**
1. **Exemption-packet generator.** It reads the problem list, diagnosis codes and SUD or behavioral-health treatment status from the EHR, or from a CSV export. It maps these to each state's exemption criteria, including the IFR's frailty definition, and produces a signed clinician attestation plus supporting documents in the state's required format.
2. **Six-month renewal and "look-back month" calendar** for the clinic's Medicaid panel, using the clinic's own eligibility checks (270/271 eligibility queries) to flag who is due.
3. **SMS/voice agent** that collects pay stubs or hours from at-risk patients and pushes them to the state portal or to Emmy with the patient's consent.

Build only steps 1 and 2 first. Step 3 is where Fortuna, Care Angel and others already compete.

**First 10 customers**
- Start with state behavioral-health associations and state primary-care associations in Nebraska, Montana, Iowa and Georgia. These are the states where pain is visible before 2027, and where Montana's lack of claims access puts the evidence burden on providers.
- Use National Council webinars as the channel.
- Target CCBHCs and SUD treatment programs. Their patients are the most likely to qualify for exemptions and the most likely to fail procedurally.
- Offer free pilots through Jan 2027, then convert to paid before the 2028 documentation cliff.
- [UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND] Montana Medicaid is largely fee-for-service, so providers rather than MCOs carry the loss there. Verify this.

**Realistic price** (my inference; no comparable prices found)
- **$300–1,500 per site per month** for clinics, or **$15–40 per completed exemption or renewal packet**.
- For hospitals, a contingency or per-secured-coverage fee modeled on self-pay conversion vendors.
- MCO per-retained-member pricing ($25–100 per cycle) is theoretically supported by the economics, but practically blocked by incumbents.

**What would revive the broad thesis**
- CMS clarifies that MCO navigation spend counts toward MLR.
- Early-state data (Nebraska and Montana after the grace period) show large procedural terminations of people who are actually eligible.
- Fortuna or Findhelp stumble on delivery.

**What would kill even the wedge**
- An injunction that broadens frailty exemptions to ex parte or self-attestation only.
- Findhelp, Unite Us or Epic shipping a provider-side exemption module.
- States widely extending deadlines to 2028.

### Gaps

- I found no data on what share of disenrollments are frailty-exemption misses versus work-hour non-reporting versus six-month renewal paperwork.
- KFF mentioned "53% procedural" terminations, but the snippet was truncated and its context unclear. [UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND] In the 2023–2024 unwinding, roughly 69% of disenrollments were procedural (KFF).
