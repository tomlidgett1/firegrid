## Objective: Which specific, concrete problems would businesses clearly pay to solve where no great solution exists? (Candidate catalogue + top-3 ranking)

### Takeaway
The strongest openings combine three things: a new mandate with harsh financial penalties, document-heavy evidence work that LLMs can now do, and tooling that isn't mature yet. The best examples are (1) US FEOC/"material assistance" compliance evidence for solar and storage tax credits, which became mandatory on 1 Jan 2026 and can wipe out the whole credit, (2) subcontractor cash recovery (retainage, closeout and unpaid change orders), where the pain is huge and measured but Siteline, Clearstory and Procore Pay already serve part of it, and (3) installer-side utility interconnection and PTO paperwork. Permitting/plan review, takeoff/estimating, submittals, property-management AI and SMB carbon accounting are crowded with funded startups. Founders should avoid them or take only a sharp niche.

### Cited Findings

#### Candidate 1 — FEOC / "prohibited foreign entity" (MACR) compliance evidence for solar and storage tax credits (US)
- (b) Under the OBBBA, FEOC rules limit credit eligibility for projects that receive "material assistance" from a prohibited foreign entity starting 1 Jan 2026. The test is the material assistance cost ratio (MACR). The non-PFE share for solar starts at 40% in 2026 and for storage at 55%, rising over time. Non-compliance can mean 100% ITC recapture over 10 years. — [Anza Renewables](https://www.anzarenewables.com/blog/feoc-compliance-understanding-new-restrictions-for-solar-and-energy-storage-procurement/); [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/02/25/supplier-attestations-alone-wont-satisfy-feoc-scrutiny/)
- (b) Practitioners read IRS Notice 2026-15 (12 Feb 2026) as requiring developers to look past finished equipment into embedded components. Supplier attestations alone "won't satisfy FEOC scrutiny", and non-compliance can be applied retroactively years after commercial operation. — [pv magazine USA, 25 Feb 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/02/25/supplier-attestations-alone-wont-satisfy-feoc-scrutiny/)
- (b) SEIA's May 2026 presentation flags that Notice 2026-15 requires proof that interconnection costs fall below FEOC thresholds, even though utilities control much of that infrastructure and data. The Identification Safe Harbor does not cover qualified interconnection property. — [NY DPS / SEIA FEOC presentation, May 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/si-seia-qip-feoc-presentation-may-2026)
- (b) Guidance is still evolving. Treasury must publish more safe-harbor tables by 31 Dec 2026. — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/02/25/supplier-attestations-alone-wont-satisfy-feoc-scrutiny/)
- (c) Failing any one FEOC test means losing the whole credit, which is harsher than under earlier IRA rules. An Oct 2025 survey reportedly found more than 90% of respondents had started ownership reviews or supply-chain mapping, but only 38% felt "fully prepared" for 2026. (Search summary; the exact underlying survey wasn't verified.) — [Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/feoc-compliance-emerges-as-key-risk-for-us-clean-energy-credits-report)
- (a) It reaches residential too. With 25D gone (customer-owned systems lost the federal credit after 31 Dec 2025), residential is moving to third-party ownership, which relies on the commercial 48E credit. Wood Mackenzie expects TPO share to go from 45% (2024) to 70% (2026), and Ohm Analytics projects 69%. — [Mayer Brown, Jun 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/the-rise-of-third-party-solar-navigating-a-new-regulatory-frontier); [pv magazine, Dec 2025](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/12/17/us-residential-solar-faces-structural-shifts-heading-into-2026/); [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/prepaid-leases-data-center-der-enphase/808619)
- (a) Installer-facing service firms are publishing FEOC guidance aimed at installers. — [GreenLancer](https://www.greenlancer.com/post/feoc-rules-for-solar-installers)
- (d) Existing solutions: empact "NexusIQ" (launched as a "complete FEOC compliance solution" that bundles labor, supply-chain and FEOC evidence) — [North American Clean Energy](https://www.nacleanenergy.com/alternative-energies/empact-launches-industry-s-first-complete-feoc-compliance-solution-to-meet-obbba-requirements-for-clean-energy-tax-credits); Realizse (audit-ready traceability data for OEMs and suppliers) — [Realizse](https://www.realizse.com/blog/insight-series-clean-energy-oems-suppliers); Anza (proprietary FEOC risk data plus diligence services) — [Anza webinar](https://www.anzarenewables.com/webinars/inside-the-feoc-black-box-achieving-compliance-with-on-demand-due-diligence/). I found no clear startup built only for FEOC traceability for small and mid-size installers or EPCs (search summary).
- (h) Residential market contraction: forecasts for 2026 include Jefferies at -30% and the Enphase CEO at roughly -20%, with heavily leveraged solar-only installers at risk. — [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/prepaid-leases-data-center-der-enphase/808619)

#### Candidate 2 — Subcontractor cash recovery: pay-app errors, retainage/closeout collection (US; also UK/AU retention)
- (b/c) Siteline's May 2026 survey of 492 construction finance and ops professionals: 92% of subcontractors floated payroll from their own pocket while waiting for payment in the past year, and 28% did so most months. 43% of subs waited more than 90 days for final payment and retainage, against 15% of GCs, and nearly 1 in 5 subs wait 6 months or more. 59% said payment typically takes 46+ days. Pay-app errors and omissions were the biggest internal cause of late payment. — [Siteline](https://www.siteline.com/blog/92-percent-of-subcontractors-floated-payroll-last-year-new-siteline-report-finds); [GPA summary](https://gpa.net/blogs/top-posts/us-92-of-subcontractors-floated-payroll-in-2025)
- (b) Billd's 2026 National Subcontractor Market Report (600+ respondents): subs say they wait 51 days on average, while GCs estimate they pay in 35 days. — [Contractor Magazine](https://www.contractormag.com/management/news/55403190/subcontractors-are-still-financing-their-own-jobs-2026-survey-finds)
- (b, older) An earlier ENR survey of 640 subs found more than 30% of GC payments were late, by 36.4 days on average. — [ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/38953-subcontractors-report-longer-payment-delays)
- (e) New regulation: California SB 440 applies a mandatory claims-resolution and prompt-payment framework to private-works contracts signed after 1 Jan 2026. Owners face steep interest exposure (2% per month, per a law-firm alert). — [Nixon Peabody alert, May 2026](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/-/media/files/alerts/2026/05/construction_alert_re_ca_change_order_requirements.pdf); [Beancount guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/16/california-sb-440-prompt-payment-law-contractor-guide)
- (d) Siteline sells pay-app and lien-waiver software for subs. It generates pay apps in custom GC formats, integrates with ERPs and GC portals, and claims a 30% cut in invoice aging (vendor claim). Reviews are thin: 4 ratings on Capterra, and complaints about support not taking ownership and limited customization. — [Zoftwarehub](https://zoftwarehub.com/products/siteline/reviews); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/252004/Siteline/)

#### Candidate 3 — Change-order capture and recovery for specialty contractors (US)
- (b/c) In the Dodge and Clearstory 2026 study, only about one-third of GCs and specialty contractors say their change-order process works "very well". 91% of GCs report sometimes not paying the full change-order amount, mostly because of disputed pricing, incomplete documentation and scope disagreements. 96% of GCs say poor change-order visibility hurts cost control. — [Las Vegas Sun / Business Wire, Jun 2026](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2026/jun/23/only-1-in-3-contractors-say-their-change-order-pro/)
- (b/c) Clearstory reports that 83% of subs see a direct cash-flow hit from change orders that aren't fully paid, approval takes 26 days on average, and 66% wrote off change-order revenue in the last 12 months. Sample sizes are inconsistent across Clearstory pages ("nearly 200" vs "hundreds"). — [Clearstory webinar page](https://www.clearstory.build/webinar-the-2026-state-of-change-orders-subcontactors); [Clearstory reports](https://www.clearstory.build/industry-reports)

#### Candidate 6 — EV charger reliability: first-time charge success and regulatory uptime reporting (US, esp. California)
- (b) ChargerHelp's 2025 reliability report: self-reported uptime of 98.7–99%, but only 71% of charge attempts succeed (100k+ sessions, 2,400 chargers). First-time charge success rate is about 85% at new stations and below 70% by year three, and software overstates functionality. — [ChargerHelp report](https://www.chargerhelp.com/reliability-report); [CCJ](https://www.ccjdigital.com/alternative-power/article/15767696/ev-charging-report-why-success-rate-beats-uptime-in-2025)

- (e) The CEC EV Charger Data and Reliability Standards took effect 1 Apr 2026. They require 97% uptime per port with defined exclusions, semiannual uptime reporting for DCFCs, and OCPP 2.0.1 for chargers installed after late Sep 2026. #### Candidate 7 — Building performance standards (BPS) compliance and 2030 retrofit planning (NYC LL97, WA CBPS, Denver, EU EPBD)

The DOB commissioner said the penalty structure is "still a work in progress". The $268/tCO2e figure comes from consultants. The 2030 limits are much tighter and will push deep retrofits. — [NYC DOB release, Apr 2026](https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/dob/pr-ll97-improve-sustainability.page); [Planetizen, Oct 2026](https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/10/138592-nyc-building-owners-overwhelmingly-comply-new-emissions-limits); [Habitat, May 2026](https://www.habitatmag.com/Publication-Content/Green-Ideas/2026/May-2026/climate-mobilization-act-compliance); [Cotocon](https://thecotocongroup.com/blog/nycll97-guide/)

Extension rules weren't final. — [WA Commerce](https://www.commerce.wa.gov/?p=51300); [WA Commerce Tier 1](https://www.commerce.wa.gov/cbps/tier-1-compliance/)
- (e) Denver adopted updated Energize Denver rules (effective 27 Aug 2026).
