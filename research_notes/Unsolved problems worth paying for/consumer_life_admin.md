Research notes for the report writer. Scope: consumers and households (US, UK, Australia, Canada, EU). Research date: 18 Oct 2026.

## Which specific consumer problems are unsolved, high-WTP and buildable, and how do the top 3 rank? (Objective: candidate profiles and ranking)

### Takeaway
The best openings are high-stakes, one-off or crisis-driven bureaucratic fights where people already pay professionals: long-term-care financing and benefits for aging parents, estate administration by executors, and post-disaster property insurance claims. In each, AI agents that can make calls, fill forms and send documents shift the economics. Low-ticket "life admin" (subscriptions, bill negotiation, flight compensation, data-broker removal, auto-apply job tools) is already crowded or being commoditized by regulation, and should be avoided as standalone businesses.

### Cited Findings

#### C1. Estate administration: executors closing accounts, finding assets, probate (US, UK, CA, AU)
- (a)/(b) **Pain and frequency.** Empathy's Cost of Dying research says families take about 15 months to wrap up a deceased person's affairs, or 18 months when the person doing it is the executor — [Securian/Empathy](https://www.securian.com/employers/financial-wellness-360/empathy.html); [Hospice News, May 2025](https://hospicenews.com/2025/05/30/bereavement-care-company-empathy-raises-72m-in-series-c-round/). Families can spend 500+ hours on administrative, legal and financial tasks — [Hospice News, Jan 2024](https://hospicenews.com/2024/01/17/bereaved-families-face-serious-health-financial-hardships/). An earlier Empathy report put the process at about 13 months on average, or 20 months with full probate — [Empathy blog](https://empathy.com/blog/cost-of-dying).
- (c) **Money spent.** The 2024 Cost of Dying report found families spent about $12,616 on death-related expenses, with average legal fees of $2,788. 35% were not fully reimbursed from the estate — [Hospice News 2024](https://hospicenews.com/2024/01/17/bereaved-families-face-serious-health-financial-hardships/); [Jotup summary](https://www.jotup.co/node/2711161). The 2023 report (2022 survey, n=1,485) said bereaved families spend about $5,000 on legal matters — [Empathy Cost of Dying 2023 PDF](https://research.empathy.com/hubfs/Cost-of-Dying-2023.pdf). Note: different years use different categories, and a $12,702 figure is also cited for 2022.
- **UK institutional friction.** In a Which? survey of 1,600 executors, one in six said closing accounts took more than three months; during the pandemic the share taking over three months rose from about 17% to 40%. 38% had to send the same document (often the death certificate) to the same bank more than once. Banks lost death certificates and failed to close accounts — [Digit.fyi](https://digit.fyi/banks-are-failing-bereaved-families-with-costly-admin-errors-research-finds/); [Scottish Legal News](https://scottishlegal.com/articles/covid-19-bereaved-families-face-unacceptable-mistakes-from-banks-during-probate). These data date from the pandemic era. Rules on what institutions release without a grant of probate vary from bank to bank — [Everloved](https://everloved.com/articles/post-death-logistics/what-to-do-with-a-deceased-persons-bank-account/).
- **US first-person account.** An AARP writer spent "dozens of hours on hold" cancelling a deceased person's accounts, and some companies made it hard enough that a regulator had to step in — [AARP Alaska](https://www.aarp.org/states/alaska/estate-executor-listen-up/).
- **UK probate timing (2026).** HMCTS digital applications averaged about 4.3 weeks and paper about 16 weeks as of June 2026. Stopped digital applications averaged 13.7 weeks in May 2026, and practitioners report some taking a year or more — [ICAEW June 2026](https://www.icaew.com/regulation/regulatory-news/regulatory-news-2026-06/hmcts-update-for-probate-firms-june-2026); [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/surge-in-probate-cases-taking-more-than-a-year-can-you-avoid-delays-aTJfG8p1HeN1). HMRC IHT clearance can add 8–12 weeks (adviser estimate) — [MP Estate Planning](https://mpestateplanning.uk/probate-delays-how-to-chase-hmcts-and-hmrc-steps-to-take-when-waiting-times-stretch-in-the-uk/). One headline reports the probate application fee rose to £526 — [HJA](https://www.hja.net/?p=526324).
- (d) **Main competitor: Empathy.** Raised a $72M Series C in May 2025 led by Adams Street (with General Catalyst and Index), bringing total funding to $162M. It operates in the US and Canada and sells largely B2B2C: hospice provider Compassus and insurer MetLife are named, and employers offer it as a benefit — [Hospice News](https://hospicenews.com/2025/05/30/bereavement-care-company-empathy-raises-72m-in-series-c-round/); [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2025/05/29/exclusive-empathy-raises-72-million-series-c-to-tackle-the-agonizing-logistics-of-death). It raised a $47M Series B (Index) in March 2024 — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/empathy-47-million-funding/amp/). ClearEstate (Canada) is a separate firm in the same space — [ClearEstate](https://www.clearestate.com/blog/introducing-empathy-to-estate-bureaucracy).

#### C2. Long-term-care financing and benefits for aging parents (US Medicaid LTC; AU aged care; UK care funding)
- (a)/(b) **Scale.** There are 63 million US family caregivers. They average 27 hours a week, about 24% provide 40+ hours a week, and they spend roughly $7,200 a year out of pocket — [AARP press release, 24 Jul 2025](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html); [John A. Hartford Foundation / NAC data hub](https://www.johnahartford.org/resources/view/national-alliance-for-caregiving-caregiving-in-the-us-2025-report). Survey n=6,858 (Ipsos probability panel).
- **Complexity of US Medicaid LTC.** Typical individual asset limit is $2,000, with a 5-year lookback on transfers that can trigger penalty periods. Spend-down timing and documentation are elder-law-attorney specialties, and attorneys "often help applicants avoid denials on the first try" — [ElderLawAnswers](https://attorney.elderlawanswers.com/newsletter/link-to/45854/article/12594); [Littman Krooks](https://www.littmankrooks.com/elder-law/elder-law-medicaid-planning/medicaid-faq-questions/); [PolicyRisk wiki (2026 estimates)](https://policyrisk.com/wiki/medicaid-ltc-spend-down). Legal fees can themselves be part of spend-down — [VJ Russo Law](https://vjrussolaw.com/medicaid-spend-down-pay-for-more-than-just-medical-bills/). Community-spouse protections are about $154k in assets and $3,900/month in income (2026 estimate, unverified) — [PolicyRisk](https://policyrisk.com/wiki/medicaid-ltc-spend-down).
- (c)/(d) **Existing paid help.** Wellthy (human care coordinators who find providers, book appointments, contest medical bills and claims, arrange in-home aides and facility moves) sells mainly through employers, per-employee-per-month or per-use. Direct-to-consumer price is cited at $350/month in one source, and at $400/month (3-month plan) or $475/month (1-month plan) for 2026 in another — [Voya](https://voya.com/financial-professionals/workplace-solutions/retirement/concierge-support-for-caregivers); [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/wellthy); [HR Executive](https://hrexecutive.com/eldercare-emergency-how-employers-are-addressing-an-urgent-need/). Wellthy raised $25.5M — [ImpactAlpha](https://impactalpha.com/wellthy-secures-25-5-million-to-provide-caregiving-support-as-an-employee-benefit/).
- **Australia.** Fee-paying aged-care navigation and placement businesses exist. My Aged Care warns they are not regulated by the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission, and that fees are paid either by the family or by the provider — [My Aged Care PDF](https://myagedcare.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/Fee-paying-aged-care-navigation-and-placement-services.pdf). One placement firm charges $2,950 ex GST, paid by the provider, and says by law this cannot be passed on — [Aged Care Decisions](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/faq/). The new Aged Care Act 2024 and Aged Care Rules 2025 now apply. Sources conflict on the commencement date (1 July vs 1 November 2025) — [Aged Care Decisions](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/aged-care/new-aged-care-act-residential-aged-care/).

#### C3. Elder financial oversight and scam protection (US-led; global)
- (b)/(c) **Losses.** In FBI IC3's 2025 report, people aged 60+ reported $7.75B in losses (+59% year on year) across 201,266 complaints. The average loss was $38,500, and 12,400+ complainants lost over $100k each. Investment fraud accounted for $3.52B, tech/customer-support scams $1.04B, and romance/confidence scams $584M. Crypto-related scams hit 42,000+ older victims for $4.3B. 3,100+ complaints referenced AI, with losses over $352M — [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/older-adults-lost-billions-to-scammers-2026); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/fbi-seniors-cybercrime-2025/); [SOCRadar](https://socradar.io/blog/fbi-ic3-2025-internet-crime-report-10-takeaways/); [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/fbi-ftc-report-2025-losses). Overall IC3 2025: 1,008,597 complaints and $20.877B in losses.
- (d) **Competitors.** Carefull charges $12.99/month or $119.99/year (including credit/ID monitoring and insurance), with a $29.99/month tier that adds bill management and $1M ID-theft insurance. Family members get alerts through a "circle of care" — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/personal-technology/tools-to-avoid-elder-financial-abuse/); [Coverager (raised $16.5M)](https://coverager.com/carefull-raises-16-5-million/). It is shifting to B2B distribution: Nationwide, Schwab advisors (Carefull Pro), and bank/credit-union clients via CSI (March 2026) — [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/financial-technology/preventing-elder-fraud-nationwide-partners-with-carefull); [Schwab](https://advisorservices.schwab.com/provider-solutions/Carefull-Pro); [CUInsight](https://www.cuinsight.com/?p=202313).

#### C4. Post-disaster property insurance claims: underpayment, underinsurance, contents inventory (US; AU likely similar)
- (b) **Underpayment and underinsurance.** In one Marshall Fire (Colorado) study, 74% of claimants were underinsured and 36% severely underinsured (limits below 75% of replacement cost). An analysis of 2013–2020 California fires found homeowners typically received about 72% of what they were legally entitled to (secondary reporting) — [ColoradoBiz](https://coloradobiz.com/homeowners-struggle-with-inadequate-insurance-coverage-after-wildfires/); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/industry/new-wildfire-erupts-in-north-la-underpaid-insurance-claims-take-centre-stage). Florida's average homeowner claim denial rate was reported at 46.7% in 2024, attributed largely to hurricanes, and a Florida official said consumers often don't know AI is used to deny claims or set offers — [ABC News 4 / Sinclair, Oct 2025](https://abcnews4.com/news/nation-world/is-artificial-intelligence-unfairly-denying-insurance-claims-in-florida-hurricane-milton-florida-october-22-2025).
- (c) **What people pay professionals.** Public adjuster fees range from about 2% to 25%+ depending on claim size and complexity. Fees over about 15% are flagged as a red flag — [Bridgford Law](https://www.bridgfordlaw.com/fire/can-i-trust-a-public-adjuster-for-my-wildfire-claim/); [United Policyholders](https://uphelp.org/think-your-homeowners-insurance-payout-is-too-low-heres-how-to-push-back/). One PA firm says California caps PA fees at 10% on declared-disaster claims (unverified against statute) — [Noble PA Group](https://noblepagroup.com/blogs/california-wildfire-insurance-claim-help/). California DOI rules: fees apply only to additional money recovered, a 5-day cancellation right in declared disasters, and no solicitation until 7 days after the event — [CA Dept of Insurance](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/140-catastrophes/TopTenTips_WildfireClaimants.cfm).
- (d) **AI-native competitor.** Clayem matches policyholders with licensed public adjusters for denied or underpaid claims, contingency only (self-description, traction unverified) — [Hunted.space](https://hunted.space/dashboard/clayem).

- **Public alternative.** Maryland AG's Health Education and Advocacy Unit recovered about $2.6M for patients in FY2025, of which $1.6M+ came from appeals and grievances — [Maryland AG](https://oag.maryland.gov/News/Pages/Attorney-General-Brown-Announces-Health-Education-and-Advocacy-Unit-Saved-Nearly-$2.6-Million-for-Patients-in-FY-2025-.aspx).

### Inferences

#### Per-candidate assessment: (e) why unsolved / newly solvable, (f) market size, (g) feasibility and CAC, (h) risks
Market-size figures are rough and labelled with their assumptions. Population counts marked [unverified background] are from general knowledge and were not checked in this session.

**C1 Estate administration: LIVE, ranked #2**
- (e) It stays unsolved because each institution (banks, telcos, utilities, pensions, insurers, brokerages) has its own documentation rules (see the Which? data). The work is phone- and paperwork-heavy, and executors do it once, so they never learn it. LLM agents that hold an executor's authority, send certified documents, track each institution's case status and chase by phone and email go straight at the "38% sent the same document twice" and "dozens of hours on hold" pain. Empathy is well funded but mostly sells B2B2C through insurers, employers and hospices. A direct-to-executor, outcome-priced, done-for-you service looks like the open lane, especially in the UK and Australia, where Empathy does not operate. (Inference: the sources say Empathy operates in the US and Canada.)
- (f) Assumptions [unverified background]: about 3M US deaths a year, about 600k in the UK, about 190k in Australia. If 15% of estates pay $750–$1,500, US TAM is about $340M–$675M a year, plus about $70M–$135M in the UK.
- (g) An MVP is feasible in 6–12 months: an account inventory pulled from email, mail and statements, a library of institution-specific letters, an agent that calls and emails, and a tracker. CAC realism: buyers appear unpredictably and only once, so paid search is expensive. Better channels: (1) estate and probate lawyers and funeral homes, as B2B2C; (2) [unverified background] US probate filings are public records, which enables targeted outreach to newly appointed executors; (3) life insurers, as Empathy does. The key WTP lever is that executor expenses are generally reimbursable from the estate (the 35% "not fully reimbursed" stat cuts against this somewhat). That makes $500–$1,500 more palatable than a consumer subscription.
- (h) Risks: one-off use, so no recurring revenue; trust and identity verification (institutions may refuse an agent); unauthorized practice of law if it strays into probate legal advice; Empathy expanding direct-to-consumer; the UK's free bank-led Death Notification Service and the government's Tell Us Once [unverified background] cover some notifications.

**C2 Long-term-care financing and benefits for aging parents: LIVE, ranked #1**
- (e) It stays unsolved because Medicaid LTC (US) and aged-care means-testing (AU) are state- or program-specific. They need years of financial records (a 5-year lookback), precise spend-down timing, and repeated agency back-and-forth. Today families either pay elder-law attorneys or wait for a crisis. Wellthy-style human concierges cost $350–$475/month and mostly sell through employers. Newly solvable because LLMs can read 60 months of bank statements, flag lookback transfers, assemble agency-ready packets, and draft responses to requests for information. Open banking (Australia's CDR; US data aggregators) cuts down document gathering.
- (f) 63M US caregivers is the top of the funnel. A narrower wedge is families facing nursing-home or Medicaid LTC applications each year [figure not found; gap]. Rough guess: 500k US applications a year × $500–$2,000 = $250M–$1B. Australian aged-care entry adds a smaller market.
- (g) A small team is moderately feasible if it starts with one state or program. CAC: families arrive in crisis (hospital discharge, nursing-home admission). Channels: hospital discharge planners, elder-law firms (as a productivity tool, B2B), and nursing-home business offices. Nursing homes plausibly want applications approved fast to get paid (inference; verify). Hybrid "software plus human case manager" pricing seems likely.
- (h) Risks: unauthorized-practice-of-law boundaries (Medicaid planning is legal advice); 50-state rule variation; sensitive financial data and trust; one-off per family, though families often stay on for ongoing bills and oversight; Medicaid policy changes [unverified background: 2025 federal legislation altered Medicaid eligibility processes; verify].

**C3 Elder financial oversight and scam protection: PARTIAL, crowded at the monitoring layer**
- (e) Losses are enormous ($7.75B, $38.5k average). Monitoring products exist (Carefull, plus EverSafe [unverified background]), and distribution is moving to banks and advisors. The unsolved part is intervention and recovery after a loss: getting money back, bank disputes, reports, freezing accounts. Monitoring sells to adult children at about $10–$30/month, which brings consumer CAC and churn problems.
- (f) Large population (tens of millions of 60+ adults) but low price. Recovery services after a loss could command contingency fees on $38k-average losses, but crypto and investment-fraud recovery is often impossible, and "recovery scams" are themselves a known fraud vector (reputational risk).
- (h) Verdict: avoid monitoring as a standalone product. A post-loss "dispute and recovery case manager" for bank-transfer and card fraud is a possible niche (UK APP-fraud reimbursement rules [unverified background] create a claim path).

**C4 Post-disaster property insurance claims: LIVE, ranked #3**
- (e) It stays unsolved because total-loss claims require room-by-room contents inventories, replacement-cost proof and policy-limit arguments, all done while displaced. Public adjusters take 10–20% and quality varies. AI can now build contents inventories from phone photos, videos, email order histories and card statements; draft proof-of-loss packages; and track the insurer's correspondence and deadlines.
- (f) Episodic but large per claim. Many disasters a year (LA fires 2025, hurricanes, Australian floods). A contingency fee of 3–5% on additional recovery, or a $500–$2,000 flat fee, could be a $100M+ market. Hard to size without catastrophe-claim counts (gap).
- (g) The MVP (contents-inventory builder plus claim tracker) is buildable fast. CAC: geo-targeted outreach after each event, plus partnerships with United Policyholders, public adjusters (as a tool) and plaintiff attorneys. Demand is spiky, so revenue will be lumpy.
- (h) Risks: public-adjuster licensing (negotiating for the policyholder generally requires a license, so stay self-help or partner with licensed PAs); solicitation rules after disasters (California's 7-day rule); episodic demand; reputational risk from "disaster chasers".

**C5 Health insurance denials and medical bills: LIVE but crowding at the letter-writing layer**
- (e) Generating appeal letters is now commoditized: free nonprofit tools exist (Counterforce), and general chatbots are used. Claimable charges $50. The unsolved part is end-to-end execution (getting records, peer-to-peer coordination, external review filings, billing-office negotiation), and that is labour-intensive.
- (f) Huge (ACA denial rate of about 1 in 5 in-network claims), but WTP is weak: many denials are prior-authorization fights where no money has been paid yet, so there is no contingency pool to take a cut from.
- (h) Verdict: proceed only with a B2B2C channel (employers, patient-advocacy groups, pharma hubs; Claimable already has drugmaker deals). Not a top-3 direct-to-consumer pick.

**C6 VA claims: high WTP, high regulatory risk (wildcard)**
- (e) Veterans demonstrably pay large fees to unaccredited firms, which is strong WTP. A software or accredited-agent model that is cheaper and legally clean could take share. However, charging for initial claims without accreditation is unlawful, and the policy outcome (GUARD vs CHOICE Act) is undecided.
- (h) Regulatory risk dominates. If CHOICE-style legalization passes, a compliant AI-plus-accredited-agent firm could be very attractive. Monitor.

**C7 SSDI/SSI: served by a regulated professional market**
- The fee structure (25% of back pay, capped at about $9,200) is set by SSA, and Atticus is a scaled intermediary. The startup angle is B2B tools for disability law firms rather than direct to consumer. Not a consumer top pick.

**C8 IEP disputes (US): LIVE niche**
- Parents pay $1k–$7k+ per dispute stage, which is real WTP. Free advocates are overloaded. An AI "IEP copilot" (records review, drafting the parent's input, compliance tracking, dispute prep) at $200–$800 per case sits below advocate pricing. CAC through parent communities may be manageable, but many families are cost-constrained. Risk: districts push back; legal-advice boundaries.

**C9 NDIS reassessment wave (AU): time-boxed opportunity**
- The 2026 law creates a 3-year reassessment wave starting with budget resets from October 2026, and evidence preparation decides outcomes. Unknown: whether such a service can be paid from NDIS plan funds (gap). Without plan funding, WTP among participants may be low. Selling to support coordinators and plan managers (B2B) is more realistic.

**C10 Divorce and financial separation: PARTIAL**
- High spend ($4k–$23k). Online divorce at $150–$750 already exists. The unsolved part is financial disclosure assembly and asset division modelling, which open banking (Australia's CDR) could automate. Risks: unauthorized practice of law and emotional, adversarial dynamics. Medium attractiveness.

**C11 Childcare waitlists: weak software fit**
- The root problem is supply, not information; software cannot create places. WTP is small (waitlist fees of $20–$57). Avoid.

**C12 Immigration: crowded in skilled visas**
- Several 2024–2026 YC entrants target O-1, H-1B and EB-1. Consumer family-based immigration and naturalization may be less crowded (Boundless [unverified background] serves it). Unauthorized-practice-of-law risk is high. Not top-3.

**C13 Tradespeople: incumbent marketplaces; trust is not a software-only fix**
- Avoid, unless the angle is an AI "project manager or dispute handler" for a bad job.

## Which consumer problems involve high stakes (thousands of dollars or major life events) where people currently pay professionals or give up?

### Takeaway
Clear professional-spend markets exist in estate administration ($2.8k average legal fees, 500+ hours), long-term-care Medicaid planning (elder-law attorneys), disaster insurance claims (public adjusters at 2–25%), disability benefits (SSDI reps at 25% up to about $9,200; VA claim firms), IEP disputes ($1k–$7k+ per stage) and divorce ($4k–$23k). These are where the "pay a professional or give up" choice is sharpest.

### Cited Findings
- Estate: average legal fees $2,788, total death-related spend about $12,616, and 35% not fully reimbursed from the estate — [Hospice News 2024](https://hospicenews.com/2024/01/17/bereaved-families-face-serious-health-financial-hardships/).
- Medicaid LTC: attorneys handle spend-down timing and documentation and help avoid first-time denials — [ElderLawAnswers](https://attorney.elderlawanswers.com/newsletter/link-to/45854/article/12594); [Littman Krooks](https://www.littmankrooks.com/elder-law/elder-law-medicaid-planning/medicaid-faq-questions/).
- Disaster claims: public-adjuster fees of 2–25%+ — [Bridgford Law](https://www.bridgfordlaw.com/fire/can-i-trust-a-public-adjuster-for-my-wildfire-claim/). About 72% of entitlement typically received — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/industry/new-wildfire-erupts-in-north-la-underpaid-insurance-claims-take-centre-stage).
- SSDI: 25% of back pay, capped at $9,200 and indexed to COLA — [SSA](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/press/releases/2024-03-29-a.html).
- VA: unaccredited firms charge "exorbitant fees"; Louisiana allows fees up to $12,500 — [WHRO](https://www.whro.org/2025-12-02/firms-that-help-military-vets-with-their-disability-claims-can-charge-exorbitant-fees); [Montana Independent](https://montanaindependent.substack.com/p/states-go-after-claim-sharks-that).
- IEP: $1k–$3k for a meeting, $3k–$7k+ for a hearing — [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-a-special-education-lawyer-cost/).
- Divorce: $4.1k uncontested to $23.3k contested trial — [AI Lawyer](https://ailawyer.pro/blog/average-cost-of-divorce).
- Elder fraud: average loss for 60+ victims $38,500; 12,400+ lost over $100k (2025) — [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/older-adults-lost-billions-to-scammers-2026).

### Inferences
- The best wedges combine (1) a large one-off stake, (2) an existing professional fee to undercut, and (3) a document- and phone-heavy process that agents can now execute. Estate, LTC Medicaid and disaster claims meet all three.
- Where fees are statutorily set and reps are entrenched (SSDI), consumer disruption is hard. Selling tools to the professionals is the better play.
- "Give up" is common: low appeal rates for health denials (see Gaps) and 35% of estates not reimbursing expenses suggest money left on the table, but people who give up are often the least likely to pay.

## Which bureaucratic processes (government, insurers, banks, airlines) defeat ordinary people and could be handled by an AI agent on their behalf?

### Takeaway
The most agent-ready processes are multi-institution chase workflows with standard documents: notifying and closing a deceased person's accounts, Medicaid and aged-care applications with requests for information, insurance claim and appeal packets, disability-benefit evidence assembly, and NDIS reassessment and review evidence. Consumer agents (Pine, Duckbill, YC's Iris and Ara) have so far targeted low-stakes chores (bills, subscriptions), leaving the high-stakes processes comparatively open.

### Cited Findings
- Banks lose death certificates and fail to close accounts; 38% of executors sent the same documents to the same bank more than once — [Digit.fyi](https://digit.fyi/banks-are-failing-bereaved-families-with-costly-admin-errors-research-finds/).
- HMCTS has a formal two-step escalation for delayed probate (email, wait 10 working days, then an escalation form after 12 weeks, or 6 weeks after supplying requested info) — [MP Estate Planning](https://mpestateplanning.uk/probate-delays-how-to-chase-hmcts-and-hmrc-steps-to-take-when-waiting-times-stretch-in-the-uk/). This is a rule-based chase loop suited to automation.
- Pine (Series A $25M, Dec 2025, led by Fortwest) places calls, sends emails and operates software for consumers. It claims a 93% negotiation success rate and $3M+ saved; Xfinity, AT&T and Verizon are its most-contacted companies — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/pine-25-million-series-a-funding/); [Business Wire, Jan 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260114758958/en).
- Duckbill (human-in-the-loop AI assistant, $33M seed/A in 2023, Forerunner) — [Forerunner](https://forerunnerventures.substack.com/p/the-cq-delivering-on-one-of-techs); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/duckbill). Tempo (WhatsApp assistant) raised $4.5M in April 2025 — [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/tempo-raises-4-5m-for-whatsapp-assistant).
- YC consumer agents: Iris tracks what needs attention across accounts and pre-fills actions (1,200 downloads, 168 daily users, self-reported). Ara (P2026) is a personal agent runtime for files, emails and tasks — [YC directory](https://ycombinator.com/companies/industry/AI%20Assistant).
- Health: free AI appeal tools read denial letters plus policy text and produce tailored appeals — [Stateline via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/ai-vs-ai-patients-deploy-100051649.html).
- NDIS: internal review within 3 months, then ART; evidence of "significant and ongoing change" is required for early reassessment — [NDIS](https://www.ndis.gov.au/print/pdf/node/11731); [NDSP](https://ndsp.com.au/blog/ndis-news/ndis-plan-cut-after-reassessment-your-review-rights-explained/).
- Cautionary precedent: in January 2025 the FTC finalized an order (5-0) requiring DoNotPay to pay $193,000, notify 2021–2023 subscribers, and stop claiming it matches human lawyers without evidence — [AccountsRecovery](https://www.accountsrecovery.net/2025/02/12/robot-laywer-donotpay-to-pay-193k-in-settlement-with-ftc/); [CO/AI](https://about.getcoai.com/news/ftc-bans-donotpays-ai-lawyer-claims-and-orders-refunds).

### Inferences
- The DoNotPay lesson: broad "robot lawyer" consumer apps covering many low-value disputes failed on quality and on regulators' scrutiny of claims. Winning designs are narrow, high-stakes, outcome-verified, and include humans (attorney- or licensed-adjuster-in-the-loop where required).
- Agent feasibility depends on institutions accepting an authorized third party. Executors, powers of attorney and claim representatives already have legal authority structures, which makes these processes more agent-friendly than, say, airline customer service.
- Regulatory tailwinds are mixed. The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated (8th Circuit, 8 Jul 2025), which keeps cancellation hard and sustains demand for cancellation agents, but that space is already crowded — [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/7/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule). California has its own click-to-cancel law — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/node/15872).

### Gaps
- Status of the US open-banking rule (CFPB section 1033) in 2026 not checked; it matters for automated financial-record gathering in the US. Australia's CDR coverage for estates and aged care not checked.
- No data on whether banks, insurers or Medicaid agencies accept AI-voice callers acting under a power of attorney or executor authority.

## Where do adult children spend hours and money managing parents' affairs?

### Takeaway
US caregivers average 27 hours a week and about $7,200 a year out of pocket. The heaviest admin loads are long-term-care financing and Medicaid or aged-care applications, coordinating providers and bills, scam and fraud oversight, and finally estate settlement after death (500+ hours, 15–18 months). Paid help exists mostly through employers (Wellthy) or attorneys, and is priced out of reach for many.

### Cited Findings
- 63M US caregivers; 27 hours a week on average; 24% give 40+ hours; about $7,200 a year out of pocket — [AARP 2025](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html).
- Wellthy coordinators handle provider search, scheduling, contesting medical bills and claims, in-home aides and facility moves. DTC price about $350–$475/month; mostly employer-paid — [Voya](https://voya.com/financial-professionals/workplace-solutions/retirement/concierge-support-for-caregivers); [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/wellthy).
- Medicaid LTC: $2,000 asset limit and 5-year lookback — [Littman Krooks](https://www.littmankrooks.com/elder-law/elder-law-medicaid-planning/medicaid-faq-questions/).
- Australia: unregulated fee-paying placement and navigation services; families are advised to ask whether choices are influenced by provider-paid fees — [My Aged Care](https://myagedcare.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/Fee-paying-aged-care-navigation-and-placement-services.pdf).
- Elder fraud: $7.75B in losses for 60+ in 2025 — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/fbi-seniors-cybercrime-2025/). ### Inferences
- A "family office for aging parents" spanning the lifecycle (care financing, then ongoing bills and fraud oversight, then estate) could earn recurring revenue and lifetime value that one-off tools can't. The practical entry point is the highest-stakes crisis moment: a Medicaid or aged-care application, or an executor appointment.
- Placement referral models (provider-paid) create conflicts of interest, which My Aged Care explicitly flags. A family-paid, conflict-free navigator is a positioning opportunity, but it must overcome "free" (provider-paid) alternatives.
