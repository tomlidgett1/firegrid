# Australian Cross-Sector Demand Signals: Unsolved Problems Australians Would Pay For (signal-first, as of 18 October 2026)

**Method notes:**
- I used 22 web searches, the full budget. Every WebFetch attempt failed with DNS errors (seek.com.au, asbfeo.gov.au, lgnsw.org.au, civica.com, objective.com, xbert.io). All cited findings therefore come from search-result summaries of the linked pages. Spot-check numbers before publishing.

- Items marked **[Background, unverified]** are from my prior knowledge and were not confirmed in this round.
- The scope deliberately targets sectors between the other researchers' lanes: councils, NFPs and grantees, exporters, transport, mining services, and childcare.

## Which problems show up across several Australian signal types at once? (candidate list, top-5 ranking, traps)

### Takeaway
The strongest multi-signal problems sit in Australia's **"claims economy"**: money already owed or allocated to many small recipients, where the government is the counterparty, evidence is heavy and there is no API. The clearest cases:
- **Council disaster-recovery (DRFA) claims**: hundreds of millions overdue, approvals averaging 214 days in Victoria, and a parliamentary inquiry in NSW.
- **Fuel tax credit (FTC) under-claiming and retrospective recovery**: an ATO-measured A$345M net gap, about 87% of claimants are small businesses without systems, and councils already buy success-fee reviews.
- **Export Market Development Grant (EMDG) claims**: A$104.5M a year and an established, regulated consultant industry.

Separately, the **ASBFEO's caseload** shows two very high-volume SMB pains, digital-platform lockouts or disputes and late payment. Both are weak fits for a small software business: the first has no leverage over the platform and the second is crowded. **Public-sector FOI backlogs** are a credible Australian analogue of the US FOIA opportunity from the earlier round.

#### C1. Council disaster-recovery (DRFA) claim evidence and payment delays
- **NSW delays:**
  - LGNSW says payments totalling **hundreds of millions of dollars** are overdue to councils.
  - Its president Darcy Byrne gave evidence to a NSW parliamentary inquiry into the NSW Reconstruction Authority (March 2026).
  - Example: **MidCoast Council has received A$6.1M from the NSW Reconstruction Authority against a A$226.5M impact** on its transport and infrastructure.
  - LGNSW wants published data on claims received, approved and paid. These are advocacy figures.
  - Sources: [LGNSW media release, 20 Feb 2026](https://lgnsw.org.au/Public/Media-Releases/2026/0220-disaster-recovery-funding-delays.aspx); [Inside Local Government, 15 Mar 2026](https://insidelocalgovernment.com.au/lgnsw-president-to-give-evidence-to-parliamentary-inquiry-about-disaster-funding-delays/); [Orange City Life](https://www.orangecitylife.com.au/news/regional/local-govt-group-calls-for-urgent-release-of-disaster-funds-as-councils-face-financial-strain-pi5ncoxg)
- **Victoria:** the Victorian Auditor-General's report on the 2022 floods found the state took **an average of 214 days to approve flood claims, with a peak average of 429 days**, as reported by local news. — [Shepparton Adviser, Apr 2026](https://www.sheppadviser.com.au/?p=60137)
- **Federal DRFA changes:**
  - A proposed shift to a **50/50 funding model** would cut Commonwealth contributions (Barcoo Shire).
  - The LGAQ says Queensland cannot afford changes that reduce existing funding.
  - Sources: [Barcoo Shire release, 2026](https://www.barcoo.qld.gov.au/images/news/2026/MEDIA%20RELEASE_DRAFA.pdf); [Council Magazine](https://councilmagazine.com.au/councils-push-back-on-disaster-funding-changes/)
- **Claim process complexity:**
  - The NSW Reconstruction Authority has a **Director, DRFA Claims and Assurance** role, which covers quarterly expenditure claims and reconstruction estimates to the Commonwealth. — [NSW DCJ position description](https://dcj.nsw.gov.au/documents/reconstruction-authority/Director_DRFA_Claims_and_Assurance.pdf)
  - In Victoria, councils are "Delivery Agencies". Claims pass through four stages and are reviewed by both state and Commonwealth auditors. — [vic.gov.au DRFA info for assessors](https://vic.gov.au/drfa-information-assessors)
  - Victorian claim lodgement moved to a **half-yearly cycle** (December 2025). Councils must meet evidence requirements, and repaired damage must be directly attributable to the event. — [NDFA Bulletin 14, Dec 2025](https://www.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/NDFA-Bulletin-14-1-December-2025.pdf)
  - Victoria runs a state Claims Management System (CMS). — [NDFA Bulletin 10, Feb 2025](https://vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/NDFA-Bulletin-10-for-Councils-February-2025.pdf)
- **Evidence burden:**
  - NSW requires pre-disaster, damage and completion evidence.
  - Where pre-disaster photos are missing, a suitably qualified council professional must certify the asset's pre-disaster condition.
  - Source: [Transport for NSW fact sheet](https://www.transport.nsw.gov.au/system/files/media/documents/2023/Natural%20Disaster%20Fact%20Sheet%20-%20Pre-condition%20Certification.pdf)
  - Queensland expects photos with GPS, date and time metadata. Councils must check that external software carries the latest rates and templates for QRA's MARS system. — [QRA pre-season checklist](https://www.qra.qld.gov.au/pre-season-checklist)
- **Existing solutions:**
  - **Civica Recover** is a mobile and cloud damage-capture and DRFA submission tool. NSW set up a portal for Recover users in April 2020, and Civica claimed (in 2020) that no other mobile solution supports state and federal requirements. — [Civica Recover](https://www.civica.com/en-au/product-pages/recover/); [Civica release](https://civica.com/en-au/news-library/civica-streamlines-claims-process-for-nsw-bushfire-and-flood-affected-councils-using-new-recover-portal); [GovTech Review](https://govtechreview.com.au/content/gov-digital/news/civica-helping-nsw-councils-recover-from-fires-floods-770732268)
  - Councils also mandate field apps with offline sync and photo metadata. — [South Gippsland Recover app guide](https://www.southgippsland.vic.gov.au/info/20055/emergency_management/470/recover_app_training_guide)
  - **DCPM** (Brisbane) does DRFA project management for councils and claims to have handled over A$850M in funding (company profile claim). — [Datanyze profile](https://www.datanyze.com/companies/dcpm/358729639)

#### C2. Fuel tax credit (FTC) under-claiming and retrospective recovery (transport, mining services, construction, agriculture, councils)
- **ATO tax gap:**
  - Net FTC gap is **A$345M (3.7%) for 2023-24** and A$322.3M (4.3%) for 2022-23.
  - Over 96% of credits are claimed correctly.
  - **About 87% of claimants are small businesses that "may not have natural systems"** to manage claims.
  - Sources: [ATO FTC gap, latest estimate](https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/research-and-statistics/in-detail/tax-gap/fuel-tax-credits-gap/latest-estimate-and-trends); [ATO FTC gap overview](https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/research-and-statistics/in-detail/tax-gap/a-h-tax-gaps/fuel-tax-credits-gap/overview)
- **Retrospective window:** businesses can claim FTCs retrospectively for up to four years. — [Boxas](https://boxas.com.au/?p=28155)
- **Councils already buy recovery reviews on success fees:**
  - The LGA of South Australia's procurement arm lists a "special contract" for **FTC reviews by AIT Specialists on a success-fee-only basis**: a fee is payable only if a retrospective rebate is identified and collected.
  - The listing notes the four-year retrospective window.
  - This is the clearest Australian evidence of willingness to pay for an outcome found in this round.
  - Source: [LGA SA Procurement: Fuel Tax Credits](https://www.lga.sa.gov.au/lgaprocurement/contracts/special-contracts/fuel-tax-credits)
- **Mining:** heavy mining vehicles on private roads can claim about 38c per litre (vendor statement). Banlaw sells fuel-management and an FTC calculator focused on heavy/light vehicle separation. — [Banlaw FTC](https://www.banlaw.com/fuel-tax-credits/); [Banlaw calculator](https://www.banlaw.com/fuel-tax-credit-calculator/). Background on the scheme and its maximum rate (older): [ANAO audit](https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/fuel-tax-credits-scheme)
- **Existing software (competitor hunt):**
  - **Account Kit** (an Australian accounting-practice tool) has an FTC calculator that pulls fuel transactions from a client's Xero accounts, applies rates by start date and supports standard or simplified methods. — [Account Kit help](https://intercom.help/accountkit-a584a67b03c2/en/articles/17348106); [Account Kit support](https://support.account-kit.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003124434)
  - **Xbert** (practice-intelligence add-on for Xero) has a "Fuel tax credits may be unclaimed (AU)" alert. — [Xbert support](https://support.xbert.io/en/articles/15583349-fuel-tax-credits-may-be-unclaimed-au)
  - The **ATO's free calculator** handles original BAS, simplified claims and adjustments, but "doesn't consider specific rules that may apply to your business activity or fuel". Rates are indexed in February and August. — [Fully Loaded](https://www.fullyloaded.com.au/industry-news/1409/ato-has-apps-and-tools-for-fuel-tax-credit); [Xero FTC guide](https://www.xero.com/au/guides/fuel-tax-credits/)
- **Policy risk signals:**
  - The scheme is periodically attacked, for example by the Australia Institute and a Grattan proposal to halve it.
  - The government ruled out changes in early 2023 (older).
  - Sources: [Australia Institute](https://australiainstitute.org.au/post/government-digs-deep-for-mining-company-tax-rebates/); [Mining Weekly (2023)](https://www.miningweekly.com/print-version/miners-call-for-certainty-on-ftc-2023-02-09)
- **US analogue (venture-backed):** FuelCred claims more than US$1B a year in unclaimed off-road fuel refunds among US small businesses. This is a company claim and a US figure. — [AgFunder News](https://agfundernews.com/startup-spotlight-fuelcred-is-helping-farmers-reclaim-1bn-in-unclaimed-off-road-fuel-refunds)

### Inferences

#### Candidate profiles (a)–(h)
"Inferred" marks my judgment. Market sizes are order-of-magnitude.

**C1. Council DRFA claim evidence and packaging desk ("recovery desk for councils")**
- **(a) Who:** the asset or recovery manager, or DRFA coordinator, at rural and regional councils in disaster-prone LGAs (NSW Northern Rivers and Mid North Coast, Queensland, Victoria's north-east and Gippsland), plus state road agencies. Also engineering consultancies that prepare claims.
- **(b) Problem and frequency:** after each declared event, councils must prove pre-disaster condition, damage and completion for every asset, often thousands of road segments. They do this to state templates (QRA MARS, Victoria CMS, NSW RA), with half-yearly or quarterly claim cycles, then wait 200 to 400+ days. Cash-flow stress is acute. Events occur yearly somewhere, and each claim runs for years.
- **(c) Signals:**
  - LGNSW release and parliamentary inquiry.
  - MidCoast A$6.1M received against A$226.5M impact.
  - VAGO averages of 214 and 429 days.
  - A dedicated NSW RA claims and assurance director role.
  - A consultant (DCPM) claiming more than A$850M handled.
  - A vendor (Civica Recover).
  - DRFA reform fight (50/50 proposal).
- **(d) Existing solutions and gaps:**
  - Civica Recover handles field capture and submission.
  - State portals are submission endpoints, not preparation tools.
  - Consultancies such as DCPM and engineering firms are labour-based and percentage-priced (inferred).
  - **Gap (inferred):** AI assembly of evidence packs from existing council data. That data includes asset registers, historic Google/Nearmap/street-level imagery for pre-disaster condition, works orders and invoices, and photo-metadata validation. The output would be a claim-ready schedule per state template with exception flags before assessor review. This shortens approval time and reduces rejected line items.
- **(e) Why now:**
  - Payment delays have become politically visible through the NSW inquiry.
  - Victoria's half-yearly cycle and evidence emphasis.
  - DRFA reform would tighten evidence and cost-sharing.
  - Vision models can now assess pre/post condition from imagery.
- **(f) Market:**
  - 537 councils nationally [Background, unverified].
  - Perhaps 150–250 claim in any given year (inferred).
  - At A$20–100k a year, or 1–2% of claimed value prepared, a serviceable market of roughly A$10–40M a year (inferred).
  - NZ: 78 councils and post-Cyclone Gabrielle recovery funding [Background, unverified].
- **(g) Feasibility (6–12 months):**
  - **Moderate.** The founder's SQL, dashboard and AI stack fits back-office claim packaging.
  - Needs per-state templates, one design-partner council and possibly Australian data residency (Firebase/Vercel regions need checking).
  - Channel: IPWEA, LGNSW and LGAQ events, and LGA procurement panels (LGA SA Procurement already runs "special contracts", as for FTC).
  - Sell first to the consultancies (DCPM-type firms) as a productivity tool, which avoids council procurement.
- **(h) Risks:**
  - Lumpy, event-driven demand.
  - Long council sales cycles.
  - The Civica relationship.
  - DRFA reform could shift to upfront or pre-agreed payments, which reduces evidence burden.
  - Engineering judgment and certification must stay with humans.

**C2. Fuel tax credit retrospective recovery and ongoing claim desk**
- **(a) Who:**
  - Owner-operators and SME fleets in transport and logistics.
  - Civil and earthmoving contractors, mining services contractors and agricultural businesses.
  - Councils (graders, waste trucks).
  - Their bookkeepers and BAS agents.
- **(b) Problem and frequency:** credits are computed quarterly or monthly per BAS. Rates change twice a year, and eligibility varies by vehicle class (over 4.5t), road vs off-road use, activity and fuel type. Small claimants without systems under-claim or use the simplified method conservatively. A four-year retrospective window creates a one-off recovery opportunity per customer, plus recurring BAS work.
- **(c) Signals:**
  - ATO tax gap of A$345M net, with 87% of claimants small and "without natural systems".
  - A council-sector success-fee procurement contract (AIT Specialists via LGA SA).
  - Vendor activity (Banlaw, Account Kit, Xbert alert).
  - A US venture-backed analogue (FuelCred).
  - Total scheme size is implied at about A$9.3B a year (A$345M ÷ 3.7%; inferred arithmetic).
- **(d) Existing solutions and gaps:**
  - Account Kit computes from Xero transactions, but only for accountants and only from ledger data.
  - Xbert only flags.
  - The ATO calculator ignores activity-specific rules.
  - Telematics and fuel-management vendors (Banlaw for mining; Teletrac Navman, MTData [Background, unverified]) serve larger fleets.
  - Success-fee reviewers (AIT Specialists) are labour-based.
  - **Gap (inferred):** an automated four-year lookback that reconciles fuel-card statements, ledger and vehicle register (GVM, on/off-road), quantifies the recoverable amount and prepares BAS revision schedules for agent sign-off. Priced on success fee for the lookback and a small subscription after.
- **(e) Why now:**
  - The ATO publishes FTC gap estimates and has an ongoing compliance focus.
  - Payment pressure on transport SMBs (the top ASBFEO payment-dispute industry) makes cash recovery attractive.
  - LLM document extraction now handles messy fuel-card PDFs cheaply.
- **(f) Market:**
  - The net gap is A$345M a year, but its under-claim component is unknown.
  - If under-claims are even A$100–200M a year with a four-year window, a 20–30% success fee implies a one-off recovery pool of tens of millions and a recurring pool of a few A$M (inferred; fee % unverified).
  - NZ: road user charges and fuel excise duty refunds have an analogous admin burden [Background, unverified].
- **(g) Feasibility:** **High.** It is data plumbing (Xero/MYOB OAuth, which mirrors the founder's POS OAuth work), rules tables and dashboards. Channels:
  - Bookkeepers and BAS agents (co-branded, with revenue share).
  - Xero and MYOB app marketplaces.
  - Industry bodies (ATA, NatRoad, Civil Contractors Federation).
  - LGA procurement panels for councils.
- **(h) Risks:**
  - Tax-agent regulation: BAS revisions must be lodged or signed off by a registered BAS or tax agent (partner with one).
  - Overclaim liability (ATO audit).
  - Bundling by Account Kit, Xero or telematics vendors.
  - Political risk to the scheme.
  - The under-claim may be smaller than hoped; the net gap includes overclaims.

**C3. EMDG claim desk for exporters**
- **(a) Who:** SME exporters (A$0.5–20M turnover) in services, education, food and beverage, tech and tourism, plus their accountants.
- **(b) Problem and frequency:** annual application and progress or acquittal reporting of eligible promotional expenses across tiers, with eligibility rules and evidence trails. Each year's window is competitive because tiers close when funds run out.

I found no software product, but software search was thin. **Gap (inferred):** ledger-driven eligible-expense extraction from Xero/MYOB plus narrative drafting and a compliance checklist, at a fixed fee or a lower success fee than consultants.
- **(e) Why now:** Round 4 is funded through 2026-27, the scheme is demand-driven so speed matters, and a 2025 independent review may change rules (risk and opportunity).
- **(f) Market:** consultant fees are unverified; if 10–20% [Background, unverified], the fee pool is about A$10–20M a year. Capturing 10–20% gives about A$1–4M ARR. NZ analogue: NZTE International Growth Fund [Background, unverified].
- **(g) Feasibility:** **High.** It fits the founder's ledger integration and AI drafting skills. Channel: accountants, the Export Council of Australia, state trade agencies and Austrade events.

## Which repetitive roles are Australian businesses hiring at scale that AI agents could now perform end to end, sold per outcome?

### Takeaway
Seek counts could not be verified this round. The roles with the clearest evidence of being bought as **outcomes** (success fee or per claim) rather than salaries are claims and recovery roles facing government counterparties:
