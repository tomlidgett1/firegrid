## Which specific Australian family problems are unsolved, high-WTP and buildable, and how do the top 3 rank? (Objective: candidate profiles and ranking)

### Takeaway
The strongest Australian openings are paperwork-and-decision "outcome desks" around two forced, high-stakes life events:
1. **An ageing parent entering aged care.** Under the new Act the family faces a means assessment, a RAD/DAP decision, pension impacts, waits of about 200,000 people and wrong Support at Home statements.
2. **A death in the family.** The executor must chase super funds, banks, Centrelink and the ATO.

In both, families already pay professionals or provider-paid intermediaries: placement fees of A$1,500–A$2,950 per resident, aged-care advice at A$150–A$300/hr, fixed-fee probate at A$1,899–A$2,399, and up to A$30,000 for full estate legal work. Free government tools cover only fragments: My Aged Care, OPAN, the Australian Death Notification Service and AFCA. The third-ranked opening, catastrophe home-insurance claim advocacy, has record pain but heavier licensing and acquisition risk. NDIS, childcare, energy, health insurance, renting and tax are either crowded, covered by free services, or have weak willingness to pay (WTP).

### Cited Findings

#### C1. Residential aged-care entry: the means assessment, the RAD/DAP decision and pension impacts (adult children of a parent entering a nursing home)
- **(a)/(b) Who and what.** An adult child, typically 45–65, arranges a parent's move into permanent residential care. They must choose a home, decide how to pay the accommodation price (lump-sum RAD, daily DAP or a mix), complete the income and assets (means) assessment, and work out what happens to the family home and the Age Pension.
- **New rules make it harder.**
  - Since 1 November 2025, homes may keep a 2% retention amount per year from refundable deposits, capped at five years. DAPs are now CPI-indexed. Pre-November 2025 residents are generally grandfathered, even if they move facilities — [Challenger: To pay or not pay the RAD](https://www.challenger.com.au/adviser/knowledge-hub/Articles/To-pay-or-not-pay-the-RAD); [Challenger PDF](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/challengerg66c7-challengerprod-prod-4c11/media/Project/Challenger/Challenger/Documents/Articles/To-pay-or-not-pay-the-RAD.pdf).
  - A bill introduced on 1 July 2026 would restore the resident's choice of RAD, DAP or a combination during the interim period before the means assessment is finalised. As of the search it had not passed — [Challenger: 7 commonly asked aged care questions in 2026](https://www.challenger.com.au/adviser/knowledge-hub/Articles/7-commonly-asked-aged-care-questions-in-2026).
  - The rules keep moving, so families cannot rely on last year's advice.
- **Means-tested fee thresholds (single source, March 2026, verify):** residents with income over about A$34,762 a year and/or assets over A$64,500 pay a means-tested fee — [Aged Care Decisions fees cheat sheet](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/acd-fees-for-residential-aged-care-cheat-sheet/); [Superior Care fee calculator](https://www.superiorcare.com.au/blog/aged-care-fee-calculator/).
- **Real price lists are complex.** Benetas publishes July 2026 room-type, fee and charges sheets for each home — [Benetas Gisborne Oaks July 2026](https://www.benetas.com.au/files/documents/Benetas-Room-Types-Fees-and-Charges-July-2026-Gisborne-Oaks.pdf); [Benetas Eaglehawk July 2026](https://www.benetas.com.au/files/documents/Benetas-Room-Types-Fees-and-Charges-July-2026-Eaglehawk.pdf).
- **Waits.**
  - The inaugural Wait Times Report (November 2025 to March 2026) gave an average of **396 days** from application through My Aged Care to receiving ongoing residential care, and 434 days for short-term residential care. This comes via a secondary news source — [Weekly SOURCE, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/latest-government-data-reveals-close-to-200000-older-australians-still-waiting-for-aged-care/); [GEN timeliness publication, July 2026](https://www.gen-agedcaredata.gov.au/Resources/Publications/2026/July/timeliness-of-access-to-aged-care-services-in-australia).
  - Caveat: this metric probably includes people who apply long before they need care.
- **No current processing time for the Services Australia means assessment was found.** The only result was an old Department of Human Services-era article citing about six weeks — [Superior Care](https://www.superiorcare.com.au/blog/how-long-does-acat-assessment-take/) (old; see Gaps).
- **(c) WTP evidence.**
  - **Placement consultants are paid by providers.**
    - Aged Care Decisions lists a placement fee of **A$2,950 ex GST**, paid by the provider on a successful placement. It claims the family pays nothing and that providers cannot pass the fee on — [Aged Care Decisions FAQ](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/faq/); [Fees Fact Sheet](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/wp-content/uploads/Fees-Fact-Sheet.pdf).
    - A Gold Coast placement service charges providers **A$1,500 per new resident** — [Weekly SOURCE, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/gold-coast-aged-care-placement-service-cashes-in-charging-providers-1500-for-new-residents).
    - My Care Path (placement consultants listed on agedcareguide.com.au) says it has helped **8,000+ families** — [Aged Care Guide: My Care Path](https://www.agedcareguide.com.au/aged-care-placement).
  - **Paid advocates and advisers.**
    - A commercial advocacy site lists paid aged-care advocate rates of A$100–A$190/hr for an initial consultation, A$100–A$180/hr for complaint preparation and navigation, and **A$150–A$300/hr for specialist aged-care financial advice**. The same table repeats across unrelated suburb pages, so it is likely a template rather than researched pricing — [Carevo advocacy pages](https://carevo.com.au/services/aged-care-advocacy/miles).
    - Specialist aged-care financial advisers advertise flat-fee advice covering accommodation costs, maximising Centrelink/DVA pension, asset structuring and whether to keep or sell the family home. Examples are Alteris Financial Group, ACA PrimeCare Financial Planning, Sage Care Advice (which also negotiates fees and checks residential agreements) and Anthony Brosnan CFP (fee-for-advice since 1994). No dollar figures are published — [Aged Care Guide: Alteris](https://www.agedcareguide.com.au/alteris-financial-group); [ACA PrimeCare](https://www.agedcareguide.com.au/primecare-financial-planning); [FAAA find-a-planner](https://find-a-planner.faaa.au/sa/adelaide/anthony-brosnan-cfp/52225); [Aged Care Guide Perth financial listings](https://www.agedcareguide.com.au/financial/perth-wa).
    - An adviser article says only about **18% of Australians seek advice on aged care**. The exact source page is uncertain: it was in a result set with Fiducian and HLB pages — [Fiducian](https://www.fiducian.com.au/posts/role-of-a-financial-adviser-in-aged-care); [HLB](https://hlb.com.au/understanding-the-ins-and-outs-of-aged-care/).
  - **[Background, unverified]** A full statement of advice from an aged-care specialist adviser typically costs about A$2,000–A$5,000. Aged Care Steps is a technical, training and accreditation network for such advisers, and Rachel Lane's Aged Care Gurus is a well-known adviser and author brand. Rachel Lane also appears as an author on agedcaredecisions.com.au — [Aged Care Decisions author page](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/author/rachel-lane/).
- **(d) Free alternatives.**
  - My Aged Care (1800 200 422) is the free entry point — [Aged Care Decisions page citing My Aged Care](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/?p=38754).
  - OPAN offers free, government-funded advocacy (1800 700 600) — [My Aged Care PDF](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/sites/default/files/attachments/21/2-2349FEL1.pdf).
  - My Aged Care's own guidance warns that fee-paying navigation and placement services are **not regulated by the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission**. It says families should ask who pays the consultant (provider or family) and whether that biases recommendations. The guidance dates from 2023 — [My Aged Care PDF](https://myagedcare.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/Fee-paying-aged-care-navigation-and-placement-services.pdf) [Prior notes].
  - **[Background, unverified]** Services Australia's Financial Information Service (FIS) gives free general financial information. The government-funded Care Finder program helps vulnerable people without family support. Fee estimators exist on My Aged Care and from advisers and Challenger.

#### C2. Support at Home (home care) navigation, waits and statement checking (families of the roughly 365k people receiving home care and the roughly 107k waiting)
- **Scale of waiting.**
  - 82,656 people were waiting for an aged-care assessment at 30 June 2026. The national median assessment wait was 26 days, and 37 days for community comprehensive assessments.
  - 106,977 people were waiting for their Support at Home package as of 10 August 2026. That makes almost **190,000** older Australians waiting for an assessment or for funding.
  - Ian Yates estimates up to 30,000 more awaiting reassessment have been excluded from the figures, putting the real figure near **200,000**.
  - Sources: [Weekly SOURCE, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/latest-government-data-reveals-close-to-200000-older-australians-still-waiting-for-aged-care/); [Weekly SOURCE, Ian Yates "a public policy failure", 25 Aug 2026](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/support-at-home-a-public-policy-failure-ian-yates/).
- **Earlier figures.** At 31 March 2026 there were 364,723 people accessing ongoing Support at Home services, 100,191 waiting and 950 approved providers — [GEN Q3 2025-26 report](https://www.gen-agedcaredata.gov.au/getmedia/88363448-d30b-4958-8823-df697cb5f769/Support-at-Home-Program-Data-Report-Q3-2025-26-corrected) [Prior notes]. Referrals on hand at 31 March 2026 were 98,606, with a 25-day median assessment wait for January–March 2026 — [GEN timeliness](https://www.gen-agedcaredata.gov.au/resources/publications/2026/july/timeliness-of-access-to-aged-care-services-in-australia).
- **Systemic problems are now national news.**
  - ABC Four Corners highlighted systemic problems with Support at Home in August 2026 — [Medianet, Aug 2026](https://newshub.medianet.com.au/2026/08/abc-four-corners-highlights-systemic-problems-with-support-at-home-aged-care-program/166753/).
  - OPAN raised concerns about delays, prioritisation and the "controversial algorithm" in the Integrated Assessment Tool — [Weekly SOURCE, Senate Estimates takeaways, 25 Aug 2026](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/the-top-10-aged-care-takeaways-from-senate-estimates/).
  - A national RVRA survey found Support at Home confuses retirement village residents — [Weekly SOURCE, 21 Jul 2026](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/rvra-national-survey-reveals-support-at-home-confuses-retirement-village-residents/).
  - COTA issued a waitlist and assessment media release in June 2026 — [COTA](https://cota.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/COTA-MR-0626-waitlist-and-assessment-final.pdf).
  - National Seniors wants the waitlist cut to three months by 1 January 2027 — [National Seniors budget submission](https://nationalseniors.com.au/uploads/0120266691PAR-2026FederalBudget-Recommendations-A4-011-210x297-HomeCare.pdf).
- **Statements are frequently wrong.**
  - The Department's September 2026 payment-integrity review found monthly-statement problems at **51 of 60 providers**. A pricing review attributed most apparently high prices to incorrect claiming — [Department summary report](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/support-at-home-payment-integrity-rolling-review-cycle-1-summary-report.pdf); [pricing review](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/support-at-home-payment-assurance-pricing-review-summary-report.pdf) [Prior notes].
  - From 1 October 2026 personal-care contributions fall to zero, but services delivered before 1 October still attract them. That creates a cut-over error risk on statements — [Hellocare](https://hellocare.com.au/how-support-at-homes-personal-care-funding-changes-from-october-2026/) [Prior notes].
  - A 10% care-management fee is deducted from the quarterly budget (from an advocacy site; verify) — [Carevo](https://carevo.com.au/services/aged-care-advocacy/anula).
- **Competitors and alternatives.** OPAN is free. Commercial advocates include Carevo (paid hourly), which publishes wait-time guides — [Carevo wait times](https://carevo.com.au/blog/support-at-home-wait-times). Provider care managers are paid from the 10% care-management deduction.

#### C3. Estate administration for executors: post-death logistics beyond the grant (accounts, super, Centrelink, ATO)
- **(a)/(b) Who and what.** An adult child or spouse is named executor, usually with no professional executor. They must:
  - notify and close bank, utility and telco accounts;
  - claim super death benefits (which sit outside the estate);
  - notify Centrelink, Medicare and the ATO;
  - register as legal personal representative with the ATO and lodge a date-of-death return, plus a trust return where needed;
  - apply for probate where assets require it;
  - distribute the estate while carrying personal liability.
- **Sources for those tasks.**
  - Banks each have their own forms. NAB requires each estate representative to complete its form, and Bank Australia closes accounts once documents and instructions arrive — [NAB form](https://www.nab.com.au/content/dam/nabrwd/documents/forms/banking/finalising-deceased-estate.pdf); [Bank Australia](https://bankaust.com.au/support/deceased-estates); [Beyond Bank guide](https://brand.beyondbank.com.au/m/57fe6460c189a4a9/original/Deceased-Estates-Information-Guide.pdf).
  - The LPR must register with the ATO before accessing records or refunds. One guide says date-of-death returns must use ATO paper forms rather than online lodgement. That may be outdated for tax-agent lodgement — [Bare: ATO and capital gains for executors](https://bare.com.au/blog/tax-return-ato-capital-gains-on-deceased-estate-executor); [Willed: lodge a tax return for a deceased estate](https://www.willed.com.au/guides/lodge-tax-return-deceased-estate); [H&R Block](https://www.hrblock.com.au/tax-academy/deceased-estate-tax-return-australia).
  - The Inspector-General of Taxation's 2021 "Death and Taxes" investigation examined the ATO's systems for deceased estates (old) — [IGT report](https://www.igt.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/065_Death-and-Taxes-An-Investigation-into-ATO-Systems-and-Processes-for-Dealing-with-Deceased-Estates.pdf); [IGT implementation update, Aug 2022](https://www.igt.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Death-and-Taxes-Implementation-Update-August-2022.pdf).
  - Executors carry personal liability for mistakes — [Willed: after probate](https://www.willed.com.au/expert-answers/what-does-an-executor-need-to-do-after-probate-is-granted-in-australia).
- **(c) WTP evidence (AUD).**
  - **Bare Law** sells fixed-fee probate at about A$1,899 (letters of administration) to A$1,999 or A$2,399 inc. GST (grant of probate), plus government fees. The fee is due only once the executor can access estate assets, and Bare offers a 10% price-beat promise — [Bare probate](https://bare.com.au/probate); [Bare deceased estates](https://app.bare.com.au/deceased-estates).
  - A cost guide puts a probate lawyer's fixed fee at about A$800–A$2,000, but the full grant-to-administration cost at **A$2,500–A$30,000**. Court filing fees range from A$0 (ACT estates under A$50k) to **over A$17,500 in Victoria** for high-value estates — [JustFund cost guide](https://justfund.com.au/cost-to-administer-a-deceased-estate-in-australia/).
  - Public trustees typically charge a percentage of the estate plus ongoing administration fees — [Willed: alternatives to the Public Trustee](https://www.willed.com.au/expert-answers/what-are-the-most-affordable-alternatives-to-using-the-public-trustee-for-wills-and-estate-management-in-australia); [Willed: legal fees](https://www.willed.com.au/expert-answers/how-much-do-legal-fees-cost-for-managing-a-deceased-estate-in-australia).
  - Professional executor and trustee options include Australian Unity Trustees, the Public Trustee Queensland, ADLV Law (acts as professional executor) and **Sage Executor Solutions**, which "coordinates and manages the deceased estate administration workload" and is listed in the ACT Seniors Card discount directory — [Australian Unity brochure (2023)](https://Australianunity.com.au/wealth/-/media/RebrandWealth/Trustees-And-Advice/Brochures/Trustee/Estate_administration_overview_brochure_2023.pdf); [Public Trustee Qld](https://pt.qld.gov.au/deceased-estates/administering-an-estate); [ADLV Law](https://adlvlaw.akebbell.com/?p=48282); [ACT Seniors Card: SAGE Executor Solutions](https://actseniorscard.org.au/index.php/act-seniors-card-discount-directory/item/sage-executor-solutions).
- **(d) Free government alternative: the Australian Death Notification Service (ADNS).**
  - The ADNS is free, launched in 2021 and supported by all states. It is aimed at executors, administrators or people acting for them. It needs a registered death with BDM, and selected organisations contact the notifier within 10 business days.
  - **Coverage is limited and sources conflict:** one says 14 participating organisations (7 of them banks), another says about 60 — [Tasmanian BDM](https://www.justice.tas.gov.au/bdm/australian-death-notification-service); [IDEAS](https://www.ideas.org.au/blogs/australian-death-notification-service.html); [Palliative Care NSW](https://palliativecarensw.org.au/?p=6404); [Bega Valley Council](https://www.begavalley.nsw.gov.au/Our-Community/Cemeteries-burial-and-cremation/Australian-death-notification-service).
  - Services Australia publishes a separate "who to notify" checklist (January 2026) for organisations outside the ADNS, such as the ATO, Centrelink and Medicare — [Services Australia checklist](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-01/03-who-to-notify-checklist.docx).
- **Empathy (US) in Australia: no evidence found.**
  - A search for Empathy and Australian estate administration returned no Australian operation. A US comparison site describes Empathy as distributed through life insurers and employers — [Elayne comparison](https://www.elayne.com/resources/best-probate-support-services-for-executors).
  - Prior notes: Empathy operates in the US and Canada and has raised US$162M — [Hospice News](https://hospicenews.com/2025/05/30/bereavement-care-company-empathy-raises-72m-in-series-c-round/) [Prior notes].
- **Global pain benchmark.** In the US, families take about 15 months (18 for executors) and can spend 500+ hours. In a Which? survey of UK executors, 38% had to send the same document to the same bank more than once (pandemic-era data) — [Hospice News](https://hospicenews.com/2024/01/17/bereaved-families-face-serious-health-financial-hardships/); [Digit.fyi](https://digit.fyi/banks-are-failing-bereaved-families-with-costly-admin-errors-research-finds/) [Prior notes]. No equivalent Australian hours survey was found (see Gaps).

#### C4. Superannuation death benefit claims and binding nominations (executors and beneficiaries chasing super funds)
- **(b) Pain: ASIC's evidence.**
  - ASIC Report 831 (June 2026) reviewed 45 trustees. It found uneven progress: some trustees "failed to make basic changes aimed at reducing delays".
  - Internal complaints about delays fell 53% between early 2024 and late 2025. But **death benefit claim volumes rose 10% in the 12 months to October 2025** and are expected to keep rising.
  - ASIC wants funds to measure end-to-end claim times, take care with "claims staking" on low-value claims, and proactively help members make valid binding death benefit nominations.
  - Sources: [RiskInfo, 10 Jun 2026](https://riskinfo.com.au/news/2026/06/10/asic-warns-super-funds-over-benefit-delays/); [Super Review](https://www.superreview.com.au/asic-warns-lagging-super-funds-on-death-benefits-claims/); [Regulation Tomorrow on REP 831](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/06/rep-831-delivering-on-death-benefits-have-super-trustees-stepped-up/).
- **Enforcement.**
  - The Federal Court ordered **Cbus to pay A$23.5m** (November 2025) over delays affecting more than 7,000 death and TPD claims.
  - ASIC alleges **AustralianSuper took between four months and four years** to assess at least 6,897 death benefit claims. The case was ongoing, with a hearing listed for 29 May 2026; current status unknown — [ASIC 25-034MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2025-releases/25-034mr-asic-sues-australiansuper-alleging-significant-death-benefit-claims-failures); [Super Review: ASIC sues AustralianSuper again](https://www.superreview.com.au/asic-sues-australiansuper-again-over-delays-death-benefit-claims/).
  - Government has proposed mandatory member services standards — [RiskInfo](https://riskinfo.com.au/news/2026/06/10/asic-warns-super-funds-over-benefit-delays/).
- **Tax stakes.** Death benefits paid to non-dependants are taxed up to 32% on the taxable component. Advice is recommended for SMSFs — [Willed guide](https://www.willed.com.au/guides/lodge-tax-return-deceased-estate); [ITP](https://itp.com.au/what-are-the-tax-implications-for-deceased-estates/).
- **Competitors.** No Australian service was found that specifically chases super death benefit claims for families. Lawyers handle disputed claims (background), and AFCA is free; super insurance claim-rejection complaints rose 82% in 2025-26 — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/claims/insurer-claim-delays-top-complaints-again-as-brokers-report-no-change-589274.aspx).

#### C5. NDIS participants: budget resets, reassessments and appeals (participants and families, support coordinators)
- **Timeline (government sources).**
  - Budgets for social, civic and community participation and improved daily living skills supports are being reset for all participants between **1 October 2026 and 30 September 2027**. For existing participants the reset is applied at plan reassessment, and participants are told how much their funding was reduced.
  - Participants can request an unscheduled reassessment if they meet the criteria.
  - **New framework planning has been delayed to 1 April 2027.**
  - Plan rollover changes start 1 February 2027. Access changes start 1 January 2028, with existing participants reassessed over three years.
  - Sources: [health.gov.au funding changes](https://www.health.gov.au/resources/publications/ndis-supports-funding-changes); [Securing the NDIS timeline PDF, July 2026](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/securing-the-ndis-for-future-generations-timeline.pdf); [abilityNEWS](https://www.abilitynews.org/p/timeline-for-ndis-reform-set).
  - The Securing the NDIS for Future Generations Bill received Royal Assent on 20 August 2026 — [NDIS](https://www.ndis.gov.au/print/pdf/node/11731) [Prior notes].
- **Appeal avenue narrowed.** The Guardian (4 December 2025, via DARU) reports that participants will in most cases no longer be able to appeal their total funding amount to any external review body. A related summary says the Tribunal can no longer alter a plan or reinstate funding — [DARU repost of Guardian](https://daru.org.au/resource/most-ndis-participants-will-lose-external-avenue-to-appeal-funding-amounts-under-new-system-senate-estimates-told/). This **reduces the value of an appeals-focused outcome desk**.
- **Internal review then Tribunal** remains the process for reviewable decisions — [NDIA guideline](https://www.ndis.gov.au/print/pdf/node/8572). The NDIS Appeals Program provides free advocacy — [NDSP](https://ndsp.com.au/blog/ndis-news/ndis-plan-cut-after-reassessment-your-review-rights-explained/) [Prior notes].
- **Competitors.** Provider software (Brevity, Flowlogic, Leapin) already publishes participant guides on the 2026 changes, and free advocacy exists — [Leapin](https://www.leapin.com.au/?p=6094); [Brevity](https://www.brevity.com.au/?p=8172); [Flowlogic](https://flowlogic.com.au/?p=77122).

#### C6. Centrelink and Services Australia: Age Pension and Carer Payment claims, debts
- **Processing times (mostly 2024–25; no 2026 data found).**
  - Age Pension claims averaged 84 days in January–March 2025. A ministerial release says this fell by more than 40%, with most claims now within the 49-day KPI — [Former Minister Shorten release](https://formerministers.dss.gov.au/?p=20559).
  - In mid-2024 SBS reported averages of 84 days for Age Pension, 58 days for Carer Payment and 41 days for all claims — [SBS](https://phoenix.alego.edsqa01.aws.sbs.com.au/news/article/centrelink-payment-times-the-claims-most-likely-to-be-processed-late/qzb3d0deg).
  - The Nightly reported an Age Pension backlog of more than 30,000 claims (undated) — [The Nightly](https://thenightly.com.au/business/nick-bruining-centrelink-backlog-for-new-age-pension-applications-tops-more-than-30000-source-says-c-12803943).
  - Age Pension approval time rose from 33 days (2021/22) to 61 days (FY to August 2023) — [The Senior (2023)](https://www.thesenior.com.au/story/8450304/centrelink-approval-time-doubles-frustrating-seniors/).
  - National Seniors reported seniors out of pocket from delays — [National Seniors](https://nationalseniors.com.au/news/news/seniors-out-of-pocket-as-centrelink-struggles-to-cope).
- **Income-apportionment debts (the "robodebt aftermath").**
  - The Ombudsman found income apportionment unlawful in 2023. About 91% of affected debts (**A$3.93bn**) have been repaid, and **170,000 debts totalling A$347m** remain outstanding.
  - A compensation scheme of up to **A$600** per person opened in January 2026 and closes **29 January 2027**.
  - The Federal Court's Chaplin decision (July 2025) concerned recovery remedies.
  - Wording caveat: one syndicated article says up to 2 million recipients were "underpaid" between 2003 and 2020. That is likely a wording error for "affected", so verify.
  - Sources: [ACM syndicated, The Senior](https://www.thesenior.com.au/story/9334284/centrelink-pension-debt-review-how-to-challenge-and-claim-refund/); [The New Daily, Jul 2025](https://www.thenewdaily.com.au/news/national/2025/07/28/robodebt-federal-court-welfare).
- **Competitors.** **[Background, unverified]** Welfare rights centres and Economic Justice Australia are free, Services Australia's FIS is free, and financial advisers help asset-holding retirees with Age Pension claims. Many affected people have low incomes, so direct WTP is low.

#### C7. Home and car insurance claims and disputes after floods, storms and bushfires

### Inferences

#### Per-candidate assessment: (e) why unsolved and why now, (f) market size, (g) feasibility and channel, (h) risks

**C1 Residential aged-care entry desk** (RAD/DAP modelling, means-assessment form preparation, pension and home impacts, fee-agreement review, comparing facilities' fee sheets)
- **(e) Why unsolved, why now.**
  - The 1 November 2025 Act changed the maths (2% retention for up to five years, DAP indexation), and a July 2026 bill may change interim-period choices again.
  - Facility fee sheets are PDFs that vary by home, and the means assessment is a Services Australia form with no API.
  - Today's helpers are either conflicted (provider-paid placement consultants) or expensive and scarce (advisers at A$150–A$300/hr, with only about 18% of families seeking advice).
  - An LLM-plus-calculator desk can parse fee sheets, model RAD/DAP/combination outcomes, pre-fill the means-assessment form from statements, and give a human adviser or the family a sign-off pack.
- **(f) Market size.**
  - **[Background, unverified]** Assume about 60–75k permanent residential admissions a year.
  - If 25–40% of families pay A$500–A$2,500 for a family-paid "decision and paperwork pack", the market is about **A$7.5m–A$75m a year**. The provider-paid placement model at A$1,500–A$2,950 per placement is a parallel revenue line.
  - **NZ:** the residential care subsidy is means-tested (background).
  - **UK:** self-funder care-home decisions and local-authority means tests are a larger analogue, with different rules.
- **(g) Feasibility and channel.**
  - The founder's strengths (data ingestion, SQL, dashboards, OpenAI) fit fee modelling and form assembly well. An MVP is feasible within 3–6 months.
  - The best B2B2C channels are:
    1. aged-care specialist financial advisers, sold as a white-label paraplanning and modelling tool (they hold the AFSL and the clients);
    2. placement consultants;
    3. retirement villages and hospital discharge planners;
    4. employers and EAPs as a carer benefit.
  - Direct-to-family is possible via SEO on "RAD vs DAP" queries.
- **(h) Risks.**
  - **[Background, unverified]** Recommending how to fund a RAD (selling shares or the home, annuities) is likely personal financial product advice requiring an AFSL. Stay factual and computational, or operate under an adviser's licence.
  - Placement consultants are unregulated, and positioning as conflict-free helps.
  - Demand is one-off per family. Trust risk is high.
  - Rules churn: rule changes are both the reason families buy and a maintenance burden.

**C2 Support at Home navigation and statement checking**
- **(e) Why unsolved, why now.** About 190k–200k people are waiting, there is an algorithmic assessment tool, and statements were found wrong at 51 of 60 providers. Families need someone to read monthly statements against the budget and chase providers.
- **(f) Market size.** About 365k recipients. **However**, consumer WTP for a monthly statement check is likely small, around A$10–A$30/month (inference), and families can switch providers instead.
- **(g) Feasibility.** Technically easy: parse statements, compare to the budget, draft complaints.
- **(h) Risks.** Low WTP, free OPAN, and fast-changing rules. It works better as a feature bundled into C1, or sold to providers as assurance (covered in the prior notes as a B2B idea).

**C3 Executor outcome desk** (post-death logistics: inventory, institution-by-institution notifications and closures, super claims, Centrelink and ATO steps, tracker)
- **(e) Why unsolved, why now.**
  - The ADNS covers a limited set of organisations (14–60, sources conflict) and only notifies; it does not close accounts or claim funds.
  - Fixed-fee probate firms (Bare, Willed) cover the grant, but the legwork stays with the executor.
  - ASIC is pressuring super funds over delays (Report 831, the Cbus penalty, the AustralianSuper suit), and claim volumes are rising 10% a year.
  - No evidence was found that Empathy operates in Australia.
  - LLM agents can now draft institution-specific packs, track cases and chase by email.
- **(f) Market size.** **[Background, unverified]** About 185–190k deaths a year. If 15–25% of estates buy a A$800–A$2,000 desk, the market is about **A$22m–A$95m a year**, paid from the estate. **UK:** about 600k deaths a year [Prior notes]. **NZ:** about 35–40k (background).
- **(g) Feasibility and channel.**
  - B2B2C candidates:
    1. fixed-fee probate law firms (Bare, Willed and small-law estate practices), as a white-label "post-grant admin" module (likely the fastest first revenue);
    2. funeral directors;
    3. trustee companies and public trustees, for efficiency;
    4. life insurers;
    5. palliative care networks, which already promote the ADNS.
  - First paying customers within 6–12 months is realistic if one law-firm partner pilots it.
- **(h) Risks.**
  - **[Background, unverified]** Preparing probate documents for a fee may constitute legal practice. Preparing date-of-death tax returns for a fee requires a registered tax agent (Tax Agent Services Act), so partner with one.
  - Institutions may refuse an agent without authority, so use executor-signed authorities.
  - Demand is one-off, and death timing is unpredictable, which keeps CAC high outside B2B2C channels.
  - Bare could add this itself.

**C4 Super death benefit claim desk**
- **(e) Why now.** There is a regulator-driven spotlight, multi-month to multi-year delays at large funds, and BDBN validity problems. Families face tax up to 32% if benefits go to non-dependants, so getting the right nominee path matters.
- **(f) Market size.** It is a subset of deaths, but high-value: super balances are often the largest asset outside the home.
- **(g) Feasibility.** It works best as a module of C3. A B2B variant sells to funds (claim-assembly and staking tooling) and is enterprise-sales heavy.
- **(h) Risks.** Disputes over who receives the benefit become legal advice. Funds may improve on their own under ASIC pressure.

**C5 NDIS budget-reset and reassessment evidence prep**
- **(e) Why now.** Budget resets run 1 October 2026 to 30 September 2027, and new framework plans start in April 2027.
- **Weaknesses.**
  - The external appeal avenue for total funding amounts has been largely removed, so an "appeals desk" has less leverage.
  - Free NDIS Appeals Program advocates exist.
  - **[Background, unverified]** Advocacy is generally not claimable from plan funds, so participant WTP is weak.
  - Provider software vendors are already in the channel.
- **(f) Market size.** **[Background, unverified]** About 700k+ participants. Payer uncertainty makes the market hard to size.
- **(g)/(h) Risks.** High policy volatility, vulnerable users, low WTP. Not top 3.

**C6 Centrelink Age Pension and Carer Payment claim desk**
- **(e) Why unsolved.** Backlogs (84-day averages in 2024–early 2025, more than 30k backlog at one point) and complex income and assets tests.
- **(f)/(g) Market and channel.** WTP is mainly among asset-holding retirees, who often already have advisers. The most realistic use is as a module of C1, since aged-care entry triggers Age Pension and means reassessments.
- **(h) Risks.** Debt-dispute users are often low-income and served free by welfare rights centres. The income-apportionment compensation (up to A$600) is too small to support contingency pricing.

**C7 Catastrophe home-insurance claim advocacy desk** (claim file building, scope-of-works comparison, IDR and AFCA submissions)
- **(e) Why now.** Record AFCA volumes, delay as the number-one issue, Code breaches 59% claims-related, and 12-month-old storm claims still open. Families have paid up to A$50k for claim managers.
- **(f) Market size.** 36k general-insurance AFCA complaints a year, plus a larger unknown pool of unsatisfied claims. At A$500–A$3,000 per claim or 5–10% of uplift (inference), the market is about **A$15m–A$60m**. Catastrophe-year spikes make it lumpy.
- **(g) Feasibility.** The document-heavy workflow fits LLMs. Channels: brokers (NIBA members, whose clients expect advocacy), builders and restorers, councils and recovery centres post-disaster, strata managers.
- **(h) Risks.** **[Background, unverified]** Since 2022, "claims handling and settling services" are a financial service under the Corporations Act, and paid third-party claim managers may need an AFSL or to operate under one. AFCA is free. Demand is episodic and CAC spikes. Insurers may resist representatives.

**C8 Partner visas:** high WTP (A$2.5k–A$10k agent fees) but legally reserved for registered agents and lawyers. Feasible only as a SaaS tool for migration agents, which is a different (B2B) thesis. Not top 3 for consumers.

**C9 Separation financial disclosure:** real spend and an unsolved disclosure-assembly problem. But amica (government) and Adieu (an AI disclosure tool) are already in market, dynamics are adversarial, and legal-practice risk is high. Middle ranking.

**C10 Scam protection for older parents:** large losses, but consumer WTP for prevention is weak and banks are the obligated payers. Better as a B2B bank or telco play, outside this consumer scope.

**C11 Childcare:** WTP is small (waitlist fees of A$20–A$50) and government tools (Starting Blocks) exist. Not attractive.

## Which Australian bureaucratic processes defeat families during major life events, and what do they currently pay to get help?

### Takeaway
The processes that defeat families and draw paid help are:
- entering aged care (assessment, waitlists, means assessment, RAD/DAP);
- administering a deceased estate (probate, banks, super death benefits, ATO);
- disputed or delayed insurance claims after disasters;
- partner visas;
- separation property settlements.

Spend ranges from provider-paid placement fees of A$1,500–A$2,950, through A$1,899–A$2,399 fixed-fee probate and A$2,500–A$10,000+ migration agents, to A$50,000 paid to a storm-claim manager. Centrelink, NDIS and childcare processes defeat many families too, but are served mostly by free advocacy, or by payers with low WTP.

### Cited Findings
- **Aged care.**
  - About 190k–200k are waiting for an assessment or a Support at Home package (June–August 2026), and the average wait from application to ongoing residential care is 396 days — [Weekly SOURCE](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/latest-government-data-reveals-close-to-200000-older-australians-still-waiting-for-aged-care/).
  - Placement fees are A$2,950 ex GST (Aged Care Decisions) or A$1,500 (Gold Coast service), paid by providers — [Aged Care Decisions](https://agedcaredecisions.com.au/faq/); [Weekly SOURCE](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/gold-coast-aged-care-placement-service-cashes-in-charging-providers-1500-for-new-residents).
  - Advocates charge A$100–A$190/hr and aged-care financial advice A$150–A$300/hr (templated source) — [Carevo](https://carevo.com.au/services/aged-care-advocacy/kalkie).
- **Estates.**
  - Probate costs A$1,899–A$2,399 fixed fee (Bare) and A$800–A$2,000 from lawyers for the grant alone, against A$2,500–A$30,000 for full administration. Filing fees reach over A$17,500 in Victoria — [Bare](https://bare.com.au/probate); [JustFund](https://justfund.com.au/cost-to-administer-a-deceased-estate-in-australia/).
  - AustralianSuper allegedly took 4 months to 4 years on 6,897 death claims, and Cbus was fined A$23.5m — [ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2025-releases/25-034mr-asic-sues-australiansuper-alleging-significant-death-benefit-claims-failures); [RiskInfo](https://riskinfo.com.au/news/2026/06/10/asic-warns-super-funds-over-benefit-delays/).
- **Insurance.** AFCA logged 119,949 complaints in 2025-26, with delay the top issue, and a A$50k claim-manager fee was paid and not reimbursed — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/claims/insurer-claim-delays-top-complaints-again-as-brokers-report-no-change-589274.aspx); [insurancenews.com.au](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/daily/policyholder-fails-to-force-insurer-to-pay-for-claim-manager).
- **Visas.** Partner visa agent fees are A$2,500–A$8,000+, and the temporary stage takes a median of about 17 months — [What's the Damage](https://whatsthedamage.com.au/migration-agent-cost/); [Migration Expert](https://www.migrationexpert.com.au/uncategorized/migration-agent-cost-australia/); [Ethos Migration](https://ethosmigration.com.au/australian-partner-visa-processing-times-2026/).
- **Centrelink.** Age Pension claims averaged 84 days (2024 to early 2025), with a backlog of more than 30k reported. 170,000 apportionment debts (A$347m) are outstanding, with compensation of up to A$600 — [Shorten release](https://formerministers.dss.gov.au/?p=20559); [The Nightly](https://thenightly.com.au/business/nick-bruining-centrelink-backlog-for-new-age-pension-applications-tops-more-than-30000-source-says-c-12803943); [The Senior](https://www.thesenior.com.au/story/9334284/centrelink-pension-debt-review-how-to-challenge-and-claim-refund/).
- **NDIS.** The budget reset from 1 October 2026 and new framework planning from 1 April 2027 are under way, and the external appeal of total funding has been largely removed — [health.gov.au](https://www.health.gov.au/resources/publications/ndis-supports-funding-changes); [DARU/Guardian](https://daru.org.au/resource/most-ndis-participants-will-lose-external-avenue-to-appeal-funding-amounts-under-new-system-senate-estimates-told/).

### Inferences
- The processes with the highest dollars at stake and the most existing paid help are aged-care entry, estates, insurance claims and visas.
  - Aged care and estates are the two where a software founder can operate without being a licensed professional, provided the product stays factual, computational and administrative, and partners with licensed advisers, lawyers or tax agents for sign-off. Licensing assumptions are unverified.
  - Visas (registered agents) and insurance claims (probable AFSL) have stronger licensing walls.
- A common pattern across all of them: the counterparty (Services Australia, super funds, insurers, banks) has no consumer API, so the work is forms, PDFs, emails and phone calls. That is the "outcome desk" shape the brief favours.

### Gaps
- No Australian time-cost survey (hours spent) was found for any of these processes.
- No current price data was found for consumer-side insurance claim advocates (Claim Assistance Bureau and others).

## Where are adult children spending hours and money managing ageing parents' care and finances under the new aged care system?

### Takeaway
Adult children's time and money go to six tasks:
1. getting an assessment (about 26-day median; 82,656 waiting at 30 June 2026);
2. waiting for and then choosing a Support at Home provider (about 107k waiting in August 2026);
3. checking error-prone monthly statements;
4. searching for a residential place (396-day average from application);
5. deciding RAD vs DAP under the new 2% retention rules;
6. completing means and Age Pension paperwork with Services Australia.

The paid help today is provider-paid placement consultants, hourly advocates and specialist financial advisers. Only about 18% of families seek advice.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The low 18% advice uptake, against a high-stakes decision (a RAD is often several hundred thousand dollars; background, unverified), suggests price and access barriers to advice rather than lack of need. A A$300–A$1,500 software-plus-human-sign-off product sits in the gap between free government information and A$2k+ advice (inference).
- Placement consultants are paid by providers per resident, so a conflict-free family-side product has a positioning advantage. Provider-paid economics also show providers will pay about A$1.5k–A$3k per filled bed, which is an alternative revenue model with conflict risk.
- Statement checking (C2) is a sticky but low-price use case. It is best bundled as retention for a family that first bought the entry desk.

## Which areas are already crowded or covered by free government services (avoid)?

### Takeaway
Avoid consumer plays in:
- energy switching, private health insurance comparison and insurance renewal comparison (crowded with comparison sites and free government comparators);
- childcare search;
- renter applications;
- tax returns and HECS;
- standalone death notifications (the ADNS is free);
- NDIS appeals (free advocacy, and external appeal of funding totals largely removed);
- scam reporting (Scamwatch and IDCARE are free).

Free government or community services also cover parts of aged care (My Aged Care, OPAN), insurance disputes (AFCA, Legal Aid, Financial Rights) and separation (amica). These cover information and dispute lodgement, not done-for-you paperwork with sign-off.

### Cited Findings
- **Death notification.** The ADNS is a free government service and the Services Australia checklist covers the rest — [Tasmanian BDM](https://www.justice.tas.gov.au/bdm/australian-death-notification-service); [Services Australia checklist](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-01/03-who-to-notify-checklist.docx).

- **Insurance disputes.** AFCA is free, Legal Aid Qld publishes guides, and Financial Rights Legal Centre advocates for policyholders. Maurice Blackburn and Chamberlains offer no-win, no-fee — [AFCA](https://afca.org.au/media/814/download); [Legal Aid Qld](https://legalaid.qld.gov.au/files/assets/public/v/3/publications/work-and-money/when-disaster-strikes-guide/laq_00076-when-disaster-strikes-cyclones-storms-and-floods-guide-web.pdf); [Maurice Blackburn](https://www.mauriceblackburn.com.au/insurance-claims-disputes/).

### Inferences
- **[Background, unverified; not searched this round]**
  - **Energy:** Energy Made Easy and Victorian Energy Compare (both free, government-run), the Default Market Offer and Victorian Default Offer, retailer "better offer" notification rules, plus Compare the Market, Canstar, Finder, iSelect, Bill Hero and Look Up My Bill. This is crowded, with low per-user stakes (a few hundred dollars a year).
  - **Private health insurance:** privatehealth.gov.au (free, government-run), the Medical Costs Finder (government-run), iSelect and Compare the Market. Crowded.
  - **Home and car premium price-walking:** the General Insurance Code requires showing last year's premium on renewal notices, and comparison sites are crowded. A "renewal negotiator" faces low WTP and insurers not on the comparison sites.
  - **Renting:** state tenant advocacy services are free, tribunals handle bond disputes, and application platforms (Snug, 2Apply, Ignite) are crowded. Renter WTP is low.
  - **Tax returns and HECS:** myTax pre-fill, H&R Block, Etax and thousands of tax agents. Crowded.
  - **Childcare search:** Care for Kids, Kindicare, Toddle and Starting Blocks. Crowded.
- The free services share a gap: they tell families what to do, or adjudicate disputes, but none does the paperwork end to end.
  - The ADNS notifies but does not close or claim.
  - OPAN advocates but does not model RAD/DAP or fill in forms.
  - AFCA adjudicates but does not build the claim file.
  - This gap is where a paid outcome desk can coexist with free services.
