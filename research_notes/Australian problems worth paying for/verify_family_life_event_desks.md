## Q1. Licensing and legal limits (the decisive question)

### Takeaway

**Executor desk.**
- Licensing does not technically kill it, but it removes most of the value.
- In NSW, Victoria and Queensland (and, on background knowledge, every other jurisdiction), a non-lawyer who prepares tailored court documents such as a probate application for a fee, or who advises an executor on their legal position, commits an offence of unqualified legal practice. Queensland has jailed an offender.
- Preparing a date-of-death or deceased-estate trust return for a fee is a "tax agent service". It needs TPB registration, with civil penalties up to A$412,500 for a company.
- What a non-lawyer can legally sell is administrative project management: the inventory, institution letters and forms, tracking, and handoffs to a lawyer and a tax agent.

**Aged-care desk.**
- The licence wall is much lower.
- Industry bodies' own lobbying implies that RAD/DAP accommodation payments and Centrelink rules are **not** "financial products". In February 2026 Aged Care Steps asked for s764A of the Corporations Act to be amended to include them. That means pure fee modelling, means-assessment form preparation and fee-agreement checks can be done without an AFSL, as long as the product stays factual and computational.
- Advice on which assets to sell, keep or invest in to fund care (shares, super, annuities) is personal financial product advice and needs an AFSL.
- No ASIC guidance or rule change was found. However, the adviser lobby (Aged Care Steps, FAAA) is actively campaigning to pull "strategic aged care advice" inside the AFSL perimeter. That is a live regulatory risk to any unlicensed desk.

### Cited Findings

#### Executor desk: legal practice limits by state
- **Queensland.** Under s 24 of the *Legal Profession Act 2007* (Qld), engaging in legal practice without being an Australian legal practitioner is an offence. The Legal Services Commission has prosecuted "unlawful operators":
  - In LSC v Pearson (2018) the respondent was convicted after a two-day trial and fined A$1,500 — [LSC Qld unlawful operators 2018](https://www.lsc.qld.gov.au/discipline/disciplinary-decisions/unlawful-operators/2018).
  - In LSC v Braid (2017) a respondent who took money for "legal services" got three months' imprisonment — [LSC Qld 2017–2016](https://www.lsc.qld.gov.au/discipline/disciplinary-decisions/unlawful-operators/2017-2016).
- **NSW.** The Legal Profession Uniform Law bars unqualified entities from engaging in legal practice. "Engage in legal practice" includes practising law or providing legal services, and "practise law" is not defined.
  - The Law Society of NSW says the core of legal practice is advising a particular person in a particular situation, and producing a document that affects legal rights and is tailored to that person's needs (2018 fact sheet) — [Law Society NSW: Unqualified practice fact sheet (2018)](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/sites/default/files/2018-05/LS1138_LPUL-fact-sheet_2018_Unqualified-practice%20%28002%29.pdf); [LSJ: To be or not to be engaging in legal practice](https://lsj.com.au/articles/to-be-or-not-to-be-engaging-in-legal-practice/).
  - A probate application and affidavits prepared for a specific estate fit that description.
- **Victoria.** The Victorian Legal Services Board treats unqualified legal practice as a criminal offence, punishable by a fine and/or imprisonment. It names three situations: people with no legal qualifications, **non-lawyer professionals who exceed the limits of their authority**, and lawyers without a valid practising certificate — [VLSB+C: Unqualified legal practice](https://lsbc.vic.gov.au/consumers/registers-lawyers/unqualified-legal-practice).
  - A Victorian lay person was convicted for drafting court documents and correspondence for a Federal Court matter (not probate) — [Lawyers Weekly](https://www.lawyersweekly.com.au/news/16209-legal-watchdog-pursues-imposter).
- **Western Australia.** The Legal Costs Committee's non-contentious probate determination sets what lawyers, law clerks and paralegals may charge for a Supreme Court probate application. It regulates price, not who may act — [WA LCC probate costs determinations](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/determinations-scales-legal-profession-non-contentious-probate-costs-legal-costs-committee).
- **No probate-specific prosecution of a non-lawyer document-prep service was found** in any state. Generic DIY will kits are sold openly and reviewed by CHOICE — [CHOICE: Online will kits compared](https://www.choice.com.au/money/financial-planning-and-investing/financial-planning/articles/will-kit-reviews).
- **Market practice: tech brands put a law firm inside.**
  - Safewill's estates work runs through "affiliate law firm Safewill Legal", a Sydney wills and estates practice operating Australia-wide.
  - Maurice Blackburn's online probate offer is delivered with the Safewill team and claims lodgement "in as little as three weeks" — [Maurice Blackburn online probate](https://www.mauriceblackburn.com.au/online-probate/).
  - Bare sells probate as "Bare Law" — [Bare probate](https://bare.com.au/probate).
- **Executors can self-file.** Since 1 August 2023 most uncontested NSW grant applications must be filed online — [Supreme Court of NSW: Wills & probate](https://supremecourt.nsw.gov.au/wills-probate.html).
  - **[Background, unverified]** Self-represented executors may apply in every state, and the Victorian Probate Office publishes guidance for them ([Supreme Court of Victoria probate FAQs](https://www.supremecourt.vic.gov.au/wills-and-probate/probate-faqs) — page not read).
- **[Background, unverified] Remaining jurisdictions.**
  - WA joined the Uniform Law on 1 July 2022.
  - SA (*Legal Practitioners Act 1981* s 21), Tasmania (*Legal Profession Act 2007*), the ACT (*Legal Profession Act 2006*) and the NT (*Legal Profession Act 2006*) each prohibit unqualified legal practice, with similar exceptions for trustee companies and public trustees.
  - Licensed trustee companies (Corporations Act Ch 5D plus state trustee companies Acts) and public trustees are statutorily authorised to apply for grants.
  - Non-lawyer ownership of an incorporated legal practice (ILP) is permitted under the Uniform Law, provided at least one principal holds an unrestricted practising certificate.

#### Executor desk: tax agent registration
- **Core rule.** Under the *Tax Agent Services Act 2009*, an unregistered entity that provides a tax agent service "for a fee or other reward" contravenes a civil penalty provision. Penalties run up to **A$82,500 for an individual and A$412,500 for a body corporate** — [TPB: Who needs to register?](https://www.tpb.gov.au/registration).
  - "Fee or other reward" is read broadly and decided on the facts — [TPB(GS) 45/2023 What is a fee or other reward](https://www.tpb.gov.au/tpb-gs-45-2023-what-is-fee-or-other-reward).
- **LPR exemption.** TPB guidance lists as exempt "a service provided as a legal service in the course of acting for a trust or deceased estate as trustee or legal personal representative". So an executor, or a professional executor, can do the estate's tax work while acting as LPR, but a third-party desk serving the executor cannot. Further exclusions are in s 26 of the Tax Agent Services Regulations 2022 (not retrieved) — [TPB GS 08/2011: trustee registration considerations](https://www.tpb.gov.au/tpb-gs-08-2011-tax-practitioners-conducting-business-capacity-trustee-trust-registration-considerations).
- **Software boundary.** The TPB's digital service provider guidance was last modified 30 April 2026. It says registration is probably required where a software provider configures or customises a system to deliver a specific tax outcome, or runs a help desk giving customised advice to help clients meet a specific tax outcome — [TPB: Digital service providers guidance](https://www.tpb.gov.au/node/192012).
- **ATO process.** An authorised LPR may appoint a tax agent, who can prepare and lodge the date-of-death return online. Only a registered agent can lodge electronically on the estate's behalf. Tax-agent fees incurred by the LPR are deductible expenses — [ATO: final return for a deceased person](https://ato.gov.au/Individuals/Deceased-estates/Doing-a-final-tax-return-for-the-deceased-person); [ATO trust return instructions (2021)](https://www.ato.gov.au/Forms/Trust-tax-return-instructions-2021/?page=23).
  - The Inspector-General's 2021–2022 "Death and Taxes" work is old context and is already cited in the prior file.

#### Aged-care desk: AFSL and the advice perimeter
- **February 2026 Aged Care Steps paper** (advocacy, five recommendations):
  - clarify aged-care financial advice as *personal financial product advice*;
  - require providers to hold an AFSL or be authorised under one;
  - **amend s764A of the Corporations Act to include aged-care advice and/or the RAD (and its DAP equivalent) in the definition of financial products**;
  - give ASIC a stronger oversight role, add specialisation categories on the Financial Advisers Register, run a consumer education campaign, and add affordability measures.
  - Sources: [Financial Newswire: Regulatory gaps fuelling "unlicensed" aged care advice](https://financialnewswire.com.au/financial-planning/regulatory-gaps-in-aged-care-advice-called-out); [Adviser Voice, Feb 2026](https://www.adviservoice.com.au/2026/02/advice-leaders-call-for-urgent-action-on-regulatory-gaps-in-aged-care-advice/); [Financial Newswire: call to tighten licensing](https://financialnewswire.com.au/financial-planning/call-to-tighten-licensing-of-aged-care-advice).
- **Who it says is advising without a licence.** The paper says unlicensed providers, including **aged-care providers, placement services and individuals no longer authorised to give financial advice**, offer "strategic aged care advice" without regulatory oversight. It also cites a proliferation of "advice errors" by Services Australia, aged-care providers and other unlicensed players — [Professional Planner, Feb 2026](https://www.professionalplanner.com.au/2026/02/older-australians-at-risk-of-conflicted-and-inaccurate-aged-care-advice/); [ifa: Aged Care Steps flags unlicensed advice](https://www.ifa.com.au/aged-care-steps-flags-worrying-trend-of-unlicensed-aged-care-advice/).
- **History of the push.** The FAAA backed stricter controls in its submission to the Senate inquiry on the Aged Care Bill 2024 (October 2024) — [ifa: FAAA calls for stronger controls](https://www.ifa.com.au/faaa-calls-for-stronger-controls-on-unregulated-aged-care-advice/).
  - Aged Care Steps first consulted in April 2024 — [Adviser Voice, Apr 2024](https://www.adviservoice.com.au/2024/04/aged-care-steps-launches-a-consultation-into-the-delivery-and-regulation-of-aged-care-advice/).
  - The SMSF Association's 2024 submission warned that new rules could add regulatory burden and reduce access to advice. It noted that direct property, often the main asset, is not a financial product — [SMSFA submission (May 2024)](https://www.smsfassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/20240523_SMSFA_Aged-Care-Steps-Licensing-and-Regulations-for-Aged-Care-Advice-Providers-WEBCOPY.pdf).
- **No ASIC regulatory guide, info sheet or enforcement action on aged-care advice was found**, and no legislative change had passed by October 2026. The sources are industry advocacy, not neutral.
- **How existing players stay outside the AFSL.**
  - **Government:** Services Australia's Financial Information Service (132 300) and Aged Care Specialist Officers (1800 227 475) explain aged-care costs and their effect on pensions for free, but "are not financial planners and do not give or sell advice or investment products" — [Services Australia: changes under the new Aged Care Act](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/we-can-help-you-understand-changes-under-new-aged-care-act); [OPAN: where to go for further help](https://opan.org.au/toolkit/where-to-go-for-further-help/); [Services Australia leaflet SA485 (July 2026)](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/sa485-2607en.pdf).
  - **Advisers:** one adviser sells a one-hour aged-care consultation at **A$880 inc GST**, labelled "educational session only" that "does not include personal financial advice" — [Adviser Ratings: Henry Ho](https://www.adviserratings.com.au/adviser/1305990/Henry-Ho).
  - **Calculators:** sum.money labels its RAD/DAP results "estimates only" — [sum.money aged care calculator](https://sum.money/au/aged-care-calculator/).
  - **Placement consultants:** My Care Path describes "explaining fees and charges" and "negotiating options and fees as your advocate" — [Aged Care Guide: My Care Path](https://www.agedcareguide.com.au/aged-care-placement). Fee-paying navigation and placement services are not regulated by the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission [prior file].
- **[Background, unverified]**
  - ASIC RG 36 and RG 244 separate *factual information* (not advice), *general advice* (needs an AFSL plus a general advice warning) and *personal advice* (needs an AFSL plus best-interests duty and a statement of advice).
  - Fee-based advice on the tax consequences of selling the home (CGT, main-residence exemption) may be a "tax (financial) advice service" under the TASA.
  - Lodging Services Australia forms for another person needs that person's authority or a Centrelink nominee arrangement.

### Inferences
- **Executor desk: what is legal for a non-lawyer, non-tax-agent business** (inference from the findings above):
  - **Safe:** estate inventory, a document vault, institution-specific notification and closure letters signed by the executor, completing banks' and super funds' own claim forms with the executor's data, a task tracker and chasing, beneficiary update statements, and referral to a lawyer or tax agent.
  - **Grey:** software that auto-assembles a *tailored* probate application or affidavits for a fee. This is the NSW Law Society's "document affecting legal rights tailored to the person" test. Safe only if the executor is the author and the tool gives no estate-specific legal guidance; untested in court.
  - **Prohibited without a licence or partner:** advising on distribution, the will's interpretation, family provision claims or BDBN validity disputes; preparing date-of-death or trust returns or giving customised tax advice for a fee.
  - The parts with the highest willingness to pay (probate, tax, legal comfort) sit behind those walls. A founder therefore needs a law firm and a tax agent inside the product, which is exactly how Bare (Bare Law) and Safewill (Safewill Legal) are built. The alternative is an ILP with a salaried principal solicitor, PI insurance and trust accounting **[background]**. Either way the cost base rises well beyond what a solo software founder can carry.
- **Aged-care desk: the licence-free core is large.** These activities are factual and calculational, not advice, as long as RAD/DAP and Centrelink rules are not financial products:
  - modelling RAD vs DAP vs combination;
  - computing means-tested care fees and DAP from the published maximum permissible interest rate (MPIR);
  - estimating Age Pension effects of keeping or selling the home;
  - parsing facility fee sheets;
  - pre-filling the means-assessment form;
  - checking a residential agreement against published prices and statutory caps.
- **Aged-care desk: guardrails.** The product must:
  - never recommend a specific funding strategy that involves financial products;
  - carry clear "factual information, not advice" framing;
  - route "what should Mum do with her shares or super" to an AFSL holder.
- **Aged-care desk: regulatory direction.** If the Aged Care Steps and FAAA proposal were adopted, an unlicensed desk would be squeezed. A tool *sold to* licensed advisers would gain. That argues for building the adviser- and provider-facing version first.
- **Net.** Licensing is the decisive differentiator between the two candidates. It is close to fatal for a solo-founder executor desk, and manageable for an aged-care desk.

## Q2. Competitor landscape: executor desk

### Takeaway
- The executor space is **more crowded than the prior file assumed**, at both ends.
- **At the legal end:**
  - Fixed-fee online probate is now offered by Bare Law, Safewill Legal (also white-labelled for Maurice Blackburn), Willed and many small firms.
  - Licensed trustee companies and public trustees act as professional executors at roughly 4–5.5% of gross assets, with **A$19,800 minimums** at the trustee companies.
- **At the admin end:**
  - The free Australian Death Notification Service now claims **80+ participating organisations** (up from the 14–60 in the prior file).
  - The big four banks run dedicated estate-settlement teams.
  - AustralianSuper insourced death claims into a 75-person Bereavement Centre and reports **80% of cases resolved within four months**.
- **Players that did not turn up:**
  - No UK or US post-death admin platform (Life Ledger, Settld, Kinherit, Empathy) was found operating in Australia. The only claim of Australian presence is a press release from The Estate Registry's NotifyNOW.
  - "Bereavement Assist" and "Estate Assist" do not appear to exist as Australian estate-admin firms.
- **The remaining gap** is narrow: post-grant and no-probate legwork for DIY executors.

### Inferences
- **Price anchors** (inference, from the schedules above).

  - Fixed-fee probate costs about A$2k (Bare), plus hourly post-grant work.
  - A A$790–A$1,990 admin desk is 90%+ cheaper than a professional executor, which is a strong value story.
  - But the buyer who would take a professional executor is not the DIY buyer. The DIY executor is comparing against "doing it myself for free with the ADNS and bank hotlines".
- **Where the gap is closing.**
  - Notifications: the ADNS has 80+ organisations.
  - Super claims: AustralianSuper is insourcing and improving, and the regulator is pushing all funds.
  - Banks: dedicated teams.
- **Where a gap remains.**

- **Defensibility.** Both gaps are easy for Bare, Safewill or Willed to close by adding a fixed-fee "post-grant admin" SKU. They already own the executor relationship at the moment of the grant.

  - (b) RAD, DAP and mix cash-flow and estate outcomes, including the 2% retention and Age Pension effects;

A faster, complete means-assessment submission therefore brings forward RAD inflows to providers. This mechanism should be validated with two or three provider finance managers.

## Q6. Best wedge, first 10 customers, kill criteria, verdicts and re-scores (which desk is stronger?)

### Takeaway
- **Executor desk: KILL** for this founder as a consumer, estate-paid desk.
  - The work families pay for (probate, tax returns, legal comfort) is reserved to lawyers and registered tax agents.
  - The unlicensed admin layer is being absorbed by free or insourced services: ADNS (80+ organisations), bank estate teams and AustralianSuper's Bereavement Centre.
  - The incumbents that own the executor at the moment of the grant (Bare Law, Safewill Legal plus Maurice Blackburn, Willed, the trustee companies) can bolt on post-grant admin.
- **Aged-care entry desk: DOWNGRADE, but keep as the stronger of the two.**
  - Its core (fee modelling, means-assessment paperwork, agreement checks) sits outside the AFSL and legal-practice walls while it stays factual.
  - It matches the founder's data, SQL and LLM skills.
  - It has evidenced third-party payers: providers, super funds and seniors organisations.
  - But family-paid WTP is unproven against free calculators and provider-paid placement.
  - Retirement Essentials, Aged Care Decisions and Aged Care Steps are well placed to copy it.
  - The adviser lobby is pushing to bring "strategic aged-care advice" under the AFSL regime.
  - Pursue it only as a provider- or partner-paid "admission finance pack", with hard kill criteria.

### Cited Findings
These were all established above:
- **Legal-practice offences:** Qld, NSW and Vic.
- **Tax-agent registration:** applies to fee-based estate returns, with penalties up to A$412,500.
- **Aged-care advice gap:** Aged Care Steps (February 2026) wants RAD/DAP brought into s764A; no ASIC action.
- **Executor competitors:** fixed-fee probate firms, trustee companies (A$19,800 minimum), ADNS (80+ organisations), AustralianSuper (80% resolved within four months).

#### Best wedge

**Aged care (recommended to test): the provider-paid "admission finance pack".**
- **What the provider's admissions team gets for each incoming resident:**
  - a family upload and interview;
  - an auto-filled, checked Services Australia means-assessment pack;
  - a RAD/DAP/mix scenario sheet (cash flow, 2% retention, Age Pension effect) labelled factual information;
  - a plain-English agreement and fee schedule summary;
  - "see a licensed adviser" handoffs for funding-strategy questions.
- **Price:** A$250–A$500 per admission, billed to the provider. Alternatively, a family-paid A$490–A$990 version is offered through super-fund or seniors-organisation partners.
- **Why this wedge:**
  - The provider is an evidenced payer: it already pays A$1.5k–A$2.95k per placement and builds its own calculators.
  - The provider has a cash-flow reason to speed the means assessment while the interim-period bill is unpassed (to be validated).
  - It avoids competing with free family-facing calculators.
- **Support at Home statement checks** come later, as a retention or upsell line.

**Executor (only if pursued despite the kill): a "post-grant admin module" white-labelled to small-law estate practices.**
- The law firm holds the licence, the founder supplies the workflow and letter engine, and the tax piece is handed to the firm's tax agent.

#### Kill criteria (aged-care wedge; 90-day test)
- **Providers.** Fewer than 2 of the first 6 providers agree to a *paid* pilot at A$250 or more per admission, or the provider cash-flow motive (RAD timing during the interim period) is denied by their finance teams.
- **Bill passage.** The interim-period bill passes in a form that removes the RAD-timing benefit, *and* providers then see no other reason to pay.
- **Family-paid test.** Family-paid conversion through a partner channel is below 3% at A$490 or more. Alternatively, families say a free calculator plus a placement consultant is "good enough" in 7 of 10 interviews.
- **Competitor overlap.** Retirement Essentials or Aged Care Decisions already offer means-assessment preparation plus RAD/DAP modelling at or below the target price.
- **Licensing.** A legal opinion says the scenario sheet is personal financial product advice without a licence, or Treasury or ASIC formally adopts the Aged Care Steps s764A proposal.
- **Unit cost.** Median means-assessment pack completion time stays above 3 hours of human time per admission after 30 cases, because unit economics fail at A$250–A$500.

#### Kill criteria (executor; already met, in my judgement)
- The value-carrying tasks need licences: verified for legal practice in Qld, NSW and Vic, and for tax agent services.
- The admin layer is being commoditised: ADNS 80+ organisations, bank teams, super-fund insourcing.
- **Revisit only if:** a fixed-fee probate firm or public trustee commits to paying for a white-label post-grant module, or a funeral group offers a paid referral arrangement on the UK model.

#### Which is stronger, and why
The **aged-care entry desk is stronger**, narrowly (34 against 32), for four reasons:
1. **Licensing:** its core output is legally deliverable without an AFSL or a practising certificate, while the executor desk's core output is not.
2. **Founder fit:** the work is computation over messy fee sheets and forms (SQL, data sync, LLM extraction) rather than institution-by-institution chasing.
3. **Payers:** it has evidenced third-party payers (providers, super funds, seniors organisations).
4. **Durable demand:** policy churn (November 2025 Act, unpassed interim bill, accommodation pricing review) keeps creating demand for current rules.

**Neither clears the bar of a strong consumer business for a solo founder.** The aged-care desk is viable only as a B2B2C product that provider-side customers pay for in a 90-day pilot. If the kill criteria trip, neither life-event desk should proceed.

**Linkage (inference).** The same family meets both events: aged-care entry, then death, then a RAD refund to the estate (the OzBargain anecdote mentions RAD refunds "held in probate"). An aged-care desk that succeeds could later add a narrow "RAD refund and estate checklist" feature without becoming a full executor desk.

**NZ and UK (secondary).** The UK funeral-referral model (Kings Court Trust, SAIF Probate) shows that the executor channel can work where non-lawyer estate administration is permitted. In England and Wales, probate activities are reserved legal activities, but estate administration is not **[background, unverified]**. NZ was not researched.
