## Q1. Exact rules: the 90-day limit, transition, scope, and how evidence requests and holds work

### Takeaway
**The rule:**
- The Securing the NDIS Act changes "2 years" to "90 days" in s45A of the NDIS Act, with a fixed commencement of 1 December 2026.
- The 90 days run from each support's delivery date.
- The limit binds whoever lodges the claim: the provider for agency-managed claims, the plan manager for plan-managed claims, and the participant for self-managed claims.
- Late claims are reported to be auto-rejected. The only relief is a narrow "exceptional circumstances" discretion.
- How supports delivered before 1 December are treated is **still unresolved**. The Act reportedly has no transition wording, and the Minister can make transitional rules.

**Already in force:** since 15 October 2026 the NDIA runs extra checks on any claim lodged more than 90 days after delivery. Such claims can be held for up to 28 days, and providers get at least 14 days to answer evidence requests.

**Implication:** the "recovery" framing is weak. After 1 December, late lines are mostly unrecoverable, so the value moves to prevention (ageing) and fast turnaround on rejections and holds.

### Cited Findings

**The 90-day limit**
- **What the law changes:** the Securing the NDIS for Future Generations Act 2026 changes "2 years" to "90 days" in **section 45A** of the NDIS Act. The Act's commencement table fixes **1 December 2026** for that Part. The 90 days run from the day the support is provided, not from the invoice date. — [Centre of Hope (secondary guide)](https://centreofhope.com.au/ndis-90-day-claim-rule-2026/); [Centre of Hope](https://centreofhope.com.au/?p=4605)
- **The NDIA's own wording:** the NDIA's update page (last updated August 2026) lists the 90-day rule ("Claims must be submitted within 90 days of delivering a support") but no start date. — [NDIS update PDF](https://www.ndis.gov.au/media/8801/download)
- **Who must meet the limit:** whoever submits the claim. That is the participant or nominee if self-managed, the **plan manager** if plan-managed, and the **provider** if the NDIA manages the funding. — [Centre of Hope](https://centreofhope.com.au/?p=4605)
- **Plan-managed chain:** the 90 days cover the whole chain, from delivery to invoice to plan-manager checks to claim. "Your plan manager can only claim once it has the invoice, so slow invoices eat into the 90 days." — summary of [Flowlogic](https://flowlogic.com.au/?p=77958), [Centre of Hope](https://centreofhope.com.au/?p=4605) and others (exact attribution within the summary is unclear)
- **Per-date deadlines:** each service date on a multi-date invoice has its own deadline. — summary of [Orange Plan](https://orangeplan.com.au/guides/ndis-90-day-claim-rule/), [Moreton Regional Support](https://moretonregionalsupport.com.au/blogs/guides/ndis-90-day-claim-rule-december-2026) and [Provider Choice](https://providerchoice.com.au/blog/ndis-90-day-claiming-rule) (attribution within the summary is unclear)

**Late claims and exceptions**
- **Auto-rejection:** an NDIA guideline dated 1 October 2026 reportedly says the claims and payments system "will automatically reject a claim that was not submitted within the required time frames". — [Centre of Hope](https://centreofhope.com.au/?p=4605). Not confirmed against the guideline text.
- **Exception:** a late claim can be accepted only if exceptional circumstances justify it and the claim was made within a reasonable time given those circumstances (s45A(6)). The discretion is described as "limited". — summary of [Kismet newsroom](https://www.kismet.healthcare/company/newsroom/new-90-day-claims-rule-expected-from-1-december-26), [Moreton Regional Support](https://moretonregionalsupport.com.au/blogs/guides/ndis-90-day-claim-rule-december-2026) and [Centre of Hope](https://centreofhope.com.au/ndis-90-day-claim-rule-2026/) (secondary sources)

**Transition for supports delivered before 1 December 2026 (unresolved)**
- One self-management guide says the Act contains **no transition wording** for this change and that the Minister can make transitional rules. — [Loving Life](https://www.lovinglife.care/post/self-managed-ndis-record-keeping-90-day-claims)
- Another source says the NDIA "hasn't yet confirmed" whether pre-1 December supports keep the longer window. — summary of [Orange Plan](https://orangeplan.com.au/guides/ndis-90-day-claim-rule/) and [Wholii](https://www.wholii.com.au/2026/09/03/how-long-do-ndis-providers-have-to-submit-a-claim-december-2026/) (attribution unclear)
- **Precedent:** the 2-year limit took effect on 3 October 2024 with a 12-month grace period for earlier supports, which ended on 2 October 2025. From 3 October 2025, NDIA systems "automatically reject any claim that falls outside this two-year window". — [DARU](https://daru.org.au/?p=10005794); [Appoyo](https://www.appoyo.com.au/blog/ndis-claim-limits-mastering-the-2-year-rule); [Ask DSC](https://teamdsc.com.au/resources/ask-dsc-timeframes-for-invoicing)

**Checks and holds already in force**
- **From 15 October 2026:** in an NDIA news item dated 5 October 2026 (date per the search summary), the NDIA says it will run "extra checks on claims submitted more than 90 days after a support is delivered". These claims may be held for up to 28 days, and the NDIA may ask for more information or evidence before paying. The NDIA's advice is to "Submit claims within 90 days of a support being delivered to avoid the extra integrity checks." — [NDIA: Increasing integrity checks on older claims](https://www.ndis.gov.au/news/11575-increasing-integrity-checks-older-claims); [Centre of Hope](https://centreofhope.com.au/?p=4605)
  - This replaces the June 2026 threshold (12 months) and the planned July 2026 threshold (6 months). — [Orange Plan: July 2026 holds](https://orangeplan.com.au/guides/why-the-ndis-is-holding-your-claims-july-2026-changes/)
- **Hold and evidence process (NDIA "Guide to getting paid"):**
  - A claim can be put on hold while more information is verified.
  - **Providers get at least 14 days to respond** and must ask in writing for more time.
  - The review can take up to 28 days.
  - In a bulk upload, only the claim under review stays open.
  - A non-compliant claim "will be rejected and not paid", and the NDIA writes to the provider with the reasons.

  — [NDIA Guide to getting paid](https://www.ndis.gov.au/providers/pricing-and-payments/payments/guide-getting-paid)
- **Earlier hold regime (2024):** the NDIA sent a request for information with a fact sheet, and a claim stayed on hold until satisfactory information arrived. As of 1 August 2024 there were **more than 1,000 active payment holds across providers, covering about 9,000 documents, with a median review time of about 60 days**.
  - In *Northern Disability Services* the Federal Court found the NDIA's delay on more than A$500,000 of payment claims "unreasonable" and ordered it to decide "forthwith". Another ruling found withholding payment and requiring more information lawful.

  — [Insurance Business (Moray & Agnew)](https://insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/life-insurance/moray-and-agnew-breaks-down-court-rulings-impact-on-ndis-providers-519918.aspx); [The Lawyer Mag](https://www.thelawyermag.com/au/practice-areas/insurance/unregistered-disability-insurance-provider-loses-legal-challenge-over-manual-payment-reviews/515260); [NDIA](https://www.ndis.gov.au/print/pdf/node/10916). Exact attribution within the summary is unclear, and these are 2024 data.

### Inferences
- **The "cliff" date is 2 September 2026 [Inference].** If no transitional rule is made, then on 1 December 2026 any support delivered on or before about 2 September 2026 that is still unclaimed or unfixed becomes unclaimable overnight. Under the 2-year rule that backlog could reach back to December 2024. The 2024 precedent (a 12-month grace period) makes a transitional rule plausible, and a grace period would soften the one-off cliff.
- **The rule is now "prevent", not "recover" [Inference].** After 1 December, a line past 90 days is auto-rejected, and the only remedy is an exceptional-circumstances request. A recovery desk therefore has little to recover once the cliff passes. The durable jobs are:
  - ageing every delivered line against day 90;
  - fixing rejected lines within the remaining window;
  - answering hold and evidence requests inside the 14-day response window.
- **Day-90 checks are the near-term pain [Inference].** The 15 October hold regime already penalises day-90-plus claims with 28-day holds and evidence requests. That is a cash-flow pain today, before the legal cliff.
- **Pre-payment evidence review is concentrated [Inference].** About A$420m of high-risk claims was reviewed over two years. Against roughly A$51bn a year of payments (Q3), that is about 0.4% of value a year. Evidence requests are a high-risk-segment problem, not a universal one.
- **Possible niche:** exceptional-circumstances late-claim applications under s45A(6) could become an "outcome desk" service from December 2026, but the NDIA's process for them is unknown.

### Gaps
- The Act's text, its commencement table and any transitional rule were not read (WebFetch failed). The 1 October 2026 "automatic rejection" guideline is reported only by a secondary blog.
- No Integrity Portal letter text, evidence format list or Portal-specific deadline was found. The 14-day minimum response time comes from the general "Guide to getting paid".
- No NDIA figures since June 2026 on volumes of held claims, rejection rates or Portal throughput were found. No trade-press reporting on Portal outcomes (DSC, Disability Support Guide, NDS) surfaced in searches.
- The process for applying for the s45A(6) exceptional-circumstances discretion is unknown.

## Q2. Full competitor landscape, including outsourced billing services and any 90-day or Integrity Portal features

### Takeaway
No vendor was found shipping a named "90-day ageing" or "Integrity Portal evidence pack" feature. But the base layer is well served:
- Client-management systems already handle remittance and rejection files (Brevity, Flowlogic, Astalty, ShiftCare).
- Plan-manager platforms and HICAPS have NDIA claim APIs.
- Vendors and bookkeepers are publishing 90-day guidance (Kismet, Flowlogic, Wiise, Astalty, True Tally).
- A **large, cheap outsourced-labour market** already does NDIS claiming, from A$12–22/hr offshore to A$325+GST a month for a base package and A$1.5–3.5k a month for managed back office.

The gap is real but narrow, and an ageing report is easy for incumbents to bundle.

### Inferences
- **Line-level reconciliation exists [Inference].** Brevity, Flowlogic and Astalty all ingest NDIA result or remittance files and flag rejected lines. Adding a "days to 90" column and an unclaimed-shift report is a small feature for them. Given the volume of 90-day content they publish, at least some client-management vendors will likely ship ageing views within 3–9 months. **High bundling risk.**
- **Outsourced labour sets a low price anchor [Inference].** Offshore NDIS claims VAs at A$12–22/hr and packages from A$325 a month mean a small provider's alternative to software is cheap. A tool must either make those bureaus more productive (sell to them) or beat A$325 a month for small providers.
- **The real whitespace is narrow [Inference]:**
  - cross-system (client-management export + NDIA result file + plan-manager remittance) per-service-date ageing;
  - hold and evidence response packs built from existing records;
  - multi-client dashboards for billing bureaus.

  None was found as a product, but each is a feature, not a platform.
- **The founder cannot easily get on the claim rails [Inference].** Claim-submission APIs exist for approved vendors (HICAPS, LanternPay, client-management systems), and approval depends on entity type and cyber maturity. A newcomer is limited to read-only ingestion plus human submission, which is fine for a monitor but caps automation.

## Q3. Size: providers actively claiming, plan managers, claim volumes and rejection rates, and revenue at risk

### Takeaway
- **Providers:** the NDIA counted **280,258 active providers** in the June 2026 quarter, but only **10,842 supported NDIA-managed participants** (where the provider itself lodges and carries the 90-day risk). 210,956 supported plan-managed participants and 133,703 self-managed ones.
- **Registered providers:** 26,282, of which 17,464 were active (December 2025 quarter).
- **Payments:** A$51.45bn in FY2025-26.

**Implication:** most of the scheme's 90-day risk sits with plan managers and self-managers, not with the long tail of small providers. The directly exposed provider pool is about 10–17k (mostly registered), plus plan managers, who will consolidate onto a "significantly smaller" panel from October 2027.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Revenue at risk, scheme-wide (illustrative only; no data on the late-lodged share) [Inference]:**
  - Each **0.1%** of payment value lodged more than 90 days late is about **A$51m a year**.
  - If 0.5–2% of value is currently lodged late, about **A$0.26–1.0bn a year** is exposed before providers change behaviour.
  - Signals that the late tail is material: the NDIA has tightened its check threshold three times (12 months, then 6 months, then 90 days), and MyPlanManager "often" receives invoices more than 90 days after a plan ends.
  - Most of this will be avoided by faster invoicing, not lost.
- **Per mid-sized provider (illustrative) [Inference]:** at A$5m of NDIS revenue, each 1% of lines missing day 90 is about A$50k a year. That supports a tool at A$300–1,000 a month, if the provider believes it has a tail.
- **Serviceable market, revised down from the sector notes [Inference]:**
  - **Primary buyers** are providers that lodge their own agency-managed claims (about 10.8k serve NDIA-managed participants; mid-sized ones are a few thousand), plus plan managers (probably several hundred to about 1,000 today, **[Background, unverified]**, shrinking after 2027) and outsourced billing bureaus (unknown count).
  - **Small plan-managed providers** (the 211k) face only an invoicing-speed problem, which Xero, any client-management system or the plan manager's nagging solves. They are a weak buyer.
  - **Revised ceiling:** about 2,000–4,000 paying entities × A$3–12k a year ≈ **A$6–48m ARR**, down from A$18–100m in the sector notes.

### Gaps
- No registered versus unregistered split of active claiming providers was found. It is likely in the full NDIA quarterly report, which was not read.
- No current plan-manager count was found (NDIS Commission registration group 0127).
- No 2026 scheme-wide rejection rate, and no share of claims lodged more than 90 days after delivery, was found. This is the key number for sizing the cliff.

## Q5. Best wedge for a solo Australian software founder

### Takeaway
Provider-direct "cliff recovery" is the wrong wedge. The best remaining wedge is a **read-only claims-ageing and exception monitor**:
- **who it is for:** sold first **through and to outsourced NDIS billing bureaus**, and to **mid-sized providers that lodge agency-managed claims and employ 1–3 claims staff**;
- **what it does:** per-service-date "days to 90", rejected and held lines, a 14-day evidence-response tracker, and evidence packs assembled only from existing records;
- **how to test it:** a paid manual "90-day cliff health check" before 1 December.

Plan managers are a 12-month side segment at most. Allied health is too small and too cheap.

### Cited Findings
- **Bureaus are aggregators and partner with client-management systems:** Positively Sorted is listed as a Zanda Health partner, and True Tally, Care BPO and offshore firms all sell NDIS claiming. — [Zanda partner page](https://zandahealth.com/au/partners/positively-sorted/); [True Tally](https://www.truetally.com.au/blog/ndis-bookkeeping-for-providers); [Care BPO](https://carebpo.com.au/the-ndis-back-office-support-that-will-propel-you-forward/)
- **Data to ingest is available as files:**
  - NDIA bulk-upload receipt and payment-summary files;
  - the myplace Payment Request result file;
  - remittance files showing paid, partial and rejected lines with reasons;
  - NDIS error files.

  — [Astalty](https://astalty.com.au/articles/how-astalty-handles-NDIS-invoicing-PRODA-bulk-upload-and-payment-reconciliation); [Flowlogic guide](https://guide.flowlogic.com.au/?p=1899); [Brevity](https://brevityau.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/BS/pages/1006469521)
- **The evidence-response window is short:** at least 14 days to respond to a hold, and holds of up to 28 days. — [NDIA Guide to getting paid](https://www.ndis.gov.au/providers/pricing-and-payments/payments/guide-getting-paid)
- **Hiring signals give a prospect list:** Flintwood, CoAct, Firstchance, Collective Hope and Life Without Barriers (see Q4), plus Healthcare Australia, Waverley Social Enterprises and People First Workforce from the sector notes.

### Inferences
- **Buyer ranking [Inference]:**
  1. **Outsourced NDIS billing bureaus and VA firms.**
     - They feel multi-client ageing pain.
     - They already hold client logins and exports, and they price per client.
     - One sale reaches 10–100 small providers.
     - The product makes their A$12–22/hr staff more productive rather than competing with them.
  2. **Mid-sized registered providers that lodge agency-managed claims** (SIL, community participation, SDA, behaviour support; A$3–30m revenue).
     - Their own 90-day risk is legal, not delegated.
     - They have claims staff and client-management exports.
  3. **Plan managers.**
     - They carry the 90-day liability for about 211k providers' invoices and have a hard fixed fee (A$104.45 a month).
     - But the panel from October 2027 shrinks and consolidates them, so they are only a 12-month revenue bridge.
  4. **Allied-health NDIS providers.** Mostly plan-managed, low WTP (A$50–150 a month), and well served by practice software and remittance-extraction tools. **Avoid.**
- **Product scope for v1 (read-only, about 6–8 weeks for this founder) [Inference]:**
  - **Ingest:**
    - client-management delivered-service exports (ShiftCare, Brevity, Astalty, Flowlogic, Lumary as CSV);
    - NDIA bulk result and remittance files;
    - plan-manager remittances.
  - **Reconcile to line level:** delivered, claimed, paid, rejected, open or held.
  - **Age every line:** days since delivery, against day 90 and against the day-90 extra-check threshold already in force since 15 October.
  - **Queue rejections** by reason with suggested fixes (price limit, suffix codes, my-provider, duplicates).
  - **Track holds** with a 14-day countdown and an evidence-pack builder that only *compiles* existing service agreements, shift notes, timesheets and GPS logs into a PDF for human upload, with attestation.
  - **Report to the bureau** across all its clients.
  - **Out of scope:** submission, any claim-API integration, and any generation of evidence content.
- **AUD pricing [Inference]:**
  - **"90-day cliff health check" (manual, delivered with SQL and a dashboard) before 1 December:** A$990 for small providers, A$2,500 for mid-sized.
  - **Monitor:** A$249 a month for small providers and A$600–1,200 a month for mid-sized. This is below the A$325 a month outsourced floor for small providers and below 15–20% of a claims officer's cost for mid-sized.
  - **Bureau licence:** about A$30–50 per client per month.
  - **Avoid** success fees on "recovered" claims after 1 December, because there is little to recover.
- **Channels [Inference]:**
  - billing bureaus as resellers;
  - client-management partner directories (the Zanda partner page shows that model exists; ShiftCare and Astalty integrations);
  - NDS state networks and webinars before 1 December;
  - accountants and bookkeepers who specialise in NDIS (True Tally, Scale Suite, Allied Admin Partners);
  - LinkedIn outreach to finance managers at organisations hiring claims roles.
- **First 10 customers [Inference]:**
  - 3–4 bureaus as design partners (for example Positively Sorted, True Tally, Allied Admin Partners, and one offshore VA firm such as Care BPO or Offshore Talent);
  - 4–5 mid-sized providers currently advertising claims roles (Flintwood, Firstchance, CoAct, Collective Hope, Waverley Social Enterprises);
  - 1–2 smaller plan managers as a short-term segment.
  - Run the health check for all of them in November 2026 and convert to the monitor in December–February, when the first auto-rejections land.

### Gaps
- No discovery calls were made, so it is unknown whether bureaus would pay for tooling or see it as a threat.
- Client-management export formats and API availability were not verified, beyond the background knowledge that ShiftCare has an API.
- It is unknown whether the NDIA accepts evidence-pack PDFs compiled by third parties through the Integrity Portal, or what file types and sizes it accepts.

## Q6. Kill criteria, verdict and re-score

### Takeaway
**Verdict: DOWNGRADE (from #1 at 38/60 to 30/60). Not killed, but no longer the lead idea on its own.**

Reasons for the downgrade:
- The legal cliff is real but is a one-off event 44 days away, and its size depends on a transitional rule not yet made.
- After 1 December, late lines are auto-rejected and unrecoverable, so "recovery" turns into "ageing prevention", a feature client-management vendors can easily bundle.
- Evidence requests hit a concentrated high-risk minority.
- Claims labour is already cheap through offshore and outsourced bureaus.
- The plan-manager segment consolidates from October 2027.

It survives as a narrow bureau and mid-provider wedge, or as one module of a cross-program "government-funder claim assurance" engine.

### Cited Findings
- **Incumbents shipping the feature:** not yet. No vendor announced a 90-day ageing or Integrity Portal evidence feature in searches. But Brevity, Flowlogic and Astalty already process rejection and remittance files, and many vendors publish 90-day guidance. — [Brevity](https://brevityau.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/BS/pages/1006469521); [Flowlogic](https://guide.flowlogic.com.au/?p=1899); [Kismet](https://www.kismet.healthcare/company/newsroom/new-90-day-claims-rule-expected-from-1-december-26); [Wiise](https://www.wiise.com/blog/ndis-updates-2026-how-to-prepare)
- **NDIA API or automated evidence:** partly in place. The NDIA runs B2G claim APIs for approved entities, and the new Act brings an "Electronic form system" and "Proof for claims". No Integrity Portal API was found. — [NDIA APIs](https://ndis.gov.au/providers/working-provider/connecting-ndia-systems); [Easy Read fact sheet](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/ndis-amendment-act---ndia-measures-fact-sheet-part-2-electronic-forms-easy-read.pdf)
- **Low margins and WTP:** "nearly half" of providers made a loss in 2024-25, claims admin is unbillable to participants, and outsourced claims run from A$12/hr. — [CareMaster on NDS 2025](https://caremaster.com.au/post/the-state-of-the-disability-sector-report-2025---the-real-risk-were-not-talking-about); [NDIA](https://www.ndis.gov.au/print/pdf/node/11253)
- **Plan-manager panel:** a panel by 1 October 2027, with non-panel plan managers exiting by April 2028. — [NDIS](https://www.ndis.gov.au/changes-ndis/changes-plan-management)
- **Transitional-rule risk:** the 2024 precedent gave a 12-month grace period. — [DARU](https://daru.org.au/?p=10005794)
- **Fraud and reputation context:**
  - the Integrity Portal and pre-payment checks target high-risk and fraud-adjacent claims;
  - over two years, A$420m of high-risk claims was reviewed and more than 135k were rejected;
  - providers have been banned after taskforce investigations.

  — [NDIA news 11698](https://www.ndis.gov.au/news/11698-stronger-ndis-integrity-controls-save-billions); [NDS: providers banned](https://www.nds.org.au/news/providers-banned-following-ndis-fraud-taskforce-investigation)

### Inferences
- **Kill-criteria status [Inference]:**

  | Criterion | Status |
