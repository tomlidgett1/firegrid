## Q1. Which Australian obligations newly bite in late 2026–2027 on large numbers of businesses, and what is the CURRENT status of each deadline?

### Takeaway
Five obligations are confirmed live or legislated for the window from October 2026 to July 2027:
- the RBA card-surcharge removal, in force from 1 October 2026 and affecting every card-accepting merchant;
- Privacy Act automated decision-making (ADM) transparency, from 10 December 2026 for APP entities;
- the Payment Times Reporting regulator's move from education to enforcement in 2026;
- Scams Prevention Framework (SPF) obligations, from 31 March 2027 (the rules are still drafts);
- the Unfair Trading Practices (UTP) prohibition, plus drip-pricing and subscription rules, from 1 July 2027.

Two items are proposed but not yet law: the modern slavery "failure to prevent" offence and any easing of the ASRS climate-reporting rules. Two expected deadlines are not happening: there is no B2B e-invoicing mandate, and the mandatory AI guardrails were paused.

**Detail on selected obligations**
- **ADM scope.** A privacy policy must describe the kinds of personal information used in computer programs that make, or "do a thing substantially and directly related to making", decisions that could reasonably be expected to significantly affect an individual's rights or interests. It must also describe the kinds of such decisions. The rule applies to decisions made after 10 Dec 2026 even when the data was collected earlier. It does not create a new right to human review. Firms disagree on breadth: one says "many routine software-enabled processes" may be in scope, while another calls it a narrow disclosure duty. — [Gilbert + Tobin](https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/automated-decision-making-transparency-under-the-privacy-act); [Cowell Clarke](https://cowellclarke.com.au/insights/what-the-new-obligations-surrounding-automated-decision-making-mean-for-app-entities); [Maddocks](https://www.maddocks.com.au/insights/automated-decision-making-privacy-obligations); [HWL Ebsworth](https://hwlebsworth.com.au/?p=34237)

### Inferences
- The obligations that newly bite **broadly** (hundreds of thousands of businesses) in the window are:
  - the surcharge removal, which hits every card-accepting merchant now;
  - the unfair trading practices, drip-pricing and subscription rules, which hit every B2C business from July 2027;
  - ADM transparency, which applies to APP entities, meaning roughly businesses with turnover above A$3m.

  The obligations that bite **narrowly but expensively** are the SPF (banks, telcos and platforms), Payment Times Reporting (large businesses), ASRS Group 2 (large entities) and the modern slavery reforms (A$100m+ entities). [inference]
- Deadlines that look **sturdy**: the surcharge ban (already in force), ADM (legislated in the 2024 Act, no delay found), Payment Times Reporting (an ongoing scheme) and the unfair trading practices rules (passed). Deadlines that look **fragile**:
  - the modern slavery offence (not yet law);
  - ASRS (Treasury easing review; Group 3 in July 2027 not re-verified);
  - the Children's Online Privacy Code (start date conflicting);
  - the SPF (draft rules, and a staged "end of 2027" tail). [inference]
- A pattern repeats across the table: regulators are moving from "education" to "enforcement" in 2026. That applies to the Payment Times regulator, the OAIC sweep and Home Affairs' ransomware reporting. Products that produce evidence of correctness ("show your working") gain value as a result. [inference]

- The current status of ASRS Group 3 (1 Jul 2027) and the content of Treasury's easing proposals.
- **Not searched (budget):**

## Q2. Where are SMBs being forced by large customers, insurers, banks or government funders to produce evidence they can't produce?

### Takeaway
Supplier flow-down is real, but it is fragmented across many incompatible questionnaires and platforms. The main sources are:
- modern slavery questionnaires from retailers, miners and governments;
- WHS, insurance and licence prequalification through Cm3, Avetta, Rapid Global and iPRO;
- cyber-control attestations for insurers;
- emissions requests from ASRS reporters, which have first-year Scope 3 relief and so are weaker in 2026–27.

The proposed modern slavery "failure to prevent" offence, with its "reasonable steps" defence, is the clearest upcoming accelerant of supplier evidence demands, but it is not law yet.

### Cited Findings
- **Modern slavery questionnaires are proliferating, and each buyer uses its own format.**
  - Myer runs a supplier modern slavery questionnaire with its own FAQ. — [Myer](https://myersupplier.myer.com.au/documents/Modern%20Slavery%20Supplier%20Questionnaire%20-%20FAQs%20&%20Definitions.pdf)
  - South32 co-developed a shared resources-industry pre-screening questionnaire with 18 core questions in several languages. — [South32](https://south32.net/sustainability/operating-ethically/modern-slavery)
  - Queensland Government's procurement toolkit provides a "comprehensive" supplier questionnaire. It says the questionnaire should be proportionate to risk "to prevent barriers to participation for small and medium enterprises", and that responses can even be captured verbally. — [QLD forgov tool 4](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0025/247057/modern-slavery-tool-4.pdf)
  - Reporting entities describe reviewing questionnaire responses together with supporting documents (policies, training materials) before onboarding. Quantium is one example, per the search summary of statements on the [Modern Slavery Register](https://modernslaveryregister.gov.au/statements/QjDfNlyH8sxVha0/pdf). I did not verify which statement PDF corresponds to which company.
  - Informed 365 is used by genU and the "For Purpose Consortium" so that "all questionnaires are stored and filled out on one platform". This comes from a statement on the Register (same caveat). — [Modern Slavery Register](https://modernslaveryregister.gov.au/statements/QjDfNlyH8sxVha0/pdf)
- **The proposed reform changes the incentive.** The "failure to prevent" offence comes with a "reasonable steps" defence, and civil penalties would apply to reporting breaches. — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/australia-new-modern-slavery-offence-proposed). A construction-focused law firm is already briefing construction businesses on it. — [McCullough Robertson](https://mccullough.com.au/2026/08/21/modern-slavery-reform-what-construction-firms-need-to-know/)
- **Contractor prequalification (WHS, insurance, licences).**
  - Cm3 is an Australian WHS prequalification system. Its assessors review contractors' safety procedures, risk assessments, insurance policies and licences before site work. It says up to 40% of a new client's contractors are typically already registered. — [Cm3](https://www.cm3.com.au/2021/03/what-is-contractor-prequalification/)
  - Clients pay annual subscriptions tiered by risk profile, but no dollar figures were found. A **sale agreement to Achilles Group in July 2026** appears in a single source. — [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/cm3_contractor_management)
  - Alternatives include Avetta, Rapid Global (Adelaide HQ) and iPRO. — [TrustRadius](https://tr-web.dev.trustradius.com/compare-products/cm3-contractor-safety-compliance-management-vs-rapid-global)
  - Cm3 is listed in the simPRO trades-software marketplace. — [simPRO marketplace](https://marketplace.simprogroup.com/apps/cm3)
  - Contractors upload "up-to-date insurance, safety, and corporate documentation". — [BSG Commercial](https://www.bsgcommercial.com.au/commercial-building/why-is-cm3-prequalification-important-for-construction-a-comprehensive-guide-for-facility-asset-and-tenancy-managers/)
- **Cyber insurers want evidence, not just "yes" answers.**
  - Underwriters ask about MFA, tested backups, patching, endpoint protection, email security, privileged access, incident response plans and training. Weak answers mean higher premiums, ransomware sublimits, exclusions or no cover. — [Upcover](https://www.upcover.com/blog/cyber-insurance-requirements-australia)
  - One provider says insurers want MFA *enforced*, not merely available, on remote access, email, privileged and cloud accounts. It adds that external underwriting scans can contradict a "yes" answer, and a missing MFA control can jeopardise a claim. — [All IT Services E8 guide](https://allitservices.com.au/essential-eight-compliance-for-australian-smbs-complete-guide-2026/)

  - The NT Government's small business office publishes "what insurers and regulators now expect". — [NT OBM](https://obm.nt.gov.au/node/681)
- **Emissions requests.**
  - ASRS Group 2 first-year reports have relief from Scope 3. — [Nexia](https://nexia.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026-Financial-Reporting-Update_Nexia.pdf)
  - From the prior research round: SMEs are generally out of scope but receive customer requests, and estimates and industry averages are accepted. — [Sustainability Tracker](https://www.sustainabilitytracker.com/article/aasb-s2-group-2-climate-reporting/); [CA ANZ](https://www.charteredaccountantsanz.com/news-and-analysis/insights/research-and-insights/understanding-and-navigating-scope-3-emissions); [HLB](https://hlb.com.au/climate-reporting-and-the-impact-on-smes/)
- **Government funders.**
  - Each grant funder has different acquittal requirements: "no two funding providers have the same reporting requirements". — [Calxa](https://www.calxa.com/grant-acquittals)

This is a soft push on suppliers to government. — [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/blog/australia-e-invoicing-2026-deadlines-government-adoption.html)

### Inferences
- The SMB pain is less any single obligation than **re-answering the same evidence requests in different formats**: modern slavery, WHS, insurance certificates, cyber controls, privacy and emissions. These come through buyer-chosen platforms (Cm3, Avetta, Rapid, Informed 365, and customer portals) and ad-hoc questionnaires. An "evidence wallet plus AI questionnaire answerer" on the supplier side is the natural product. It competes with incumbents whose business model makes *suppliers* subscribe, as Cm3, Avetta and Achilles do. [inference]
- Cyber evidence is the area where SMBs most clearly "can't produce" what is asked. However, MSPs already own that relationship and sell Essential Eight uplift, so a software entrant would sell *through* MSPs. [inference]
- Emissions flow-down to SMBs will be weak until Group 2 entities' second reporting period (from FY2027–28). The prior round's verdict (crowded, weak legal pull) still holds. [inference]

### Gaps
- No pricing found for Cm3, Avetta, Rapid Global, Informed 365, Fair Supply or FRDM. No count of contractors on each platform.
- No independent verification of the Cm3–Achilles sale.
- No Australian survey quantifying hours SMB suppliers spend on questionnaires.
- Seek job-ad evidence (for example "modern slavery analyst" or "supplier compliance officer") was not gathered, because the search budget was used up.

## Q3. Where do businesses pay consultants for repetitive compliance writing that AI could now do?

### Takeaway
The clearest consultant-heavy, repetitive writing tasks are these:
- privacy policies and collection notices (now needing ADM disclosures), currently done by law firms and privacy consultancies;
- modern slavery statements and supplier questionnaires;
- tender responses;
- climate (ASRS) report drafting, where the prior round found A$80–270k first-year costs;
- grant acquittal narratives.

Tender AI in Australia is already crowded with local startups. Privacy-policy generation has cheap local tools from A$29 a month. That leaves "inventory-driven" privacy and ADM disclosure, and modern slavery evidence, as the less-served writing jobs.

### Cited Findings

  - Law firms and privacy boutiques: expensive and point-in-time.
  - MSPs: advisory content only. [cited; inference]
- **(e) Why now:** The 10 Dec 2026 commencement (53 days away), plus the 2026 enforcement turn. OAIC guidance is pending. [cited]
- **(f) Market:** APP entities number roughly 100–170k businesses (background, unverified estimate of Australian businesses above A$3m turnover). × A$1–3k a year gives roughly A$100–500m in theory. A realistic early segment is about 20–40k businesses in the targeted sectors plus the mid-market. [inference] For NZ, background (unverified): new IPP3A indirect-collection notification duties applied from 2026, which offers a similar "policy accuracy" angle.

**C4. Unfair trading practices, drip pricing and subscription-trap audit (B2C online businesses)**
- **(a) Buyer:** E-commerce operators, subscription businesses (gyms, SaaS, boxes, media), ticketing, travel and hospitality booking flows.
- **(b) Pain:** From 1 Jul 2027, checkout flows, pricing displays and cancellation journeys must avoid "unreasonable manipulation or distortion". Drip-pricing and subscription rules tighten. [cited]
- **(c) WTP:** Legal advisory demand is evident [cited firm alerts]. ACL penalties are large (background: the greater of A$50m, 3x the benefit, or 30% of turnover). Tool WTP is unproven. [gap]
- **(d) Competitors:** Law firms. Dark-pattern scanners (background, unverified; not searched).
- **(e) Why now:** The Act has passed and there is a one-year runway. [cited]
- **(f) Market:** Tens of thousands of B2C online businesses (inference) × A$50–300 a month.
- **(g) Feasibility:** Medium. A crawler with an LLM rubric is feasible, but proving "unreasonable" is subjective.
- **(h) Risks:** Vague standard, early ACCC test cases needed, possible scheme amendments. Better as an add-on to C1 for hospitality and retail pricing. [inference]

**C5. Modern slavery "reasonable steps" evidence for SMB suppliers**
- **(a) Buyer:** Suppliers (manufacturers, importers, labour-hire, cleaning, construction subcontractors) to A$100m+ entities. Also the buyers' procurement teams.
- **(b) Pain:** Many non-standard questionnaires plus supporting documents. The proposed offence would make buyers demand more evidence. [cited; inference]
- **(c) WTP:**
  - Buyer-side platforms exist and are paid for (Informed 365 [cited]; background, unverified: Fair Supply, FRDM, Sedex and EcoVadis, where suppliers often pay).
  - SMB supplier WTP is low unless a contract is at stake. [inference]
- **(d) Competitors:** Informed 365 (consortium), sector shared questionnaires (the South32 coalition), Achilles (now buying Cm3), consultancies. [cited; background]
- **(e) Why now:** US tariff pressure and the July 2026 proposal. [cited]
- **(f) Market:** Background, unverified: about 3,000+ reporting entities, each with hundreds to thousands of suppliers.
- **(g) Feasibility:** Medium. Better as a module within C6.
- **(h) Risks:** **The offence is not legislated.** Timing is unknown, and incumbents are entrenched. [cited; inference]

**C6. Supplier and contractor prequalification "evidence wallet + AI questionnaire answerer"**
- **(a) Buyer:** Trades and services SMBs (electrical, plumbing, cleaning, security, facilities, construction subcontractors) that sell to large clients, councils and governments.
- **(b) Pain:**
  - Keeping certificates of currency, licences, SWMS, WHS policies, modern slavery and cyber attestations current across multiple platforms.
  - Re-answering similar questions on every platform.
  - Expiry-driven lockouts from sites. [cited platform behaviour; inference on pain]
- **(c) WTP:** Contractors already pay platform subscriptions (Cm3 tiers [cited]; dollar amounts not found). Consultants sell WHS document packs (background, unverified).

- **(f) Market:** Background, unverified: about 400k+ construction-related businesses, plus services. × A$20–60 a month gives roughly A$100–290m in theory.

**C7. Cyber insurance and Essential Eight evidence pack for SMBs**
