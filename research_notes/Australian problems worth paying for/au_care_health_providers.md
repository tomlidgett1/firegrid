## Q1. Which provider pains created by the 2025–2026 aged care and NDIS reforms are still handled with spreadsheets and phone calls?

### Takeaway
The most manual reform-driven pains sit at the boundary between provider data and a government channel that has no API or only a thin one. The clearest cases:
- **NDIS:** the NDIA's new evidence-before-payment regime (NDIS Integrity Portal, June 2026), checks on old claims, PACE "my provider" auto-rejections, and the coming 90-day claim deadline (from 1 December 2026). Providers handle these with dedicated claims officers, Excel, PRODA and myplace portals, and phone enquiries.
- **Residential aged care:** care-minutes supplement exposure (in force since 1 April 2026), AN-ACC reclassification requests through the My Aged Care portal, and resident fee and cap checking under the new Act.
- **Support at Home:** reconciliation with brokered (associated) providers.

In allied health, workers' compensation and CTP approvals and invoicing are still manual everywhere except icare's digital invoicing channel.

### Cited Findings

**NDIS: evidence, rejections and deadlines (new in 2026)**
- The NDIS Amendment (Integrity and Safeguarding) Act 2025, passed in April 2026, mandates electronic claim forms and gives the NDIA power to request evidence before claims are paid. A later Act (August 2026) adds civil penalties and expanded monitoring and investigation powers. — [NDIA news: Stronger NDIS integrity controls save billions](https://ndis.gov.au/news/11698-stronger-ndis-integrity-controls-save-billions); [Minister McAllister media release](https://www.health.gov.au/ministers/senator-the-hon-jenny-mcallister/media/stronger-ndis-integrity-controls-save-billions)
- The **NDIS Integrity Portal** launched in June 2026. It is separate from myplace and the my NDIS provider portal, and its only function today is "submitting evidence for new claims". Providers are told by **letter** when and how to use it, and more functionality "will be added over time". — [NDIA provider guide](https://www.ndis.gov.au/media/8698/download); [NDIA news](https://ndis.gov.au/news/11698-stronger-ndis-integrity-controls-save-billions)
- From 18 June 2026 the NDIA checks claims submitted more than 12 months after the support. It plans to extend this to claims submitted 6 months or more after the support. Older claims "may be held for up to 28 days". The checks cover providers, plan managers and self-managed participants. — [NDIA](https://www.ndis.gov.au/print/pdf/node/11575)
- For SDA, home and living, and behaviour supports, claims are automatically rejected unless the provider is recorded as a "my provider" on the participant's plan. My-provider claims are generally paid in 2–3 days, against about 10 days for others. Unauthorised claims show as "rejected", and the remedy is a payment enquiry through the portal **or a phone call to 1300 311 675**. — [NDIA](https://www.ndis.gov.au/print/pdf/node/11370); [Leap in!](https://www.leapin.com.au/?p=4131)
- A vendor commentary says an "Open" claim status in 2026 often means a permissions or endorsement issue, not a billing error. — [RomeoHR](https://romeohr.com/blog/ndis-claim-disputes-a-resolution-playbook)
- **90-day claim window:** "From 1 December 2026 the time to make a claim for supports under a participant's plan will be reduced from 2 years to 90 days." Commentary says this covers agency-managed, plan-managed and self-managed claims. — [Department provider FAQs (July 2026)](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/securing-the-future-of-the-ndis-for-future-generations-provider-faqs.pdf); [Bill fact sheet, Part 5](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-05/ndis-amendment-securing-the-ndis-for-future-generations-bill-2026---fact-sheet.pdf); [Flowlogic](https://flowlogic.com.au/?p=77958)

Some measures commenced 7 days after assent. — [Department timeline (Sept 2026)](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/securing-the-ndis-for-future-generations-timeline.pdf); also [NDIS](https://www.ndis.gov.au/print/pdf/node/11731), cited in prior notes

### Inferences
- **The NDIS is where reform has most recently created new manual work.** Three things arrive together: evidence requests delivered by letter and answered through a separate portal with a single function; claims on hold for up to 28 days; and a hard 90-day deadline from 1 December 2026. Until now providers could clear backlogs over two years. **[Inference]**
- **Phone and portal remedies show there is no API.** The remedies are a call to 1300 311 675, a portal payment enquiry and an evidence upload. No API for evidence submission was found. **[Inference]**
- **These are "outcome desk" conditions:** an agent can prepare the case and a human signs it off.
- Residential aged care's manual pain is less about tools and more about workforce. Care-minute shortfalls are mainly a staffing problem (see Q2), which caps what software can fix. **[Inference]**

### Gaps
- No primary provider quotes (Reddit or forums) were captured within the search budget.
- The 1300 phone channel and the Integrity Portal letter process are documented, but complaint volumes and turnaround are not.
- It was not confirmed whether the NDIA plans a B2G API for Integrity Portal evidence. Its Digital Enrolment, Access and Payment Program began July 2026 with full rollout by June 2030 ([NDIA](https://ndis.gov.au/news/11698-stronger-ndis-integrity-controls-save-billions)), and that program could add one.
- Seek itself returned no listings. The job evidence comes from Employment Hero, Ethical Jobs and career pages.

## Q2. Where do providers lose measurable money (rejected/unpaid claims, underclaiming of funding classifications, payroll tax assessments, audit findings)?

### Takeaway
The largest measurable and recurring losses are in two places. The first is the residential care-minutes supplement: up to about A$33–34 per resident per day withheld, with roughly 500 metro homes estimated at risk and 854 homes missing both targets in the June 2026 quarter. The second is NDIS claims: A$420m of high-risk claims reviewed before payment over two financial years and more than 135,000 rejected. Early Integrity Portal reviews rejected about 72% of the claims they examined, and from 1 December 2026 a claim not lodged within 90 days is lost outright.

Other leakage is smaller or harder to measure:
- AN-ACC underclassification is real but contested and served by consultancies;
- Medicare compliance debts carry a 20% penalty;
- GP payroll-tax exposure has been softened by Queensland's exemption, NSW and Victorian bulk-billing relief, and BBPIP-driven bulk billing;
- allied health loses unrecoverable workers' compensation treatment and unpaid report time.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **The A$33.41 vs A$34.26 conflict is probably a price-year difference. [Inference]** 0.5 − 0.387 = 0.113 NWAU, and 0.113 × A$303.19 (the 2026-27 price) = **A$34.26**, matching the news figure. A$33.41 ÷ 0.113 ≈ A$295.7, consistent with the previous year's price **[Background, unverified]**. So the maximum penalty now appears to be about A$34.26 per resident per day.
- **Exposure per home: about A$1.25m a year [Inference].** A$34.26 × 100 occupied beds × 365 days ≈ A$1.25m a year for a 100-bed metro home that gets zero supplement. This is the largest single, recurring, measurable funding risk found in this sector. However, closing it mainly requires recruiting and rostering nurses, not software.
- **Implied number of homes: about 2,536 [Inference].** If 854 homes = 33.68% (100% − 66.32%), the reporting population is about 2,536 homes, consistent with background knowledge of about 2,500–2,600 residential homes **[Background, unverified]**.
- **NDIS rejection rates on reviewed claims are high [Inference]:** about 72% by count and about 66% by value in the early Integrity Portal sample, and about 51% of high-risk claims over two years. These are selected high-risk claims, not the whole book. For an honest provider, the realistic loss is legitimate claims rejected for missing evidence, plus claims that miss the 90-day window.
- **A claims officer costs about A$80–90k a year fully loaded [Inference]** (A$69–72k salary plus roughly 15–25% on-costs). This anchors the value of an automation or outcome-desk product at about A$1–3k a month per mid-sized provider.
- **GP payroll tax:** no evidence was found of 2026 assessment amounts. With the Queensland exemption permanent, the NSW and Victorian relief tied to bulk billing, and BBPIP lifting bulk billing (about 3,800 practices), exposure for GP-only practices is probably shrinking. Exposure persists for mixed-billing practices, specialists, dentists and allied health **[Inference]**.

### Gaps
- No official count of homes whose care-minutes supplement was actually reduced after April 2026, and no aggregate dollars withheld.
- No aggregate NDIS rejection rate across all claims, and no figure for legitimate claims lost to evidence requests.
- No 2026 payroll-tax assessment figures for medical centres, and no 2026 revenue-office ruling changes. NSW and Victorian thresholds should be checked against the revenue offices.
- No 2026 Professional Services Review (PSR) or Medicare debt totals.
- No current AN-ACC reclassification volumes for 2025–2026; the volumes found are from 2022.
- No 2026 workers' compensation or CTP payment-time statistics, and nothing for Victoria (TAC/WorkSafe) or Queensland (WorkCover).

## Q3. Which areas are already crowded with local vendors?

### Takeaway
The following are crowded or bundled: GP practice analytics, including payroll tax and bulk-billing modelling (Cubiko plus accountants); care-minutes measurement (Mirus Care Minute Manager, OneAdvanced, rostering vendors); AN-ACC funding optimisation (Mirus, Health Metrics eCase, consultancies, clinical systems); NDIS and Support at Home claim generation (ShiftCare, Lumary, Brevity, CareMaster, SupportAbility, Flowlogic, Lookout, AlayaCare, QuickClaim); and icare invoicing (Tyro Health, HICAPS Digital).
