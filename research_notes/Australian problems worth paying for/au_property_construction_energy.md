# Unsolved, High-Willingness-to-Pay Problems in Australian Property, Construction, Strata, Real Estate and Household Energy (as of 18 October 2026)

Method note: 22 web searches (the shared budget) on 18 Oct 2026. WebFetch failed with DNS errors on every attempt (qbcc.qld.gov.au, reneweconomy.com.au, thegoodbuilder.com.au), so findings rely on search-result summaries of the pages cited. Most of those pages are secondary sources: law-firm alerts, trade press, vendor pages. Anything not confirmed by a search this session is labelled **background knowledge, not verified** and kept in Inferences or Gaps. Dollar figures are AUD unless stated.

## Objective: Which specific Australian problems would businesses clearly pay to solve where no great solution exists? (Candidate catalogue of 11 + top-3 ranking)

### Takeaway
The strongest Australian candidate in this sector is a **subcontractor "security-of-payment cash-recovery desk"**. It would track statutory deadlines, check payment schedules, prepare adjudication applications, and chase retention and performance-security release. Queensland's adjudication registry alone handled about 477 valid applications claiming $1.94bn, plus 82 applications worth $14.1m that were ruled invalid, mostly for missed deadlines. The year attribution is probably 2025–26 but needs checking. Victoria widened what can be adjudicated from 15 April 2026. Construction remains the most insolvency-prone sector, with about 3,472 insolvencies in FY2025–26.

The big caveat, and the lesson from the last round: **claim drafting itself is already filling with cheap local tools**: PayClaim at a flat $79, paymentclaims.ai, ClaimDone, TenderBuilt and Archdesk, plus Retention Track for retentions. The open gap is the *post-claim* outcome work, which law firms and claims preparers still do by hand: schedule rebuttal, adjudication application and enforcement.

The #2 and #3 picks are more speculative:
- **#2: Victorian rental minimum energy-efficiency compliance plus a VEU-funded upgrade desk for property-management agencies.** The standards phase in from 1 March 2027 and tooling is immature.
- **#3: An NSW/Vic strata levy-arrears and payment-plan desk.** NSW changed debt-recovery rules from 27 October 2025, and a 2026 VCAT decision requires itemised fee notices.

Battery-rebate (STC) paperwork, subcontractor licence/insurance tracking, bond-claim evidence and general PM software all look **crowded or bundled free by incumbents**.

### Cited Findings

#### Candidate 1 — Security of Payment (SOPA) claims → payment schedule → adjudication → enforcement, for subcontractors (QLD, VIC, NSW first)
- (b/c) **Queensland volumes (conflicting year attribution):**
  - Lawyers Weekly, citing the QBCC 2024–25 annual report: the Adjudication Registry received 324 valid applications (273 standard claims up to $750,000 and 51 complex claims above it), together claiming $516.7m. — [Lawyers Weekly](https://www.lawyersweekly.com.au/sme-law/44981-adjudication-is-won-on-site-not-in-the-registry)
  - A table in the QBCC 2025–26 annual report shows "TOTAL 82 $14,135,507 477 $1,939,433,565". The Good Builder reads this as 82 applications worth $14.1m ruled invalid, with "missed deadlines topped the list", and 477 valid applications carrying $1.94bn. $1.88bn of that came from just 38 applications above $1.24m. The registry released 290 decisions, averaging 25 business days to decide. The sources do not state the year clearly, so treat $1.94bn as likely 2025–26. — [The Good Builder](https://thegoodbuilder.com.au/14-1-million-in-queensland-adjudication-claims-ruled-invalid-missed-deadlines-topped-the-list/); [QBCC Annual Report 2025–26 (PDF)](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/publications-qbcc-annual-report-2025-2026.pdf)
  - For 2023–24: 363 adjudication applications received, of which 325 were referred to an adjudicator. — [QBCC Annual Report 2023–24](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/corporate-qbcc-ar-23-24-annual-report.pdf)
- (c) **Fees subbies already pay (government and nominating authorities):**
  - QBCC application fees are tiered by claim size: $68.77 for claims up to $11,345.70 and $206.38 up to $56,728.80, plus adjudicator fees and expenses. — [QBCC Apply for adjudication](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/complaints-disputes/payment-contract/commercial-payment-dispute/adjudication/apply-adjudication)
  - Adjudicate Today says it charges no application fee (promotional claim). — [Adjudicate Today fee policy](https://www.adjudicate.com.au/company/fee-policy)
  - RICS says it has no application fee outside WA and NT, with an optional four-tier fixed fee. — [RICS Australasia](https://www.rics.org/dispute-resolution-service/drs-services/non-uk-dispute-resolution-services/dispute-resolution-service-in-australasia/adjudication-in-australasia)
  - In Tasmania and South Australia, claimant and respondent share the adjudicator's fees equally by default. — [Tas Building Standards](https://buildingstandards.tas.gov.au/topics/products-services/problems/resolving-a-building-dispute/adjudication-process); [SA SOPA s30](https://www8.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/sa/consol_act/bacisopa2009606/s30.html)
- (e) **Why now (Victoria):** Victoria's SOP amendments (Building Legislation Amendment (Fairer Payments on Jobsites and Other Matters) Act) were proclaimed on **15 April 2026**. The changes:
  - The "excluded amounts" regime is repealed, so disputed variations, delay costs, latent conditions and changes in law can now be adjudicated.
  - Respondents cannot raise reasons in adjudication that were not in the payment schedule.
  - Notice-based time bars can be declared unenforceable where compliance was unreasonably onerous.
  - A new process lets claimants seek release of performance security, including retention money.
  - Minter Ellison says the amended Act applies to all payment claims from 15 April 2026. Baker McKenzie says it does not apply to claims served before commencement. This conflict is unresolved.
  - Sources: [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/changes-to-victorias-security-of-payment-regime-have-now-commenced); [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/04/australia-victorias-security-of-payment-regime-overhauled); [Clayton Utz, Sept 2025](https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2025/september/critical-amendments-to-legislation-regulating-entitlement-to-progress-payments-under-construction-contracts-in-victoria)
- (e) **NSW:**
  - According to Holding Redlich, a broader NSW SOP reform package had been dropped by late November 2025. The Fair Trading and Building Legislation Amendment Bill 2026 proposes only a one-word change to the SOP Act ("business days" to "working days"). — [Holding Redlich](https://www.holdingredlich.com/nsw-security-of-payment-decisions-in-2025-learnings-for-claims-this-year)
  - Global Law Experts describe the same Bill as mainly expanding regulator enforcement powers (administrative penalties, stop-work) and say it was introduced in May 2026. The descriptions and timing conflict. — [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/building-law-reforms-australia/)
  - In NSW a subcontractor can also use the Contractors Debts Act 1997 to recover from the principal when the head contractor fails to pay. This requires an adjudication certificate and then a court debt certificate. — [NSW Gov, Enforcing payment](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/compliance-and-regulation/security-of-payment/enforcing-payment)
- (b) **Deadline complexity is real and sources disagree:**
  - Payment schedules are due in about 10 business days in NSW. In QLD the deadline is 15 business days or the contract period, whichever is shorter.
  - NSW subcontractor payment is due within 20 business days for non-exempt residential work.
  - Sources conflict on claim-service windows: "12 months in most states" versus "6 months in QLD, WA, SA and VIC".
  - Sources: [TenderBuilt guide](https://tenderbuilt.com.au/security-of-payment-australia/); [ClaimDone](https://claimdone.com.au/subcontractor-payment-dispute-australia-demand-vs-sop/)
- (d) **Competitors found (local, mostly small, several AI-native):**
  - **PayClaim** prepares a SOP claim online for a flat $79. — [PayClaim](https://payclaim.com.au/security-of-payment-act-explained-how-australian-subcontractors-actually-get-paid/)
  - **paymentclaims.ai** offers "AI-assisted payment claims for AU + NZ construction". It drafts statutory claims and matches the head contractor's payment schedule against the line items it answers. — [paymentclaims.ai](https://www.paymentclaims.ai/)
  - **ClaimDone** has subbie-facing content on demand letters versus SOP claims. — [ClaimDone](https://claimdone.com.au/subcontractor-payment-dispute-australia-demand-vs-sop/)
  - **Archdesk** builds progress-claim applications from site progress and approved variations. — [Archdesk](https://archdesk.com/en-au/solutions/progress-claims)
  - **Built Simple** handles scheduling and defects but per a comparison page does not produce formatted SOP claims, payment schedules or statutory declarations. — [Built Simple Payapps comparison](https://builtsimple.com.au/payapps-alternative/); [Built Simple subcontractor software guide](https://builtsimple.com.au/blog/subcontractor-management-software-australia/)
  - **Retention Track** (Xero App Store, AU/NZ/UK) also "helps prepare a claim or adjudication submission if a head contractor won't pay". See Candidate 2. — [Xero App Store](https://apps.xero.com/au/app/retention-track)
  - **Labour-based and association options:** private nominating authorities (Adjudicate Today, RICS) and construction law firms, e.g. [Boss Lawyers](https://bosslawyers.com.au/qbcc-retention-money-claim-builder-liquidation-queensland/).
- (h) **Exposure to the cycle (demand is counter-cyclical):**
  - Construction insolvencies were 3,472 in FY2025–26, down 3.4% on FY2024–25, the first annual fall in five years. Other cuts of the data give 3,435 and 3,475; Ai Group gives 3,475, up from 1,515 in 2018–19. — [The Good Builder](https://thegoodbuilder.com.au/construction-insolvencies-just-fell-for-the-first-time-in-five-years/); [Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/business/22687-company-insolvencies-climb-to-14-152-for-fy-2025-26)
  - Construction was 24.5% of all 14,152 external administrations in FY2025–26. — [Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/business/22687-company-insolvencies-climb-to-14-152-for-fy-2025-26)

#### Candidate 2 — Retention money and performance-security release / recovery (subcontractor side)
- (b) **Queensland:** project trust accounts (PTA) and retention trust accounts (RTA) create statutory trusts over money on eligible contracts and roll out in phases. — [QBCC Trust accounts](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/running-your-business/trust-accounts); [QBCC regulatory guide v5, Jan 2024](https://Www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-04/reg-guide-trusts-project-retention.pdf)
  - A Queensland law firm says many head contractors don't comply with the RTA requirement, and subbies often learn in liquidation that their retention was never held separately. A missing RTA doesn't extinguish the claim, but the subbie then ranks as an unsecured creditor. This is law-firm marketing. — [Boss Lawyers](https://bosslawyers.com.au/qbcc-retention-money-claim-builder-liquidation-queensland/)
- (c) **NSW:** in an undated NSW inquiry account, a liquidator found more than $7m of subcontractor retention had been used as working capital, and a witness estimated recovery at about 3 cents in the dollar. — [Urban.com.au](https://www.urban.com.au/news/nsw-subcontractors-to-get-greater-protection-against-builder-collapse)
- (b) **Recent collapse:** the Open Projects Group (QLD) liquidation in May 2026 had estimated creditor losses above $80m and close to 1,000 creditors. — [Boss Lawyers](https://bosslawyers.com.au/open-projects-group-liquidation-subcontractors-creditors/)
- (e) **Victoria:** the reforms of 15 April 2026 add a process for claimants to seek release of performance security, including bonds, guarantees and retention money. — [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/changes-to-victorias-security-of-payment-regime-have-now-commenced)
- (d) **Competitor:** **Retention Track** is a cloud platform "to account for, track and recover retention money". It has release reminders, claims "matching local legislation in AU, NZ and developing UK legislation", and two-way Xero sync. It has few reviews; one AU reviewer says it automates when claims and retention fall due. — [Xero App Store listing](https://apps.xero.com/au/app/retention-track); [reviews](https://apps.xero.com/au/app/retention-track/reviews)
  - Also relevant: Plexa publishes a retention-vs-bank-guarantee calculator. — [Plexa](https://www.plexapro.com/tools/retention-vs-bank-guarantee-calculator)

#### Candidate 4 — Cheaper Home Batteries Program (CHBP): installer STC paperwork, rectification and audit risk
- (b/e) **Rebate changes from 1 May 2026:**
  - The STC factor tapers by capacity: 100% up to 14 kWh, 60% for each kWh from 14 to 28, and 15% for each kWh from 28 to 50.
  - The rebate follows the installation date, not the contract date.
  - The CER told retailers not to promise installs they couldn't finish before 1 May, and said it "will inspect installations and remove bad players".
  - Sources: [CER, Mar 2026](https://cer.gov.au/news-and-media/media/2026/march/battery-rebates-are-changing-1-may-2026); [pv magazine Australia](https://www.pv-magazine-australia.com/?p=15819)
  - The regulator warned the change could mean price differences of thousands of dollars. — [RenewEconomy](https://reneweconomy.com.au/home-battery-rebate-changes-could-mean-price-difference-of-thousands-of-dollars-regulator-warns/amp/)
- (f) **Money flowing:** the program budget was raised to about $7.2bn over four years, and is expected to help more than 2 million households install a battery by 2030. — [RenewEconomy](https://reneweconomy.com.au/another-5-billion-tipped-into-cheaper-home-batteries-but-rebates-slashed-for-bigger-systems)
  - Per Solahart, the STC factor falls every six months, with the May–Dec 2026 factor proposed at 6.8, down from 8.4 (subject to regulations). — [Solahart](https://solahart.com.au/incentives/changes-cheaper-home-batteries-program)
- (b) **Paperwork pain:**
  - New CER battery-labelling requirements apply from **1 March 2026**. Non-compliant labelling is treated as "unqualified" and sent back for rectification, "usually requiring the installer to revisit the site". — [Greendeal help](https://help.greendeal.com.au/en/articles/67664-summary-of-stc-battery-labelling-requirements)
  - Evidence required includes the STC assignment form, on-site verification statements from owner and installer, start/mid/end photos, switchboard and signage photos, and battery label photos. — [BridgeSelect docs](https://bridgeselect.com.au/docs/docs-faqs/battery-stc-scheme.html); [Pylon](https://getpylon.com/blog/stc-trading-solar-installers-and-retailers/)
- (d) **Incumbents bundle the paperwork free:**
  - Greendeal says the CER charges $0.47 per STC created, but Greendeal charges no separate admin fee for battery STC claims. GD Finance charges no processing or upfront fees, and Greendeal runs a next-day payment promotion. — [Greendeal battery FAQ](https://www.greendeal.com.au/battery_faq); [Greendeal trading T&Cs](https://www.greendeal.com.au/tradingtermsandconditions)
  - Formbay and BridgeSelect support battery STC submission. Per Formbay, eligible batteries are 5–100 kWh nominal, with at most 50 kWh usable counting for STCs. — [Formbay](https://formbay.com.au/battery-guide-pack); [MAC Trade Services](https://mactradeservices.com.au/were-making-battery-rebates-easier-faster-and-more-profitable)
  - Other agents: [Trade in Green](https://tradeingreen.com.au/battery-stcs.html).

#### Candidate 5 — Victorian rental minimum energy-efficiency standards (from 1 March 2027) plus VEU-funded upgrades
- (b/e) **Scope and timing:** new standards apply to all Victorian residential rentals, covering heating, cooling, hot water, shower heads, ceiling insulation and draughtproofing.
  - Phase-in starts 1 March 2027.
  - Ceiling insulation to R5.0 is triggered at the start of a new lease.
  - Draughtproofing applies from 1 July 2027 at a new agreement or a move to month-to-month.
  - Heating and hot water must be upgraded when the existing system fails.
  - Efficient cooling is required in all rentals by 1 July 2030.
  - Sources: [Energy Vic](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/renewable-energy/victorias-gas-substitution-roadmap/energy-efficiency-for-rental-properties-in-victoria); [Consumer Affairs Vic](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/resources-and-tools/legislation/public-consultations-and-reviews/new-minimum-energy-efficiency-standards)
- (b) **Evidence burden:** exemptions apply where compliance is impractical or too costly (e.g. shared ceiling space, centralised systems, heritage). Rental providers "should keep proof that an exemption applies". — [Energy Vic info for rental providers](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0025/775321/energy-efficiency-for-rental-homes-in-victoria-information-for-rental-providers-accessible.docx)
- (c) **Money flowing:** every upgrade under the standards is eligible for a Victorian Energy Upgrades (VEU) discount, with ceiling-insulation discounts available from late 2026. Solar for Rentals offers 50% of up to $1,400. Government figures put renter savings at up to $454/yr for insulation, $220 for electric hot water and $215 for efficient heating and cooling. — [Energy Vic](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/renewable-energy/victorias-gas-substitution-roadmap/energy-efficiency-for-rental-properties-in-victoria)
- (d) **Competitors:** agencies are publishing explainers, e.g. [Kay & Burton](https://www.kayburton.com.au/victorias-evolving-minimum-standards-for-rental-properties-what-you-need-to-know/) and [Raine & Horne Sunbury](https://legacy.raineandhorne.com.au/sunbury/rental-minimum-standards-victoria).
  - A targeted search for a compliance-check provider offering this (e.g. Detector Inspector) **returned nothing**. Absence of evidence, not proof of a gap.

#### Candidate 6 — Strata levy arrears: mandatory payment plans, notices and tribunal recovery (NSW, VIC, WA)
- (e) **NSW changes from 27 October 2025:**
  - Owners corporations must offer a payment plan before recovery action.
  - At least 30 days' notice is needed before starting proceedings.
  - Interest and recovery expenses generally can't be recovered without an NCAT or court order.
  - An OC can reasonably refuse a plan if it would leave the capital works or administrative fund short.
  - Sources: [Strata Choice](https://www.stratachoice.com.au/nsw-strata-reforms-changes-to-debt-recovery-payment-plans-from-27-october-2025/); [NSW Gov guide](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/strata/guide-to-strata-law-changes-for-strata-committees-and-owners); [LookUpStrata](https://www.lookupstrata.com.au/nsw-levy-collection-practice-is-changing-are-you-ready/amp/)
- (b) **Victoria:** in *Owners Corporation PS415135N v Imam* [2026] VCAT 704, a lump-sum "balance brought forward" on a Final Fee Notice was held not recoverable unless each component is itemised. — [LookUpStrata](https://www.lookupstrata.com.au/vic-recovering-oc-arrears-vcat-says-balance-brought-forward-is-not-enough/)
  - Bankruptcy is available for lot-owner debts of at least $10,000; another source says $5,000 (conflict). — [LookUpStrata](https://www.lookupstrata.com.au/vic-when-can-an-owners-corporation-commence-bankruptcy-proceedings-for-unpaid-levies/); [Jamesons](https://jamesons.com.au/blog/will-happen-dont-pay-strata-levies/)
- (b) **Arrears delay repairs (NSW).** — [LookUpStrata](https://www.lookupstrata.com.au/nsw-what-can-strata-do-if-levy-arrears-delay-repairs/)
- (b) **WA:** strata managers commonly pass recovery and notice costs to owners in arrears. Whether that is lawful depends on the specific rules. — [LookUpStrata WA](https://www.lookupstrata.com.au/wa-owners-liable-for-strata-recovery-charges/)
- (b) Recovery processes are also described by [PICA Group](https://picagroup.com.au/?p=56815).
- (d) **AI-native strata entrants:** AIBUILD's **StrataXcel** (Melbourne, "AI-native", live with first Victorian clients) aims to cut strata-manager admin. — [IT Brief](https://itbrief.com.au/story/aibuild-launches-strataxcel-for-australia-s-strata-sector)
  - **StrataView** offers committee software with AI by-law answers and AI-drafted reports. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10043508/StrataView/)
  - A UQ-led ARC Linkage project (2025–2028) is building "Australia's first strata management system powered by LLMs". — [UQ](https://about.uq.edu.au/experts/project/70086)

#### Candidate 7 — NSW 10-year capital works fund plans (standard form mandatory from 1 April 2026)
- (e) **NSW strata reforms were staged:**
  - 1 July 2025: managing agents report six-monthly instead of annually, a statutory agent defence was introduced, and fund planning and capital-works contribution forecasts were tightened.
  - 27 October 2025: building-manager definitions and duties.
  - 1 April 2026: a standard form must be used for the 10-year capital works fund plan, which lists proposed work, timing, anticipated cost and funding source and must be reviewed at least every 5 years.
  - Mandatory committee training is still to be announced.
  - Sources: [REINSW](https://www.reinsw.com.au/Web/News/REINSW_Updates/2025/june/Strata-reforms-Key%20changes-coming-into-effect-from-1-july-2025.aspx); [NSW Gov guide](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/strata/guide-to-2025-strata-law-changes); [Strata Schemes Legislation Amendment Act 2025 No 14](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/pdf/asmade/act-2025-14); [PBL Legal](https://pbl.legal/insights/capital-works-plans-nsw-strata-reforms/)

## Key Q1: Where do Australian subcontractors, strata managers, property managers and installers lose measurable money or time to paperwork against counterparties (head contractors, tribunals, regulators, councils)?

### Takeaway
The clearest measured losses are at the **subcontractor–head contractor–adjudication registry** boundary. In QLD, about $14.1m of adjudication claims were ruled invalid in one year, with missed deadlines the top reason. Retention is routinely not held in trust and is lost in liquidations at cents in the dollar. Construction failures run at about 3,400–3,500 a year.

Installers lose time and site revisits when battery STC claims are rejected for labelling defects. Strata managers face new pre-litigation steps before recovering levy arrears. All of these are counterparty paperwork with no API.

### Cited Findings
- **QLD adjudications:** 82 applications worth $14,135,507 were ruled invalid, with "missed deadlines topped the list". 477 valid applications claimed $1,939,433,565. — [The Good Builder](https://thegoodbuilder.com.au/14-1-million-in-queensland-adjudication-claims-ruled-invalid-missed-deadlines-topped-the-list/); [QBCC AR 2025–26](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/publications-qbcc-annual-report-2025-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Invalid claims are a direct paperwork loss.** Invalid QLD claims averaged roughly $172k each ($14.1m ÷ 82). A deadline-and-validity checker that prevents even a few invalid applications per customer pays for itself many times over. This is the strongest "measurable money lost to paperwork" datapoint found.
- **The SME sweet spot is mid-sized claims.** Excluding the 38 mega-claims, the remaining QLD claims average ~$135k. That is too big to write off, but often too small to justify $10k+ of legal spend. This is the gap an outcome-priced desk fills.
- **Rules that tie respondents to their schedules help claimants.** Vic now bars respondents from raising new reasons at adjudication. Subbies benefit most if they act on deficient schedules quickly, which again rewards deadline tracking and automated schedule analysis.
- **Strata arrears shift from passed-through to absorbed costs.** The NSW changes turn arrears recovery from a cost largely passed through to defaulters (via law firms) into a multi-step, tribunal-gated process whose costs managers can't automatically recover. That creates time pressure on agents, who are already doing six-monthly reporting.

### Gaps
- No national data on unpaid subcontractor amounts (no ASIC breakdown found), NSW/Vic adjudication volumes, or average lawyer and claims-preparer fees.
- No CER data on rejected or failed battery STC claims, and no measured cost per site revisit.
- No data on PM hours spent on bond disputes or NCAT/VCAT/QCAT outcomes.
- No strata arrears totals.

### Inferences
- **Last round's error would recur here.** A venture-database check would miss every competitor above: none surfaced as funded startups, all are local bootstrapped tools, agents or listings.
- **What the crowding means for #1.** The SOP space shows the classic pattern: the cheap document-generation layer fills quickly, and the outcome layer (adjudication, enforcement, money actually recovered) stays with lawyers. Enter at the outcome layer, use claim drafting as a free or cheap funnel, and price on recovery.
- **Avoid product categories that sit inside incumbents' core workflow.** PM inspections, smoke-alarm compliance, trust accounting, conveyancing, STC trading and trades job management fall in this group. Incumbents ship new rules as free features there.
