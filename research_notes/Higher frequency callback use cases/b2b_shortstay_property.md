# Short-stay co-hosts and adjacent property segments as buyers of a "text a number, the right human calls you within 15 minutes" dispatch line (Australia, Melbourne focus, as of October 2026)

**Method:**
- All 28 permitted web searches were used.
- The single permitted WebFetch attempt (insideairbnb.com/melbourne) failed with a DNS error. Every finding below therefore comes from search-result snippets and summaries, not full-page reads.
- **[BK]** marks background knowledge or my own assumptions, not verified in this session.
- Undated or pre-2025 data is labelled "older".
- This note builds on `business_power_users.md` in the same folder and avoids repeating it. That note already covers: the AirDNA count of about 33.3k active Melbourne listings (June 2026, down 5.3% year on year); Turno's bid mechanism; Melbourne management commissions of 15–30%; Tapi's basic description; Realhub; Victorian agent rebate rules; and the first ranking (co-hosts 44/60, sales agents 36/60, PM agencies 33/60). Those points are referenced here, not re-cited.

**Bottom line:**
- Short-stay co-hosts still look like the best beachhead, but a narrower and smaller one than the first ranking implied.
- The "AI dispatches your vendors" layer is now being built by property-management-system vendors and YC startups, so the defensible part is a local, vetted fallback network with a speed guarantee.
- Per-listing software prices in this market are about US$6–20 a month. The A$5–9k a year account sketch is therefore the top of the plausible range, not the middle.
- Sales agents' pre-sale fix-ups are a better second segment than PM agencies, where Tapi is already large.

## Q1. Market size and structure in Melbourne and Victoria

### Takeaway
Victoria has roughly 40–45k short-stay listings, and growth stopped in 2025, the year the 7.5% levy began. Melbourne's supply has since edged down (see sibling note).

Multi-listing operators are a small minority of hosts but hold an outsized share of listings. Only about 7% of Melbourne Airbnb hosts list 6 or more properties, and about 2% list 20 or more (vendor data, undated). Rough maths puts the Melbourne pool of professional co-hosts and multi-listing operators at **about 650–1,500 accounts**. That is a niche: enough for a beachhead, but not a large business on Melbourne alone.

The market is fragmented. Large national managers have small Melbourne footprints, and Airbnb's Co-Host Network (launched in Australia in October 2024) is formalising independent co-hosts.

### Cited Findings

Treat it as secondary. — [Republished article, "Victoria's Airbnb boom is over, but don't expect a rental windfall"](https://retrui.duckdns.org/en/news/victorias-airbnb-boom-is-over-but-dont-expect-a-rental-windfall-zxua7)

### Inferences
**Sizing the Melbourne co-host pool:**
- Start from roughly 17–19k active Airbnb listings.
- Assume about 1.5–2 listings per host [BK], which gives roughly 9–12k hosts.
- Apply the vendor shares (5.18% with 6–20 listings, 2.04% with 20+). That gives about 470–640 operators with 6–20 listings and about 180–250 with 20+.
- Total: **about 650–900 Airbnb-based multi-listing operators**, and perhaps up to about 1,500 if multi-platform-only operators are included.
- At A$3–6k a year per account, 100% share of Melbourne would be about A$2–9m in annual recurring revenue (ARR). A realistic 10–20% share is about **A$0.3–1.5m ARR**.
- Melbourne therefore works as a proving ground. A venture-scale outcome needs Sydney, the Gold Coast and regional holiday markets.

**Revenue per listing:**
- AirDNA's A$91 RevPAR × 365 nights ≈ **A$33k gross a year** per average listing. Premium managed listings are about A$64k (Airbtics' Hometime estimate).
- A co-host on 15–20% earns about A$5–6.6k a year per average listing. A A$20-a-month subscription would take about 4–5% of that.

**Effect of the levy, council rules and fees:**
- Together they cap growth and push marginal hosts out. Supply plateaued in 2025 and fell in 2026.
- The same pressure plausibly drives the remaining owners to outsource to professional co-hosts, who need tools. This is an inference; no data on co-host growth in Victoria was found.

**Fragmentation is good for a bottom-up product:**
- Hometime has an estimated 65 Airbnb listings in Melbourne.
- Nationally, MadeComfy has about 1,300 listings and Hometime had about 1,500 (older figure).

**Where the listings are:**
- Commercial listings cluster in the CBD, Southbank, Docklands, Fitzroy and St Kilda.
- That density suits a vetted local supply network. A founder can cover the inner city with a few dozen cleaners, handymen and locksmiths [BK].

### Gaps
- Inside Airbnb's current Melbourne host-concentration data (calculated host listings) could not be retrieved because of the DNS failure. This remains the single most useful sizing input.
- No official count of Victorian co-hosts, or of growth in co-host numbers, was found.
- A figure of about 4,100 active listings in the City of Melbourne (2023) appeared in a search summary but could not be tied to a source page.
- Actual 2025 levy collections were not found.
- Average length of stay in Melbourne is behind AirDNA's paywall, so turnover frequency still rests on [BK] assumptions.
- No City of Yarra-specific rules were found.

## Q2. How often do urgent or non-routine needs arise per listing per month?

### Takeaway
No Australian operator data or per-listing incident rate was found. The best evidence comes from a US-heavy vendor survey: **34.3% of hosts and managers lost bookings or got negative reviews because of staffing or contractor problems in 2025**, and nearly 40% struggle to find dependable cleaners. That shows the pain is real and common. Frequency per listing still has to be estimated:
- **Cleaner no-shows:** an unverified Turno figure says cleaners ghost 12% of turns; the sibling note assumed 3–5%.
- **Lockouts:** rare, about 1 per few hundred turnovers on one host's anecdote.
- **Maintenance:** skews after hours, per one unsourced claim.

### Cited Findings
- **Hospitable survey (vendor):**
  - It surveyed 554 hosts and property managers in November–December 2025.
  - 34.3% reported lost bookings or negative reviews tied to staffing or contractor issues in 2025.
  - Nearly 40% said dependable local cleaners were hard to find, which hurt turnover times and occupancy.
  - Managers with several outside vendors felt the pressure most.
  - Hospitable is a vendor and the full methodology was not published.
  - — [Short Term Rentalz](https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/new-hospitable-data-reveals-causes-of-booking-losses/); [10 Minutes News for Hoteliers](https://en.10minhotel.com/?p=175349); [Rental Scale-Up](https://www.rentalscaleup.com/why-finding-reliable-cleaners-is-getting-harder-and-why-it-likely-wont-improve-in-2026-short-term-rental-cleaning-staff-shortages-2026/)
- A blog cites "Turno's 2025 industry survey" as saying cleaners ghost 12% of scheduled turns. The original report was not found, so this is **unverified**. — [STR Specialist](https://strspecialist.com/cleaner-agreements-that-prevent-no-shows-price-creep-blame-games)
- **Lockouts:**
  - One host reported one lockout (a dead battery) across about 300 turnovers over two years. This is an anecdote. — [STR Specialist forum](https://strspecialist.com/keys-lockouts-and-i-m-outside-at-1am-a-zero-stress-access-backup-system)
  - Hostfully describes lockouts as rare, mainly caused by dead batteries, network outages and sync problems. — [Hostfully](https://www.hostfully.com/blog/keyless-access-backup-plan/)
  - Guesty advises replacing a lock model if it fails twice. — [Guesty](https://www.guesty.com/blog/emergency-response-stages/)
  - Precise-looking claims (15–20% of first-night complaints come from lockouts; 25% of bookings lost to access issues) have **no traceable source**. — [STR Specialist](https://strspecialist.com/keys-lockouts-and-i-m-outside-at-1am-a-zero-stress-access-backup-system)
- A maintenance-software vendor claims "60% of STR maintenance issues occur after 6 PM". It cites no source. — [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/property-management/property-maintenance-software-vacation-rentals-short-term-needs)
- No Breezeway or other operator dataset of maintenance issues per property per month was found in public sources. — [Capterra: Breezeway](https://www.capterra.com/p/186514/Breezeway/reviews/4913247/)
- After-hours labour typically costs 1.5–2 times standard rates (US, Property Meld). UK data shows after-hours general-maintenance callouts cost up to 51% more. — [Property Meld](https://propertymeld.com/the-true-cost-of-after-hours-property-maintenance/); [Insurance Edge](https://insurance-edge.net/2025/10/20/property-maintenance-needs-call-out-cost-control/)

### Inferences
Estimate for a 15-listing Melbourne co-host, built on the sibling note's roughly 80–90 turnovers a month:

| Need | Rate assumed | Events a month |
|---|---|---|
| Cleaner no-show or late cancel | 3% (sibling assumption) to 12% (unverified Turno figure) | about 3–10 |
| Lockouts | 1 per 300 turnovers (anecdote) | about 0.3 |
| Pre-check-in or guest-in-residence maintenance (hot water, appliances, doors, Wi-Fi, blocked drains) | about 0.2–0.4 per listing [BK] | about 3–6 |
| Linen shortfalls | Often absorbed by the cleaner or linen service [BK] | — |
| **Total urgent events** | | **about 6–16** |

- The founder needs to know what share of those urgent events the co-host's own roster **cannot** cover within the deadline. A 30–50% share [assumption] would mean about **2–8 network-fallback events a month** per 15-listing account.
- On those assumptions, frequency is high enough for a business tool. But the result is very sensitive to the no-show rate, which is unverified.
- The Hospitable 34% figure is an *annual incidence per host*, not a per-listing monthly rate. It shows that failures cost real money for about a third of operators each year. It does not show that two-thirds never suffer.
- Melbourne's steady demand (AirDNA seasonality score 90/100) means incident volume should not collapse in winter, unlike seasonal coastal markets.

### Gaps
- No Australian survey of cleaner no-show rates, lockout rates or maintenance incidents per listing was found.
- The original Turno 2025 survey could not be located.
- No data was found on what share of incidents operators solve with their existing roster versus scrambling for a new vendor. This is the critical unknown, and only discovery interviews or a concierge pilot can answer it.

## Q3. Existing tools: do they already dispatch urgent help, and how fast?

### Takeaway
Since the first research pass the competitive picture has shifted. Tools are moving from "task management for your own team" to **AI agents that dispatch vendors**:
- Trellis (YC) explicitly "dispatches and coordinates cleaners and maintenance".
- TIDY claims to automate vendor coordination.
- Hostaway shipped an AI co-host.
- Hospitable launched a cleaner marketplace of more than 150k vetted cleaners.
- Guesty's marketplace now includes on-demand partners.

However:
- None found **promises a human on the phone within 15 minutes**.
- Most are US-centric, with unproven Australian supply.
- Australian managers (MadeComfy, Hometime) solve this in-house for listings they fully manage, which is a different customer.

The gap is narrower than "nobody does dispatch". It is closer to "nobody guarantees fast, local, vetted fallback humans in Melbourne over a text thread".

### Cited Findings
**Turno**
- The subscription is $0 if you use only marketplace cleaners, with platform fees on those jobs.
- Using your **own** cleaners costs US$10 per property per month on the monthly plan, or US$8 billed annually.
- Paying non-marketplace cleaners through Turno carries a payment fee with a US$1.50 minimum.
- The marketplace fee percentage is not published.
- — [Turno pricing](https://turno.com/pricing/); [Turno help: host fees](https://help.turno.com/en/articles/2142069-what-are-the-fees-for-using-turno-as-a-host)

**Breezeway**
- Priced per property, billed monthly. Capterra lists a starting price of US$6 and a free version.
- PricingSaaS lists "Host Essentials" at US$19.99 a month, with Operations Pro and higher tiers undisclosed.
- No source mentions urgent dispatch.
- — [Capterra AU: Breezeway](https://www.capterra.com.au/software/186514/breezeway); [PricingSaaS: Breezeway](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/breezeway)

**Hospitable**
- Launched a Cleaner Marketplace with more than 150,000 vetted cleaners. — [Short Term Rentalz](https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/new-hospitable-data-reveals-causes-of-booking-losses/)
- Reported $1 million in cleaner payouts "as STR operators consolidate operations" (currency as reported, probably USD). — [Breaking Travel News](https://www.breakingtravelnews.com/news/article/hospitable-hits-1-million-in-cleaner-payouts-as-str-operators-consolidate-o/)
- Australian supply depth was not confirmed.

**Hostaway**
- Launched "AI CoHost", which answers natural-language questions about revenue, occupancy, costs and operations, and drafts listing updates and messages. It is an analytics and messaging assistant, not a dispatcher. — [Short Term Rentalz](https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/hostaway-launches-ai-assistant/)

**Trellis (Y Combinator)**
- Describes "autonomous agents for short-term rentals" that dispatch and coordinate cleaners and maintenance staff, file costs and follow up until issues close.
- Pitches this as fixing tasks that span 5–10 separate systems.
- — [YC launch: Trellis](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Q15-trellis-autonomous-agents-for-short-term-rentals)

**TIDY**
- A June 2026 comparison post, written by TIDY itself, calls TIDY the only tool that automates turnovers, vendor coordination and maintenance. — [TIDY blog](https://www.tidy.com/blog/best-ai-property-managers-for-airbnb-2026)
- It integrates with Hostaway to schedule cleaning and maintenance in sync with turnovers. — [TIDY + Hostaway](https://go.tidy.com/blog/simplify-the-property-management-process-with-tidy-and-hostwaway)

**Guesty Marketplace**
- New partners include Crew (on-demand trash removal, "Can-to-Curb") and Pacho (automated cleaning, inspections and maintenance with real-time task coordination).
- No general handyman or emergency offering was found.
- — [LinkedIn post on Guesty Marketplace](https://ca.linkedin.com/in/cbyman)

**Older precedent**
- Handy launched a vacation-rental cleaning service in a few US cities in 2015. — [TechCrunch (2015)](https://techcrunch.com/2015/05/19/handy-expands-its-vacation-rental-cleaning-service/)

**Airbnb**
- Co-Host Network tools cover hiring human co-hosts: setup, pricing, messaging and cleaning. No Airbnb-native vendor dispatch was found. — [Airbnb co-host pages](https://airbnb.com.au/host/south-coogee-australia/co-hosts)

**Australian managers**
- MadeComfy is full-service only, with cleaning coordination, linen and compliance bundled. — [Enso Connect directory](https://ensoconnect.com/resources/top-vacation-rental-management-companies-in-australia)
- Some Melbourne managers charge separately for cleaning, linen and maintenance (sibling note).

**Melbourne cleaner sourcing on Airtasker today**
- Airbnb cleaning jobs are actively posted in Melbourne suburbs, which shows hosts already use a bid marketplace to find cleaners. — [Airtasker Melbourne Airbnb cleaning jobs](https://airtasker.com/au/jobs/airbnb-cleaning/melbourne)

### Inferences
- **Speed:** no incumbent publishes time-to-human or time-to-fill for urgent short-stay jobs in Australia. The 15-minute callback promise is still unclaimed.
- **The threat is software, not service.** Trellis-type agents and PMS-native AI could add "find me a backup cleaner" within 12–24 months [BK]. They will be supply-limited in Melbourne, though, because US marketplaces (Hospitable, Turno) have thin Australian rosters [unverified].
  - The founder's defensible asset is **local vetted supply plus the guarantee**, not the AI layer.
- **Integration expectation:** professional co-hosts on Hostaway or Guesty will eventually expect the dispatch line to read reservations and access codes. An iMessage-first product can defer this, but not for long with 20+ listing operators [BK].
- Not researched this session: **Properly, Enso Connect's own product, Hospitable's Australian coverage, and local Melbourne cleaner platforms**.

### Gaps
- No Australian availability or supply figures were found for Hospitable's marketplace, Turno (AU) or TIDY.
- Trellis traction and pricing are unknown.
- Properly and Enso Connect features were not checked (search budget).
- Hometime's and MadeComfy's internal urgent-response times are not public.

## Q4. Willingness to pay

### Takeaway
Co-hosts already pay per property for operations software:
- Turno: US$8–10 per property per month, even just to coordinate their **own** cleaners.
- Breezeway: from about US$6 per property, up to US$19.99 for Host Essentials.

They spend far more on cleaning, about A$100–200 per Melbourne one-bedroom turnover with linen. Guest-review and booking-loss risk is real (34% of operators lost bookings or reviews to contractor issues), but the precise "rating threshold" claims are vendor marketing.

Realistic subscription pricing is **about A$10–25 per listing per month**, plus per-incident or provider fees. A typical 10–15 listing co-host is therefore about **A$3–6k a year**. The original A$5–9k sketch fits only larger or heavier-use accounts.

### Cited Findings
**Cleaning spend in Melbourne (Airtasker listings, 2026)**
- North Melbourne one-bedroom turnover including linen change: A$200 (clean due September 2026).
- Hawthorn one-bedroom including washing linen and towels: A$100.
- Doncaster three-bedroom regular Airbnb clean: A$220.
- — [Airtasker Melbourne jobs](https://airtasker.com/au/jobs/airbnb-cleaning/melbourne); [Airtasker Melbourne Eastern Suburbs](https://www.airtasker.com/au/jobs/airbnb-cleaning/melbourne-eastern-suburbs/)
- A Brunswick West page shows a median of A$80 for "consistent Airbnb cleans (3–6 per week)", but its low/median/high figures are out of order. — [Airtasker Brunswick West](https://www.airtasker.com/cleaning/airbnb-cleaning/brunswick-west-vic)

**Australian cleaning fees and turnover costs**
- Hospitable says the average Australian Airbnb cleaning fee is A$50–80, about A$65 for self-cleaning hosts and about A$105 for larger properties using a service. — [Hospitable: AU cleaning fee](https://hospitable.com/airbnb-cleaning-fee-australia)
- Hostfully (US) says a standard turnover costs about US$150–300 in total. — [Hostfully](https://www.hostfully.com/blog/airbnb-cleaning-fee/)

**Software spend per listing**
- Turno: US$10 a month per property for own cleaners, or US$8 annual. — [Turno pricing](https://turno.com/pricing/)
- Breezeway: from US$6 per property, Host Essentials US$19.99 a month. — [Capterra AU](https://www.capterra.com.au/software/186514/breezeway); [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/breezeway)

**Platform fees squeezing margins**
- A third-party blog says that since December 2025 Airbnb charges all Australian hosts a single 15.5% host-only service fee. This was not confirmed on Airbnb's own pages. — [Houst: Airbnb fees Australia](https://www.houst.com/blog/airbnb-hosting-fees-australia)
- An older Beyond Pricing article describes the earlier split-fee model of about 3% charged to hosts. — [Beyond Pricing](https://beyondpricing.com/blog/airbnb-fees-in-australia-and-new-zealand)

**Review risk**
- Airbnb does not publish its ranking formula. — [Rental Scale-Up](https://www.rentalscaleup.com/optimising-your-vacation-rental-for-airbnb-bookings/)
- Superhost requires a 4.8+ overall rating over a rolling 365 days. Guest Favorite is described either as a rating above 4.9 or as the top 10% of listings; sources conflict. — [RedAwning](https://www.redawning.com/pm/post/airbnb-guest-favorite-badge-property-managers); [Hostaway](https://www.hostaway.com/blog/airbnb-star-ratings/)
- Hostaway, citing AirDNA, says listings averaging 4.9+ show higher daily rates and occupancy. This is a correlation, not a penalty rule. — [Hostaway](https://www.hostaway.com/blog/airbnb-star-ratings/)
- The claim that cleanliness ratings below 4.7 earn "up to 30% less" comes from a cleaning-company blog and is unverified. — [Dubai Housekeeping blog](https://dubaihousekeeping.com/the-importance-of-housekeeping-services-for-airbnb-hosts/)

**Business cost of failures**
- 34.3% of surveyed hosts and managers lost bookings or got negative reviews from staffing or contractor issues (Hospitable, 2025). — [Short Term Rentalz](https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/new-hospitable-data-reveals-causes-of-booking-losses/)

**After-hours premium**
- After-hours labour costs 1.5–2 times normal rates (Property Meld, US). — [Property Meld](https://propertymeld.com/the-true-cost-of-after-hours-property-maintenance/)

### Inferences
- **Cleaning dwarfs software.** Assuming about 6–8 turnovers a month at A$100–200 [BK, about 55–65% occupancy with roughly 3-night stays], cleaning costs about **A$600–1,600 a month per listing**, while software costs about A$10–30.
  - A dispatch subscription at A$15–25 per listing is about 1–3% of cleaning spend and about 4–5% of the co-host's fee income.
  - This is affordable *if* it demonstrably prevents lost bookings.
- **Payback logic for the pitch:**
  - One lost booking ≈ A$185 average daily rate × about 3 nights [BK] ≈ **A$550 of gross revenue**. A one-star cleanliness review costs ranking for months.
  - A 15-listing co-host paying A$20 per listing (A$3,600 a year) breaks even by saving about 6–7 bookings a year, roughly one per listing every two years.
  - That is plausible given 34% annual incidence, but unproven.
- **The sketch needs re-pricing:**
  - Per-listing subscription comparables cluster at US$6–20 (about A$9–30 [BK FX]).
  - A typical 10–15 listing account at A$15–20 per listing plus about A$20–40 per network connection (provider-paid) gives roughly **A$3–6k a year**.
  - The A$5–9k range needs 20+ listings or a premium SLA tier.
- **Evidence of willingness to pay specifically for a guaranteed 15-minute callback was not found.** Turno's US$10 charge for routing *own* cleaners is the closest proof that hosts pay per property for coordination even without a marketplace.
- **A natural price split:**
  - A low per-listing fee for "your vendors first" routing.
  - A per-incident "urgent fallback" fee (e.g. A$20–49 [assumption]) that the co-host can pass through to the owner as a maintenance cost. Managers already bill owners separately for maintenance (sibling note).

### Gaps
- No Australian co-host or manager software spend per listing was found.
- No survey of willingness to pay for speed guarantees was found.
- Co-host fee levels in Melbourne were not verified (the sibling note gives 15–30% for managers).
- Average length of stay was not verified.

## Q6. Leakage and defensibility

### Takeaway
Leakage is inherent. Once a co-host finds a good backup cleaner or handyman, they will text them directly next time. The market evidence suggests this is fine *if* the product is priced as coordination software per listing, not per introduction.

Turno charges US$8–10 per property per month **to coordinate hosts' own cleaners**. Breezeway and Tapi similarly sit on top of the operator's existing vendors. The "your vendor first, network fallback" design matches how incumbents monetise.

Defensibility against AI-agent entrants (Trellis, TIDY, PMS-native AI) must come from **local supply density, the response-time guarantee and saved operational data** (access codes, vendor history), not the AI.

### Cited Findings
- Turno's pricing shows hosts pay per property for coordinating their own cleaners (US$10 a month, or US$8 annual), separately from marketplace jobs. — [Turno pricing](https://turno.com/pricing/)
- Tapi sits on agencies' existing tradie panels and contractor compliance records, and charges per property. — [PropertyMe blog](https://www.propertyme.com.au/blog/propertyme/integrator-spotlight-tapi); [Capterra: Tapi](https://www.capterra.ca/software/205692/tapi)
- Strata managers publish after-hours contractor lists for direct calls, so direct routing to known vendors is the norm. — [ESM Strata](https://esmstrata.helpjuice.com/en_US/maintenance-repairs/after-hours-emergencies)
- AI dispatch agents are entering short-stay operations: Trellis (YC) for dispatch and follow-up, and TIDY for vendor coordination (vendor claim). — [YC: Trellis](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Q15-trellis-autonomous-agents-for-short-term-rentals); [TIDY blog](https://www.tidy.com/blog/best-ai-property-managers-for-airbnb-2026)
- Hospitable, a PMS, is bundling a cleaner marketplace of more than 150k cleaners. — [Short Term Rentalz](https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/new-hospitable-data-reveals-causes-of-booking-losses/)

### Inferences
- **Leakage by job type** (inference, building on sibling Q5):
  - Routine cleans: very high leakage, effectively "own vendor".
  - Backup cleans: medium. Backups get saved and reused, but availability varies day to day.
  - Lockouts and locksmiths: low (rare, urgent, varies by suburb).
  - Appliance and plumbing faults: low to medium.
  - Pre-sale trades: medium.
- **How the design answers leakage:**
  1. The saved vendor list lives in the service. Every job, own or network, flows through the same thread, so bypassing saves the operator nothing under a flat per-listing fee.
  2. Network fallback is the reason to keep paying: own vendors fail exactly when urgency is highest.
  3. Provider-pays fees apply only to *new* connections. After a vendor is saved, revenue shifts to subscription. The model should expect provider-fee revenue per account to fall over time and subscription to carry it.
- **Defensibility ranking (strongest first):**
  1. **Local vetted supply density** with measured response times per suburb (hard for US software to copy quickly).
  2. **The guarantee (SLA with credit),** which only works with supply density.
  3. **Operational memory:** access instructions, appliance models, owner approval limits, vendor performance history. This creates switching cost, but a PMS already holds much of it.
  4. **Channel lock-in** through co-host communities and agency partnerships.
  5. The AI and iMessage front end, which is the weakest. PMS vendors and Trellis-type agents will commoditise it.
- **Biggest structural risk:** a PMS (Hostaway, Guesty, Hospitable) adds "urgent dispatch" and partners with a local Australian services marketplace. **Mitigation:** be PMS-agnostic and integrate early as a marketplace partner (Guesty already lists on-demand partners).

### Gaps
- No empirical off-platform leakage rates were found for short-stay service marketplaces.
- No evidence was found on whether co-hosts keep routing known vendors through a third party when it costs them per listing. Pilot data is needed.

## Q8. Risks

### Takeaway
The main risks are:
- **Regulatory drag** in Victoria: the levy, owners-corporation bans and council powers.
- **A shrinking or plateaued host base.**
- **Platform dependence:** Airbnb's fee changes and its own co-host tooling.
- **Fast-moving AI-dispatch competition.**
- **A small Melbourne pool.**

Seasonality is a *low* risk in Melbourne (AirDNA seasonality score 90/100), and concentration in a few large managers is *low* (the market is fragmented).

### Cited Findings
- **Regulation:**

- **Regulatory direction is tightening but incremental.** None of it bans short stays in inner Melbourne. Owners-corporation bans hit CBD and Southbank apartment towers hardest [BK], and those are exactly where commercial listings cluster.

**PM agencies (31)**
- **Gap 4:** Tapi runs maintenance for more than 160k properties, and AI voice agents (Waboom) sell after-hours triage.
- **Willingness to pay 4:** owners pay for repairs.
- **Leakage 3:** panels.
- **Fit 5:** integration with PropertyMe, Console and Tapi is expected.
- **Better as:** a supply partner (an after-hours vendor network behind Tapi-type tools) than a direct buyer.

**Verdict on the best beachhead:**
- **Validate, don't kill, short-stay co-hosts. Narrow the target to independent co-hosts with 5–50 listings in inner Melbourne.**
- **Lead with "urgent fallback with a 15-minute human guarantee"**: backup cleaner, locksmith, hot water, handyman before check-in. Do not lead with "dispatch all your vendors". Present "your vendor first" as the free or low-cost routing layer that keeps the operator in the thread.
- **Re-price** to about A$15–20 per listing per month plus a per-incident urgent fee or provider-paid connection fee. Expect about **A$3–6k a year per typical account**, and A$5–9k only for 20+ listing operators.
- **Kill criteria for a 4–6 week concierge pilot** (run manually, with the founder as the dispatcher, with 10–20 co-hosts). Kill or pivot if:
  - (a) fewer than about 1 network-fallback event occurs per 10 listings per month;
  - (b) the founder cannot deliver a human callback within 15 minutes for at least about 80% of requests from local supply;
  - (c) fewer than about a third of pilot co-hosts convert to paid at about A$15 or more per listing.
- **Second segment:** real estate sales agents' pre-sale fix-ups, using the same handyman, painter and cleaner supply. Agencies that refer short-stay owners are a shared channel.
- **PM agencies:** approach as a supply partnership with Tapi or Waboom-type triage tools, not as direct buyers.
- **Geography:** Melbourne alone supports roughly A$0.3–1.5m ARR at realistic share. Plan the Sydney and Gold Coast expansion from the start if the pilot passes.
