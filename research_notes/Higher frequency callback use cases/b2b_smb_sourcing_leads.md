# B2B flip: "get someone to call me about X" for Australian SMB owners — how often businesses need new suppliers, what leads are worth, who already does it, how crowded AI sourcing is, and the regulation (as of October 2026)

How this was done: 28 web searches and 1 WebFetch attempt (failed with a DNS error, so everything relies on search results and snippets). Data older than 2025 is labelled with its year. Some findings are marked "reused from sibling note". Those were verified by another researcher in `quotes_and_experts.md` or `business_power_users.md` in this folder, not re-checked here. Anything not backed by a source I retrieved is labelled "Unverified background" and kept in Inferences or Gaps.

## 1. Frequency: how often Australian SMBs look for new service providers or suppliers, time spent sourcing, and the most frequent categories

### Takeaway
No Australian survey measures how often SMBs look for a *new* service provider, by size band or in total. That evidence gap matters, because the founder's whole hypothesis rests on frequency. Three things point the same way. Most SMB time goes on admin and repeat ordering with suppliers they already use. Switching in big contract categories is low: in 2017 only 19% of Australian small businesses switched insurer each year. And most of the 2.81 million businesses are non-employing sole traders with very few supplier needs. Searches for a new provider are episodic, apart from repairs and maintenance, one-off cleaning and skip-bin jobs, and ad-hoc freight. That makes a typical SMB a moderate-frequency user, not obviously a high-frequency one.

### Cited Findings
**Market size by band (ABS, latest)**
- 2,814,778 actively trading businesses at 30 June 2026, of which 996,203 employ staff. Non-employing businesses grew 4.8% (+83,105) in 2025–26. Employing businesses rose 0.2% (+2,025), the first net rise since 2021–22. Band counts include 689,600 businesses with 1–4 employees and 232,912 with 5–19 — [ABS Counts of Australian Businesses, Jul 2022–Jun 2026](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/business-indicators/counts-australian-businesses-including-entries-and-exits/jul2022-jun2026)
- ASBFEO (June 2025): 1,735,470 non-employing businesses, about 63.6% of businesses measured by employment size. 232,129 had 5–19 employees, out of 2,729,648 in-scope businesses in 2024–25. Businesses not registered for GST are excluded — [ASBFEO, Number of small businesses in Australia (Aug 2025)](https://asbfeo.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/Number%20of%20small%20businesses%20in%20Australia_Aug%202025.pdf)
- In 2024–25, businesses with 20–199 employees fell to 67,857 and those with 200+ rose to 5,322 (ABS figures, via search summary). The ABS revised its 2024 employing count from 999,161 to 994,178 in September 2025 — [ABS release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/business-indicators/counts-australian-businesses-including-entries-and-exits/jul2022-jun2026); [ASBFEO](https://www.asbfeo.gov.au/contribution-australian-business-numbers)

**Time spent on sourcing, quoting and ordering**
- OpenAI-commissioned research (reported September 2026) surveyed more than 1,000 Australian SMB owners. 62% said quoting, invoicing or scheduling takes up more of their week than the work itself. 45% of construction respondents had lost potential work because they were slow to respond. CommBank and OpenAI are positioning AI against this "tradie admin problem". The survey describes the *provider* side, which is useful for supply-side pitching — [Dynamic Business](https://dynamicbusiness.com/news/commbank-and-openai-are-betting-ai-can-fix-the-tradie-admin-problem)
- Beacon research covering hospitality, leisure and healthcare businesses (UK-focused, undated) found 21% of owners say searching for and finding the right supplier is "a real issue" in their business. The publication was identified from a search summary; the likely page is Hospitality Interiors — [Hospitality Interiors](https://hospitality-interiors.net/?p=94436)
- US (not Australian): a Walmart Business survey found 37% of business decision-makers spend 4–7 hours a week on routine ordering alone. That is repeat purchasing, not new-supplier sourcing — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/37-of-business-leaders-are-losing-nearly-a-full-workday-to-this-hidden-operational-tax)

**Switching and shopping rates by category**
- Insurance (2017, dated): only 19% of Australian small business owners switch insurer each year, although nearly three-quarters are dissatisfied with or not loyal to their insurer — [McKinsey, "SME insurance in Australia — a market ripe for change"](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/sme-insurance-in-australia-a-market-ripe-for-change)
- Insurance brokers (2025): the Vero SME Insurance Index (1,750 SMEs) found satisfaction with brokers fell from 87% to 69% in a year, and SMEs are re-evaluating broker relationships — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/sme/australian-smes-are-reevaluating-insurance-broker-relationships--vero-528165.aspx)
- Public liability cost shock (March 2026): the Insurance Council of Australia told a parliamentary inquiry into small business public liability that average premiums have risen 55–60% since 2019. Rising premiums are a trigger to shop around — [ICA submission, 6 March 2026](https://insurancecouncil.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/20260306-Public-Liability-submission-ICA.pdf)
- Accountants (2015, dated): in an MYOB survey of 400 small businesses, 48% said they turn to their accountant first in financial difficulty. That points to a sticky, trusted relationship that rarely gets re-sourced. No switching rate was found — [Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/business/8498-smes-turn-to-accountants-first-says-myob)
- Business finance demand: brokers facilitate at least four in ten Australian small business loans (AFCA submission) — [AFCA](https://afca.org.au/media/1721/download). In an OnDeck survey (undated, older), six in ten small businesses said they would consider a broker for future finance — [Broker News](https://brokernews.com.au/news/breaking-news/brokers-service-in-demand-across-australias-small-business-community--survey-281180.aspx). Trade press in 2026 reports business loan demand "surging" as asset finance pulls back (headline only) — [MPA](https://www.mpamag.com/au/specialty/commercial/australian-business-loan-demand-surges-as-asset-finance-pullback-deepens/590807)
- UK energy (2024, for comparison): 34% of small businesses switched energy supplier in the past year (IFF Research for Ofgem), up from 26% across all businesses in 2023 — [Current News](https://current-news.co.uk/ofgem-research-shows-business-supplier-switches-on-the-rise); [Solar Power Portal](https://www.solarpowerportal.co.uk/solar-technology/ofgem-research-shows-business-energy-supplier-switches-on-the-rise)
- UK other services (2018, dated): only 6% of small businesses had switched water supplier in the first year of the open market, and only 39% were switching providers of other services such as energy, stationery, IT, healthcare and pensions (SES Business Water / YouGov) — [Energy Live News](https://www.energylivenews.com/2018/04/25/bulk-of-small-businesses-not-switching-to-cheaper-water-suppliers/)
- US goods suppliers (2023): SMB supply chain managers said they had switched an average of four suppliers to North American ones over two years (Capterra). That is goods sourcing, not services — [Business Wire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20230612005106/en)

### Inferences
- **The hypothesis that businesses make more calls is only partly supported.** SMBs spend a lot of time on supplier admin: quoting, ordering, chasing. But most of it is with existing suppliers, which creates no new connection anyone would pay a lead fee for. Searches for a new provider are lumpier.
- **Rough frequency by category.** These are my estimates from the switching data plus unverified background knowledge, not measured figures:
  - **High (monthly to several times a year for venues and operators):** repairs and maintenance (plumbing, electrical, refrigeration, commercial kitchen equipment, locksmiths, pest control), one-off or deep cleans, skip bins, ad-hoc couriers or pallet freight, temporary labour.
  - **Medium (yearly):** insurance renewal (about 1 in 5 switch each year, more under premium shock), energy contract renewal, marketing or agency changes, IT break-fix.
  - **Low (every 2–7 years):** accountant, MSP or IT contract, commercial lease or agent, fit-out, signage, security monitoring contract, business loans (except heavy equipment users).
- **The addressable core is the roughly 1 million employing businesses**, especially the roughly 300k with 5–199 staff. The 1.8 million non-employing sole traders mostly need insurance, accounting and occasional finance. All of those are low-frequency or regulated.
- **For a typical employing SMB, my guess is 3–10 new-provider searches a year across all categories.** It is far higher for multi-site hospitality or short-stay operators, who need repairs and cleaning, and far lower for professional-services micro-firms. This is speculative and needs validating with founder interviews.
- **OpenAI's 45% "lost work through slow response" finding is a strong supply-side pitch:** a pre-qualified business that has asked to be called *now* is exactly what slow-quoting providers miss.

### Gaps
- No Australian survey was found that measures how often SMBs look for new service providers, by size band or in total, or the hours spent getting quotes. Searches on ASBFEO, Xero, MYOB and Sensis/Yellow returned nothing usable. Note: the Sensis Business Index has not been published for several years (unverified background).
- No Australian switching rates for accountants, MSPs, cleaners, waste or energy (SME) were found.
- The OpenAI/CommBank full report, its methodology and its category breakdown were not retrieved.

## 2. Lead values: what providers pay per commercial lead or per client won in Australia

### Takeaway
B2B lead values roughly follow deal size and regulation:
- **Business finance** pays 0.5–2% of the loan from mainstream and specialist lenders. Fintech unsecured lenders paid 1–4% (Prospa, around 2016). That is A$500–4,000 on a A$100k loan.
- **SME insurance brokers** earn 0–30% of base premium, recurring each year. BizCover pays referral partners 8% of base premium in year one and 5% on renewals, which is a ready-made price for a referral.
- **MSPs** cite customer acquisition costs of about US$1,000–2,500 (dated US data).
- **Accountants:** vendor benchmarks put qualified leads at roughly A$150–600.
- **Commercial cleaning** leads trade at A$25–100 shared, or around A$500 a month for an exclusive territory.
- **Bark** charges roughly A$/£5–40 per lead.

Commercial cleaning, signage, waste and freight are cheap per lead. The rich categories are finance, insurance and IT.

### Cited Findings
**Business loans and equipment finance (broker commissions)**
- Commercial commission schedules from 2019–2023, which may be out of date:
  - Bank of Queensland: 0.55% on commercial/business loans under A$3m (A$1m minimum).
  - Bank of Sydney: 0.50% upfront on business loans of A$100k or more.
  - CBA: 0.50% of the approved facility limit for A$150k–3m.
  - Australian First Mortgage: 0.55% upfront.
  - One aggregator policy lists commercial commission of 0.55% up to 2.00% at some specialist lenders.
  - Connective ran a limited 2021 business-lending promotion at 0.75% upfront.
  - Clawback, for example at BOQ: 100% if the loan ends within 0–12 months, 50% at 12–18 months.
  - Some commercial lenders instead charge the borrower a deferred establishment fee of 1.50%, which avoids broker clawback.
  - Sources (attribution via search summary): [Connective commission schedule](https://resources.connective.com.au/media/docs/Commission_Schedule.pdf); [Connective wiki, Adelaide Bank commercial](https://wiki.connective.com.au/en/articles/2347-commissions-guide-adelaide-bank-commercial); [MLB commercial remuneration schedule (2023)](https://files.helpdocs.io/okybgmroht/other/1708904545237/mlb-remuneration-schedule-commercial-02-10-2023-2.pdf); [Connective broker resources (2021)](https://resources.connective.com.au/broker-resources/?p=45024); [Your Finance Guide](https://www.yourfinanceguide.com.au/mortgage-broker/how-brokers-are-paid)
- Prospa (unsecured SME lender): a broker article it hosted around 2016 says it pays brokers **1–4%**, depending on loan features and the relationship. The current schedule is only in its partner portal. A 2024 promotion added +1% for a limited period — [Prospa broker article c.2016 (PDF)](https://www.prospa.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/mpa-16.9-prospa.pdf); [Prospa partner page](https://www.prospa.com/?p=112564); [Prospa promotion page](https://www.prospa.com/?p=112612)

- UK comparison: the Insurance Times Schemes Index found public liability pays brokers 22.3% commission at renewal, against 16.9% on a customer's first policy — [Insurance Times](https://www.insurancetimes.co.uk/analysis/want-to-make-more-money-out-of-schemes-index-reveals-most-profitable-lines/1431791.article)

### Inferences
- **Illustrative value per connected lead.** Each figure is the provider's revenue from a closed deal multiplied by a plausible close rate. None is measured.
  - **Business loan:** A$100k unsecured at 1–4%, or A$250k equipment finance at about 1%, gives A$1,000–4,000 of commission. A broker could rationally pay **A$100–400** for a qualified callback.
  - **SME insurance:** a business pack or public liability premium of A$1,500–3,000 (not verified here) at 15–25% gives A$225–750 a year, recurring. BizCover's 8%/5% referral rate implies about A$120–240 in year one plus renewals per referred policy. So **A$50–200** per qualified callback, or a revenue share.
  - **MSP:** a US$1,000–2,500 acquisition cost suggests **A$150–500** per qualified meeting.
  - **Accountant or bookkeeper:** **A$100–300** per qualified lead.
  - **Commercial cleaning:** **A$30–120** per lead. Contracts are A$3–7k a year, but the work is low-margin and competitive.
  - **Skip bins, signage, couriers:** **A$10–50** per lead (unverified).
  - **Fit-out:** potentially **A$200–1,000+** because projects are large, but they are rare.
- **The best-paying categories are also the most regulated (insurance and consumer credit) or the least frequent (fit-out, commercial lease, MSP contracts).** Business-purpose finance is the exception: high value and lighter licensing (see section 5).
- **Recurring commissions (insurance renewals, finance trail, MSP and cleaning contracts) favour revenue share over a flat per-lead fee.** The BizCover partner programme shows insurers and brokers already accept that structure.

### Gaps
- No published Australian per-lead prices were found for B2B leads on Bark AU, ServiceSeeking, hipages or Airtasker. No commercial solar, waste, signage, freight, security or fit-out lead prices were found.
- No current (2025–26) Australian commission percentages for SME insurance lines (public liability, business packs) were found beyond BizCover's 2018–2019 FSG range. Typical Australian public liability or business-pack premium levels were not retrieved.
- Current Prospa, Moula or other fintech broker commission schedules are partner-only.
- No Australian MSP acquisition-cost or per-lead benchmark was found; the figures above are US and dated.
- Unverified background: commercial leasing agents typically earn a percentage of first-year rent. Lawyers generally may not pay referral fees to non-lawyers under professional conduct rules, which limits lead-fee models in legal.

## 3. Existing B2B lead marketplaces and directories in Australia, and SMB owner complaints

### Takeaway
The Australian "get quotes" market for SMB services is fragmented, and most of it is built on residential and trade demand. Players include Bark (multi-category, credit-based, shared leads), hipages (trades subscription), Airtasker (bids), ServiceSeeking, and niche pay-per-lead vendors such as WhatsTheDamage. In insurance there are online brokers (BizCover) fed by comparison sites (Compare the Market). In finance, a broker channel already writes at least 40% of small business loans and about 67–70% of equipment finance. The loudest complaints come from *providers*: shared leads, fake or stale leads, and expiring credits. Buyers complain about being called repeatedly once their details are sold on. No player found offers "text one line, one vetted business calls you" for B2B services.

### Cited Findings
**Bark**
- ProductReview.com.au rates Bark 3.2 from 676 reviews, with a 1.5 rating for rates and fees. Its summary says professionals often report financial losses from "predatory" credit policies and poor lead quality. Complaints about expired credits and automatic charges rose after a late-2025 shift to a "Marketplace" subscription model — [ProductReview.com.au](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/barkcom)
- On G2, users most often cite poor leads, and some allege fake or "manufactured" leads, which Bark denies. One reviewer says job requests stay live indefinitely unless the client removes them, producing stale leads — [G2](https://g2.com/products/bark-com/reviews?page=10); [G2 filtered](https://g2.com/products/bark-com/reviews_and_filters?page=5)
- Bark Australia's Trustpilot page shows about 109,102 reviews. A professional reviewer alleges fake leads are posted so pros spend credits that are never returned — [Trustpilot AU](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/bark.com?page=5)
- Each Bark lead is sold to up to about five professionals, and credits expire after three months (from November 2025) — [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/trades/research/uk-trade-directory-costs/)
- Buyers tend to like Bark's speed and the number of quotes. Providers are mostly negative, according to a cross-site search synthesis — [ProductReview.com.au](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/barkcom)

**Trades and odd-jobs platforms (reused from sibling notes)**
- hipages shares each lead with up to three businesses and averaged about A$2,475 a year per subscriber in FY26 — [What's The Damage](https://whatsthedamage.com.au/tradies/hipages-cost/); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/hipages-fy26-slides-cash-flow-surges-66-as-ai-platform-evolves-93CH-4872530)
- Oneflare (owned by Airtasker) closed on 30 June 2026 — [Oneflare help](https://help.oneflare.com.au/hc/en-au/articles/58074479988377-Oneflare-is-closing-here-s-what-you-need-to-know)
- Airtasker charges posters a connection fee of A$4.90–49.95 per task, and Taskers pay 12.5–20%. Its US Business Membership costs $849 a year with no connection fees and up to 5 team accounts; availability in Australia is unconfirmed — [Trustpilot (Airtasker reply)](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/www.airtasker.com?page=2); [Airtasker US](https://www.airtasker.com/us/?p=29999)

**Pay-per-lead and exclusive-territory vendors**
- WhatsTheDamage positions itself against hipages and other aggregators, offering pay-per-lead from A$25 and exclusive commercial-cleaning territories at A$500 a month — [WhatsTheDamage](https://whatsthedamage.com.au/tradies/commercial-cleaning/)

**Insurance and comparison**
- BizCover is an online broker, part of the Steadfast network, paid by insurer commission. Compare the Market sends it business insurance customers for a commission — [BizCover FSG](https://www.bizcover.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/BC1360_BizCoverFSG_V5_20.11.19.pdf); [Compare the Market disclosures](https://comparethemarket.com.au/business-insurance/disclosures/)
- Consumer-side comparison sites draw complaints about being called within minutes and repeatedly after entering details. The ACCC found personal data from comparison and quote tools is routinely sold through data brokers to drive unsolicited sales (reused from sibling note) — [ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/misleading-and-high-pressure-unsolicited-sales-practices-widespread); [Trustpilot, Compare the Market](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/www.comparethemarket.com.au)

**Finance broker channel**
- Brokers facilitate at least 4 in 10 small business loans — [AFCA](https://afca.org.au/media/1721/download). They originate about 67–70% of equipment finance — [MPA](https://mpamag.com/au/news/general/commercial-lending-guide-asset-and-equipment-finance/295877); [East & Partners](https://eastandpartners.com/?p=3967)

**UK precedent**
- Expert Market is a UK B2B marketplace that collects quotes from suppliers of office equipment and business services, free for buyers. Its supplier pricing was not found, and the source is a low-quality profile — [Expert Market profile](https://mindmaps.femtech.health/firms/70816)

### Inferences
- **Provider complaints are an opening for supply-side sales.** Shared leads, fake or stale leads and expiring credits are all things a "verified business, asked for a call, one or two providers only" model fixes. Pitch to providers: "pay only when a real business owner picks up and talks to you."
- **The buyer-side complaint (being called repeatedly once details are sold) applies equally in B2B.** Business owners give out their mobile on quote forms. A masked-number, owner-initiated design is a real advantage, especially in finance and insurance, where data resale is under ASIC and ACCC scrutiny.
- **Incumbents are organised by category, not as a single entry point.** Nobody owns "the business owner's one number for any supplier". But that is because B2B categories need different qualification (insurance needs a risk profile, cleaning needs a site walk-through, finance needs financials). A broad line inherits all of that complexity at once.

## 5. Regulation: financial-services lead generation (ASIC, Treasury 2026), insurance broker referrals, and credit licensing for business-loan referrals

### Takeaway
For a B2B line, regulation splits three ways:
- **Ordinary services** (cleaning, IT, signage, waste, freight, repairs): light-touch. Australian Consumer Law, Spam Act and Privacy Act only.
- **SME insurance:** heavy. Arranging or comparing quotes is a financial service needing an AFSL or authorised-representative status. Broker commissions are also under ASBFEO and code pressure.
- **Business-purpose finance:** a middle path. Credit "predominantly for business purposes" generally falls outside the NCCP consumer regime, so referring business owners to commercial finance brokers carries much less licensing burden than consumer mortgage or car loans. Misleading-conduct rules still apply, and ASIC is actively suing lead-generation sites for loans and insurance (September 2026).

Treasury's April 2026 lead-generation consultation is aimed at superannuation and retail financial advice. I found no sign that it targets business-purpose credit or commercial insurance, though licensee due diligence on lead generators will flow down to partner brokers.

### Cited Findings
**Business-purpose credit and the NCCP**
- In June 2026 the MFAA reminded brokers that credit "provided predominantly for business or investment purposes may fall outside the NCCP framework", so the loan's purpose must be identified — [MFAA](https://www.mfaa.com.au/news/personal-loans-and-asset-finance-a-reminder-on-nccp-obligations)
- The FBAA argues the NCCP Act "has no place in commercial property loans, leases or other types of commercial and SME lending", while noting lenders must still lend responsibly — [The Adviser](https://theadviser.com.au/broker/35136-commercial-lending-too-dynamic-and-complex-for-nccp)
- Responsible lending duties apply to credit for personal, domestic or household purposes, or residential investment property — [AFSL House](https://afslhouse.com.au/insights/nccp-act-responsible-lending-lenders-mortgage-brokers/)
- CAFBA's position is that government should stay out of commercial finance and that the industry should self-regulate — [MPA](https://www.mpamag.com/au/specialty/commercial/cafbas-david-bushby-government-should-stay-out-of-commercial-finance/535416)
- For *consumer* credit, the reg 25(5) referrer exemption requires consent, no fee charged to the consumer, a referral agreement, and commission disclosure. It does not cover a business whose main activity is passing on leads (reused from sibling note) — [Brightlaw](https://www.brightlaw.com.au/new-credit-licensing-exemptions-for-referrers-and-temporary-employees/); [MFAA referrer guide](https://portal.mfaa.com.au/broker-toolbox/legal-and-compliance/nccp-bid/Referrer%20exemption%20File)

**Insurance referral and quoting**
- ASIC's longstanding position (2012): websites that let people get or compare insurance quotes are generally providing financial services, so they need an AFSL or authorised-representative status, and should disclose commissions and referral fees (reused from sibling note) — [ASIC 12-304MR](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2012-releases/12-304mr-asic-warns-comparison-websites)
- BizCover runs a referral-partner programme paying 8% of base premium in year one and 5% on renewal. Compare the Market's business insurance flow refers customers to BizCover. That shows a working, compliant model of referral into an AFSL holder — [BizCover referral guide](https://www.bizcover.com.au/wp-content/uploads/BCAU-Referral-Partner.pdf); [Compare the Market](https://comparethemarket.com.au/business-insurance/disclosures/)
- Broker remuneration is under pressure. ASBFEO wants conflicted-remuneration rules extended to general insurance brokers, and in April 2026 pushed NIBA to make its revised Code of Practice contractually enforceable — [NIBA](https://www.niba.com.au/news/niba-defends-broker-commissions-submission-asbfeo); [ASBFEO letter, April 2026](https://asbfeo.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-04/260409%20SB%20-%20NIBA%20Insurance%20Brokers%20Code%20of%20Practice%20revision.pdf)

**Treasury 2026 lead-generation consultation**
- On 8 April 2026 the government released three consultation papers on consumer protection in superannuation, with submissions closing 22 May 2026 — [Treasury ministers' media release](https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/daniel-mulino-2025/media-releases/strengthening-consumer-protection-superannuation-system)
- The lead-generation paper (released 7 April 2026, per HSF Kramer) covers four things. Making lead generators more accountable. Tightening unsolicited selling, including an option to ban unlicensed communications with consumers about super. Conflicted payment structures. Earlier action against harmful advertising. HSF Kramer warns some options could catch legitimate marketing — [HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/fr/notes/fsraustralia/2026-posts/hsf-kramer-submission-treasury-consultation-on-curbing-lead-generation-activity)
- AustralianSuper's submission backs a legislated ban on the lead-generation business model and wider coverage of anyone who originates or profits from consumer leads — [AustralianSuper submission (June 2026)](https://www.australiansuper.com/-/media/australian-super/files/about-us/policy-submissions/2026/treasury-submission-super-consumer-protections-and-cslr.pdf)
- Law-firm summaries describe the post-Shield and First Guardian package. It would ban unlicensed real-time communication about super, strengthen anti-hawking rules, and require licensees to do due diligence on lead-generation arrangements — [King & Wood Mallesons](https://www.mallesons.com/au/en/insights/latest-thinking/treasurys-post-shield-and-first-guardian-reforms-key-implications-for-superannuation-trustees.html); [Regulation Tomorrow, Aug 2026](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/08/treasury-protecting-consumers-and-the-promise-of-superannuation-in-an-evolving-financial-ecosystem/)

**Enforcement climate (reused from sibling note)**
- ASIC's list of known lead-generation entities reached 63 by June 2026 — [ASIC 26-125MR](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-125mr-asic-expands-list-of-known-entities-involved-in-lead-generation/)
- ASIC v Clark Family (September 2026) alleges up to 70 "free assessment" loan and insurance websites that auctioned enquiries to brokers. These are allegations only — [ASIC 26-221MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-221mr-asic-sues-lead-generation-business-over-alleged-misleading-comparison-claims/)
- The ACCC's July 2026 unsolicited-selling report recommends bringing digital lead generation explicitly within the unsolicited-selling rules, with an opt-in model — [ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/misleading-and-high-pressure-unsolicited-sales-practices-widespread)
- A general unfair trading practices prohibition starts on 1 July 2027 — [Allens](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2026/07/australias-new-unfair-trading-practices-regime-what-businesses-need-to-know/)

### Inferences
- **Business finance is the most attractive "money" category for a B2B flip.** Lead value is high (A$1–4k commission per deal), and referring a *business-purpose* loan to a commercial broker does not appear to need an Australian Credit Licence or credit-representative appointment the way consumer credit does. Remaining risks:
  - ASIC Act misleading-conduct rules (do not call yourself a "comparison" if you are not one; ASIC v Clark Family is the cautionary case).
  - Mixed-purpose loans, such as a sole trader's vehicle, can tip into NCCP.
  - Possible future regulation of commercial broking (CAFBA is resisting it).
  - Get legal advice before launch.
- **Insurance must go through a licensed partner.** The cleanest route is a BizCover-style referral agreement (pass the lead, no advice, disclose the fee) or authorised-representative appointment under a broker's AFSL. The agent must not recommend products or discuss cover suitability. "Get a broker to call me about public liability" is fine as a referral. "Which public liability cover do I need?" is advice.
- **Treasury's direction (licensees accountable for their lead generators) will make partner brokers and insurers demand consent logs, scripts and call recordings.** The founder's design (owner-initiated text, explicit opt-in, masked callback, no resale) is the opposite of the conduct regulators are targeting, and is a selling point to compliance-conscious partners.
- **Ordinary B2B services (cleaning, IT, waste, signage, repairs, freight) carry minimal licensing burden on the platform side.** Providers may need their own trade or security licences; the platform should check them. That is where a solo founder can move fastest.

### Gaps
- The text of the Treasury lead-generation paper was not retrieved, so it is unconfirmed whether its options reach insurance or credit lead generation beyond super and advice. The government's final response (if any by October 2026) was not found.
- No ASIC guidance was found on whether an unlicensed referrer that passes SME contact details to a general insurance broker for a fee, with no advice, falls outside "dealing" or "arranging". An exemption or relief instrument may apply. Unverified.
- Unverified background:
  - Under Corporations Act s761G(5), general insurance counts as "retail" only for listed product types (motor vehicle, home building, contents, sickness and accident, consumer credit, travel, personal and domestic property). Most commercial lines such as public liability and business packs are therefore treated as wholesale even when sold to small businesses. That lightens disclosure (no PDS or FSG obligations to wholesale clients) but not AFSL licensing.
  - The Australian Consumer Law definition of "consumer" includes business purchases up to A$100,000. ACL protections, including unsolicited consumer agreement rules, can therefore apply to SMB service purchases.
  - The Spam Act applies to B2B commercial messages.
  - Legal-profession conduct rules restrict lawyers paying referral fees.
  - State real-estate laws require agents to disclose referral rebates.
  - All of these should be confirmed with a lawyer.

## 6. Assessment: a broad "get someone to call me about anything for your business" line, or a vertical beachhead?

### Takeaway
A broad B2B line works as a **brand and front door later, not as the starting point**. The evidence points to a **vertical beachhead in hospitality venues and short-stay operations**: repairs and maintenance, commercial cleaning, skip bins, pest control, equipment repair. Those categories score best on frequency, solution gap and regulatory ease, and share supply with the consumer product. Next come **office operations** (commercial cleaning, IT support, signage). The **money categories** come last and only through licensed partners: business finance first, because it is high value with lighter licensing, then SME insurance as a referral or authorised representative. The richest leads (finance, insurance, MSP, fit-out) are the least frequent or the most regulated. The frequent ones (repairs, cleaning, waste) are cheap per lead, so frequency has to be monetised by subscription, not per lead.

### Inferences

**Why not lead with "anything for your business":**
1. **Qualification differs by category.** Insurance needs occupation, turnover and claims history. Finance needs time trading, revenue and purpose. Cleaning needs square metres and frequency. MSP needs seat count and current stack. A general agent can collect these, but the *supply side* (signed-up providers who agree to take calls and pay) has to be recruited category by category and city by city. Breadth means thin supply everywhere and slow first callbacks, which breaks the "a human calls you fast" promise.
2. **Value and frequency pull in opposite directions.** No single category is both frequent and high-value. Breadth averages them, but the operating cost of each category stays fixed.
3. **The two richest categories carry regulatory overhead** (an AFSL partner for insurance, care with ASIC misleading-conduct rules for finance). That should be built deliberately once, not stumbled into because an owner texted "get a broker to call me".
4. **Providers buy leads by category.** A cleaner wants cleaning leads in their suburbs; nobody buys "SMB leads".

**Why a vertical beachhead (hospitality venues and short-stay operators) works:**
- **Highest real frequency.** Venues and multi-listing hosts break things weekly: refrigeration, dishwashers, plumbing, electrical, locks, pests, glass. Many needs are urgent, which is where a callback within 15 minutes is clearly better than Bark's or Airtasker's async bids.
- **Same supply as the consumer product** (trades, cleaners), so liquidity carries over.
- **Clear subscription case.** A venue or co-host paying A$49–149 a month for unlimited "get someone here" requests captures the frequency that per-lead pricing on A$30 cleaning leads cannot. This is a pricing suggestion, not market-tested.
- **Platform-side regulation is close to zero.**

**Sequenced expansion:**
1. **Hospitality and STR operations** (repairs, cleaning, waste, pest).
2. **Office and premises operations** for the same customers and similar SMBs (commercial cleaning contracts, IT support/MSP, signage, skip bins, security).
3. **Money categories through licensed partners:** first business finance (equipment, unsecured, working capital) with one or two commercial brokers on revenue share, then SME insurance as a referral partner or authorised representative of one broker.
4. **Only then a broad "anything for your business" line**, using logged unmet requests to choose the next vertical.

Early on, the broad line can quietly accept any request as a *demand-discovery* tool: log it, fulfil what is supported, and politely decline or hand off the rest.

**Overall verdict.** Start vertical: hospitality venues and short-stay operators, on a subscription plus provider-pays model. Expand into office and premises operations next. Add business finance, then insurance, only through licensed partners and with consent and recording infrastructure in place. Treat "get someone to call me about anything for your business" as the long-term positioning and a demand-discovery inbox, not the launch product. Two reasons. Breadth multiplies supply-side recruiting and category-specific qualification without adding frequency per customer. And the categories that would make breadth pay (finance, insurance) are exactly where ASIC and Treasury scrutiny is sharpest in 2026.
