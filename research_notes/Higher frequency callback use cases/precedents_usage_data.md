## 1. Request logs and usage patterns from text-a-number concierges and AI assistants (Magic, GoButler, Operator, Facebook M, Fancy Hands, Path Talk, Assist, Hello Alfred, Poke, Pine AI, Duckbill)

### Takeaway
No public request logs or repeat-rate figures exist for any of these products. The qualitative pattern is consistent, though. "Text for anything" concierges got a viral burst of novelty requests (food delivery, gifts, flowers, reservations), lost money per order, and then narrowed: to one high-value vertical (GoButler went to flights), to a paid business-assistant model (Magic), or to a sponsor-paid B2B2C model (Hello Alfred through landlords). The 2025–2026 AI-native winners (Poke, Pine, Duckbill) cluster around recurring "life admin": email, calendar, bills, subscriptions, disputes, appointments. Those needs repeat often and don't need a relationship with one particular human.

### Cited Findings

- It hires category-specialist humans (e.g., a "Dining & Entertainment Personal Assistant" role) — [General Catalyst jobs board](https://jobs.generalcatalyst.com/companies/duckbill-2/jobs/53885315-dining-entertainment-personal-assistant)

### Inferences
- **The broad-concierge lifecycle repeats:** viral launch, then novelty and luxury requests (food, flowers, gifts, odd errands), then negative unit economics (Magic loses money on 25% of orders), then narrowing. GoButler narrowed to flights, the most common high-value category. Magic narrowed to paid business assistants. Hello Alfred narrowed to a landlord-sponsored amenity. Facebook M was shut down after reaching ~2,000 users. None of the 2015-era general consumer concierges survived as a general consumer product.
- **The categories that survived or narrowed** were high-value and recurring: travel/flights (GoButler), executive admin (Magic B2B), household management within a building (Alfred), and calls, research and appointments (Fancy Hands). The 2025–2026 AI cohort converges on **recurring admin with institutions**: bills, subscriptions, disputes, appointments, email and calendar (Pine, Duckbill, Poke).
- **Admin requests repeat; "find me a person" requests mostly don't.** Most of these recurring tasks deal with *businesses or institutions* (call Telstra, dispute a bill, reschedule an appointment) rather than finding a *new human provider*. That makes them repeat naturally and makes them immune to the "found my regular, bypass the service" leakage the founder worries about.
- **Engagement estimate for Poke:** if 100M messages over ~90 days is accurate and the base is 200k–900k users, that is roughly 1–6 messages per user per day. This is rough arithmetic on secondary figures, and it likely includes agent-initiated proactive messages. It still suggests that proactive AI-in-iMessage can reach daily-habit engagement in a way the 2015 human concierges never reported.

### Background, unverified (prior knowledge, not confirmed in this session)
- **Magic:** reportedly raised ~$12M led by Sequoia in 2015 and went viral on Hacker News. There were reports that food delivery dominated early volume.
- **Facebook M:** contemporaneous reporting (The Information / Wired, ~2017) suggested the AI automated well under half of requests, with humans doing most of the work. This is a key reason the approach was considered unscalable.
- **Path Talk (2015):** let users text any local business through Path's acquisition of TalkTo, and was discontinued within roughly a year.
- **Operator:** wound down or pivoted around 2016–2018.

### Gaps
- No public request-category breakdowns or repeat/retention rates were found for Magic, GoButler, Operator, Facebook M, Fancy Hands, Poke, Pine or Duckbill. Fancy Hands' "most common requests" page exists but could not be fetched.
- No search was spent on Path Talk or Assist (budget). Operator's shutdown and lessons were not found in search results.
- Poke's retention, its per-user message counts, and the Cognition acquisition are unverified (secondary or single sources). The launch date is also in conflict: Sept 2025 versus March 2026.
- Pine's savings figures are company claims and inconsistent ($300 / $400 / $750).

## 2. Human-connection services that achieved high frequency or sustained usage, and how (Papa, JustAnswer, TaskRabbit/Airtasker, Thumbtack, telehealth, nurse lines, employer concierge, Amex concierge)

### Takeaway
The human-connection services that last are rarely high-frequency per user. They survive because a **third party pays a per-member fee whether or not the user calls**: Medicare Advantage plans for Papa, employers and health plans for Teladoc, employers for corporate concierge, card annual fees for Amex, and government for healthdirect and NDIS-funded Mable. Measured per-user frequency for "ask a human" access products is low: roughly 0.1 telehealth visits per member per year, roughly 0.4 concierge requests per employee per year, and roughly 0.05 nurse-line calls per Australian per year. The genuinely high-frequency human services are relationship or care services (companionship visits, support workers, cleaning). Those either leak, or they are locked on-platform by a funder who pays through the platform.

### Cited Findings

**Papa (companionship for seniors, paid by Medicare Advantage plans)**
- Medicare Advantage plans pay Papa a per-member monthly fee (not per visit) (2022) — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/medicare-advantage-plans-senior-companions-profits/)
- The founder estimated 1M+ hours of companionship in 2022, and 65+ MA plans had signed up since 2020 — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/medicare-advantage-plans-senior-companions-profits/)
- Uptake example: at SummaCare, 12%+ of 23,000 Ohio Medicare members used the Papa benefit in a year — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/medicare-advantage-plans-senior-companions-profits/)
- Why plans pay: visits help plans earn more from Medicare by nudging members to get annual wellness exams, complete health-risk assessments and take covered screenings — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/medicare-advantage-plans-senior-companions-profits/)
- Vendor claim: a 2024 claims study found an 18% reduction in inpatient admissions among MA members with at least one Papa visit — [Papa blog](https://www.papa.com/resources/blog/reducing-healthcare-costs)

**JustAnswer (expert Q&A across categories, subscription)**
- Subscriptions are the main revenue source, with one-time per-question payments as a secondary source — [Vizologi](https://vizologi.com/business-strategy-canvas/justanswer-business-model-canvas/)
- Pricing (sources conflict, third-party): $65/month per category plus a $5 signup ([Clark](https://clark.com/education/justanswer-review/)); a $5–$25 join fee plus $28–$125/month ([ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/online/justanswer_com.html))
- Breadth: 150–200+ categories, commonly medical, legal, automotive, veterinary, tax/finance, home improvement and tech support, plus a "General" catch-all — [Clark](https://clark.com/education/justanswer-review/); [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/online/justanswer_com.html)
- Volume: 250,000 questions per month in 2012, when it was briefly rebranded Pearl.com — [TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/06/19/justanswer-becomes-pearl-com-raises-25-million-series-a/amp/)

**Telehealth subscription (Teladoc as the benchmark)**
- The business model is a per-member-per-month access fee plus a per-visit fee paid by the client, the member or both (2015 S-1) — [Teladoc S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001477449/000104746915005109/a2224910zs-1.htm)

- 2018: 2.6M visits and a 9.4% annual utilization rate (visits ÷ US paid members), up from 6.9% in 2017 — [Teladoc Q4 2018 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2019/02/28/teladoc-tdoc-q4-2018-earnings-conference-call-tran.aspx); definition in [Teladoc 8-K 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1477449/000155837022015396/tdoc-20221026xex99d1.htm)

**"Ask a nurse" line (Australia: healthdirect)**

### Inferences
- **Measured frequency of "access to a human" products is low per user**, even with subscriptions:
  - Teladoc: ~0.09 visits per member per year (2018).
  - Circles: ~0.4 requests per employee per year (4,000 requests ÷ 10,000 employees).
  - healthdirect: ~1.3M calls ÷ ~27M Australians ≈ 0.05 calls per capita per year (the population figure is background knowledge).

  These products work commercially because a sponsor pays per member, not per use.
- **High-frequency human services are relational and recurring:** companionship (Papa), disability and aged-care support (Mable), cleaning (TaskRabbit's best repeat category). Where a funder pays *through* the platform (MA plans, NDIS), the platform keeps the repeat relationship. Where the consumer pays the provider directly (cleaning on Airtasker or Homejoy), the relationship leaks (see Section 3).
- **JustAnswer survived through breadth** across many non-relational expert categories (medical, vet, legal, auto, tax, tech). The user rarely needs the *same* expert twice, so there is little leakage pressure. Monetisation is a subscription, often per category. This is the closest precedent to a "get me the right human to call me now" product built on multi-category expertise.
- **The after-hours pattern** (two-thirds of healthdirect calls) supports the founder's framing: the need peaks when regular providers are unreachable.
- **Mable's "replacement within 4–48 hours"** captures the "currently-available human" need. When the regular person is unavailable, even a consumer who has found a provider needs the platform again.
- **Amex concierge requests skew to gifts, flowers, dining and tickets.** These are discretionary and lifestyle requests, not urgent human-connection needs. Amex funds the concierge from annual card fees as a retention perk, not as a standalone business.

### Background, unverified
- **JustAnswer** was founded in 2003 and has been the subject of consumer complaints about auto-enrolment in recurring subscriptions. This would show that "pay-per-question" demand was converted to subscription revenue partly through billing design.
- **Papa** went through layoffs and leadership changes around 2023–2024, showing the fragility of dependence on Medicare Advantage plan budgets.
- **Australian relevance:** hipages (an ASX-listed tradie-lead marketplace) moved tradie monetisation toward subscriptions and job-management software. This is a provider-side SaaS answer to leakage and low consumer frequency.
- **Uber-style on-demand services** achieve frequency because providers are interchangeable commodities, so there is no reason to bypass the platform.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) Teladoc utilization figure, and no Australian telehealth subscription usage data (e.g., InstantScripts, Hola Health) was found.
- No Papa visits-per-member figure, no JustAnswer category mix or repeat rate, and no Amex request statistics were found.
- No Airtasker or TaskRabbit repeat-rate percentages by category, and no Thumbtack frequency data, were found.
- Concierge medicine (MDVIP and similar) and LifeCare were not searched (budget).
- Employer-concierge figures are old (1996, 2008, 2009) and vendor-reported. A recent, unsourced claim of "73% usage within six months" was excluded.

## 3. Evidence on leakage and disintermediation: how marketplaces lose repeat business, and which categories retain users

### Takeaway
Leakage is worst where the same customer repeatedly uses the same trusted provider (cleaning, babysitting, tutoring, dog walking, coaching). It is lowest where providers are interchangeable, the need is sporadic or urgent, the platform adds protection (insurance, guarantees, payments), the platform provides tools the provider depends on (scheduling, reminders, no-show policies), or a third-party funder pays through the platform. Homejoy is the canonical failure: good matches leaked and bad matches churned.

### Cited Findings
- **Homejoy** (home cleaning, shut down 2015): customers with a good experience were a leakage risk because both sides could deal off-platform, and customers with a poor experience didn't return — [Harvard D3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/homejoys-not-so-joyous-demise/index.html); [Harvard D3 (sibling piece)](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/?p=7251)
- **Homejoy:** only 15–20% of customers booked another cleaning within a month (Forbes, as cited by a University of Sydney policy blog; the original Forbes piece was not checked) — [University of Sydney Internet Policy blog](https://internet-policy-meco.sydney.edu.au/?p=3581)
- Oxford's Vili Lehdonvirta argues that repeat buyers and sellers can communicate directly and often drop the platform — [University of Sydney Internet Policy blog](https://internet-policy-meco.sydney.edu.au/?p=3581)
- **Homejoy:** single cleanings did not recover acquisition cost, so the business depended on repeat use, which is exactly where leakage happened. The cleaner learns the home and the client learns to trust the cleaner. (AI-generated research summaries; treat with caution) — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/829c3e65-8244-4aa1-8334-44c2d0ca8c63/); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/b9efced2-378a-4732-948b-57d3af21ca8e/)
- Cleaning is "high-touch and trust-heavy" with a recurring client–pro relationship, which makes it leakage-prone — [Entrepreneur](https://entm.ag/1Ox7Wjx)
- Proposed fixes for Homejoy included partnering with a large retailer, as TaskRabbit did, to cut acquisition cost. Handy, a competitor, still faced leakage — [Harvard D3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/homejoys-not-so-joyous-demise/index.html)
- **Leakage-prone list:** babysitting, dog walking, coaching, tutoring, cleaning and gardening marketplaces (Care.com, Wag!, CoachUp, Preply, Handy). "Repeated transactions between the same counterparties" raise fee savings from going direct and build the trust needed to do so — [Platform Chronicles I](https://platformchronicles.substack.com/p/platform-leakage)
- **StyleSeat (the "good" case):** scheduling, reminders, no-show policies and reviews keep repeat bookings on-platform. CoachUp is the "bad" case and Homejoy the "ugly" one — [Platform Chronicles II](https://platformchronicles.substack.com/p/platform-leakage-ii-the-good-the)
- Highly sensitive services (e.g., babysitting) and frequent repeat transactions with the same customer increase leakage. Lack of urgency also encourages leakage — [Mobile Spoon](https://www.mobilespoon.net/2019/08/minimizing-leakage-marketplace-offline.html)
- **Tutoring (opinion):** once you find a good tutor, later purchases go offline. The author attributes Tutorspree's failure to this — [Ashish Bhatia](https://ashishb.net/tech/when-marketplaces-work-and-when-they-dont/)
- **Rover counterpoint (anecdotal):** consumers use the app 1–3 times a year when travelling, and saving ~10% isn't worth the hassle and risk if something goes wrong, so leakage is lower than expected — [ValuePunks comments](https://valuepunks.substack.com/p/small-cap-idea-rover-rovr/comments)
- **Thumbtack:** pros discuss moving clients off-app to avoid repeat lead charges for annual jobs. One pro says "the repeat business after is free" — [Thumbtack Community](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/2198/phone-numbers); [Pressure Washing Resource](https://pressurewashingresource.com/community/t/who-on-here-uses-thumbtack/6812)
- **Airtasker's** recurring-job subscription option is a very small share of activity, which suggests recurring work rarely stays on-platform — [Jordan Martens analysis](https://jordanmartenstyn.substack.com/p/airtasker)
- **Mable (Australia):** NDIS funding is linked to the account, payments run through Mable, every booking is insured, and Mable sources short-notice replacements. Together these give the platform a role in each repeat booking — [Mable](https://mable.com.au/); [Mable platform terms](https://mable.com.au/legal/terms/platform-terms/)

### Inferences
- **Leakage is a property of repeat counterparty relationships, not of frequency itself.** This matches the founder's framing. The founder's highest-retention opportunities are therefore needs where:
  1. the right human differs each time (expert advice across categories, JustAnswer-style; urgent or after-hours needs);
  2. the regular human is unavailable (backup or substitute: Mable replacement, Rover trips);
  3. the counterparty is an institution, not an individual (customer-service callbacks, Pine-style);
  4. a funder pays through the platform (NDIS, health plans, employers, landlords).
- **Platform protection and provider tools are the known levers** for keeping repeat relationships on-platform: insurance and guarantees, payment rails, scheduling and reminders, no-show enforcement (StyleSeat), and funder billing (Mable). A text-only callback service has none of these by default, so it should expect near-total leakage on any category where the user wants the same person again.
- **Low urgency drives leakage too** (Mobile Spoon). The service's "human calls you within minutes" value is strongest for urgent needs, which are also the least leakage-prone.

### Gaps
- No hard platform-wide retention or leakage percentages were found for Care.com, Rover, Wag!, Preply, Airtasker, TaskRabbit or Thumbtack. Investor filings would be needed.
- No a16z or NfX primary posts on leakage were retrieved in search results (budget). The Lenny's Newsletter retention benchmarks were only available as a second-hand summary: consumer transactional products have "good" 30% and "great" 50% retention — [Korean summary of Lenny's](https://maily.so/marcus.lee/posts/g0zm8ed7oql). This is unverified against the paywalled original.

## 4. The "frequency through breadth" thesis: do multi-category concierges get used more often, or do people forget them? What drove habit?

### Takeaway
The historical evidence leans against breadth producing frequency by itself. Multi-category concierges (Magic, GoButler, Operator, Facebook M) either shrank to one category or shut down. Employer concierge usage depends on how aggressively it is marketed. Fancy Hands had to push weekly "suggested requests" to prompt use. Breadth works when paired with (a) a prepaid subscription or sponsor that makes each use feel free, (b) proactive prompts, or (c) a standing connection to the user's data (email, calendar). Poke's proactive, data-connected iMessage agent is the only example here with reported daily-scale engagement.

### Cited Findings
- GoButler narrowed from "anything" to flights in March 2016 because flights were a long-popular category, then pivoted away from consumers entirely — [Deutsche Startups](https://www.deutsche-startups.de/2016/03/21/gobutler-ist-kein-full-service-butler-mehr/); [Deutsche Startups](https://www.deutsche-startups.de/2016/09/21/amazon-schnappt-sich-gobutler-nachfolger-angel/)
- Magic moved from general concierge to paid ($100/hr) and then to dedicated business assistants — [YesPress](https://yespress.io/magic)
- Facebook M reached only ~2,000 users with the human tier before shutting down — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/facebook-m-beta-discontinued/)
- Corporate concierge usage "varies, depending upon how aggressively the service is marketed" (Circles, 2008) — [Workforce](https://workforce.com/news/an-intriguing-development-for-workplace-concierge-services)
- Fancy Hands ran weekly "suggested requests" posts (HVAC repair, tickets, volunteering, moving a child to college) to prompt use — [Fancy Hands blog](https://legacy.fancyhands.com/blog/suggested-requests-for-the-week-of-august-15th)
- Fancy Hands used a monthly task allotment ($35 for 15 tasks), which creates use-it-or-lose-it pressure — [Marshall Kirkpatrick](https://marshallk.com/fancyhands-a-review-of-my-last-two-months-of-tasks)
- Poke is proactive: it monitors connected email, calendar and files and surfaces what matters. Reported scale is 100M+ messages over three months across hundreds of thousands of users (secondary) — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/06/04/first-ai-agent-for-messages-business-chat-approved-by-apple); [The Note](https://thenote.app/post/en/poke-an-ai-agent-that-lets-users-automate-tasks-via-text-message-raised-10m-on-mlxhve9q5b?amp=true)
- Duckbill offers breadth (75+ categories) on a ~$100/month subscription, reached through text, email, app and a Claude integration — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/how-meghan-joyce-and-duckbill-unlocked-life-efficiency-with-ai-and-human-collaboration)
- JustAnswer sells breadth (150–200+ expert categories) but prices subscriptions per category in at least some reported plans ($65/month per category) — [Clark](https://clark.com/education/justanswer-review/)
- Amex Centurion's dedicated concierge (the same person each time) is positioned as the premium differentiator — [Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/credit-cards/reviews/amex-centurion-black-card/how-to-get)
- Thumbtack's Instant Book (instant booking without back-and-forth) was associated with higher repeat and retention in limited testing — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210211005662/en/thumbtack-launches-instant-book-to-make-hiring-pros-even-easier)

### Inferences
- **Breadth widens the funnel but doesn't create habit; recall is the bottleneck.** People forget a number they text once or twice a year (this matches the low per-member utilization in Section 2).
- **Habit drivers seen in the evidence:**
  1. Proactive outreach from the service (Poke monitoring; Fancy Hands weekly prompts).
  2. Prepaid allotments or subscriptions that make each request feel free (Fancy Hands, Duckbill, JustAnswer, Teladoc PMPM).
  3. A sponsor that markets the service to its members (employer concierge, MA plans, landlords, Amex).
  4. Continuity with a known agent (Amex Centurion dedicated concierge).
  5. Low-friction instant fulfilment (Thumbtack Instant Book).
- **For an iMessage number**, the pinned conversation itself is the "saved contact". Proactive, context-aware nudges are the 2025–2026 lever that 2015 concierges lacked. Examples: "your car rego is due, want a mechanic to call?" or "it's 40°C tomorrow, want an aircon tech check?" This is an inference: no direct evidence was found on nudge-driven retention for human-callback services.

### Gaps
- No controlled or quantitative evidence was found comparing usage frequency of multi-category versus single-category concierges.
- No data was found on the effect of a saved contact or pinned iMessage thread on recall or repeat usage.

## 5. Emerging AI-native patterns (2025–2026): AI agents calling businesses for consumers, "AI chief of staff" products, and which use cases show traction

### Takeaway
Big-tech AI calling (Google Ask for Me since early 2025; Gemini "Call for Me" test from Sept 24, 2026) started with narrow, standardised quote and availability calls: auto services, nail salons, then reportedly pet groomers, dry cleaners and home repair. It later added reservations, stock checks and rescheduling. Startups with traction (Pine, Duckbill, Poke) focus on recurring admin: bills, cancellations, disputes, appointments, email and calendar. All of these have the AI *calling the business*. None has an agent getting a *human professional to call the consumer back*. That is the founder's differentiator, and also where Google's home-repair calling could compete at the quote stage.

### Cited Findings
- **Google Ask for Me (Search Labs, early 2025):** supported oil changes, tire and brake replacements, emissions tests, and manicure/pedicure appointments. The AI calls several businesses and returns a summary of prices and availability. Businesses can opt out — [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/google-search-ask-for-me-3521795); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-launches-ai-phone-assistant-to-call-businesses-for-you/538759)
- A Business Insider hands-on said it could be a game changer if it expands to medical offices and customer-service lines, but "it's not there yet" — [Business Insider](https://businessinsider.nl/i-tried-googles-new-tool-that-calls-businesses-for-you-to-get-information-heres-how-helpful-it-actually-is)
- Later third-party reports mention pet groomers, dry cleaners and auto shops (date unclear) — [CO/AI](https://case-studies.getcoai.com/news/googles-ai-assistant-now-makes-business-calls-for-users-nationwide). A 2026 marketing blog claims plumbing and HVAC were announced for a US-wide rollout (not confirmed from a primary Google source) — [FindSkill](https://findskill.ai/blog/google-ai-calls-plumbers-hvac-15-minute-check/)
- **Gemini "Call for Me" (from Sept 24, 2026):** a test for select US Pixel 11 owners with paid Gemini subscriptions. Calls cover in-stock checks, restaurant reservations, moving appointments and placing items on hold. Gemini identifies itself as an AI and the user can follow a live transcript and step in — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/google-tests-letting-gemini-make-phone-calls-initially-for-us-pixel-owners/); [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/phones/google-pixel/pixel-11s-newest-trick-is-calling-local-businesses-so-you-dont-have-to)
- **Pine AI:** bills, complaints, cancellations and disputes by phone, email and web. Company-claimed savings are $300–$750 per user and a 93% negotiation success rate — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/05/06/pine-ai-aims-consumer-ai-agent-complex-customer-service-interactions/); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/pine-ai-profile-stanley-wei-interview/); [AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/pine-ai-assistant-agent/6746403769?country=py)
- **Duckbill:** AI plus human copilots for medical-bill disputes, appointments and cancellations, across 75+ categories, at ~$100/month — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/how-meghan-joyce-and-duckbill-unlocked-life-efficiency-with-ai-and-human-collaboration); [Forerunner](https://forerunnerventures.com/perspectives/delivering-on-techs-age-old-holy-grail-duckbill-the-indispensable-ai-powered)
- **Poke** (an "AI chief of staff" in iMessage): email, calendar, invoices, rescheduling, travel and proactive surfacing. Apple approved it for Messages for Business (June 2026) with per-user fees paid to Apple — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/06/04/first-ai-agent-for-messages-business-chat-approved-by-apple); [TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/apple-messages-for-business-first-ai-agent-poke-approved)
- **Magic** now sells a "human-plus-AI" assistant: AI answers instantly and humans handle complex tasks — [YesPress](https://yespress.io/magic)

### Inferences
- **Traction concentrates on high-friction, recurring interactions with institutions** (customer service, bills, subscriptions, appointments) and on daily personal admin (email and calendar). Both repeat without the "regular provider" leakage problem.
- **Google's chosen categories are standardised, quotable services** (auto, nails, groomers, dry cleaners, reportedly home repair). If Google's home-repair calling reaches broad availability (the US first; no Australian launch found), it competes directly with a tradie quote-gathering flow. The founder's moat would then be the *human callback*, *Australian coverage*, and *iMessage presence*, not quote collection.
- **The hybrid AI-plus-human model is now standard** (Magic, Duckbill, Pine's escalation and the user being able to step in). This supports the founder's design: an AI triages, a human delivers.
- **Apple's Messages for Business approval of Poke** (June 2026) shows a sanctioned path for AI agents in iMessage, with per-user platform fees as a cost line. This is relevant to the founder's distribution channel.

### Gaps
- No usage or retention data for Google Ask for Me or Call for Me was found, and no evidence of either launching in Australia.
- No independent user counts or retention figures for Pine or Duckbill were found.
- Other "AI chief of staff" products (beyond Poke and Duckbill) were not surveyed (budget).

## 6. Synthesis: (a) request categories with the strongest evidence of frequency and retention, (b) design patterns that create habit, (c) business models that worked for high-frequency human-connection services

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence points to three request types:
- recurring admin with institutions (customer-service callbacks, bills, cancellations, disputes, appointments);
- "the right expert, right now" across many categories (health/nurse, vet, legal, auto, tax, tech) with no need for the same person twice;
- backup or substitute humans when the regular provider is unavailable (care, cleaning, childcare, pets), especially after hours.

Habit comes from proactivity, prepaid allotments, sponsor marketing and continuity. Most durable models were sponsor-paid per member (health plans, employers, landlords, card issuers, government funders), not pay-per-request consumer models.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Request categories, ranked by strength of evidence**

1. **Customer-service and admin "digital chores"**
   - Evidence: the 2025–2026 consumer agents with funding and traction focus on bills, cancellations, disputes and appointments ([Pine/SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/05/06/pine-ai-aims-consumer-ai-agent-complex-customer-service-interactions/); [Duckbill/HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/how-meghan-joyce-and-duckbill-unlocked-life-efficiency-with-ai-and-human-collaboration)), and Poke reports high message volume on email and calendar admin ([The Note](https://thenote.app/post/en/poke-an-ai-agent-that-lets-users-automate-tasks-via-text-message-raised-10m-on-mlxhve9q5b?amp=true)).
   - Legacy precedent: Fancy Hands' core was phone calls, appointments and research ([Fancy Hands](https://www.fancyhands.com/press/mashable)).
2. **Expert advice across categories**
   - Evidence: JustAnswer sustained subscription revenue across medical, legal, auto, vet, tax and tech, at 250k questions per month in 2012 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2012/06/19/justanswer-becomes-pearl-com-raises-25-million-series-a/amp/); [Clark](https://clark.com/education/justanswer-review/)).
   - Australia: healthdirect handles ~1.3M nurse calls a year, two-thirds after hours ([healthdirect](https://about.healthdirect.gov.au/resources/news/healthdirect-expands-virtual-care-pathways)).
3. **Care, companionship and support work (high frequency, relational)**
   - Evidence: Papa delivered 1M+ hours in 2022 ([KFF](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/medicare-advantage-plans-senior-companions-profits/)) and Mable reports 32M+ care hours ([Mable](https://mable.com.au/)).
   - Caveat: these stay on-platform only because a funder pays through the platform.
4. **Cleaning (most repeatable gig category, but the most leakage-prone)**
   - Evidence: TaskRabbit names it as its best repeat category ([TaskRabbit](https://www.taskrabbit.com/blog/cleaning-course-intro/)), while Homejoy's experience shows the leakage ([Harvard D3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/homejoys-not-so-joyous-demise/index.html); [University of Sydney](https://internet-policy-meco.sydney.edu.au/?p=3581)).
5. **Travel/flights and lifestyle (dining, gifts, tickets)**
   - Evidence: these were among the most popular requests at GoButler and Amex ([Deutsche Startups](https://www.deutsche-startups.de/2016/03/21/gobutler-ist-kein-full-service-butler-mehr/); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/travel/american-express-concierge)).
   - Fit: these are not "get a human to call me" needs. They are better served by booking tools or perk programs.

**(b) Design patterns linked to habit**
- **Proactive monitoring and outreach:** Poke ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/06/04/first-ai-agent-for-messages-business-chat-approved-by-apple)); Fancy Hands weekly suggestions ([Fancy Hands blog](https://legacy.fancyhands.com/blog/suggested-requests-for-the-week-of-august-15th)).
- **Prepaid allotment or subscription:** Fancy Hands' 15 tasks per month ([Marshall Kirkpatrick](https://marshallk.com/fancyhands-a-review-of-my-last-two-months-of-tasks)); Duckbill ~$100/month; JustAnswer membership ([Clark](https://clark.com/education/justanswer-review/)).
- **Sponsor-driven marketing to members:** employer usage depends on how aggressively the service is marketed ([Workforce](https://workforce.com/news/an-intriguing-development-for-workplace-concierge-services)).
- **Continuity with a known agent:** Amex Centurion's dedicated concierge ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/credit-cards/reviews/amex-centurion-black-card/how-to-get)).
- **Instant fulfilment with no back-and-forth:** Thumbtack Instant Book ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210211005662/en/thumbtack-launches-instant-book-to-make-hiring-pros-even-easier)).
- **Platform-held protection, payments and backup:** Mable's insurance, NDIS payment rails and 4–48h replacement ([Mable](https://mable.com.au/)); StyleSeat's scheduling, reminders and no-show policies ([Platform Chronicles II](https://platformchronicles.substack.com/p/platform-leakage-ii-the-good-the)).

**(c) Business models that worked, or at least endured, for human-connection services**
- **Per-member fee from a health plan, independent of use:**
  - Papa is paid by Medicare Advantage plans, which are motivated by wellness-exam and risk-assessment revenue ([KFF](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/medicare-advantage-plans-senior-companions-profits/)).
  - Teladoc charges PMPM plus visit fees, despite only 9.4% utilization ([Teladoc S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001477449/000104746915005109/a2224910zs-1.htm); [Q4 2018 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2019/02/28/teladoc-tdoc-q4-2018-earnings-conference-call-tran.aspx)).
- **Employer-paid benefit:** corporate concierge, with roughly 0.4 requests per employee per year but sold as a perk ([Workforce](https://workforce.com/news/an-intriguing-development-for-workplace-concierge-services)).
- **Landlord-paid amenity (B2B2C):** Hello Alfred's pivot lowered acquisition cost and attracted real-estate investors ([Harvard D3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/hello-alfred-automating-the-on-demand-economy/index.html); [Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2018/05/invesco-divcowest-lead-40m-series-b-funding-in-butler-service-hello-alfred)).
- **Card annual fee as a retention perk:** the Amex concierge ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/travel/american-express-concierge)).
- **Government or scheme funding through the platform:** Mable with the NDIS ([Mable](https://mable.com.au/)); healthdirect is government-funded ([healthdirect](https://about.healthdirect.gov.au/resources/news/healthdirect-expands-virtual-care-pathways)).
- **Consumer subscription for expert access:** JustAnswer ([Vizologi](https://vizologi.com/business-strategy-canvas/justanswer-business-model-canvas/)).
- **Consumer subscription for an AI-plus-human assistant:** Duckbill (~$100/month); Magic for businesses ([YesPress](https://yespress.io/magic)).
- **Outcome-linked value for customer service:** Pine frames value as dollars saved ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/05/06/pine-ai-aims-consumer-ai-agent-complex-customer-service-interactions/)).

**What failed:** pay-per-request general consumer concierges with markup on fulfilment. Examples: Magic losing money on 25% of orders ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1257663)), GoButler ([Deutsche Startups](https://www.deutsche-startups.de/2016/09/21/amazon-schnappt-sich-gobutler-nachfolger-angel/)), Facebook M ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2018-01-08-facebook-m-ai-assistant-closure.html)), and per-job marketplaces in relational categories (Homejoy).

### Inferences
- **Highest-frequency, lowest-leakage wedges the evidence supports** for an Australian "text a number, a human calls you" service:
  1. **"Get me a real person at [company]"**: escalation and callbacks from telcos, energy retailers, banks, insurers, government agencies. These are institutional, recurring, and impossible to bypass by "finding a regular".
  2. **"Talk to an expert now"** across nurse, vet, pharmacist, lawyer, accountant, mechanic and IT. These are non-relational, often urgent or after-hours, JustAnswer-style breadth delivered as a callback.
  3. **"My regular can't make it"**: backup cleaner, support worker, babysitter, dog walker. The need is to find someone available now, and Mable's replacement feature shows platforms can own this moment.
- **Tradie needs still fit as an urgent subset** (after-hours plumber or electrician). Quote-gathering faces potential competition from Google's AI calling, though no Australian launch was found.
- **The monetisation lesson is consistent:** frequency per user is low for nearly every "access a human" product. The durable answer has been a sponsor paying per member rather than relying on frequency. Australian candidates are employers (EAP-adjacent benefits), private health insurers (extras/wellbeing), landlords and build-to-rent operators (an Alfred analogue), card issuers and banks (concierge perks), and NDIS or aged-care funding (Mable analogue). This is an inference from the precedents; the researcher did not verify any Australian sponsor's appetite.
- **The founder's frequency metric** ("how often a user needs a new or currently-available human they can't already reach directly") is well supported by the leakage literature. Categories pass the test when providers are interchangeable, the need is urgent, the regular is unavailable, or the counterparty is an institution.

### Gaps
- No quantitative cross-product comparison of request frequency by category exists in public sources. The rankings above are drawn from qualitative and vendor evidence, and from adjacent data (Teladoc, healthdirect, Circles).
- No Australian data was found on customer-service hold-time pain or callback demand, on demand for backup care or cleaning, or on Australian employer, insurer or landlord appetite for sponsored concierge benefits. These would be needed to validate the inferred wedges.
