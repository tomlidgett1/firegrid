# Business and power-user use cases for a text-a-number "a real human calls you" service (Australia, Melbourne focus, as of October 2026)

Method note: 28 web searches used (the full allowance). One WebFetch attempt failed with a DNS error (labourhireauthority.vic.gov.au), so every finding below comes from search-result snippets and summaries, not full-page reads. Items marked **[BK]** are background knowledge or my own assumptions, not verified in this session. A few facts are reused from sibling notes in this repo (named where used), cited to their original sources. Undated or pre-2025 data is labelled "older".

Segments evaluated: (1) short-stay co-hosts and multi-listing hosts; (2) hospitality venues needing urgent repairs and services; (3) hospitality venues needing last-minute shift staff; (4) small and independent property-management agencies; (5) real estate sales agents (pre-sale prep and vendor coordination); (6) self-managing landlords; (7) small construction and site managers; (8) office and facilities managers; (9) event organisers; (10) pharmacies and medical/dental practices (added: locum staffing); (11) strata and building managers (added, mostly [BK]); (12) small businesses in general (IT, bookkeeping, legal).

## Q1. How often does each segment need a NEW or currently-available human it cannot already reach directly?

### Takeaway
No segment has a published "new-vendor requests per week" statistic, so frequency has to be built from proxies. The highest proxy frequency is in operators of many physical sites who face short-notice gaps: short-stay co-hosts (turnovers several times a week per listing), hospitality venues (daily shifts, with rising no-shows; frequent equipment faults) and property managers (about 3.3 maintenance requests per property per year on a US benchmark, times rent rolls of roughly 70–130+ properties per manager). Sales agents generate several vendor tasks per listing, but most go to repeat preferred suppliers. Self-managing landlords, small offices and general small businesses look close to consumer frequency.

### Inferences
Each estimate below is built from assumptions, shown explicitly so the report writer can adjust them.
- **Short-stay co-host with 15 listings in Melbourne:**
  - Assumptions [BK]: about 65% occupancy and stays of about 3–4 nights.
  - That gives roughly 80–90 turnovers a month.
  - If the regular cleaner falls through on 3–5% of turnovers [assumption], that is 3–4 backup-cleaner needs a month.
  - Add urgent guest-in-residence repairs and lockouts at about 0.3–0.5 per listing per month [assumption], or 5–7 a month.
  - Total: about **2–3 new or available-human needs a week**. A single-listing host has about one-fifteenth of that, close to consumer frequency.
- **Café or restaurant group with 3 venues:**
  - Shift gaps that the venue's own staff group chat can't cover: about 1 per venue per week in peak periods [assumption], so around 12 a month.
  - Equipment and services (coffee machine, refrigeration, dishwasher, plumbing, pest, glazier, locksmith, electrician): about 1–2 per venue per month [assumption], so 3–6 a month.
  - The repair categories vary widely, and after-hours availability often rules out the venue's usual technician.
- **Property-management agency, per PM with 150 properties:**
  - At the US rate of 3.3 requests per unit, that is about 500 jobs a year, or roughly 10 a week.
  - Most go to the panel tradie. If 10–20% need a non-panel, specialist, after-hours or "first available" tradie [assumption], that is about **1–2 a week per PM**, or 5–10 a week for a 5-PM agency.
- **Sales agent with 25–40 listings a year** [BK: typical agents are far below the Top-100 average of 140]:
  - Photography, floorplans, copy, signboards and print go to repeat suppliers through Realhub-type networks, so leakage is already "built in".
  - The novel needs are pre-sale fix-ups: handyman, painter, gardener, rubbish removal, a one-off clean, minor plumbing or electrical. At about 1–3 per listing [assumption], that is roughly **0.5–2 a week**.
- **Self-managing landlord with 1–2 properties:** about 3–7 maintenance events a year, which is consumer-like.
- **Small construction site manager [BK]:** variable daily needs (labourers, traffic control, skip bins, concrete pump, plant hire). Most are met by established suppliers, so the share needing a *new* provider is probably small, but it is spiky.
- **Office and facilities managers, and general small businesses [BK]:** monthly-to-quarterly needs. Once a bookkeeper or IT provider is found, the relationship persists, so new-human frequency is low.
- **Event organisers [BK]:** spiky. Very high need in event weeks, near zero otherwise.

### Gaps
- No Australian maintenance-requests-per-property figure was found. The Tapi PMI may hold one, but it could not be fetched (gated download).
- No national average of listings per agent was found. The Reapit 2026 report might contain one.
- No baseline Australian hospitality no-show rate was found. Deputy's data could supply it but was only reported as a match-day delta.

- Average length of stay for Melbourne STRs (which drives turnover frequency) was not found. The 3–4 night figure is [BK].
- No current ABS Counts of Australian Businesses figure for Victorian ANZSIC 45 (cafés, restaurants, takeaway) was retrieved.

## Q2. What do these segments use today, and what does it cost? (named Australian players and pricing)

### Takeaway
Each segment already has incumbent tools, but most are booking forms, bid marketplaces or software workflows, not "someone calls you now":
- **Hospitality staffing:** Sidekicker (margin on hours), Jitjatjo Ondemand (hourly, no subscription, 2-hour minimum notice), plus newer apps (Shift Filla, Apr 2026; Squaddle).
- **Hospitality repairs:** diagnostic or call-out fees around $140–$150.
- **Short-stay:** Turno (bid-based cleaner marketplace, live in Australia) and full-service managers at 15–30%+ commission.
- **Property managers:** Tapi (AI tenant intake plus tradie routing).
- **Sales agents:** Realhub (400+ marketing suppliers).
- **Construction:** Yakka and the Dragon App.
- **Pharmacy:** Locumate.
- **Odd jobs:** Airtasker, which charges posters A$4.90–A$49.95 per task plus a 12.5–20% Tasker fee.

"Hirely" could not be found. Shiftly is rostering software, not a staffing marketplace.

## Q3. Willingness to pay: what model works and who controls the budget?

### Takeaway
Willingness to pay is strongest where a missed gap costs revenue immediately:
- **Short-stay operators:** a missed turnover means a cancelled booking or a bad review.
- **Hospitality venues:** a dead coffee machine or a missing chef means lost covers.
- **Pharmacies:** a pharmacy cannot open without a pharmacist.

Each of these already pays visible premiums: labour-hire margins, 15–30% STR commissions, $148.50 diagnostic fees, and 30–50% locum agency fees. In each, the person texting is also the person with the budget (the venue owner or manager, the co-host, the pharmacy owner).

Property managers and sales agents text on behalf of someone else, because owners and vendors pay for the work. That pushes monetisation toward a provider-pays-per-connection model or a small agent subscription. Victorian rebate rules also restrict agents keeping supplier kickbacks.

### Cited Findings

Pricing hypotheses by segment. These are for the founder to test; none was found in market.

Co-hosts earn 15–30% of booking revenue, so a few dollars a turnover to guarantee backup is affordable. The pitch is "never miss a turnover; backup cleaner or handyman confirmed in 15 minutes".
- **Hospitality repairs (budget: the owner or venue manager, often the same person in independents):** venues already pay $140–$150 just for a diagnostic, so there is room for (a) a provider-pays exclusive live-lead fee of about $30–$60 on commercial jobs, and/or (b) a venue subscription of about $49–$99 a month per site for priority after-hours dispatch. Commercial technicians value business clients because they recur.
- **Hospitality shift staffing (budget: the owner or manager):** buyers are used to pay-per-hour with no subscription (Jitjatjo, Sidekicker), and some apps are free (Squaddle). A non-employer-of-record introducer can charge only a placement or connection fee (about $20–$50 a shift [assumption]). Capturing the larger hourly margin requires being the employer of record, which triggers labour-hire licensing (see Q6).
- **Property managers (budget: the agency principal; repair costs: landlords):** PMs seldom pay out of pocket for repairs, and principals resist new software spend [BK]. Provider-pays per connection is more realistic. PM work is attractive to tradies (volume, repeat, landlord pays), and an "after-hours or overflow dispatch" subscription per agency of about $99–$299 a month is plausible but unverified.
- **Sales agents (budget: the agent or principal; prep costs: vendors):** agents pay for their own time-savers (CRM, PA, VA). Charging the agent about $49–$99 a month, or provider-pays, avoids Victorian rebate issues. **Do not** design any flow where the agent receives a referral fee or benefit from a supplier without vendor disclosure or pass-through.
- **Pharmacies (budget: the pharmacy owner):** the highest willingness to pay per fill, because agencies charge 30–50%. But this requires credentialing (see Q7).
- **Self-managing landlords:** consumer-like willingness to pay, close to per-job only. Rent Better-style platforms may be a channel.

- Agency-side software budgets per property (PropertyMe or Console pricing) were not checked.

## Q4. Solution gap: where do existing tools fail, and is "text one number and the right person calls within 15 minutes" meaningfully better?

### Takeaway
The callback format is meaningfully better for **diagnostic and quote-type needs** (repairs, trades, lockouts, urgent property issues), where the buyer wants an informed human on the phone fast. Incumbents there are async (Airtasker and Turno bids), shared (hipages, up to 3 tradies per lead) or assume a known vendor (Tapi, Realhub).

It is less differentiated for **commodity labour** (shift staff, routine cleans). There the buyer wants a confirmed booking, not a phone call, and the real bottleneck is short-notice supply. Platforms like Sidekicker, Jitjatjo, Shift Filla and Locumate already offer app booking with quick fills.

### Cited Findings
- Turno's cleaner marketplace is bid-based: hosts post, cleaners bid, hosts choose. That is a workflow for finding a regular team, not for same-day emergencies. — [Turno VIC](https://turno.com/airbnb-cleaners/au/vic/australia/)
- Jitjatjo bookings need at least two hours' notice (bookable "two hours to two months in advance"). — [Spice News](https://spicenews.com.au/sponsored_content/on-demand-staffing-solution-jitjatjo-is-now-in-melbourne)
- Locumate reports a 16-minute median fill in a credentialed niche (self-reported, one event). Speed parity exists for pharmacy. — [LinkedIn](https://au.linkedin.com/in/surgesingh)
- Tapi already gives tenants a 24/7 AI assistant to report repairs and routes jobs to tradies, so the reporting-and-routing gap for agencies using Tapi is largely closed. — [Tapi](https://tapihq.com/en-au)
- Realhub links agents to 400+ marketing suppliers; its scope is marketing (print, photography, floorplans, copy), not pre-sale trades. — [Domain: Realhub](https://agent.domain.com.au/residential/realhub/)
- hipages shares each lead with up to three businesses (sibling note). — [What's The Damage](https://whatsthedamage.com.au/tradies/hipages-cost/)
- Airtasker is bid-based and asynchronous. Its poster connection fee and Tasker fee apply per task. — [Trustpilot (Airtasker reply)](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/www.airtasker.com?page=2)
- Intermittent coffee-machine faults can take up to two hours to diagnose, so the first triage call has real value in routing the right technician. — [Service Sphere](https://www.servicesphere.com.au/FAQ)
- Yakka claims employers can "hire crews in minutes" in construction, so a speed claim already exists there. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/labour-jobs-hire-yakka/id1668719409)

### Inferences
Gap by segment, from biggest to smallest:
1. **STR co-hosts, urgent and backup needs:** HIGH. Turno and Airtasker are async, and full-service managers bundle it at 15–30%. A text line that confirms a backup cleaner or handyman, or gets a locksmith calling within 15 minutes while a guest is locked out, is clearly better. A text-based interface also suits co-hosts managing many properties from their phone.
2. **Hospitality repairs and services:** HIGH. Managers currently google and ring around mid-service, after hours, across many trade types. One text that results in a commercial-grade technician calling back is clearly better, especially if the service knows the venue's equipment (brand and model) from previous requests.
3. **Sales agents, pre-sale fix-ups:** MEDIUM-HIGH. Agents have phone lists, but campaign deadlines are tight and the trade mix is variable. Realhub doesn't cover trades.
4. **Small PM agencies, overflow and after-hours:** MEDIUM. Tapi and the like cover routine work. The gap is "panel tradie unavailable, emergency, regional property or rare trade". Agencies without Tapi have a bigger gap.
5. **Hospitality shift staffing:** MEDIUM-LOW. The interface is not the bottleneck. A text front door that fans out to Sidekicker, Jitjatjo, agencies and a worker pool and returns a *confirmation* would help, but the core value is supply depth.
6. **Construction labour:** LOW-MEDIUM. Yakka and labour-hire firms, plus deep relationships.
7. **Pharmacy locums:** LOW. Locumate is fast and specialised.

**Design implication:** for business users, the deliverable should flex by request type.
- Repairs: "a human calls you".
- Labour and cleans: "a confirmed person is booked".
- Both: an iMessage thread with ETA and status.

The 15-minute promise matters most when something is broken or missing *right now*.

### Gaps
- No fill-rate or time-to-fill data was found for Sidekicker, Jitjatjo, Turno (AU) or Yakka.
- No user complaints from AU Reddit or Whirlpool about these platforms surfaced, so failure modes are inferred, not evidenced.

## Q5. Leakage risk, and how to reduce it

### Takeaway
Leakage is highest wherever the vendor category is repeatable and the quality bar is personal: regular cleaners, photographers, signboard and print suppliers, a PM's panel plumber, a builder's skip-bin company. It is lowest where the need is about *availability now*, *a rare trade*, or *a new location*: after-hours emergencies, backup cover, multi-site and multi-suburb operators, and the long tail of trade types.

For business users, the strongest counter is to stop fighting leakage and become the dispatcher for **both** saved and new vendors, charged by subscription, so that bypassing the service saves nothing.

### Cited Findings
- Realhub is already integrated with 400+ suppliers for marketing tasks. Agents' repeat vendor needs are captured by existing workflow software, so leakage on those categories is structurally high. — [Domain: Realhub](https://agent.domain.com.au/residential/realhub/)
- Turno's model has hosts "add cleaners to their Turno team to delegate, manage, and pay". The incumbent's design assumes a stable roster, which shows the regular-cleaner relationship is sticky. Turno keeps those relationships by also handling scheduling and payment. — [Turno VIC](https://turno.com/airbnb-cleaners/au/vic/australia/)
- Sidekicker's "smarthire" lets businesses convert a temp to permanent for a fee. Even incumbents monetise leakage (temp-to-perm) instead of blocking it. — [Sidekicker hospitality](https://sidekicker.com/industries/hospitality)
- Jitjatjo lets venues "curate your virtual talent pool for free". It keeps favourites inside the platform rather than letting them leak. — [Spice News](https://spicenews.com.au/sponsored_content/on-demand-staffing-solution-jitjatjo-is-now-in-melbourne)
- Locumate lets pharmacies build an internal talent pool inside the platform. — [G2](https://www.g2.com/sellers/locumate)
- Airtasker's US Business Membership removes per-task fees for an annual price, which is a subscription that removes the incentive to bypass. — [Airtasker US](https://www.airtasker.com/us/?p=29999)

### Inferences
Leakage by segment:
- **STR co-hosts:** cleaning has high leakage; urgent repairs and lockouts have low leakage (different trade each time, different suburbs, after hours).
- **Hospitality repairs:** low-to-medium. Venues keep one coffee technician, but refrigeration, plumbing, electrical, pest, glazing and locksmiths each come up rarely, and after-hours availability varies.
- **Shift staffing:** medium. Good workers get hired directly or added to rosters, but the need is always for "whoever is free this Saturday", so demand persists.
- **PM agencies:** high leakage for routine trades (panels); low for overflow, specialist and after-hours.
- **Agents:** high for marketing suppliers; medium for prep trades.
- **Construction:** high, because of established suppliers and relationships.
- **SMB generic (IT, bookkeeping, legal):** very high. One find lasts years.

Mitigations ranked by fit for this product:
1. **"Your vendors + our network" dispatcher.** The user texts "get the fridge fixed at the Collingwood site". The agent tries the user's saved vendor first and falls back to the network if they don't answer within X minutes. The saved list lives in the service, so leakage becomes retention. Charge per site or listing per month.
2. **Subscription over per-connection for business users,** so there is no saving from bypassing.
3. **Consolidated invoicing and records:** one monthly statement across all vendors and sites, with job history per property or venue, and licence and insurance evidence attached (useful for PMs and STR operators). [BK] PMs need tradie insurance and licence records for compliance.
4. **Availability guarantee (SLA)** with a credit if no one calls within 15 minutes. Direct vendor numbers carry no guarantee.
5. **Masked numbers** for the first contact only. This adds little for businesses, because they want the vendor relationship.
6. **Multi-location coverage:** operators across many suburbs can't keep a vendor list for every area.

### Gaps
- No empirical leakage rates were found for Australian service marketplaces (e.g. Airtasker off-platform rates).
- It is untested whether business users will keep routing known vendors through a third party. This needs pilot data.

## Q6. Regulatory issues (labour-hire licensing, employment classification, real estate rules)

### Takeaway
Any staffing model where the service supplies workers (employer of record, or "on-supplying" workers employed by others) is labour hire. In Victoria, Queensland, South Australia and the ACT that needs a licence. Victoria tightened its regime in 2026: suitability and financial-viability tests from 1 June, and a new definition from 1 October 2026 that explicitly captures "bare intermediaries" anywhere in the supply chain. Penalties for hosts using unlicensed providers are reported above A$660,000.

That makes **last-minute shift staffing the most regulated segment** and a poor fit for a solo founder unless the service only refers venues to licensed providers, and even that needs legal advice under the new Victorian definition.

Trade and repair dispatch, where the provider contracts directly with the business, is the usual introducer model and avoids labour hire. In real estate, Victorian rebate rules forbid agents keeping supplier benefits.

### Cited Findings
**Victoria**
- From 1 June 2026, new rules govern who is suitable to hold a labour hire licence. Applicants must declare financial viability and the regulator must be satisfied of it. — [Labour Hire Authority (1 June 2026 changes)](https://www.labourhireauthority.vic.gov.au/latest-news/be-aware-of-labour-hire-law-changes-that-come-into-effect-from-1-june-2026/); [LHA: law changes 2026](https://www.labourhireauthority.vic.gov.au/about-us/labour-hire-law-changes-2026/)
- From 1 October 2026, the general definition of labour hire "focuses on whether labour supply is involved". It covers "bare intermediary" companies that do not directly employ workers but on-supply workers employed by others. The LHA says that "in most cases, this will not affect when a labour hire licence is required, but will make clearer that businesses require a licence to supply labour anywhere in a supply chain". — [LHA (October 2026 changes)](https://www.labourhireauthority.vic.gov.au/latest-news/final-changes-to-labour-hire-law-to-take-effect-from-october-2026/)
- The LHA also flagged a proposal, subject to consultation, to bring additional construction activities in specific contexts under labour hire law from 29 October 2026. Its status was unconfirmed at the time of writing. — [LHA (October 2026 changes)](https://www.labourhireauthority.vic.gov.au/latest-news/final-changes-to-labour-hire-law-to-take-effect-from-october-2026/)
- The changes come from the Labour Hire Legislation Amendment (Licensing) Bill 2025, passed in December 2025. Ai Group says it makes the scheme "more onerous". — [Ai Group](https://www.australianindustrygroup.com.au/news/member-advices/2025/workplace-relations/nat-04125-victoria-passes-labour-hire-licensing-changes-that-will-make-the-scheme-more-onerous/)
- Reported penalties exceed A$660,000 for businesses using unlicensed providers, and A$160,000 for individuals. This comes from a single trade-press source. — [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/victoria-tightens-labour-hire-licensing-rules-for-supply-chains)

**South Australia and Queensland**
- Newly affected labour hire providers in SA had to be licensed by 29 July 2026, with significant penalties for both providers and hosts using unlicensed labour hire. — [Baker McKenzie (Jul 2026)](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/australia-labour-hire-reform-lands-in-vic-sa)
- Queensland's scheme requires fit-and-proper-person, financial viability and compliance tests. — [Labour Hire Licensing Qld](https://www.labourhire.qld.gov.au/about-licensing-scheme)

**Employment classification and gig rules**
- The Fair Work Commission's first employee-like minimum standards order took effect on 17 August 2026: the Interim On-Demand Delivery Employee-like Worker Minimum Standards Order. It covers app-based food, drink and grocery delivery workers and their platforms, with minimum pay, a gig worker information statement, insurance, consultation and other terms. The Commission is considering further applications (last-mile delivery). — [Fair Work Ombudsman](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/newsroom/news/minimum-standards-order-on-demand-delivery-workers); [Fair Work Commission](https://www.fwc.gov.au/about-us/news-and-media/news/minimum-standards-order-and-decision-made)
- Sidekicker presents itself as a "casual labour hire provider" in a client case study, which suggests incumbents use the employer-of-record labour-hire model rather than contractor marketplaces. — [Sidekicker / Pact](https://sidekicker.com/case-studies/pact)

**Real estate (Victoria)**
- Victorian agents may not retain rebates or discounts on goods or services such as advertising; these must be returned to the vendor. The authority must include a CAV-approved rebate statement, and a defective authority risks the commission. — [Consumer Affairs Victoria](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/licensing-and-registration/estate-agents/running-your-business/authorities-commissions-and-contracts/authorities-rebates-and-commission)
- (Older precedent) Courts held that a Ray White agent using a REIV-standard authority whose rebate statement departed from the prescribed wording was not entitled to commission. The REIV then changed its forms. — [Holding Redlich (2018)](https://www.holdingredlich.com.au/decision-sparks-uncertainty-for-property-industry); [Apartments.com.au](https://www.apartments.com.au/news/commissions-at-risk-after-reiv-estate-agent-commission-form-bungle)

**Short-stay**
- Victoria's 7.5% levy applies to stays under 28 nights (from 1 January 2025). Platforms collect it on Airbnb bookings; hosts handle it on direct bookings. — [Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/covers/investing/airbnb-levy-regulations-new-york-australia/); [Houst](https://www.houst.com/blog/best-airbnb-management-companies-melbourne)

### Inferences
- **Shift staffing:**
  - Option A: be the employer of record. This needs a labour hire licence in Victoria, Queensland, South Australia and the ACT [BK: ACT scheme since 2021], plus payroll, award interpretation, WorkCover and insurance. Not feasible solo.
  - Option B: be an introducer, where the venue employs the worker directly. This creates onboarding friction for one-off shifts (TFN, super, award, payroll) [BK], and treating workers as ABN contractors for hospitality shifts risks sham contracting [BK: Fair Work Act s357].
  - Option C: be a front door that refers to licensed providers (Sidekicker, Jitjatjo, agencies), who contract directly with the venue. This is probably not labour hire, but Victoria's 1 October 2026 "anywhere in a supply chain" wording makes this a legal-advice item, especially if the service takes a margin on hours or handles worker payment.
- **Construction labour:** labour-hire licensing applies, the 29 October 2026 Victorian construction expansion is proposed, and there is WHS and white-card verification [BK]. Avoid.
- **Trades, repairs and cleaning dispatch** (provider contracts and invoices the business directly): an introduction service, with no labour-hire issue as long as the platform does not supply or pay workers. Licensing of the trades themselves still matters (e.g. VBA-registered plumbers and electricians in Victoria [BK]). Verify licences for licensed categories.
- **Real estate:**
  - Never pay agents referral fees or benefits that they would have to return or disclose under rebate rules. Monetise from the agent's own subscription or from providers.
  - [BK] Agent referral relationships (e.g. to conveyancers or mortgage brokers) carry disclosure duties under Victorian conduct rules. Verify before building referral flows.
- **Locums:** AHPRA registration checks and pharmacy-regulation obligations [BK], plus possible labour hire if the service supplies locums.

### Gaps
- No authoritative reading was found on whether an AI text-front-door that merely refers a venue to a licensed staffing provider counts as "labour hire" under the amended Victorian definition. The LHA page could not be fetched. Legal advice is needed.
- Queensland and ACT 2026 changes were not confirmed (the Queensland result was a site index). ACT details are [BK].
- The A$660,000 / A$160,000 penalty figures are single-sourced.
- Victorian estate agent conduct rules on referral-fee disclosure were not verified.

## Q7. Feasibility for a solo founder and fit with the architecture (iMessage/SMS front door, AI agent outreach, masked callback, provider-pays or subscription)

### Takeaway
The best architectural fit is a **"business dispatch line" for operators of many physical sites** (STR co-hosts, café and restaurant groups, small PM agencies, sales agents' pre-sale fix-ups). It reuses the same supply (tradies, handymen, cleaners, locksmiths, appliance and refrigeration techs) and the same callback mechanic as the consumer product, while multiplying frequency through sites times trade types times short-notice gaps.

### Inferences
- **Architecture fit by segment:**
  - **STR co-hosts:** excellent. iMessage-native, multi-property, urgent. The AI agent needs a property profile (address, access codes, appliance list) and a roster of backup cleaners, handymen and locksmiths per area. Provider-pays plus a per-listing subscription.
  - **Hospitality repairs:** excellent. A venue profile (equipment brands, trading hours, access). The agent phones commercial techs, and a masked callback lets the tech triage. Business-hours and after-hours tiers.
  - **Sales agents:** good. Per-listing context: address, vendor contact, campaign deadline. The agent coordinates multiple trades per listing, and an iMessage thread per listing works well.
  - **PM agencies:** medium. Integration expectations (PropertyMe, Console, Tapi) and owner-approval workflows (quotes over a threshold) add complexity. Start with after-hours and overflow for small agencies without Tapi.
  - **Shift staffing:** poor solo fit (labour hire, payroll, supply). If pursued, do it only as a referral front door to licensed providers, after legal advice.
  - **Construction and locums:** poor (licensing, credentialing, WHS).
- **Go-to-market reach for a Melbourne solo founder [BK]:**
  - STR co-hosts cluster in Facebook groups (e.g. Airbnb host groups for Melbourne and Victoria), co-host networks and the Short Term Accommodation association, and can be found through multi-listing Airbnb profiles.
  - Hospitality operators can be reached by walking inner-north and inner-east strips (Fitzroy, Collingwood, Richmond, Brunswick) and through suppliers such as coffee roasters, who already have relationships and technician networks. Partnering with roasters is a distribution idea, unverified.
  - Agents are highly reachable via office visits and LinkedIn, but are sold to constantly.
  - PM principals are reached through REIV events.
- **Suggested wedge sequence** (inference):
  1. STR co-hosts and multi-listing hosts in inner Melbourne: highest frequency per account, owner-operator buyer, low regulatory load.
  2. Café and restaurant groups for repairs and urgent services: same supply plus commercial techs.
  3. Sales agents for pre-sale fix-ups: same supply.
  4. Small PM agencies for overflow and after-hours.
  5. Hospitality shift staffing only via referral to licensed providers, if demand pulls.
- **Unit-economics sketch** (assumption): a co-host with 15 listings at A$20 per listing per month is A$300 a month. Plus about 8–12 provider connections a month at A$20–$40 each is A$160–$480 a month. That is roughly A$5–9k a year per co-host account, versus a consumer who triggers 1–2 connections a year.

### Gaps
- The number of Melbourne STR operators with 5+ listings (co-host density) is unknown, because Inside Airbnb data was not retrieved.
- The commercial-technician supply pool for Melbourne (refrigeration, coffee, commercial kitchen) was not sized.
- No evidence was found that coffee roasters or hospitality suppliers refer technicians at scale.
- Founder time-cost of after-hours coverage (an SLA at 11pm) was not modelled.

Further conclusions from the ranking:
- **Pattern:** the winners share four traits. (1) Many sites or properties per account. (2) Many trade types per site. (3) Revenue-critical, short-notice breakdowns. (4) Owner-operator buyers who text from their phone. "Platform frequency" for business users is roughly sites × trade categories × incident rate × the share of incidents where the usual vendor is unavailable.
- **Positioning recommendation** (inference): a "dispatch line for people who run places". Text the problem and the site, and the service calls your saved vendor first, then the network, with a human calling within 15 minutes. Charge a subscription per site or listing, plus provider-pays per new connection. This turns leakage (saved vendors) into retention.
- **Avoid as a solo founder:** taking on employer-of-record shift staffing or construction labour hire in Victoria, Queensland, SA or the ACT. If hospitality venues ask for staff, route them to licensed partners, only after legal advice on Victoria's October 2026 "anywhere in a supply chain" definition.

### Gaps
- The scores are not validated by customer interviews. The founder should run about 10 discovery calls each with Melbourne co-hosts, café and restaurant group owners, and sales agents to measure actual weekly incident counts and the share of incidents not handled by known vendors.
- The strata, event-organiser and office-manager rows rely mostly on background knowledge (no searches spent on them).
- Co-host density (hosts with 5+ listings in Melbourne) would most sharpen the top-ranked segment's sizing. It can be obtained from Inside Airbnb's Melbourne dataset (not retrieved).
