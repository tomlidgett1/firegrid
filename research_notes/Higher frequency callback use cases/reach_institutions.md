# Use case: "Get a human at a company or government agency to call me" (Australia, as of October 2026)

Research date: 18 October 2026. I used 28 web searches. WebFetch failed with DNS errors (whistleout.com.au, apple.com), so the findings below rely on search-result summaries of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Notation: anything marked **[BK]** is background knowledge that I did not verify in this session. Everything else carries an inline source. Figures older than FY2025-26 are labelled with their period.

## 1. Frequency and pain: how often Australians contact institutions, hold times, unanswered calls, complaint volumes

### Takeaway
Australians spend a lot of time on hold. Vendor surveys estimate about 123 million hours a year, or about 11 hours per person. The worst pain is at Services Australia (Centrelink, Medicare): average answer times reached about 32 minutes in 2023-24, and millions of calls got "congestion" messages. Both have improved sharply since. Average waits at most private companies are now short (ACXPA mystery-shopping: about 2 minutes across the industry in 2025). Banks are the slowest private sector at about 4.5 minutes. In the private sector, the main complaint is getting an issue resolved ("no or delayed action"), not the hold itself.

### Inferences
- Services Australia handled 44.3m calls. With a population of about 27-28m **[BK]**, that is about 1.6 calls per Australian per year to Services Australia alone. Use is concentrated among welfare, pension, family-payment and Medicare customers, so a Centrelink-dependent user might call several times a year. For the general population, institutional calls (telco, bank, energy, insurer, airline, government) probably number about 0.5 to 2 per month. That is higher than tradies (1 to 2 per year) but irregular and event-driven (bill shock, travel disruption, blocked card, claims).
- The ServiceNow figure of 11.1 hours per person per year is equivalent to about 20 to 45 hold episodes a year at 15 to 30 minutes each. The source is a vendor survey that probably over-represents frequent contacters, so I treat it as an upper bound.
- The pain is **uneven**. Long holds are concentrated at Services Australia (social security lines especially), some banks, and airlines during disruption. Most private contact centres answer in about 2 minutes on average (ACXPA). The ATO is about 7 minutes. Saving 2 to 7 minutes is weak value. The real pain in the private sector is getting a problem actioned and resolved (TIO's top issue), and that takes multiple contacts over days.
- Services Australia's pain is falling quickly. Congestion messages fell 85.5% in FY2024-25, and the agency already offers free callbacks (1.6m offered). The case built on "Centrelink is impossible to reach" was stronger in 2022-24 than it is in late 2026.

## 2. Existing solutions and their status in Australia

### Takeaway
The "wait on hold for me" layer is already free and built into phones in Australia. **Apple's iOS 26 Hold Assist lists English (Australia)**, and **Google Pixel's Hold for Me is officially available in Australia**. The agentic layer (navigating menus, stating the problem, handling the call) exists only in the US: Google's Gemini "Call for Me" (Sept 2026, US Pixel 11 only), Google "Ask for Me" (US Search Labs) and Pine AI (US focus, $25M Series A). Institutions' own callback options (Services Australia PiQ, bank apps) remove more of the need. I found no Australian startup offering an AI agent that waits on hold and connects you.

### Cited Findings
**Apple Hold Assist (iOS 26, launched Sept 2025)**
- Supported languages and regions for Hold Assist / Call Screening include **English (Australia, Canada, India, Singapore, UK, US)**. — [Appleosophy (June 2025, pre-release)](https://appleosophy.com/2025/06/09/apple-brings-call-screening-hold-assist-and-live-translation-to-ios-26/); [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/exploring-ios-26-10-new-features-in-the-phone-app/)
- Conflicting source: iGeeksBlog lists a rollout in the US, UK, Canada, India, Germany and Japan, and does not name Australia. — [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/iphone-hold-assist-in-ios-26/)
- How it works: when the phone detects hold music, it offers to keep the call active, then alerts the user when a person joins. It is on by default (Settings > Apps > Phone). — [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/iphone-hold-assist-in-ios-26/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3016749)

**Google Pixel Hold for Me**
- Available on Pixel 3 and newer in **Australia** (English only), Canada, Japan, the UK and the US. The user must start it manually on each call once on hold, and it "may not detect every hold scenario". — [Google support](https://support.google.com/phoneapp/answer/10071878); [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/how-to-use-hold-for-me/)

### Inferences
- What is unsolved in Australia, as of October 2026:
  - (a) **Menu navigation and describing the problem.** Hold Assist and Hold for Me only wait. The user still dials, navigates the menu and authenticates.
  - (b) **Congestion and redial loops.** When Services Australia drops the call with a congestion message, phone-based Hold Assist cannot help. An agent that keeps redialling could.
  - (c) **Multi-step follow-up.** Chasing a telco complaint over days, escalating to the TIO or AFCA.
  - (d) **Acting for someone else.** Carers for elderly parents, or people with disability or limited English.
  - (e) **Android users without a Pixel** (most Android phones in Australia are Samsung **[BK]**).
- On the most common case (an iPhone user calls Telstra or a bank and waits), Apple has already solved the waiting part for free. iPhone has a majority share of Australian smartphones (about 55-60%) **[BK]**. That leaves a text-a-number service with only the navigation and initiation steps to add, in exchange for the friction of texting a third party and sharing account details.
- Gemini "Call for Me" already does the full agentic flow (menus, hold, transcript, takeover). The barrier to Australia is rollout policy, not technology. Google released Hold for Me in Australia early, so a later Australian release of Call for Me is plausible. That is a forecast.
- Pine's pricing (success fees or tips on savings) shows that monetisation follows a measurable dollar outcome (a negotiated bill, a refund), not time saved on hold.

- I could not verify Pine's current pricing from Pine's site, or whether Pine accepts non-US numbers.
- Bank in-app callback features (e.g., CBA, ANZ, NAB, Westpac "call me back" or in-app messaging) were not researched in this budget. **[BK]** Several major banks push in-app chat or messaging and some offer callbacks, but I did not verify this.
- I could not establish whether any Australian call-centre outsourcer or B2B vendor sells "virtual queue" products to consumers.

## 3. Technical feasibility: IVR navigation, detecting a human, bridging, identity verification, blocking, telephony costs

### Takeaway
The technology is feasible with off-the-shelf parts. Reference architectures from Telnyx and LiveKit cover DTMF and speech menu navigation, staying silent on hold, and detecting a human (LiveKit reports 95.7% F1 for the human class). Phone costs in Australia are low: about A$1.50 per hour holding on a landline leg via Twilio, and more for the leg to the user's mobile. The hard constraint is identity. Centrelink and the ATO use **voice biometrics** and verbal ID checks, and telcos and banks require the account holder or an authorised person. So the user must usually be **bridged in live**, not represented by the AI. Carrier anti-scam measures (Optus's IVR challenge for off-network Optus caller IDs, the C661 scam-call code) affect which caller ID the service can present.

### Cited Findings
**Navigating menus and detecting a human**
- Telnyx reference "outbound hold agent": two assistants. One navigates menus and sends DTMF tones. When hold is detected, the backend stops it and switches to transcription-only monitoring, then hands over to a second assistant for live conversation. — [Telnyx guide](https://telnyx.com/resources/how-to-build-outbound-hold-agent)
- LiveKit Agents answering-machine detection classifies outbound calls as human, voicemail, IVR or unavailable. It can start IVR navigation when a menu is detected. Benchmark: **95.7% F1 for human, 98.2% for IVR**. — [LiveKit blog](https://livekit.com/blog/answering-machine-detection)
- Sierra describes IVR navigation as hard. The agent must decide whether to speak, press keys or stay silent. Reported **85% success** (secondary summary). — [ZenML LLMOps database](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/ai-agents-navigating-ivr-systems-for-automated-outbound-calling)
- Practitioners report trouble detecting a live agent after a long hold when speech recognition and DTMF are mixed. — [Asterisk community](https://community.asterisk.org/t/route-call-to-agent-once-live-person-detected-at-ivr-side/79968)
- A US patent family, "Automated telephone host system interaction", covers navigating an IVR over a bridged call (or having the user's device enter responses), monitoring hold for a live agent, and muting or resuming audio. — [USPTO 11025778](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11025778); [USPTO 10498904](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10498904)
- Google Hold for Me "may not detect every hold scenario", which shows hold and human detection is still imperfect even at Google. — [Google support](https://support.google.com/phoneapp/answer/10071878)

**Identity verification (why the user must be bridged)**
- Services Australia uses a **voice biometric** system that matches a caller's voice to a stored voiceprint. Enrolment is by phone or the Express Plus app. — [Australian Government Architecture: Services Australia voice biometric solution](https://architecture.digital.gov.au/services-australia-voice-biometric-solution)
- The ATO voice authentication is optional and enrolled after giving a TFN. It matches the caller's voice to a voiceprint. The 2026 privacy notice collects TFN or ABN, date of birth and a voice sample. — [ATO voice authentication](https://www.ato.gov.au/online-services/voice-authentication); [ATO privacy notice – voice biometrics](https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/commitments-and-reporting/in-detail/privacy-and-information-gathering/privacy-notices/privacy-notice-voice-biometrics)
- The ATO held voiceprints for about one in seven Australians (older Computerworld report, date uncertain). — [Computerworld](https://www2.computerworld.com.au/article/633461/ato-now-holds-voiceprint-one-seven-australians/)
- In 2023, Guardian Australia showed that an AI-cloned voice plus a customer reference number could access a Centrelink self-service phone account. Services Australia says it detects impersonation and recordings and applies extra tests for unusual behaviour. — [Slashdot summary of Guardian report](https://it.slashdot.org/story/23/03/17/2117211/ai-fools-voice-recognition-used-to-verify-identity-by-australian-tax-office)
- At Telstra, only the Account Owner, Full Authority, Limited Authority or Third Party Authority contacts can deal on an account. Third Party Authority **cannot be added in My Telstra** and must be set up separately. — [Telstra support](https://telstra.com.au/support/account-payment/give-another-person-access-to-account)

**Carrier blocking and caller ID**
- Industry Code **C661:2022 Reducing Scam Calls and Scam SMs** requires telcos to identify, trace and block scam calls and handle caller-ID (CLI) spoofing. Telcos reported blocking more than **2.2 billion scam calls** from Dec 2020 to Nov 2024. — [ACMA caller ID scams](https://www.acma.gov.au/caller-id-scams)
- From 31 July 2025, Optus is progressively introducing an **IVR challenge** for calls that present an Optus mobile caller ID but originate outside the Optus network (secondary source). — [Aircall support](https://support.aircall.io/en-gb/articles/29225068597533)
- The ACMA has found carriers in breach for failing to monitor scam calls by CLI (Symbio, NetSIP, 2024). — [ACMA NetSIP investigation](https://acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-12/NetSIP%20-%20Final%20investigation%20report%20-%20Reducing%20Scams%20Code%20-%201%20November%202024.pdf)

**Telephony costs**
- Twilio Australia pay-as-you-go outbound: about **US$0.0252/min to landlines**, **US$0.0750/min to mobiles**, US$0.0240/min outbound toll-free. Inbound: local number US$0.01/min plus $3/month, mobile number US$0.05/min plus $20/month. — [Twilio Voice pricing AU](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/voice/pricing/au)
- Plivo's Australian rates are lower than Twilio's. — [Plivo AU voice pricing](https://www.plivo.com/voice/pricing/au/)

### Inferences
- **Cost per hour of hold:** the institution leg costs about US$1.51/hour at Twilio's landline rate. AI monitoring during hold can be cheap audio classification rather than full speech-to-text or an LLM. A typical Centrelink case (45 minutes holding, then 15 minutes talking with the user bridged on mobile) costs about 60 × 0.0252 + 15 × 0.075 ≈ **US$2.65 in phone charges**, plus model costs. With wholesale SIP (Plivo, Telnyx), costs fall further. Phone costs are not the barrier.
- **The 13/1300/1800 number caveat:** most Australian institutions use 13/1300/1800 numbers **[BK]**. How Twilio prices calls to these smartnumbers (as opposed to geographic landlines) was not verified. They may be priced differently or restricted.
- **The user must join live:** voice biometrics (Centrelink, ATO), verbal ID questions (banks, telcos) and authority rules all mean the AI cannot pass identity checks for the user. The AI also should not try, because cloning the user's voice to beat biometrics would be fraud or a breach of terms. The workable flow is: the AI navigates the menu and holds, detects a human, says "connecting the customer now", and bridges the user, who then verifies. A risk is that agents may hang up during the few seconds it takes to bridge, or treat an AI announcer as suspicious. This needs testing.
- **Congestion messages:** Services Australia's congestion messages need repeated redialling. Many automated redials from one number range could look like abuse or toll fraud to a carrier or to the agency's systems. Spreading calls across a pool of numbers looks like evasion. This is a risk to manage.
- **Caller ID:** the service should present its own legitimately held Australian number when calling the institution. Presenting the user's own mobile number would be spoofing and runs into the C661 code and Optus's IVR challenge. But institutions often use the caller ID for automatic account lookup **[BK]**, so this lookup advantage is lost.
- For a solo founder, IVR navigation of the top ~30 Australian institutions is a finite, mappable problem. The menus can be pre-scripted and maintained, which is an operational moat but also ongoing maintenance.

### Gaps
- I found no Australian institution's published policy on AI or automated callers or AI agents calling on a customer's behalf.
- I did not verify how long an Australian contact-centre agent typically waits before hanging up on silence (relevant to bridge latency).
- I did not verify Twilio or Telnyx pricing and permissions for calls to 13/1300 numbers.
- The IP risk from the "Automated telephone host system interaction" patent family in Australia (whether there are AU equivalents) was not researched.

## 4. Legal: acting as a customer's agent, privacy and ID rules, call recording, institutions blocking the service

### Takeaway
Australian institutions have formal authority-to-act schemes. Services Australia has "person permitted to enquire" and nominees, set up separately for each service. Telstra has Full, Limited and Third Party Authority levels. These schemes are designed for a known individual, not an AI company acting at scale. So the legally safe model is a **"connector" model**: the AI only navigates and holds, and the customer speaks and verifies for themselves. Call-recording law varies by state (WA and SA are strictest, Qld, Vic and NT more permissive), and federal interception law applies to a third party in the middle of a call. Institutions could block the service by its caller ID, as Google lets businesses opt out of "Ask for Me".

### Cited Findings
- **Services Australia:** there are two common arrangements. A **"person permitted to enquire"** can be set up with the SS313 form, online or **by calling the main payment line**. A **nominee** can be a correspondence or payment nominee, and a payment nominee must act in the customer's best interest and keep records. Authority is **service-specific**: a Centrelink nominee cannot act for Medicare or Child Support unless separately authorised. Both parties can set up a nominee online if each has a myGov-linked Centrelink account, with 14 days to accept. — [Services Australia "Someone to act on your behalf" factsheet (Jan 2026)](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-01/services-australia-someone-to-act-on-your-behalf-factsheet.pdf); [Services Australia Operational Blueprint](https://operational.servicesaustralia.gov.au/public/Pages/complaints-privacy-and-foi/104-17071700-01.html)
- **Telstra:** authority levels are Account Owner, Full Authority, Limited Authority and Third Party Authority. Third Party Authority cannot be added in My Telstra. — [Telstra support](https://telstra.com.au/support/account-payment/give-another-person-access-to-account)
- **Older industry practice (2008):** an Optus-style nominated person could request, change and supply information. They could not do anything requiring the account holder's signature or verbal electronic authorisation. — [ALRC Report 108, ch. 70](https://www.alrc.gov.au/publication/for-your-information-australian-privacy-law-and-practice-alrc-report-108/70-third-party-representatives/third-party-decision-making-under-the-privacy-act/)
- **Call recording:** no single national rule. Qld, Vic and NT are generally described as allowing a party to record (one-party consent), with restrictions on sharing. WA and SA are strictest (all-party consent). Sources **conflict** on NSW, ACT and Tasmania (all-party consent, or a party may record if reasonably necessary to protect their lawful interests). The federal Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act also applies. The common advice for calls that may span states is to announce recording and get consent from everyone. — [Sprintlaw](https://www.sprintlaw.com.au/articles/is-it-legal-to-record-a-phone-call-in-australia); [Sonetel](https://sonetel.com/en/help/help-topics/telephony/free-call-recording/is-it-legal-to-record-calls/is-it-legal-to-record-calls-in-australia/); [Telnyx](https://telnyx.com/resources/phone-recording-law-australia); [Fireflies](https://fireflies.ai/blog/is-it-illegal-to-record-a-conversation)
- **Disclosure norms:** Google's Ask for Me and Gemini Call for Me both **identify themselves as AI at the start of calls**, and Ask for Me lets businesses opt out. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-wants-ai-to-make-some-of-your-phone-calls-again/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/google-tests-letting-gemini-make-phone-calls-initially-for-us-pixel-owners/)
- **Claims risk:** the FTC's DoNotPay order shows regulators will act on overstated AI capability claims (US). — [ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/robot-lawyer-website-donotpay-settles-ftc-claims-it-couldnt-deliver-on-promises)

### Inferences
- **The connector model is the safe design.** If the AI only navigates menus and waits, and the customer joins before any account discussion, the service never needs authority to act. It also never receives account information over the call and avoids impersonation. Pitch it as "we get you to the front of the line", not "we handle it for you", until formal authority is set up.
- **The agent model is harder.** Having the AI discuss account details would need the customer to register the service, or a named person at it, as an authorised representative with each institution. Services Australia and Telstra schemes assume a natural person. There is no API for a company to act as agent. For carers this is different: the carer is often already a nominee, and the service would bridge the carer.
- **Recording and transcripts:** if the service records or transcribes the bridged call (useful for follow-up), it is a third party on the call. The safest course is an announcement at the start ("this call is being recorded by [service] on behalf of the customer") and consent from all parties. Without that, the service risks breaching the strict states' laws and, possibly, the federal interception law **[BK: the TIA Act treats listening to or recording a call without the knowledge of the parties as interception. Whether a bridging provider is a "party" is unresolved and needs legal advice]**.
- **Privacy Act:** the service will collect sensitive personal information (Centrelink status, debts, health via Medicare). The Australian Privacy Principles probably apply even to a small startup **[BK: the small-business exemption under A$3m turnover may not apply to organisations that trade in personal information or provide health services. Needs advice]**.
- **Blocking risk:** institutions can block known caller-ID ranges or ask agents to end calls with AI announcers. Services Australia has policy reasons to resist third-party queue jumping, which could be framed as unfair to other callers or as congestion-gaming. Banks have fraud-team reasons to distrust intermediaries. I found no evidence that any Australian institution has done this yet.

### Gaps
- I did not research bank-specific authority rules (the Banking Code of Practice "trusted contact" or authority provisions, or the big four's third-party authority forms).
- I found no Australian legal commentary specifically on AI agents calling institutions for consumers.
- I did not check Services Australia's terms of use or position on third-party automated callers, or whether congestion redialling breaches any rule.
- The conflict among sources on recording law in NSW, ACT and Tasmania needs a lawyer's read of the statutes (e.g., Surveillance Devices Act 2007 (NSW), Invasion of Privacy Act 1971 (Qld)).

## 5. Monetization: willingness to pay, B2B buyers, evidence from Pine and similar services

### Takeaway
There is little direct evidence that Australians will pay to skip hold queues. The free OS features (Hold Assist, Hold for Me) and free institutional callbacks set the reference price for "waiting" at zero. The best evidence of willingness to pay (Pine's $25M raise, its success or tip billing and a reported $19-49/month premium) is tied to **dollar outcomes** such as bill reductions and refunds, not to time saved. B2B channels (carers, employers, small businesses dealing with the ATO) are plausible but untested here.

### Cited Findings
- Pine monetises through success-based or tip pricing, pay as you go (one listing). — [Futurepedia](https://www.futurepedia.io/tool/pine-ai). A free tier plus premium plans at **$19-49/month** and enterprise plans for small businesses (another listing). — [AI Indigo](https://aiindigo.com/tool/pineai)
- Pine's traction claims: more than $3M saved, a 93% negotiation success rate, 270 minutes saved on average. $25M Series A in Dec 2025. — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251203384902/en)
- Gemini Call for Me requires a **Google AI subscription** (US). This shows Google bundles agentic calling into a paid AI tier rather than selling it separately. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/google-tests-letting-gemini-make-phone-calls-initially-for-us-pixel-owners/)
- Preference evidence (not willingness to pay): 37% to 75% prefer callbacks in vendor surveys. — [TCN](https://www.tcn.com/?p=19438); [Nextiva](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/consumers-prefer-callbacks-over-hold.html). An academic study found bank customers preferred holding. — [UNC Kenan-Flagler](https://www.kenan-flagler.unc.edu/news/dont-call-us/)
- Productivity framing: about A$1.28b in lost productivity from time on hold in 2023 (vendor estimate). This could support an employer-benefit pitch. — [Business News Australia](https://www.businessnewsaus.com.au/articles/aussies-waiting-on-hold-for-longer--costing-the-economy--1-28b--says-report.html)
- The ATO runs a separate registered-agent line (tax practitioner wait about 7:30 in Aug 2026), which shows that professionals who call the ATO often have their own channel. — [ATO Tax Time 2026 update, 25 Aug](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-and-super-professionals/for-tax-professionals/prepare-and-lodge/tax-time/tpsg-tax-time-updates/tax-time-2026-update-25-august)

### Inferences
- **Consumer subscription:** hard. At 0.5 to 2 institutional calls a month, mostly short (about 2 minutes outside Centrelink and banks), and with Hold Assist free on iPhone, a subscription of A$5-10/month is a tough sell for the general public. Per use (A$2-5 per call) is more aligned with the irregular pattern, but the sums are small. Centrelink-dependent users have the most hold pain and the least ability to pay, which is a structural mismatch.
- **Outcome pricing** (a share of savings or refunds, as Pine does) is the proven model. It requires the agent to *negotiate or resolve*, not just connect. That moves the product from "get a human to call me" into "fix my bill", which is a different and more defensible product, with heavier authority and legal requirements.
- **Plausible B2B buyers (untested):**
  - (a) Carers and families acting for elderly parents. The Services Australia nominee scheme exists, and the carer could be the bridged party. Possibly via aged-care providers or disability support coordinators.
  - (b) Employers as a wellbeing or productivity benefit (weak; the benefits budget is crowded).
  - (c) Small businesses dealing with suppliers, telcos and the ATO. But accountants already use the registered-agent line.
  - (d) Community legal centres and financial counsellors, who spend time on hold to Centrelink and creditors for clients. Likely budget-constrained.
- **Leakage is low but substitution is high:** the user cannot bypass the service through a direct line, but they can always just dial the 13 number themselves with Hold Assist running. The competition is "do it myself with a free tool", not another provider.

### Gaps
- I found no Australian survey on willingness to pay for queue-skipping or AI calling.
- I found no Pine usage frequency data (calls per user per month), retention data or verified price list.
- I did not find evidence of Australian employers, aged-care providers or financial counsellors buying similar services.

## 6. Overall assessment: frequency, leakage, willingness to pay, Apple/Google risk, fit with an iMessage + AI agent + call-bridging architecture

### Takeaway
Calling institutions is **more frequent than tradies but still irregular** (probably 0.5 to 2 times a month for a typical adult, more for Centrelink-dependent households). Leakage through direct lines is low. But the core value, "wait on hold for me", is already **free and built into iPhones and Pixels in Australia**, and Google is shipping full agentic calling (menus, hold, conversation) in the US. A text-a-number connector that only navigates and holds has thin, shrinking value and weak willingness to pay. A defensible version would have to own **outcomes and follow-up** (chasing a telco complaint, carers handling Centrelink for a parent, refund and bill disputes). That is a different product with heavier legal and authority requirements. It is a decent *add-on feature* for the existing callback architecture, and a weak *standalone wedge*.

### Cited Findings
- Apple Hold Assist (iOS 26) lists English (Australia), and Google Hold for Me is live in Australia. — [Appleosophy](https://appleosophy.com/2025/06/09/apple-brings-call-screening-hold-assist-and-live-translation-to-ios-26/); [Google support](https://support.google.com/phoneapp/answer/10071878)
- Gemini Call for Me navigates menus and waits on hold. It is US-only as of Sept 2026. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/google-tests-letting-gemini-make-phone-calls-initially-for-us-pixel-owners/)
- Services Australia congestion messages fell 85.5% in FY2024-25, and it offers free callbacks (1.6m offered). — [transparency.gov.au](https://www.transparency.gov.au/publications/social-services/services_australia/services-australia-annual-report-2024-25/part-2-%E2%80%93-our-performance/2.1-annual-performance-statements-2024%E2%80%9325); [Annual Report 2024-25](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-10/annual-report-2024-25.pdf)
- Private-sector average wait was about 1:49 in 2025. Banks are about 3x other sectors. — [ACXPA](https://acxpa.com.au/glossary/average-speed-of-answer/)
- The top telco complaint is "no or delayed action by provider". — [WhistleOut](https://www.whistleout.com.au/Broadband/News/tio-report-november-2024)
- Australians spent about 11.1 hours per person on hold in 2024 (vendor survey). — [ServiceNow](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2025/Australian-wait-times-soar-to-123-million-hours-on-hold/default.aspx)

### Inferences
- **Frequency per user per month:** about 0.5 to 2 institutional calls a month for a typical adult (inference from Services Australia's 1.6 calls per capita per year plus telco, bank, energy and insurer contacts). Only a minority of those calls have long holds. For Centrelink-dependent users, frequency and pain are much higher.
- **Fit with the architecture:** good technically. The same stack (inbound iMessage, an AI agent, an outbound call, detecting a human, bridging the user's mobile) serves both tradies and institutions, and the extra cost per call is about US$2-3 in phone charges.
  - The UX also fits: "text: get Telstra to call me" is a nice one-liner.
  - The weakness is the "call me back" framing. Institutions do not call *you*. The service calls them and conferences you in, so the user must answer when the bridge happens, possibly 40 minutes later. That works when the user is free but fails when the human answers during the user's meeting. Mitigation: ask the user for windows of availability, and fall back to institutional callbacks such as PiQ.
- **Competition risk:**
  - Apple already does the waiting step on the iPhone.
  - Google already does the whole flow in the US, and has shipped an earlier version (Hold for Me) to Australia.
  - Carriers or banks could add their own "virtual queue" features.
  - A startup's only lasting differences are: Australian institution-specific menu maps; cross-platform and SMS/iMessage access (no app, works on any phone, including for elderly parents); persistent multi-day case follow-up; and acting for carers.
- **Where it might still work:**
  - (1) As a **retention and frequency feature** inside the broader "text a number, a human calls you" product. It gives users a reason to text more than twice a year, even if it is not the main revenue source.
  - (2) As a **carer-focused service** ("handle Mum's Centrelink, Medicare, My Aged Care, Telstra"), where the carer has authority and willingness to pay is higher. My Aged Care was not researched.
  - (3) As an **outcome agent** for bill disputes and refunds, using Pine's model in Australia, where no local competitor was found.

### Scores (1-10; higher is better for the founder)
- **Need frequency: 5/10.** Clearly more frequent than tradies (monthly-ish versus yearly), but irregular. Most private-sector waits are now short. The heavy pain is concentrated in Centrelink, banks and airline disruptions.
- **Low leakage: 7/10.** Institutions never hand out direct lines, so there is no disintermediation by the counterparty. The real leak is to self-service: the user dials themselves with free Hold Assist or uses the institution's own callback.
- **Willingness to pay: 3/10.** Waiting is priced at zero by OS features and institutional callbacks. The users with the most pain (Centrelink) have the least money. Proven willingness to pay (Pine) requires dollar outcomes, not connection.
- **Solution gap in Australia: 5/10.** Waiting is solved free on iPhone and Pixel. Menu navigation, congestion redialling, multi-day follow-up, carers and non-Pixel Android are unsolved locally, and Gemini Call for Me and Pine are not in Australia yet.
- **Feasibility for a solo founder: 5/10.** The technical pieces are off the shelf (Telnyx, LiveKit, Twilio, about US$1.50/hour of hold). But identity rules force live bridging, and voice biometrics block representation. There is a patchwork of state recording laws, the risk of carrier anti-spoofing measures and institutions blocking, Privacy Act obligations for sensitive data, and per-institution menu maintenance.
- **Defensibility against Apple/Google: 2/10.** Apple and Google already ship the core (Hold Assist, Hold for Me) in Australia for free. Google has shipped the full agentic version in the US. Only carers, outcomes or follow-up, and Australian institutional know-how offer partial defence.

### Gaps
- Without Services Australia's FY2025-26 figures and current Estimates answers, I cannot say whether Centrelink pain kept falling in 2025-26 or rebounded.
- I found no data on how many Australian iPhone users have, or use, Hold Assist, or whether it works reliably with Australian contact centres' hold audio.
- I found no Google statement on an Australian timeline for Gemini Call for Me.
- Interviews or Reddit/Whirlpool research on actual user willingness to pay was not done within the search budget.
